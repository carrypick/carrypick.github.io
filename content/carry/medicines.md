## Airline by airline

We read each airline's own page on **26 November 2026**. The words in quotes are theirs.

| Airline | Cabin bag | Check-in bag | What the airline's page says |
|---|---|---|---|
| IndiGo | Yes | Only with a prescription | "Medicines (with proper prescription)": allowed in both. "Non-prescribed medicines": cabin yes, check-in no. |
| SpiceJet | Yes | Only with a prescription | "Non-prescribed medicines": cabin yes, check-in no. "Prescriptions for medications, inhalers" in the clear 1-litre bag, and diabetes supplies such as "pre-loaded syringes": allowed in both. |
| Air India Express | Yes | Only with a prescription | "Medicines": allowed in both, "with proper prescription". "Non-prescribed medicines": cabin allowed, check-in not allowed. |
| Air India | Yes | Not stated | Liquid medicine over 100 ml, "whether prescribed by a doctor or acquired over the counter", is allowed if "essential to be carried during the journey" and no more than the journey needs. |
| Akasa Air | Yes | At your own risk | "Medicines required onboard (inhalers, small medication packs)" can be your personal item. Medicines are on its list of items carried in check-in "at their own risk". |
| BCAS (airport security) | Yes | – | Liquids over 100 ml are banned in the cabin, with one exception: "Medicine/inhaler accompanied by prescription and baby food". |

## Tablets and strips

Tablets, capsules and strips are not liquids, so the 100 ml rule doesn't touch them. Carry them in your cabin bag in their original strips or bottles, so the name can be read. A prescription isn't needed for the cabin on any airline above. Vitamin tablets and other over-the-counter pills count as non-prescribed medicines: cabin yes, and on IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express, not in check-in.

## Cough syrup and other liquid medicine

- **100 ml or less:** put it in your clear 1-litre liquids bag with your other liquids.
- **Over 100 ml:** allowed in the cabin if you need it on the journey. BCAS's exception is for medicine "accompanied by prescription". Air India accepts over-the-counter liquid medicine too, in a quantity just enough for the journey, and asks that the name on a prescription label matches your boarding pass.
- **Show it at security.** Take the bottle out of your bag at the X-ray, keep it in its labelled bottle, and carry the prescription or a doctor's note.
- **A big stock for a long trip:** pack the extra in check-in, with the prescription. On IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express, check-in medicines need one.

## Insulin, insulin pens and syringes

SpiceJet's list allows diabetes supplies in both bags: "pre-loaded syringes, an unlimited supply of unused syringes" when carried with insulin, plus lancets, glucose meters, test strips and insulin pumps. Air India allows things used for medical needs that aren't medicines, "such as ice", so a cool pack for insulin is fine. Keep insulin in your cabin bag so it stays with you, and tell the security staff about the needles before screening.

## Inhalers, homeopathy and medical devices

- **Inhalers** are named in BCAS's exception and on Akasa Air's personal-item list. Carry them in the cabin.
- **Homeopathy:** the pages above don't name it. Pills are tablets. Liquid dilutions are liquids, so 100 ml or less in the liquids bag, or a bigger bottle with the prescription.
- **Medical equipment** without mercury is allowed in check-in (IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express). A **mercury thermometer** is allowed in check-in only, one per person in its case (SpiceJet).
- **Medical oxygen** needs the airline's approval first. SpiceJet doesn't accept a passenger's own cylinder; it can supply oxygen on request.

## Flying abroad with medicines

Indian airlines use the same lists on international flights. The country you fly to has its own rules, and some medicines sold freely in India are controlled abroad. We haven't checked each country's list, so check the embassy or customs website of the country you're visiting. Carry the prescription, keep the medicine in its original pack, and take only what your trip needs. Flying back into India? See our [Indian customs rules](../customs.html).

## Questions

### Is cough syrup allowed in flight on IndiGo?

Yes, in your cabin bag. IndiGo's list allows medicines in the cabin with or without a prescription. A bottle of 100 ml or less goes in your liquids bag; a bigger one needs to be needed on the journey, with the prescription. In an IndiGo check-in bag, medicines need a prescription.

### Can I carry medicines in check-in baggage?

Yes, with a prescription on IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express. Without one, those airlines don't allow medicines in check-in. Akasa Air carries medicines in check-in at your own risk. Keep the ones you need in your cabin bag anyway, because a check-in bag can be late or lost.

### Can I carry medicines in hand baggage without a prescription?

Yes. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express all allow non-prescribed medicines in the cabin. Liquid medicine over 100 ml is the exception: carry the prescription for that.

### Where should I keep tablets in a flight?

In your cabin bag or handbag, in their strips or labelled bottles. On Akasa Air, small medicine packs and inhalers you need on board can be your one personal item.

### Can I carry an insulin pen on a flight?

Yes, in your cabin bag. SpiceJet's list allows pre-loaded syringes and unused syringes with insulin in both bags. Tell security about the needles, and carry the prescription.

### Are vitamin tablets allowed in flight?

Yes, in the cabin bag. They count as non-prescribed medicines, which IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express don't allow in check-in.

### Can I carry medicines on an international flight?

Yes, under the same airline rules. Also check the rules of the country you're going to, because some medicines are controlled there. Carry the prescription and the original packs.
