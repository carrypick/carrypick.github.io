How much alcohol is allowed on a domestic flight in India? Up to 5 litres per person, in your check-in bag, in the bottle it was sold in. IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet and Air India Express all use this rule.

## Check-in: up to 5 litres

IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet and Air India Express all use the same main rule for domestic flights:

- Up to **5 litres per person** of drinks with **24% to 70% alcohol**, such as whisky, rum, vodka and gin.
- In its **retail packaging**: the bottle it was sold in, not poured into another bottle. All five airlines ask for this.
- **Nothing over 70% alcohol**, in any bag.

Does the bottle have to be unopened? IndiGo's dangerous goods table says an opened shop bottle is fine in check-in if it's packed so it can't leak. The other airlines don't say, so an unopened bottle is the safe choice.

## How many bottles is 5 litres?

| Bottle size | Most you can pack | Total |
|---|---|---|
| 180 ml (quarter) | 27 bottles | 4.86 litres |
| 375 ml (half) | 13 bottles | 4.88 litres |
| 750 ml (full) | 6 bottles | 4.5 litres |
| 1 litre | 5 bottles | 5 litres |

You can mix sizes, as long as the total stays at 5 litres or less. For example, four 750 ml bottles and two 1 litre bottles make exactly 5 litres. A seventh 750 ml bottle takes you to 5.25 litres, which is over the limit.

The 5 litres is **per person**. Two people flying together can each pack up to 5 litres. It's simplest if each person's bottles go in their own checked bag, so nobody's bag goes over the limit.

Bottles are heavy, too. The drink alone in six 750 ml bottles weighs more than 4 kg, and the glass adds more. On a 15 kg fare, that leaves much less room for your clothes, so weigh the packed bag and check it with our [excess baggage calculator](../../tools/bag-weight.html).

## Beer and wine

Beer and wine usually have less than 24% alcohol. The airlines don't all treat them the same way:

- **IndiGo and SpiceJet**: their dangerous goods tables say drinks of 24% or less aren't covered by these limits.
- **[Akasa Air](../akasa-air.html)**: accepts them in check-in, in retail packaging, with no litre limit on its page.
- **[Air India Express](../air-india-express.html)**: its 5 litres per person covers all alcohol up to 70%, so beer and wine count towards it.
- **[Air India](../air-india.html)**: its page gives only the 24 to 70% rule.

Cans and bottles still count towards your check-in weight, and state rules still apply. Ask your airline before you pack a lot of beer or wine.

## Cabin bag: only if you bought it after security

You can't carry alcohol from outside the airport in your cabin bag (IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet, Air India Express). The one exception is alcohol you **buy after the security check**, in the airport shops. SpiceJet says this exception is a BCAS rule:

- It stays sealed in the shop's clear tamper-evident bag. Akasa Air allows a bag of up to 1 litre.
- Keep the receipt.
- SpiceJet counts alcohol bought at the airport as part of your 5 litres. Its other limits, from the 7 kg cabin bag to the 30 kg Dubai allowance, are on our [SpiceJet baggage rules](../spicejet.html) page.
- BCAS's list of items banned in the cabin treats all liquids the same way: in the cabin, each must be 100 ml or less, inside one clear 1-litre bag (we read an archived copy on 21 November 2026). The airlines' rules above are stricter for alcohol, so follow them.
- Flying in from abroad and changing to a domestic flight in India? Move duty-free alcohol into your check-in bag before the next security check. The airport security list and Akasa Air both say it may not be allowed in the cabin then.

See the [duty-free alcohol rule](../can-i-carry.html?q=duty) in the full list.

How much can you bring in from abroad? Indian Customs allows **2 litres** per passenger duty free. See our [customs rules for alcohol and cigarettes](../customs/alcohol-and-cigarettes.html). Bringing duty-free cigarettes too? Lighters have their own rules: see [cigarettes and lighters](cigarettes-and-lighter.html).

## You can't drink it on board

Akasa Air's rules ban drinking alcohol on its planes, and SpiceJet's ban it on domestic flights. Bottles you carry must stay closed (SpiceJet), and an airline can refuse to fly a passenger who is drunk. Keep the bottle sealed until you reach your destination.

## Dry states

Flying to or from a dry state such as **Bihar or Gujarat**? You can't take alcohol there, even in check-in. SpiceJet's rules name Bihar, Gujarat, Tripura, Mizoram, Nagaland and Lakshadweep, and any other dry state.

Akasa Air's rules also note that other laws can lower the 5-litre limit. In India, each state sets its own rules on how much liquor you can bring in, so check the rules of the state you're flying to before you pack several bottles.

## Flying home from Goa with bottles

Many people buy bottles in Goa to take home. The airline rule is the same as anywhere else: up to 5 litres per person, in the shop's bottles, in your check-in bag, and nothing in your cabin bag unless you bought it after security at Goa's airport.

The airline rule isn't the only one, though. The state you fly home to can limit how much liquor bought in another state you bring in, and some ask for a permit. Before you buy several bottles:

- Check the excise rules of the state you're flying to.
- Keep the shop bill with the bottles.
- Don't fly into Bihar or Gujarat with alcohol at all.

## How to pack bottles

- Keep each bottle in its shop packing. An unopened bottle is least likely to leak.
- Wrap it in clothes and put it in the middle of the bag, away from the sides and wheels.
- Put each bottle in a zip bag in case it leaks.
- A hard trolley protects glass better than a soft duffle bag.
- Glass bottles are heavy. Weigh your bag before you leave.

## Questions

### How much alcohol can I carry on a domestic flight in India?

Up to 5 litres per person of 24 to 70% alcohol, in its shop packing, in your check-in bag (IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet, Air India Express).

### How many bottles of alcohol can I carry on a flight?

Six 750 ml bottles (4.5 litres) or five 1 litre bottles per person, in your check-in bag. Seven 750 ml bottles come to 5.25 litres, which is over the 5-litre limit.

### How many bottles can I bring back from Goa by flight?

The airline limit is the same 5 litres per person, so six 750 ml bottles. The state you fly home to may allow less, or ask for a permit, so check its excise rules before you buy.

### Can I carry alcohol in my cabin bag?

Not alcohol from outside the airport. Alcohol you buy after security at the airport can go in the cabin, sealed in the shop's tamper-evident bag.

### Can I carry beer or wine on a flight?

In check-in. IndiGo's and SpiceJet's dangerous goods tables don't limit drinks of 24% alcohol or less, and Akasa Air accepts them in retail packaging. Air India Express counts them in its 5 litres per person, and Air India's page doesn't mention them, so ask your airline before you pack a lot.

### Is a beer can allowed in check-in baggage?

Yes, in check-in, in its shop packing. IndiGo and SpiceJet don't limit drinks of 24% alcohol or less, and Air India Express counts beer in its 5 litres per person. Cans add weight, so weigh your bag.

### What is Akasa Air's alcohol policy?

In check-in, Akasa Air accepts drinks of 24% alcohol or less in retail packaging, and up to 5 litres per person of drinks of 24 to 70%, also in retail packaging. In the cabin, only alcohol bought after security, sealed in the shop's tamper-evident bag of up to 1 litre. Drinking alcohol on its planes isn't allowed. See [Akasa Air baggage rules](../akasa-air.html).

### What alcohol is allowed in checked baggage?

Spirits such as whisky, rum, vodka and gin of 24 to 70% alcohol, up to 5 litres per person, in the bottle they were sold in. Beer and wine (usually under 24%) are also accepted in check-in, though Air India Express counts them in its 5 litres. Nothing over 70% alcohol is allowed.

### Can I carry alcohol to Bihar or Gujarat?

No. They are dry states, so you can't carry alcohol to or from them.

### Can I drink my own alcohol on the flight?

No. Akasa Air bans drinking alcohol on its planes, SpiceJet bans it on domestic flights, and an airline can refuse to fly a passenger who is drunk.
