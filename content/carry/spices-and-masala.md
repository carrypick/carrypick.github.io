Can I carry spices in a flight? **Cabin bag: no. All spices, powder or whole, including chilli and masala, are banned in the cabin at Indian airports. Check-in bag: yes, sealed and packed so they can't spill.** This is the airport security rule, so it applies on every airline. Checked on 28 November 2026.

## Cabin bag: no spices at all

- Air India's list says **all spices, in powder or physical form, including chilli**, can't go in the cabin bag. They are allowed in check-in.
- The airport security list (based on BCAS rules) bans all spices in powder or physical form, including chilli, in the cabin bag.
- IndiGo's list doesn't name spices, but it keeps groceries out of the cabin and allows them in check-in.

"Physical form" means whole spices too, so jeera, elaichi, cloves and dry red chillies follow the same rule as masala powder.

## Check-in: yes

Spices are fine in your check-in bag on every airline we checked. No list sets a weight limit, but they count towards your check-in weight.

## Other powders

Air India's list also keeps **talcum powder** out of the cabin and allows it in check-in. If you carry other powders, keep them in their sealed packs so security can see what they are.

## How to pack spices in check-in

- Keep shop packets sealed. Put homemade masala in airtight jars or tight boxes.
- Put each packet in a zip bag, then put all the spice bags together in one more bag, so the smell doesn't get into your clothes.
- Tape the lids of chilli powder jars. A spill gets into everything.
- Pack heavy jars near the wheels of the trolley and soft clothes around them.

Whatever the airline's page says, the airport security rule below applies to every passenger at Indian airports.

## What airport security (BCAS) says

The Kannur Airport security list, based on BCAS rules, lists **"All Spices in powder or physical form including chilly"** under items banned in the cabin. Chilli powder can be thrown in someone's eyes, which is why it's a security item and not just a food rule. In check-in, spices aren't on any banned list we read.

## Taking spices on an international flight

**Leaving India,** the cabin ban still applies: pack spices in check-in, even on an international flight. **Arriving abroad,** each country has its own food rules. We read them on 28 November 2026:

| Country | Spices on arrival | What the official page says |
|---|---|---|
| USA | Yes, packaged | US Customs (CBP): "condiments, vinegars, oils, packaged spices, honey, coffee and tea are admissible." Declare all food. TSA allows dry spices in both bags; powders over 350 ml (12 oz) are screened separately in carry-on. |
| UK | Packaged flavourings: yes | GOV.UK allows "packaged soup, stocks and flavourings" and processed, packaged plant products from any country. Curry leaves are allowed without a plant health certificate. Spices aren't named on their own. |
| Singapore | Not named | The Singapore Food Agency's list doesn't name spices. Processed food together can be up to 5 kg per traveller. Keep spices sealed and declare them if asked. |
| UAE | Small amounts for yourself | Dubai Customs' 2025 guide lists food products as needing permits, and commercial quantities aren't allowed. We found no kg limit for spices. See our [Dubai customs guide](../customs/uae-dubai.html). |
| Saudi Arabia | Small amounts for yourself | The GCC customs guide (2025) allows personal belongings and gifts in non-commercial quantities. It sets no food limit. |

So shop-sealed packets of masala, with an ingredients label, in your check-in bag, are the easiest to take anywhere. Leave out spice mixes with dried meat or fish.

## What to do instead

- Flying with only a cabin bag? Buy the spices at your destination, or send them with someone who has a check-in bag.
- Cooking on the trip? Pack small sealed packets in check-in instead of big jars.
- Carrying pickle or ghee too? See our [pickle rules](pickle.html) and [ghee rules](ghee.html). Dry fruits are simpler: see [can I carry dry fruits?](dry-fruits.html)

## Questions

### Can I carry masala powder in my cabin bag?

No. All spices, including masala and chilli powder, are banned in the cabin bag. Pack them in check-in.

### Can I carry spices in my check-in bag?

Yes. Air India and the airport security list allow spices in check-in, and IndiGo allows groceries there.

### Can I carry whole spices like jeera or elaichi in the cabin?

No. Air India's rule covers spices in powder or physical form, so whole spices go in check-in too.

### Can I carry chilli powder on a flight?

Only in your check-in bag, sealed and taped so it can't spill.

### Can I carry spices in an IndiGo flight?

Only in check-in. IndiGo's list doesn't allow groceries in the cabin, and the airport security rule (BCAS) bans all spices in the cabin, powder or whole.

### Are spices allowed in Air India flights?

In check-in, yes. Air India's restricted items page says "All spices in powder/physical form including chilly" can't go in the cabin bag (checked 28 Nov 2026).

### Can I carry spices on an international flight?

Yes, in your check-in bag. Leaving India, spices are banned in the cabin even on international flights. On arrival, the USA admits packaged spices if you declare them, and the UK allows packaged flavourings (checked 28 Nov 2026).

### Can I carry spices to the USA?

Yes, packaged spices. US Customs (CBP) says packaged spices are admissible. Declare all food on arrival, and pack them in check-in when you leave India.

### Can I carry garam masala on a flight?

Only in your check-in bag. Garam masala is a spice, so the cabin ban covers it.

### Why are spices not allowed in the cabin?

Because chilli and spice powders could be used to hurt someone's eyes on board. The BCAS rule bans all spices in the cabin, in powder or physical form.
