## Airline by airline

We read each airline's own page on **26 November 2026**. Three of them name trimmers in their lists. The words in quotes are theirs.

| Airline | Cabin bag | Check-in bag | What the airline's page says |
|---|---|---|---|
| IndiGo | Yes, with a battery | Yes, switched off | "Trimmer with battery (Must be completely Switched off and prevented from accidental activation)": allowed in both. "Trimmer without battery": check-in only. |
| SpiceJet | Yes, with a battery | Yes | "Trimmer - with battery": cabin and check-in. "Trimmer - without battery": check-in only. |
| Air India Express | Yes, with a battery | Yes | "Trimmer with battery": allowed in both. "Trimmer without battery": not allowed in the cabin, allowed in check-in. |
| Akasa Air | Yes | Yes, switched off | Trimmers aren't named. Its rule for all battery devices: up to 15 per person, each battery up to 100 Wh, and in check-in "completely switched off, protected to prevent unintentional activation, and packed securely". |
| Air India | Not named | Not named | Trimmers aren't on its restricted items page. Its electronics list allows phones in carry-on only, so the cabin bag is the safe choice. |
| BCAS (airport security) | Not banned | – | Trimmers aren't on BCAS's list of items banned in the cabin. Loose razor blades and pointed scissors are. |

So on every airline here, a rechargeable trimmer in your cabin bag is the answer that always works.

## Why "with battery" and "without battery" are different

A rechargeable trimmer has a small lithium battery inside, so airlines treat it like other battery devices: fine in the cabin, and allowed in check-in only if it's switched off and can't start by itself. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express put a trimmer **without** a battery in check-in only. None of them explains why, so just follow it: pack a corded trimmer in check-in.

How do you know which kind you have? If it runs with the cable unplugged, it has a battery.

## Packing a trimmer in a check-in bag

- Turn on the **travel lock** if it has one. The manual shows how (often a long press of the button).
- Fit the cap or comb and put it in its pouch or box, so nothing in the bag can press the switch.
- If the battery comes out, take it out and carry it in your cabin bag as a spare battery, with the ends covered. Spare lithium batteries can't go in check-in, the same as [power banks](power-bank.html).
- Don't pack the charger and trimmer loose together with metal items such as keys.

## Blades, scissors and grooming kits

- **Scissors** with pointed metal tips are on BCAS's list of items banned in the cabin. Many grooming kits have a small pair, so take them out or pack the whole kit in check-in. See [nail cutter and scissors rules](nail-cutter.html).
- **Loose razor blades** are banned in the cabin. A safety razor with the blades in a cartridge is fine. See [razor rules](razor.html).
- **Trimmer oil** is a liquid: in the cabin it goes in your clear 1-litre liquids bag, in a bottle of 100 ml or less.
- A **shaving brush** is allowed in both bags (IndiGo, Air India Express).

## Electric shavers

None of the airline pages above, and not BCAS's cabin list, names electric shavers. A rechargeable shaver is a battery device, so the same thinking applies: the cabin bag works on every airline, and in check-in it should be switched off and locked. Air India's page doesn't cover it at all, so on Air India keep it in the cabin. Packing other grooming gadgets? Cordless straighteners and gas curlers have their own rules: see [hair dryer and straightener](hair-dryer-and-straightener.html).

## International flights

On IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express, SpiceJet and Akasa Air flights abroad, the same lists apply. For foreign airlines such as Emirates, Etihad or Singapore Airlines, we haven't checked their pages for this item. Read your airline's own list before you fly. The safe choice is the same everywhere: a rechargeable trimmer in your cabin bag.

## Questions

### Can I carry a trimmer in check-in baggage?

Yes. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express allow a trimmer in check-in, with or without a battery. If it has a battery, it must be fully switched off and protected from turning on: use the travel lock and fit the cap.

### Can I carry a trimmer in cabin baggage?

Yes, if it has a built-in battery (IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express). A trimmer without a battery goes in check-in only on those airlines.

### Trimmer in check-in or cabin: which bag is better?

The cabin bag. It works on every airline we checked, including Air India, whose page doesn't name trimmers. In check-in, it must be switched off and locked.

### Is a trimmer allowed in flight on IndiGo?

Yes. IndiGo's list allows a trimmer with a battery in both bags, "completely switched off and prevented from accidental activation" in check-in. A trimmer without a battery is check-in only.

### Can I carry a trimmer on Air India?

Air India's restricted items page doesn't name trimmers (checked 26 November 2026). Carry a rechargeable one in your cabin bag. Air India Express, in the same group, allows a trimmer with a battery in both bags.

### Can I carry a trimmer in an international flight?

Yes on Indian airlines flying abroad, under the same rules as above. On a foreign airline, check its own list. A rechargeable trimmer in the cabin bag is the safe choice.

### Can I carry a trimmer and a razor in my cabin bag?

Yes, if the razor is a safety razor with the blades in a cartridge. Loose razor blades and pointed scissors must go in check-in.

### Where should I keep a trimmer on a flight?

In your cabin bag. A rechargeable trimmer is allowed there on IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express, and it's the choice that works on every airline we checked. If you put it in your check-in bag instead, switch it off and use the travel lock.

### Can I put a trimmer in my luggage bag?

Yes, in your check-in bag (IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express), with or without a battery. A battery trimmer must be fully switched off and protected from turning on.

### Is a beard trimmer a sharp object at airport security?

No. BCAS's cabin list bans loose razor blades and pointed scissors, not trimmers, and three airlines allow a battery trimmer in the cabin by name.
