## Food by food

From IndiGo's list of things not allowed in flight, unless the row says otherwise:

| Food | Cabin bag | Check-in |
|---|---|---|
| Dry sweets: ladoo, barfi, soan papdi | Yes | Yes |
| Namkeen, biscuits, dry snacks | Yes | Yes |
| Dry cake | Yes | Yes |
| Dry fruits | Yes | Yes, but no dry coconut |
| Fruits and vegetables | Yes | Yes |
| Sweets in syrup: rasgulla, gulab jamun | 100 ml liquids rule | Not listed, pack a sealed tin |
| Oily food | No | No |
| Pickle | No | Yes, if packed so it can't leak |
| Raw rice and dal | No | Yes |
| Fish and meat | No | No |
| Spice powders, chilli | No (airport security list) | Yes |
| Dry coconut (copra, gola) | No (airport security list) | No |

IndiGo's list is stricter than the other airline pages we checked, so food that passes it is the safest choice on any airline.

## Sweets in syrup count as liquid

Rasgulla, gulab jamun and rasmalai sit in syrup or milk. In the cabin, that is a liquid, so the 100 ml rule applies to the whole tin. A 1 kg tin of rasgulla won't pass security in your cabin bag. Pack it in check-in, sealed and wrapped, or choose dry sweets.

## Home food for the journey

Akasa Air allows your own food on board, such as cold snacks, snack bars and biscuits, but **not messy, oily or strong-smelling food**. Good choices for a tiffin:

- Sandwiches, khakhra, biscuits, dry snacks and fruit.
- Food that won't spill or smell strongly in a closed cabin.

Curry, dal and anything runny count as liquid in the cabin, so they follow the 100 ml rule. Eat them before you reach the airport.

## How to pack food in check-in

- Use boxes or tins with tight lids, then put each one in a zip bag or plastic bag.
- Shop sweet boxes are often thin cardboard. Put them in a plastic box or wrap them in plastic so they don't get crushed or leak.
- Keep heavy tins near the wheels of the trolley, and soft boxes in the middle.
- Food counts towards your check-in weight. A few boxes of sweets add up fast, so weigh your bag at home.

## What to do instead

- Carry dry sweets and snacks in your cabin bag, and keep syrupy sweets and pickle for check-in.
- Flying IndiGo? Leave oily food at home: its list doesn't allow it in either bag.
- Taking sweets to a wedding? Pack them in check-in and keep a small box in your cabin bag for the journey.
- Flying abroad? Other countries have their own food rules at customs. This page is about flights within India.

## Questions

### Can I carry sweets on a flight?

Yes. Dry sweets like ladoo, barfi and soan papdi are allowed in both bags (IndiGo). Sweets in syrup, like rasgulla, count as liquid in the cabin.

### Can I carry home-cooked food on a flight?

Yes, if it's dry and not oily, messy or strong-smelling (Akasa Air). Runny food like dal or curry follows the 100 ml liquids rule in the cabin.

### Can I carry rasgulla on a flight?

Not in your cabin bag, unless it's 100 ml or less, because the syrup is a liquid. Pack a sealed tin in your check-in bag.

### Can I carry namkeen in my cabin bag?

Yes. Dry snacks like namkeen and biscuits are fine in both bags.

### Can I carry pickle on a flight?

In check-in, if it's packed so it can't leak or break (IndiGo, Air India). IndiGo doesn't allow pickle in the cabin, and Air India doesn't allow chilli pickle there.
