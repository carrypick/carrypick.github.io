Can you carry sweets, mithai or a tiffin on a flight in India? Dry sweets and snacks, yes, in hand luggage and check-in. Sweets in syrup and runny food follow the liquids rule.

## Food by food

From IndiGo's list of things not allowed in flight, unless the row says otherwise:

| Food | Cabin bag | Check-in |
|---|---|---|
| Dry sweets: ladoo, barfi, soan papdi | Yes | Yes |
| Namkeen, biscuits, dry snacks | Yes | Yes |
| Dry cake | Yes | Yes |
| Dry fruits | Yes | Yes, but no dry coconut |
| Fruits and vegetables | Yes | Yes |
| Sweets in syrup: rasgulla, gulab jamun | 100 ml liquids rule | Not listed, pack a sealed tin |
| Oily food | No | No |
| Pickle | No | Yes, if packed so it can't leak |
| Raw rice and dal | No | Yes |
| Fish and meat | No | No |
| Spice powders, chilli | No (airport security list) | Yes |
| Dry coconut (copra, gola) | No (airport security list) | No |

IndiGo's list is stricter than the other airline pages we checked, so food that passes it is the safest choice on any airline.

Bringing banana chips or halwa home from a [Kerala trip](../../trips/kerala.html), or dry sweets from [Jaipur](../../trips/jaipur.html)? Dry snacks and sweets go in either bag. Going by train instead? Weight is what counts there: see the [train luggage rules](../train-luggage-rules.html) for each class.

## Sweets in syrup count as liquid

Rasgulla, gulab jamun and rasmalai sit in syrup or milk. In the cabin, that is a liquid, so the 100 ml rule applies to the whole tin. A 1 kg tin of rasgulla won't pass security in your cabin bag. Pack it in check-in, sealed and wrapped, or choose dry sweets.

## Home food for the journey

Akasa Air allows your own food on board, such as cold snacks, snack bars and biscuits, but **not messy, oily or strong-smelling food**. Good choices for a tiffin:

- Sandwiches, khakhra, biscuits, dry snacks and fruit.
- Food that won't spill or smell strongly in a closed cabin.

Curry, dal and anything runny count as liquid in the cabin, so they follow the 100 ml rule. Eat them before you reach the airport.

## How to pack food in check-in

- Use boxes or tins with tight lids, then put each one in a zip bag or plastic bag.
- Shop sweet boxes are often thin cardboard. Put them in a plastic box or wrap them in plastic so they don't get crushed or leak.
- Keep heavy tins near the wheels of the trolley, and soft boxes in the middle.
- Food counts towards your check-in weight. A few boxes of sweets add up fast, so weigh your bag at home.
- Taking a tin of ghee too? It goes in check-in, up to 5 kg: see our [ghee rules](ghee.html).

## What to do instead

- Carry dry sweets and snacks in your cabin bag, and keep syrupy sweets and pickle for check-in.
- Flying IndiGo? Leave oily food at home: its list doesn't allow it in either bag.
- Taking sweets to a wedding? Pack them in check-in and keep a small box in your cabin bag for the journey.
- Flying abroad? The country you land in has its own food rules at customs: see below.

## Taking Indian sweets on an international flight

**Leaving India**, the airport security rules are the same as on a domestic flight: dry sweets, biscuits and namkeen are fine in your cabin bag, and sweets in syrup follow the 100 ml rule. Pack big tins in check-in.

**Arriving abroad** is what decides it. Milk-based sweets cause the most trouble. We read three countries' own government pages on 28 Nov 2026:

| Country | Dry sweets, biscuits, namkeen | Milk sweets: barfi, peda, rasgulla, rasmalai | What the official page says |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | Allowed | Not allowed from India | GOV.UK allows biscuits, cakes without fresh cream and confectionery from any country, but not confectionery "made with a lot of unprocessed dairy ingredients". Milk and milk-based products from outside the EU are banned. |
| USA | Not named on the page: declare them | Not named on the page: declare them | US Customs (CBP) says you must declare plant and animal products in any bag. Undeclared banned items are taken away, and the fine can be up to $1,000. Many meat products are banned, so leave out snacks with meat. |
| Singapore | Allowed | Allowed within the limit | The Singapore Food Agency lists biscuits, milk and other processed food together, up to 5 kg in all per traveller, for your own use only. |

Going somewhere else, such as Australia, Canada or the Gulf? Check that country's customs page before you pack sweets, and declare food when the arrival form asks. For the UAE, see our [Dubai customs guide](../customs/uae-dubai.html).

**The safest sweets to take abroad** are dry, sealed shop packs with an ingredients label: soan papdi, besan ladoo, chikki, biscuits and namkeen. Leave milk sweets and home-cooked food at home unless you've checked the rules of the country you're flying to.

## Questions

### Can I carry sweets in a flight, in hand luggage?

Yes. Dry sweets like ladoo, barfi and soan papdi are allowed in both bags (IndiGo). Sweets in syrup, like rasgulla, count as liquid in the cabin.

### Is mithai allowed in hand luggage?

Dry mithai is: ladoo, barfi, peda and soan papdi can go in your cabin bag. Mithai in syrup or milk, like rasmalai, follows the 100 ml liquids rule there.

### Can I carry ladoo in cabin baggage?

Yes. Ladoo is a dry sweet, so it's allowed in your cabin bag and your check-in bag (IndiGo).

### Can I carry a sweet box in cabin baggage?

Yes, if the sweets inside are dry. Shop boxes are thin, so wrap the box in a plastic bag. A box of sweets in syrup goes in check-in, sealed in a tin.

### Is sweets allowed in an international flight?

Yes, leaving India: dry sweets are fine in the cabin, and sweets in syrup follow the 100 ml rule. The country you land in decides the rest. The UK bans milk-based sweets from India, the US asks you to declare food, and Singapore allows up to 5 kg of processed food (checked 28 Nov 2026).

### Can I take Indian sweets to the UK?

Dry sweets, biscuits and confectionery without much dairy, yes. Milk sweets such as barfi, peda, rasgulla and rasmalai, no: GOV.UK bans milk and milk-based products from outside the EU.

### Can I take Indian sweets to the USA?

Declare all food on arrival. US Customs says you must declare plant and animal products, and undeclared banned items can bring a fine of up to $1,000. CBP's page doesn't name sweets, so declare them and let the officer decide.

### Can I carry home-cooked food on a flight?

Yes, if it's dry and not oily, messy or strong-smelling (Akasa Air). Runny food like dal or curry follows the 100 ml liquids rule in the cabin.

### Can I carry gulab jamun or rasgulla in a flight?

Not in your cabin bag, unless it's 100 ml or less, because the syrup is a liquid. Pack a sealed tin in your check-in bag.

### Is baked rasgulla allowed in a flight?

The airline lists we checked don't name it. Baked rasgulla is moist, so security can treat it like a sweet in syrup. Pack it in check-in to be safe, or keep a small box (100 ml or less) in the cabin.

### Is a tiffin allowed in a flight?

Yes, with dry food that isn't oily, messy or strong-smelling, like sandwiches, khakhra or fruit (Akasa Air). Dal, curry and other runny food follow the 100 ml rule in the cabin, and IndiGo doesn't allow oily food in either bag.

### Can we carry biscuits in a flight?

Yes. Biscuits, namkeen and other dry snacks are allowed in both bags (IndiGo).

### Can I carry namkeen in my cabin bag?

Yes. Dry snacks like namkeen and biscuits are fine in both bags.

### Can I carry pickle on a flight?

In check-in, if it's packed so it can't leak or break (IndiGo, Air India). IndiGo doesn't allow pickle in the cabin, and Air India doesn't allow chilli pickle there.
