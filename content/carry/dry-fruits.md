Can I carry dry fruits in a flight? **Yes. Cabin bag: yes. Check-in bag: yes. IndiGo's list allows dry fruits in both bags. The one exception is dry coconut (copra, gola), which is banned in both bags.** Checked on 28 November 2026.

## Dry fruits: yes in both bags

IndiGo's list allows **dry fruits in the cabin bag and in check-in**, and dry foods in both bags too. Air India's list doesn't name dry fruits.

IndiGo's food rules are the strictest of the airline pages we checked, so dry fruits that pass its list are a safe choice on any airline. That covers kaju, badam, kishmish, akhrot and pista.

## The one exception: dry coconut

Dry coconut, also called copra, gola or khopra, is **banned in both bags**. IndiGo allows dry fruits in check-in only "without copra", and Air India and the airport security list ban copra completely. Take it out of mixed dry fruit packs before you pack them. See our [coconut rules](coconut.html).

## Honey

Honey is runny, so in the cabin it counts as a liquid. IndiGo's list allows honey **up to 100 ml in the cabin**, and in check-in if it's **sealed and packed properly**. For other liquids, see our [100 ml rule page](liquids.html).

## Gift boxes for weddings and festivals

- A small box of dry fruits can go in your cabin bag, but it counts towards the cabin weight limit, usually 7 kg.
- Big gift boxes are easier in check-in. Put them in a plastic bag so they don't get crushed or wet.
- Dry fruits are heavy. Weigh your bag at home so you don't pay extra at the airport.

Kaju, badam, kishmish, akhrot, pista, anjeer and dates are all dry fruits, so IndiGo's rule covers them. Akasa Air allows your own snacks on board but not "messy, oily, and/or strong-smelling" food, and dry fruits are none of those.

## What airport security (BCAS) says

BCAS's cabin list doesn't name dry fruits or nuts. The Kannur Airport security list (based on BCAS rules) bans **copra** in both bags, because dry coconut can catch fire, and bans spices in the cabin. Mixed packs sometimes include coconut slices or masala-coated nuts: take those out, or pack the masala nuts in check-in. See [spices and masala](spices-and-masala.html).

## Taking dry fruits on an international flight

**Leaving India,** dry fruits are fine in both bags. **Arriving abroad** is what decides it. We read each country's own page on 28 November 2026:

| Country | Dry fruits and nuts | What the official page says |
|---|---|---|
| UK | Shelled nuts and dates: yes | From outside the EU, GOV.UK allows "peeled and processed nuts", dates, coconut and "processed and packaged plant products" without a plant health certificate. Most fresh fruit needs one, so take shop-sealed packs. |
| USA | Declare them | TSA allows nuts in carry-on and checked bags. At customs, CBP says fresh fruit is best left at home and you must declare all food. Sealed, labelled packs are easiest to clear. |
| Singapore | Yes, within 5 kg | The Singapore Food Agency lists nuts as processed food. All processed food together can be up to 5 kg per traveller, for your own use. |
| UAE | Small amounts for yourself | Dubai Customs' 2025 guide lists food products as needing permits, and commercial quantities aren't allowed. We found no kg limit. See our [Dubai customs guide](../customs/uae-dubai.html). |
| Saudi Arabia | Small amounts for yourself | The GCC customs guide (2025) allows personal belongings and gifts up to SAR 3,000, in non-commercial quantities. It sets no food limit. |

Flying back into India with dry fruits or dates as gifts? Gifts count towards your duty-free allowance. See our [Indian customs rules](../customs.html).

## What to do instead

- Carry a small pack of dry fruits in your cabin bag as a snack for the journey.
- Keep big gift boxes and honey jars for check-in.
- Packing sweets too? See our [sweets and home food rules](sweets-and-home-food.html).

## Questions

### Can I carry dry fruits in my cabin bag?

Yes. IndiGo's list allows dry fruits in the cabin bag.

### Can I carry dry fruits in my check-in bag?

Yes, but not dry coconut (copra), which is banned in both bags.

### Can I carry honey on a flight?

Yes. Up to 100 ml in your cabin bag, inside the clear liquids bag, and in check-in sealed and packed properly (IndiGo).

### Can I carry dry fruits to another country?

Indian airlines allow them, but the country you land in has its own customs rules for food. Check them before you fly.

### Are dry fruits allowed in cabin baggage on IndiGo?

Yes. IndiGo's list allows dry fruits in your cabin bag and your check-in bag, without copra (checked 28 Nov 2026).

### Are dry fruits allowed in cabin baggage on Air India?

Air India's restricted items page doesn't name dry fruits, and doesn't ban them. It bans copra and dry coconut in both bags, so take those out.

### Can I carry dry fruits on an international flight?

Yes, leaving India, in both bags. The country you land in decides the rest: Singapore allows nuts within 5 kg of processed food, the UK allows shelled nuts and dates, and the USA asks you to declare all food (checked 28 Nov 2026).

### Can I take dry fruits to the USA?

Yes, but declare them. TSA allows nuts in both bags, and US Customs (CBP) asks you to declare all food. Take sealed shop packs with a label, and leave fresh fruit at home.

### Can I take dry fruits to the UK?

Shelled nuts, dates and packaged dry fruit, yes. GOV.UK allows peeled and processed nuts, dates and processed, packaged plant products from outside the EU without a certificate.

### Can I take dry fruits to Dubai?

Small amounts for your own use, yes. Dubai Customs says food in commercial quantities isn't allowed, and gives no kg limit for personal food.
