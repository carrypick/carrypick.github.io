Russia is a big trip that is easier to plan than many Indians think. Moscow has Red Square, the Kremlin and the painted domes of St. Basil's Cathedral, and a metro with stations like palaces. St Petersburg has the Hermitage, canals, the fountains of Peterhof and summer nights that never get dark. Indians can apply for a short-trip e-visa online, with no invitation, and Aeroflot flies direct from Delhi to Moscow. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day plan, the weather by month, getting around, food for vegetarians, and the rules to know.

## Russia visa for Indians

Indians need a visa for Russia, says the Russian Embassy in India (read 24 Nov 2026). Only diplomatic and official passport holders, and aircraft crews, can enter without one. Tourists have two choices.

### The Russia e-visa for Indians (unified e-visa)

India is one of the countries whose citizens can get Russia's **unified e-visa**, says the embassy. It is the easiest choice for a holiday:

- **Apply only on the Russian Foreign Ministry's portal, [evisa.kdmid.ru](https://evisa.kdmid.ru/).** The embassy warns that going through agents can lead to mistakes, refusal of entry and your personal data reaching third parties.
- **No extra documents:** no tourist voucher, hotel confirmation or invitation. You need the online form, a photo, and your passport's photo page with the machine-readable lines.
- **One trip of up to 16 days,** within 60 days from the date the e-visa is issued. You must leave Russia by the 60th day.
- **Single entry.** It is valid for tourism, business, private visits and events, all over Russia.
- **The fee** is paid online and can't be refunded, says the embassy. The amount is shown on the portal; we couldn't open the portal on 24 Nov 2026, so we don't quote it.

### Russia tourist visa for Indians: for longer trips

Staying more than 16 days, or want more than one entry? Apply for a regular tourist visa:

- **Where:** the official Russian Visa Centers in New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, or directly at the embassy or a consulate with an appointment booked online. Travel agencies must apply through the Visa Centers.
- **Documents:** your passport, the form filled in online at [visa.kdmid.ru](https://visa.kdmid.ru/) and signed, a recent colour photo on a white background, and a tourist voucher from a Russian tour operator on the official register or from a hotel on Russia's list of classified hotels, says the embassy's FAQ.
- **Consular fee:** the embassy's tariff table lists a single-entry visa at US$80 (₹7,760) in total for issue in 4 to 20 working days, or US$320 (₹31,040) for issue in 3 working days. The Visa Center adds its own service fee.
- **Time:** the decision can take up to 30 days if the application needs more checks, and your passport may be held for that time.
- **The embassy accepts only cash** for consular fees, says its FAQ.

### Flying through Russia?

Passengers can stay in the transit zone of an international airport without a visa for up to 24 hours, says the embassy. Check with your airline about moving bags and changing airports.

### Registration after you arrive

Russia has a migration registration rule for foreign visitors. We couldn't read the current rule on an official page, so we don't give details here: ask your hotel about it when you check in, and keep any registration paper they give you with your passport.

## Flights from India to Russia

- **Aeroflot flies direct from Delhi to Moscow Sheremetyevo (SVO),** says the Wikipedia page of Delhi airport (read 24 Nov 2026). It is about 4,360 km, roughly 6 hours.
- **Goa:** Aeroflot also lists seasonal flights from Goa (Dabolim) to Moscow.
- **One stop:** Aeroflot also flies to Moscow from Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Istanbul, which have many flights from India.

## A 7-day Russia itinerary: Moscow and St Petersburg

Most first trips from India spend 3 or 4 days in Moscow and 3 days in St Petersburg, joined by a fast train or a short flight.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Moscow and rest | A walk on Red Square and Nikolskaya Street |
| 2 | Moscow: the Kremlin and its cathedrals, the Armoury, St. Basil's Cathedral | Zaryadye Park, by the river |
| 3 | Moscow: a metro tour of the most beautiful stations, the Tretyakov Gallery and Gorky Park | A ballet or opera at the Bolshoi Theatre (book ahead) |
| 4 | Train to St Petersburg on the high-speed Sapsan | Nevsky Prospekt and a canal walk |
| 5 | St Petersburg: the Hermitage (a full day) and Palace Square | A river or canal boat in summer |
| 6 | Peterhof's palace and fountains (the fountains run in summer), or Catherine Palace in Pushkin | Watch the bridges open over the Neva at night, from spring to autumn |
| 7 | Peter and Paul Fortress and St. Isaac's Cathedral, then fly home | |

- **Five days?** Do 3 days in Moscow and 2 in St Petersburg.
- **Winter trips:** add a few days in Murmansk, north of the Arctic Circle, for snow and the chance of the northern lights.
- **Book museum and theatre tickets on the official sites** of the Kremlin, the Hermitage and the Bolshoi, and carry your passport.

## Russia weather by month, and the best time to go

Long-term averages from Russia's Federal Service for Hydrometeorology (Roshydromet), published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Moscow (day / night) | St Petersburg (day / night) | Murmansk, Arctic (day / night) |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | About −6 °C / −12 °C, the coldest | About −5 °C / −11 °C | About −8 °C / −15 °C |
| March | About 2 °C / −6 °C | About 1 °C / −5 °C | About −3 °C / −10 °C |
| May | About 18 °C / 8 °C | About 16 °C / 7 °C | About 7 °C / 1 °C |
| June | About 22 °C / 12 °C | About 20 °C / 11 °C, the white nights | About 14 °C / 6 °C |
| July | About 23 °C / 14 °C, the warmest | About 22 °C / 14 °C | About 17 °C / 9 °C |
| September | About 15 °C / 7 °C | About 15 °C / 8 °C | About 10 °C / 4 °C |
| October | About 8 °C / 2 °C | About 8 °C / 3 °C | About 3 °C / −1 °C |
| December | About −4 °C / −9 °C | About −2 °C / −7 °C | About −6 °C / −12 °C |

- **May to September is best** for sightseeing: mild days, long evenings and the parks and fountains open. July is the warmest month, and also the wettest in Moscow, about 94 mm.
- **June is white nights season in St Petersburg,** when the sky stays light almost all night.
- **December to February is snow season.** Moscow is below 0 °C by day, and the cities are lit up for the New Year. Pack proper winter clothes.
- **October and November** are grey and cold, with short days.

## Getting around Russia

- **Metro:** Moscow and St Petersburg both have big, cheap metro networks.
- **Trains:** Russian Railways runs the high-speed Sapsan between Moscow and St Petersburg, and overnight trains. Book on the official Russian Railways site or app, and carry the passport you booked with.
- **Flights:** there are many daily flights between Moscow and St Petersburg.
- **Taxis:** use a ride-hailing app or an official taxi rank at airports and stations, not drivers who approach you.
- **Learn a few words.** Russian is the language and uses the Cyrillic alphabet; English is limited outside hotels and big sights. "Spasibo" means thank you. Keep a translation app on your phone and your hotel's address written in Russian.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

- **Indian food:** Moscow and St Petersburg have Indian restaurants, including some with vegetarian menus.
- **Russian food:** blini (pancakes), syrniki (cottage-cheese fritters), mushroom and potato dishes, and buckwheat (grechka) work for vegetarians. Borscht and many soups are often made with meat stock, so ask.
- **Georgian and Central Asian cafés** are common and serve khachapuri (cheese bread), vegetable dishes and salads.
- **Jain travellers:** most dishes use onion and garlic. Carry ready-to-eat food, and check what you can take in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.
- **Supermarkets** sell bread, cheese, yoghurt (kefir), fruit and nuts for easy meals.

## Phone, SIM and internet in Russia

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** check whether your mobile company offers a pack for Russia before you fly, and what it costs.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at airports and in phone shops. Ask the shop which documents it needs; carry your passport.
- **eSIM:** we haven't found a travel eSIM partner for Russia, so we don't show one here.
- **Hotel and café Wi-Fi** is common in the big cities. Keep your hotel address and booking saved offline.

## Where to stay

In Moscow, stay inside the Garden Ring, near Kitay-Gorod, Tverskaya or Arbat, close to the metro and Red Square. In St Petersburg, stay near Nevsky Prospekt or the historic centre, within walking distance of the Hermitage. For the regular tourist visa, the hotel must be on Russia's official list of classified hotels if you use its voucher; the e-visa needs no voucher. Before you pay, check the hotel's cancellation rules and whether it can take your payment method.

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the Russian rouble (RUB).** We couldn't find an official page on whether Indian bank cards work in Russia, so ask your bank before you go, and plan how you will pay.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Medicines:** the embassy advises checking Russia's lists of controlled drugs before you bring medicines, and Russia's Federal Customs Service ([customs.gov.ru](https://customs.gov.ru)) for other items. Carry prescriptions in English.
- **Carry your passport** with your visa or e-visa at all times; police can ask for it.
- **Photography:** don't photograph military sites, police or government buildings.
- **Tax free:** the embassy has a page on Russia's tax-free shopping system for foreign visitors.

## What to wear in Russia

- **Summer (June to August):** light layers, a light jacket for evenings and a small umbrella.
- **Spring and autumn:** a warm jacket, a sweater and waterproof shoes.
- **Winter (December to February):** a thermal layer, a warm padded coat, a woollen cap, gloves, a scarf and waterproof boots with a good grip for ice.
- **Churches:** women should carry a scarf to cover their hair, and wear a skirt or trousers below the knee; men should take off their caps.
- **Walking shoes:** you'll walk a lot in museums and on cobbled squares.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Russia?

Yes. Indians with ordinary passports need a visa, says the Russian Embassy in India. For a short holiday, apply online for the unified e-visa; for longer trips, apply for a regular tourist visa through a Russian Visa Center.

### Is the Russia visa free for Indians?

No. Visa-free entry is only for Indian diplomatic and official passport holders and aircraft crews, says the embassy. Tourists need the e-visa or a regular visa.

### How long can I stay in Russia on an e-visa?

Up to 16 days, on one trip, within 60 days from the date the e-visa is issued. You must leave by the 60th day.

### What is the Russia visa fee for Indians?

The e-visa fee is shown on the Foreign Ministry's portal when you apply, and it isn't refundable; we couldn't open the portal to confirm it. For a regular single-entry visa, the embassy's tariff table lists US$80 (₹7,760) in total for issue in 4 to 20 working days, plus the Visa Center's service fee.

### Do I need an invitation for the Russia e-visa?

No. The embassy says no voucher, confirmation or invitation is needed for the unified e-visa: just the form, a photo and your passport page.

### Are there direct flights from India to Russia?

Yes. Aeroflot flies direct from Delhi to Moscow, about 6 hours, and seasonally from Goa to Moscow.

### How many days are enough for Russia?

Seven days covers Moscow and St Petersburg. Five days is enough for the main sights in both cities if you keep moving.

### What is the best time to visit Russia from India?

May to September, when Moscow is about 18 to 23 °C by day. June brings the white nights in St Petersburg. December to February is snowy and well below freezing.

### Is Russia good for vegetarians?

It's manageable. The big cities have Indian restaurants, and blini, syrniki, mushroom and potato dishes and Georgian cheese bread are vegetarian. Ask whether soups use meat stock.

### What is the time difference between India and Russia?

India is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of Moscow and St Petersburg. Russia is very wide, so the far east of the country is ahead of India.
