In India, almost everything runs on your phone: cabs, train tickets, food delivery and, above all, **UPI**, the QR-code payment you'll see at every tea stall. So a working Indian number and a way to pay matter more here than in most countries. This page covers what the official rules say for foreign visitors, checked on 21 Nov 2026.

We don't recommend any SIM, bank, card or money-transfer product, and there are no paid links on this page.

New to India? Start with our [visiting India guide](visiting-india.html).

## Getting a local SIM card as a foreigner

India's Department of Telecommunications (DoT) sets the identity rules for every mobile connection. For a subscriber of foreign nationality, the DoT procedure says:

- **Take your passport and visa.** The mobile company scans your passport and the visa (or OCI card) pages.
- **A live photo is taken** at the store, and you confirm with a one-time code (OTP) sent to the new number.
- **The form asks for** your nationality, passport number, visa type and visa number, with your address as in your passport.
- **Aadhaar, India's ID card, isn't needed.** The DoT says the Aadhaar e-KYC route doesn't apply to foreign customers, so they use the passport process.
- **Up to 9 numbers per person** across all Indian mobile companies (6 in Jammu and Kashmir, Assam and the North-East), says a DoT circular of 17 Aug 2026.

**Where to buy:** go to a mobile company's own store or its counter at the airport, not a street stall, so the paperwork is done properly. Keep the receipt.

**Going to Kashmir?** News reports say prepaid SIMs from outside Jammu and Kashmir don't work there. Read our [Kashmir guide](kashmir-in-december.html) first.

## eSIM for tourists

- **An eSIM bought before you fly** from an international eSIM seller gives you mobile data from landing, with no store visit. It usually gives data only, without an Indian phone number.
- **Why an Indian number still helps:** many Indian apps and bank payments send a one-time code by SMS to an Indian number.
- **eSIMs from Indian mobile companies:** we couldn't confirm on their official pages that they sell eSIMs to foreign tourists as a new connection. Vi's page, for example, offers eSIM only to existing customers. Ask at the store.
- **Check your phone is unlocked** before you travel, or a local SIM or eSIM may not work.

## Can tourists use UPI?

UPI normally needs an Indian bank account and an Indian mobile number. For visitors, there's a prepaid version:

- **UPI One World:** in 2023 the Reserve Bank of India let travellers from G20 countries use UPI through a prepaid wallet, first at Bengaluru, Mumbai and New Delhi airports, the government said in Parliament ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1944427)). You sign up with your passport and visa and load money into the wallet.
- **In February 2026,** NPCI (which runs UPI) offered the wallet to delegates from over 40 countries at the India AI Impact Summit, sold at New Delhi airport.
- **Not confirmed today:** NPCI's UPI One World page wasn't loading when we checked on 21 Nov 2026, so we can't confirm which nationalities can sign up now, or the wallet limits. Check in the wallet's app or at the airport counter before you rely on it.
- **Your own country's app may work:** UPI is now live in 11 other countries for payments or transfers, including Singapore, the UAE, France, Sri Lanka, Mauritius and Qatar ([PIB, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2302664&lang=2&reg=48)). Ask your bank whether its app can pay Indian UPI QR codes.

**So carry a backup:** an international card for hotels and bigger shops, and some cash for small places.

## Cash: what you can bring into India

From the Reserve Bank of India's currency rules and CBIC's customs rules (checked 15 Nov 2026):

| What | The rule for foreign visitors |
|---|---|
| Foreign currency (US dollars, euros, pounds and so on) | No limit. Declare it if the notes are worth more than US$5,000, or notes and travellers' cheques more than US$10,000 in all |
| Indian rupees | Up to ₹25,000, entering through an airport (not for citizens of Pakistan or Bangladesh) |
| Leaving India | You can take out unspent foreign currency you brought in. If you declared it, keep the stamped Currency Declaration Form |

- **Declare it** on the Currency Declaration Form with your customs declaration (the Atithi app or website), and use the Red Channel.
- **Change money only at banks and authorised money changers,** and keep the receipt. Rajasthan Tourism gives the same advice to visitors.
- **Keep cash, cards and your passport** in your cabin bag or on you, never in a checked bag.

More detail, with the official documents: our [cash and currency limits](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html) and the [Indian customs guide](../baggage/customs.html) (₹25,000 duty-free allowance for foreign tourists).

## Small tips for paying in India

- **Small notes help** for autos, tea stalls, temples and tips. Big notes can be hard to change.
- **Agree the fare before you get in** an auto-rickshaw with no meter, or use a prepaid taxi counter or an app cab.
- **Buy monument tickets online** where you can. The Taj Mahal gives foreign visitors ₹50 off a ticket bought online.

## Questions

### Can a foreigner buy a SIM card in India?

Yes. Take your passport and visa to a mobile company's own store. Your passport and visa pages are scanned, a live photo is taken, and you confirm with a one-time code.

### Do I need Aadhaar to get a SIM in India?

No. Aadhaar is only for residents. The DoT's process for foreign customers uses your passport and visa.

### Can tourists use UPI in India?

Through a prepaid UPI wallet called UPI One World, where it's offered. It started in 2023 for travellers from G20 countries at Bengaluru, Mumbai and Delhi airports. We couldn't confirm who can sign up today, so check before you go.

### How much cash can I bring into India as a tourist?

Any amount of foreign currency, but declare notes over US$5,000, or notes and travellers' cheques over US$10,000 in all. Indian rupees: up to ₹25,000.

### Should I get an eSIM or a local SIM for India?

An eSIM bought before you fly gives data from the moment you land. A local SIM also gives you an Indian number, which many Indian apps and payments need for one-time codes.
