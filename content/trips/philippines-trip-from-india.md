The Philippines is over 7,000 islands of white beaches, limestone lagoons and coral reefs, and since June 2025 Indians can visit for a short holiday without a visa. The favourites are the lagoons of El Nido in Palawan, the white sand of Boracay, and the reefs and waterfalls around Cebu and Bohol. Air India flies direct from Delhi to Manila. This guide covers the visa-free rule and what to carry, flights, a 7-day island plan, the weather by month, getting around and food for vegetarians.

## Philippines visa for Indians

Indians don't need a visa for a short holiday. Since 8 June 2025, says the Philippines' Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA statement of 7 June 2025, read 23 Nov 2026):

- **14 days without a visa, for tourism.** This stay is "non-extendible and non-convertible", so you can't stretch it or change it to another visa once you're there.
- **30 days without a visa** if you hold a valid American, Japanese, Australian, Canadian, Schengen, Singapore or UK visa or residence permit (the DFA calls these "AJACSSUK"). This stay can't be extended either.

**What to show at immigration for the 14-day entry,** says the DFA:

1. A passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your stay.
2. A confirmed hotel booking.
3. Proof that you can pay for the trip (for example, recent bank statements or a forex card).
4. A return ticket, or an onward ticket to your next country.

For the 30-day entry, the DFA lists the passport, the valid US, Japan, Australia, Canada, Schengen, Singapore or UK visa or permit, and a return or onward ticket.

**Who still needs a visa:** Indians transiting the Philippines, or visiting for a long stay or anything other than tourism, must apply for a Philippine visa, says the DFA. Indians who are eligible can apply online on the official [Philippine e-Visa site](https://evisa.gov.ph/). An e-visa can't be extended or converted either. You also need a clean record with the Bureau of Immigration to enter without a visa.

### Register on eTravel before you fly

**eTravel** is the Philippines' official online arrival form, at [etravel.gov.ph](https://etravel.gov.ph/) or in the eGovPH app. The official site says "eTravel is FREE", so don't pay any website that charges for it. Fill it in before your flight and keep the QR code on your phone. We couldn't confirm on an official page how many hours before arrival you can register, so do it in the last day or two before you fly.

### Can I get a visa on arrival in the Philippines?

The Philippines doesn't offer a visa on arrival for Indians on the DFA's statement: you either enter visa-free (14 or 30 days) or get a visa before you travel. The officer at the airport always makes the final decision, so carry printouts of your hotel booking and return ticket.

## Flights from India to the Philippines

- **There are direct flights from Delhi to Manila (MNL).** Air India flies the route, on Manila airport's airline list (Wikipedia, read 23 Nov 2026). Delhi to Manila is about 4,770 km.
- **From other Indian cities,** fly one-stop via Delhi, or through Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok, Hong Kong or the Gulf.
- **For El Nido, Boracay, Cebu and Bohol,** take a domestic flight from Manila. Some one-stop routes from India land straight in Cebu or Clark, which saves time.
- **Time difference:** the Philippines is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India all year (no summer time).
- **Baggage:** domestic flights on small planes, such as those to El Nido and Caticlan (Boracay), can have lower bag limits than your international flight. Check the limits by airline in the [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) and the [carry-on size by airline](../guides/carry-on-size-by-airline.html) guide.
- **Fares go up for Christmas, New Year and Indian school holidays.** Book early.

## A 7-day Philippines itinerary: Manila, El Nido and Boracay

The islands are far apart, so most first trips from India pick two places and use domestic flights. This plan does Palawan and Boracay; swap either for Cebu and Bohol if you prefer.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Manila. Rest, then Intramuros, the old walled city | Manila Bay sunset |
| 2 | Fly to El Nido (or to Puerto Princesa and drive about 5 to 6 hours) | The beach and town |
| 3 | El Nido island-hopping by boat: the lagoons, hidden beaches and snorkelling of Bacuit Bay | Nacpan Beach or the town |
| 4 | A second island-hopping tour, or kayaking and a beach day | |
| 5 | Fly to Boracay (Caticlan airport, then a short boat ride to the island) | White Beach sunset |
| 6 | Boracay: White Beach, a sailing trip on a paraw boat, snorkelling or Puka Beach | Station 2 for food |
| 7 | Fly back to Manila for your flight home | |

- **Ten days?** Add Cebu and Bohol: whale sharks and waterfalls on Cebu, the Chocolate Hills and the tarsier sanctuary on Bohol.
- **How to reach Boracay from India:** fly to Manila (or Cebu), then a domestic flight to Caticlan, the closest airport, or Kalibo, about 2 hours away by road. A boat crosses from the Caticlan jetty to the island.
- **Island-hopping tours** in El Nido and Coron run as set routes (often called Tour A, B, C and D) with lunch on the boat. Book a day or two ahead in the high season.
- **Sea trips depend on the weather.** In the rainy season, boats are often cancelled when a storm is near, so keep a spare day.

## Philippines weather by month, and the best time to go

The Philippines has two main seasons, says PAGASA, the national weather service: the **dry season from December to May** (cool and dry from December to February, hot and dry from March to May) and the **rainy season from June to November**. Typhoons bring much of the rain and usually come from the Pacific; Mindanao in the far south is hit less often.

Long-term averages (1971–2000) for Metro Manila from PAGASA, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Manila (day / night) | Rain in Manila |
|---|---|---|
| January | About 30 °C / 24 °C | About 19 mm, dry |
| February | About 31 °C / 24 °C | About 8 mm, the driest |
| April | About 34 °C / 26 °C, the hottest | About 21 mm |
| May | About 33 °C / 27 °C | About 165 mm, rains begin |
| July | About 31 °C / 26 °C | About 420 mm |
| August | About 31 °C / 26 °C | About 486 mm, the wettest |
| October | About 31 °C / 26 °C | About 271 mm |
| December | About 30 °C / 24 °C | About 75 mm |

- **December to May is best** for beaches and boat trips. December to February is the most comfortable; Christmas and New Year are the busiest weeks.
- **March to May is hot,** about 32 to 34 °C by day in Manila, but the sea is calm and the islands are at their best.
- **June to November is the rainy season,** with typhoons. Fares are lower, but boat tours and domestic flights can be cancelled. Check PAGASA's typhoon bulletins before you go.
- **It's warm all year.** PAGASA's average temperature is about 25.5 °C in January, the coolest month, and 28.3 °C in May, the warmest. Only the hill town of Baguio is cool.

## Getting around the Philippines

- **Between islands, fly.** Domestic flights link Manila and Cebu with El Nido, Puerto Princesa, Caticlan (Boracay), Bohol and more. Ferries are cheaper but much slower.
- **In Manila, traffic is heavy.** Use ride-hailing apps such as Grab, or metered taxis, and allow extra time to reach the airport.
- **On the islands,** tricycles (motorbikes with a sidecar) are the usual short ride. Agree the fare first.
- **Boats:** island-hopping tours and island transfers need calm seas. Carry your passport or a copy, as some ports check ID.
- **Language:** English is an official language and widely spoken, which makes the Philippines very easy for Indian travellers. "Salamat" means thank you.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Filipino food is mostly meat and seafood, so vegetarians need to plan a little:

- **Easy vegetarian dishes:** pinakbet (mixed vegetables; ask for it without shrimp paste), ginataang gulay (vegetables in coconut milk), tortang talong (eggplant omelette, has egg), lumpiang sariwa (fresh spring rolls) and garlic rice.
- **Fruit:** mangoes, especially from Guimaras and Cebu, are a highlight, along with fresh coconut.
- **Ask about fish sauce and shrimp paste** (patis and bagoong), which go into many dishes, and pork in rice and noodle dishes.
- **Indian restaurants:** Manila, Cebu, Boracay and El Nido all have Indian restaurants. The Embassy of India in Manila keeps a [directory of Indian restaurants in the Philippines](https://www.eoimanila.gov.in/directory-of-indian-restaurants-in-the-philippines).
- **Jain travellers:** carry ready-to-eat food or khakhra for the islands, and check what you can take in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in the Philippines

You have three ways to stay online in the Philippines:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at the airports and in shops. SIM cards in the Philippines must be registered, so have your passport ready.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply). Signal is weak on some small islands and boats.

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the Philippine peso (₱, PHP).** Cards work in Manila and big hotels; carry cash on the islands, where ATMs can run out at busy times.
- **Some places charge fees on arrival,** such as an environmental fee in El Nido and Boracay, and terminal fees at small ports. Keep some cash ready.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Your visa-free stay is short.** Overstaying, even by a day, can bring fines and problems leaving. Book your flight home within your 14 or 30 days.
- **Reef-safe habits:** don't touch or stand on coral, and don't feed fish. Some areas ban single-use plastic.

## What to wear in the Philippines

- **Light, quick-dry clothes** all year: shorts, T-shirts, a swimsuit and a cover-up.
- **Sun protection:** a hat, sunglasses and reef-safe sunscreen. The sun is strong on boats.
- **Water shoes or sandals** for rocky beaches and boat landings, and a dry bag for your phone on island tours.
- **A light rain jacket** from June to November, and a light layer for cold air-conditioning in malls and on buses.
- **Churches:** cover your shoulders and knees.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for the Philippines?

Not for a short holiday. Since 8 June 2025, Indians can enter visa-free for up to 14 days for tourism, says the DFA. With a valid US, Japan, Australia, Canada, Schengen, Singapore or UK visa or residence permit, the visa-free stay is 30 days.

### Can I extend the 14-day visa-free stay in the Philippines?

No. The DFA says the 14-day stay is non-extendible and can't be changed to a visa. If you want to stay longer, apply for a visa before you travel.

### What do I need to enter the Philippines without a visa?

For 14 days: a passport valid 6 months beyond your stay, a confirmed hotel booking, proof of funds and a return or onward ticket. For 30 days: the passport, your valid US, Japan, Australia, Canada, Schengen, Singapore or UK visa or permit, and a return or onward ticket. Also register on the free eTravel form.

### Is there a visa on arrival in the Philippines for Indians?

No. Indians enter visa-free for 14 or 30 days if they qualify, or get a visa before they travel.

### How much does a Philippines trip from India cost?

It depends on your dates, hotels and how many islands you visit. Add your own flight, hotel, food and tour amounts to our [trip budget calculator](../tools/trip-budget-calculator.html), and split the total with friends or your partner.

### Are there direct flights from India to the Philippines?

Yes. Air India flies direct from Delhi to Manila. From other cities, fly one-stop via Delhi, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok or Hong Kong.

### How do I reach Boracay from India?

Fly to Manila or Cebu, then take a domestic flight to Caticlan (the closest airport) or Kalibo, and a short boat ride to the island.

### What is the best time to visit the Philippines from India?

December to May, the dry season. December to February is the coolest; March to May is hot and calm. June to November is the rainy season, with typhoons.

### Is the Philippines good for vegetarians?

It takes some planning, since many dishes have meat, fish sauce or shrimp paste. Vegetable dishes, fruit and Indian restaurants in Manila, Cebu, Boracay and El Nido make it easy enough.

### What is the time difference between India and the Philippines?

The Philippines is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India all year.
