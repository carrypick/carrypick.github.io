Australia is a big trip from India: Sydney's harbour and Opera House, the cafés, trams and laneways of Melbourne, the Great Ocean Road, the Blue Mountains, and beaches almost everywhere. It's a long flight, about 12 hours direct from Delhi, and Indians need a visa. The good news: the visa is applied for online, the seasons are the opposite of India's, and Sydney and Melbourne have direct flights from India. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 10-day plan, the weather by month, getting around and food for vegetarians.

## Australia visa for Indians

Indians need a visa for a holiday in Australia. The usual one is the **Visitor visa (subclass 600), Tourist stream**, says Australia's Department of Home Affairs (visa page, read 21 Nov 2026).

- **Apply online before you travel.** Log in or create an ImmiAccount on the Home Affairs website, attach your documents, and pay the fee. Home Affairs won't process your application until you pay. You must be outside Australia when you apply and when the visa is decided.
- **Visa fee:** from AUD 250 per applicant, says Home Affairs. You might also pay for health checks, police certificates or biometrics if you're asked for them. Home Affairs' Visa Pricing Estimator works out the total.
- **How long you can stay:** up to 12 months. Home Affairs decides the length case by case, based on how long and why you want to stay, and it can grant a single-entry or a multiple-entry visa. You might not get the length you ask for.
- **What you must show:** that you're coming only to visit, that you have enough money for your stay, and that you won't work. Include as much supporting information as you can, with English translations of your documents: Home Affairs says it may decide your application without asking you for more.
- **Processing time:** Home Affairs doesn't give a fixed time. Its visa processing time guide tool shows how long recently decided applications took. Apply well ahead of your trip.
- **Health insurance:** you pay all your own healthcare costs in Australia, and Home Affairs strongly recommends adequate health insurance.
- **Your passport** must be valid. If you plan to get a new passport, do it before you apply.

### Can Indians use the ETA or eVisitor?

No. The Electronic Travel Authority (subclass 601) is only for passports from a list that includes the US, the UK, Canada, Japan, Singapore and Malaysia, and the eVisitor (subclass 651) is for European passports. India is on neither list, says Home Affairs (read 21 Nov 2026). A US, UK or Schengen visa in an Indian passport doesn't change this: you still need the Visitor visa.

Apply only on the official Home Affairs website. Other websites that offer Australian visas may charge extra.

## Flights from India to Australia

- **There are direct flights from India.** Air India flies from Delhi to Sydney and to Melbourne, and Qantas flies from Bengaluru to Sydney, say the airports' airline lists (Wikipedia, read 21 Nov 2026). Qantas also lists Delhi to Melbourne as a seasonal route; check Qantas for your dates.
- **From other Indian cities,** fly one-stop via Delhi or Bengaluru, or through Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok or the Gulf (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha).
- **A direct flight from Delhi to Sydney takes about 12 hours.** It's a night flight, so you land tired: keep your first day light.
- **Time difference:** Sydney and Melbourne are 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of India from early October to early April (Australian daylight saving), and 4 hours 30 minutes ahead the rest of the year. Perth is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead.
- **Baggage:** check the limits by airline in the [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) and the [carry-on size by airline](../guides/carry-on-size-by-airline.html) guide.
- **Fares go up for Christmas, New Year and the Indian summer holidays.** Book early.

## A 10-day Australia itinerary: Sydney and Melbourne

Most first trips from India spend 8 to 12 days on Sydney and Melbourne, with one short flight between them. This plan uses 10 days.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Sydney and rest. Walk around Circular Quay to see the Opera House and the Harbour Bridge | The Rocks |
| 2 | A ferry from Circular Quay to Manly, or a harbour cruise. The Royal Botanic Garden and Mrs Macquarie's Chair | Darling Harbour |
| 3 | Bondi Beach and the Bondi to Coogee coastal walk | Back in the city |
| 4 | A day trip to the Blue Mountains: the Three Sisters at Katoomba and a bush walk | Back in Sydney |
| 5 | Taronga Zoo by ferry, or a walk across the Harbour Bridge. Fly to Melbourne in the evening | Melbourne city centre |
| 6 | Melbourne's laneways and arcades, Federation Square and the Queen Victoria Market | Southbank by the river |
| 7 | The Great Ocean Road day trip: the coast road and the Twelve Apostles | Back in Melbourne, late |
| 8 | Phillip Island for the penguin parade at sunset, or the Yarra Valley and the Dandenong Ranges | Back in Melbourne |
| 9 | St Kilda beach, the Royal Botanic Gardens and shopping | A last dinner in the city |
| 10 | Fly home | |

- **Australia is huge.** Sydney to Melbourne is about a 1.5-hour flight. Cairns and the Great Barrier Reef, Uluru and Perth each need another flight, so add 3 or 4 days for any of them.
- **Two weeks?** Add Cairns for the reef and the rainforest, or the Gold Coast and Brisbane.
- **The Great Ocean Road and Phillip Island** are long days from Melbourne. A day tour saves you the driving.

## Australia weather by month, and the best time to go

Australia's seasons are the opposite of India's: December to February is summer and June to August is winter. Long-term averages from Australia's Bureau of Meteorology, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Sydney (day / night) | Sydney rain | Melbourne (day / night) | Melbourne rain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | About 27 °C / 20 °C | About 96 mm | About 26 °C / 16 °C | About 45 mm |
| March | About 25 °C / 18 °C | About 111 mm | About 24 °C / 15 °C | About 41 mm |
| April | About 23 °C / 15 °C | About 141 mm, the wettest month | About 21 °C / 12 °C | About 50 mm |
| July | About 17 °C / 9 °C, the coldest month | About 80 mm | About 14 °C / 7 °C, the coldest month | About 45 mm |
| September | About 21 °C / 12 °C | About 70 mm | About 18 °C / 9 °C | About 53 mm |
| October | About 23 °C / 14 °C | About 83 mm | About 20 °C / 11 °C | About 59 mm |
| November | About 24 °C / 16 °C | About 104 mm | About 23 °C / 13 °C | About 63 mm |
| December | About 26 °C / 18 °C | About 79 mm | About 24 °C / 14 °C | About 63 mm |

- **September to November (spring) and March to May (autumn) are best** for Sydney and Melbourne: mild days, cool nights and fewer crowds than in the summer holidays.
- **December and January are summer:** warm to hot, long days and good for beaches. It's also school-holiday season in Australia, so flights and hotels fill up.
- **Melbourne's weather changes fast,** even in summer. A hot day can turn cool by evening, so carry a light jacket.
- **Winter (June to August)** is cool, not freezing, in Sydney and Melbourne: about 14 °C to 17 °C by day. It's the dry season in the tropical north, around Cairns.
- **The sun is strong.** Use sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses even on cloudy days.

## Getting around Australia

- **In Sydney, tap your contactless card or phone.** Transport for NSW says you can pay with a contactless credit or debit card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or Samsung Pay, or an Opal card, on trains, metro, buses, ferries and light rail. Tap on at the start and tap off at the end of each trip.
- **Sydney has daily and weekly fare caps,** with a lower cap on Fridays, weekends and public holidays. Use the same card or phone all week to get them. The Sydney Airport station access fee isn't included in those caps.
- **In Melbourne, tap your Visa or Mastercard card, phone or smartwatch** on trains, trams and buses, says Transport Victoria. Contactless fares are the same as myki fares. Tap on, and tap off the same way. You can still buy a myki card if you don't want to use a bank card, and Android phones can set up Mobile myki in Google Wallet.
- **Keep your cards apart** when you tap, so the reader charges the card you mean to use.
- **Between cities, fly.** Sydney to Melbourne is a short flight; driving takes most of a day.
- **Driving yourself?** Australia drives on the left, like India. Check what licence papers you need with your car rental company before you go.
- **Overseas cards** may charge a foreign transaction fee on every tap. Check with your bank or use a forex card.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Vegetarian food is easy to find in Sydney and Melbourne:

- **Indian restaurants** are common in both cities, from simple thali places to fine dining.
- **Cafés** have veg breakfasts: avocado toast, eggs, muesli and smoothie bowls. Menus usually mark vegetarian (V) and vegan (VG) dishes.
- **Supermarkets** like Woolworths and Coles sell bread, fruit, yoghurt, milk and ready meals. Many have an Indian or international aisle.
- **Asian food** is everywhere: Thai, Vietnamese, Malaysian and Chinese restaurants have tofu and vegetable dishes.
- **Jain travellers:** most restaurant food uses onion and garlic. Ask, or cook if your stay has a kitchen.

### Food rules at the airport: declare it

Australia is strict about food, plants and animal products coming in. The Australian Border Force says (read 21 Nov 2026):

- **Don't bring fresh fruit,** homemade food (meals, cakes or anything you made yourself) or food from the plane.
- **Declare** tea, coffee, honey products, seeds and eggs. Prescription medicines for your own use are allowed with restrictions: declare them too.
- **Biscuits, bread, cakes, chocolate and sweets** can usually come in.
- **When in doubt, declare it** and show it to the officer. Snacks like namkeen and spices may be checked. See what you can take on the plane in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in Australia

You have three ways to stay online in Australia:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at the airport, in phone shops and supermarkets. You need your passport to activate it.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the Australian dollar (A$, AUD).** Cards and phone payments work almost everywhere, even for small amounts. Some shops add a small fee for card payments.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Tipping isn't expected,** though people round up in restaurants for good service.
- **Smoking and drinking:** you can't smoke in most indoor and many outdoor public places, and drinking alcohol in public is banned in many areas.
- **Swim between the red and yellow flags** at beaches. They mark the area watched by lifeguards.

## What to wear in Australia

- **Summer (December to February):** light cotton clothes, a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen and swimwear, plus a light jacket for Melbourne's cool evenings.
- **Spring and autumn:** layers: T-shirts, a sweater or fleece and a light waterproof jacket.
- **Winter (June to August):** a warm jacket, a sweater and closed shoes. It doesn't snow in the cities.
- **Comfortable walking shoes** for the coastal walks and the Blue Mountains.

## Help in an emergency

- **Emergency number in Australia:** 000 (police, fire and ambulance).
- **High Commission of India, Canberra,** and Consulates General of India in Sydney, Melbourne, Perth and Brisbane: contact details are on the [High Commission's website](https://www.hcicanberra.gov.in/).

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Australia?

Yes. Indians need a visa for a holiday in Australia. The usual one is the Visitor visa (subclass 600), Tourist stream, which you apply for online before you travel, says Australia's Department of Home Affairs.

### What is the Australia visa fee for Indians?

From AUD 250 per applicant for the Visitor visa (subclass 600), says Home Affairs. You might pay extra for health checks, police certificates or biometrics if they're needed.

### How long does an Australia tourist visa take for Indians?

Home Affairs doesn't give a fixed time. Its visa processing time guide tool shows how long recently decided applications took. Apply well before you travel, with complete documents.

### Can Indians with a US visa visit Australia without a visa?

No. A US, UK or Schengen visa doesn't let Indians skip Australia's visa. Indians can't use the ETA or the eVisitor either, says Home Affairs, so apply for the Visitor visa (subclass 600).

### How long can I stay in Australia on a tourist visa?

Up to 12 months, but Home Affairs decides the length case by case. It may grant a shorter stay than you ask for, with single or multiple entries.

### Are there direct flights from India to Australia?

Yes. Air India flies direct from Delhi to Sydney and to Melbourne, and Qantas flies from Bengaluru to Sydney. Qantas also lists Delhi to Melbourne as a seasonal route.

### How many days are enough for Australia?

Ten days is enough for Sydney, the Blue Mountains, Melbourne and the Great Ocean Road. With two weeks, add Cairns and the Great Barrier Reef, or the Gold Coast.

### What is the best time to visit Australia from India?

September to November and March to May, when Sydney and Melbourne are mild. December to February is summer: warm and good for beaches, but busier.

### Can I take Indian food to Australia?

Some of it. Biscuits, chocolate and sweets can usually come in, but homemade food and fresh fruit can't, says the Australian Border Force. Declare tea, coffee, seeds, honey and eggs, and when in doubt, declare it.

### What is the time difference between India and Australia?

Sydney and Melbourne are 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of India from early October to early April, and 4 hours 30 minutes ahead the rest of the year. Perth is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead.
