Thailand is one of the cheapest and easiest trips abroad from India. Flights are short, there are direct flights from many Indian cities, and Indians don't need a visa for a holiday of up to 30 days. Most first trips mix Bangkok's temples and markets with a few days on the beaches of Phuket, Krabi or Pattaya. This guide covers the visa, the TDAC arrival card, flights, a 5 to 7-day itinerary, the weather in December, getting around and food for vegetarians.

## Thailand visa for Indians

Indians don't need a visa for a holiday in Thailand, says the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi (page updated 11 Nov 2026).

- **30 days without a visa, for tourism only.** The 30-day visa exemption for Indian ordinary passport holders took effect on 15 September 2026 and stays "until further announcement", says the embassy. Before that, it was 60 days.
- **It has changed several times.** Check the embassy's [visa page](https://newdelhi.thaiembassy.org/en/page/visa) again a week before you fly.
- **Staying longer, or going for work or study?** Apply for a Thai e-visa before you go. Since 1 January 2025, every visa application goes through the official [Thai e-Visa website](https://www.thaievisa.go.th), says the embassy.
- **e-Visa fees for Indians,** from the embassy's table (read 21 Nov 2026): a single-entry tourist visa costs ₹3,000 and lets you stay up to 60 days per entry; a multiple-entry tourist visa costs ₹13,500. We couldn't read the processing time on an official page, so apply well before your flight.
- **Entry is up to the officer at the airport.** Carry your return ticket and hotel bookings, printed or on your phone.
- **Your passport** should have at least 6 months left and a couple of blank pages. Most airlines check this before you board.

## TDAC: fill in the arrival card before you fly

- **All passengers must complete the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC),** says Thailand's Immigration Bureau.
- **Foreigners must submit it within 3 days before arriving.** You can change it later on the same site with "Update Arrival Card".
- **It's free.** Use only the official site, [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/). Its title says "No Fees Required", so don't pay websites or agents that offer to fill it in for you.

## A 5 to 7-day Thailand itinerary

Most first trips from India spend 5 to 7 days: 2 or 3 in Bangkok, then the beach. This plan keeps one area per day.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Bangkok and check in near a BTS Skytrain or MRT station | A night market or a dinner cruise on the Chao Phraya river |
| 2 | The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew, Wat Pho (the reclining Buddha), then the ferry across to Wat Arun | Chinatown (Yaowarat) for street food |
| 3 | A day trip to a floating market, or Chatuchak market (weekends) and shopping at Siam and Pratunam | Rooftop views or a mall |
| 4 | Fly to Phuket or Krabi (about 1 hour 20 minutes) | Patong beach (Phuket) or Ao Nang (Krabi) |
| 5 | A boat trip to the Phi Phi Islands and Maya Bay | Rest |
| 6 | Phuket: Big Buddha and Old Phuket Town. Krabi: Railay and Phra Nang beach by long-tail boat | Sunset at a viewpoint |
| 7 | Morning on the beach, then fly home from Phuket or Krabi | |

- **Only 5 days?** Do days 1 to 3 in Bangkok and then go to Pattaya, about 2 hours away by road, for Coral Island (Koh Larn) and a show. You save the domestic flight.
- **Pattaya or Phuket?** Pattaya is closer to Bangkok and cheaper to reach. Phuket and Krabi have clearer water, island trips and better beaches, but need a flight.
- **With kids?** Keep afternoons easy in the heat. Bangkok has big aquariums and theme parks, and Phuket's beaches are calm from December to February.
- **Dress for the Grand Palace.** It turns away sleeveless tops, shorts, short skirts and torn or tight trousers. Cover your shoulders and knees at every temple, and take your shoes off before you go in.
- **Book island boat trips with a licensed operator,** and don't go out if the sea is rough. See the tours below.

## Thailand weather in December and the best time to go

Long-term averages from the Thai Meteorological Department, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Bangkok day | Bangkok night | Bangkok rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | About 32 °C | About 23 °C | About 48 mm, on about 5 days |
| December | About 31 °C | About 21 °C | About 10 mm, on about 1 day |
| January | About 32 °C | About 21 °C | About 9 mm, on about 1 day |
| February | About 33 °C | About 23 °C | About 30 mm, on about 2 days |
| April | About 35 °C, the hottest month | About 26 °C | About 65 mm |
| September | About 32 °C | About 25 °C | About 344 mm, the wettest month |

- **November to February is the best time** for most trips: Bangkok is almost dry, and the nights are cooler. It's also the busiest and costliest time.
- **Phuket still gets some rain in December,** about 62 mm on about 8 days, after a wet November (about 174 mm on about 16 days). January and February are drier.
- **Pattaya is dry in December,** about 30 °C by day with about 6 mm of rain.
- **March to May is very hot,** with Bangkok days around 34–35 °C.
- **May to October is the rainy season.** Prices are lower, but island boat trips can be cancelled when the sea is rough.

## Getting around Thailand

- **In Bangkok, take the BTS Skytrain and the MRT metro.** They miss Bangkok's traffic jams and reach Siam, Sukhumvit, Silom, Chatuchak and Chinatown.
- **From Suvarnabhumi Airport to the city,** the Airport Rail Link train runs to Phaya Thai, where you change to the BTS. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are easier with luggage.
- **River boats on the Chao Phraya** go to the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun, and are a nice way to see the city.
- **Taxis:** ask for the meter. If the driver won't use it, agree the fare before you get in, or use a ride-hailing app that shows the price first.
- **Tuk-tuks** are fun for short rides, but agree the price first. Be careful of drivers who offer a cheap tour with stops at gem or tailor shops.
- **Between cities,** fly to Phuket, Krabi or Chiang Mai. Pattaya is about 2 hours from Bangkok by road.
- **Don't ride a scooter without a licence and a helmet.** Your travel insurance may not cover you if you do.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Thai food often uses fish sauce, shrimp paste and oyster sauce, even in dishes that look vegetarian. A few words help:

- **"Jay" (เจ) means Thai vegan food:** no meat, fish, eggs or dairy, and usually no garlic or onion, which suits many Jain travellers. Jay food stalls often hang yellow flags with red letters.
- **"Mangsawirat" means vegetarian.** Say "mai sai nam pla" (no fish sauce) and "mai sai kai" (no egg).
- **Safe choices:** fried rice or noodles with vegetables and tofu, vegetable green or red curry (ask for it without fish sauce or shrimp paste), mango sticky rice, fresh fruit and coconut water.
- **Indian restaurants** are easy to find in Bangkok, especially around Pahurat (Bangkok's Little India), Sukhumvit and Silom, and in Pattaya, Phuket and Krabi. Many serve pure vegetarian and Jain food.
- **Jain travellers** may want to carry ready-to-eat food or khakhra for day trips. Check what you can take in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water,** and use ice only where it comes in tubes from a factory, as in most restaurants.

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the Thai baht (฿).** Cards work in hotels, malls and big restaurants. Carry cash for street food, markets, tuk-tuks and island boats.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Declare cash in Thailand** if you carry foreign currency notes worth more than US$15,000, says the Bank of Thailand.
- **Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal in Thailand.** Don't bring them or the liquid.
- **Duty-free limits:** 200 cigarettes (or 250 g of tobacco) and 1 litre of alcohol, says Thai Customs.
- **Medicines:** up to 30 days' supply for personal use, in the original packs. Some strong medicines need a doctor's letter or a Thai FDA permit.
- **Respect the royal family.** Speaking badly about the King or the royal family is a crime in Thailand.
- **Don't touch anyone's head,** and don't point your feet at people or at a Buddha image. Women shouldn't touch monks.

## What to wear in Thailand

- **Light cotton or linen clothes,** shorts, T-shirts and dresses for the heat.
- **One temple outfit:** a top with sleeves and long trousers or a long skirt. Carry it in your day bag.
- **Sandals with a back strap** or easy slip-ons, since you take your shoes off at temples.
- **Swimwear, a cap, sunglasses and sunscreen** for the beaches.
- **A light layer** for cold malls, buses and flights.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Thailand?

Not for a holiday of up to 30 days. Since 15 September 2026, Indian ordinary passport holders can visit Thailand for tourism for up to 30 days without a visa, says the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi. For a longer stay, apply for a Thai e-visa before you go.

### Is Thailand visa free for Indians in 2026?

Yes, for tourism, for up to 30 days. It was 60 days until 15 September 2026, when the 30-day exemption took effect "until further announcement". Rules change often, so check the embassy's page before you fly.

### What is the Thailand visa fee for Indians?

There's no fee for a visa-free stay of up to 30 days. If you need a visa, the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi lists ₹3,000 for a single-entry tourist e-visa (up to 60 days) and ₹13,500 for a multiple-entry one. Apply only on the official Thai e-Visa website.

### What is the TDAC, and is it free?

The Thailand Digital Arrival Card is an online arrival form every passenger must fill in. Foreigners must submit it within 3 days before they arrive. It's free on tdac.immigration.go.th, so don't pay other sites to fill it in.

### How many days are enough for Thailand?

Five to seven days is enough for a first trip: 2 or 3 days in Bangkok, then 3 or 4 days in Phuket, Krabi or Pattaya. With only 5 days, pair Bangkok with Pattaya, which is about 2 hours away by road.

### Is December a good time to visit Thailand?

Yes. December is one of the best months: Bangkok averages about 31 °C by day and 21 °C at night with almost no rain, and Pattaya is dry too. Phuket can still get a few showers. It's also the busiest time, so book flights and hotels early.

### What is the weather in Thailand in December?

Warm and mostly dry. Bangkok averages about 31 °C by day and 21 °C at night, with about 10 mm of rain, on long-term averages from the Thai Meteorological Department. Phuket is about 32 °C, with about 62 mm of rain on about 8 days.

### Which Indian cities have direct flights to Thailand?

Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Kochi, Gaya, Guwahati, Jaipur, Lucknow, Amritsar, Bhubaneswar, Pune, Varanasi and Visakhapatnam have direct flights to Bangkok, and some cities also fly direct to Phuket or Krabi.

### Is Thailand good for vegetarians and Jains?

Yes, with a little care. Look for "jay" food (Thai vegan, often without garlic or onion), ask for no fish sauce, and use the many Indian vegetarian restaurants in Bangkok, Pattaya and Phuket.

### What is the time difference between India and Thailand?

Thailand is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of India.
