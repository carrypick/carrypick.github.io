South Korea is one of the fastest-growing trips for Indians: K-dramas and K-pop, royal palaces and street food in Seoul, the beaches and fish markets of Busan, old temples in Gyeongju, and the volcanic island of Jeju. Indians need a visa for mainland Korea, applied for in India, so plan at least six weeks ahead. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day plan, the weather by month, getting around, money and food for vegetarians.

## South Korea visa for Indians

Indian nationals must apply for a Korean visa "through the Visa Portal (www.visa.go.kr) & Visa Application Center (KVAC-VFS)", says the Consulate General of Korea in Chennai (page dated 10 Nov 2025, read 22 Nov 2026). A tourist visa is a short-term single-entry visa.

**How to apply, step by step:**

1. Check the visa type and the document list on your Visa Application Centre's website.
2. Fill in the e-Form on the [Korea Visa Portal](https://www.visa.go.kr/) and print it.
3. Submit it in person, with your passport and documents, at the Korea Visa Application Centre (VFS) for your state.
4. Track your application on the Visa Portal.

**Where to apply depends on the state you live in,** says the Embassy of Korea in India:

| Your state | Korean mission | Visa Application Centre |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, Ladakh, Chandigarh, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal and the North-East | Embassy, New Delhi | New Delhi or Kolkata |
| Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli | Consulate, Mumbai | Check the Mumbai consulate's website |
| Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Lakshadweep | Consulate, Chennai | Chennai or Bengaluru |

The Delhi and Kolkata centres are open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 15:00, and you don't need an appointment, says their website.

### Korea visa fee for Indians

The Korea Visa Application Centres in New Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai list the same normal fees for Indians, read 22 Nov 2026:

| Visa | Fee |
|---|---|
| Short-term single entry (up to 90 days), for tourists | ₹3,600 |
| Express, short-term single entry (Chennai) | ₹6,300 |
| Multiple entry | ₹8,100 |
| VFS service charge, per person | ₹1,600 |
| Courier (optional) | ₹550 (Delhi, Chennai) |

You pay at the centre by cash or card. The fee isn't refunded if the visa is refused.

### Korea tourist visa documents for Indians

The Chennai centre's checklist for a short-term tourist visa (sightseeing), read 22 Nov 2026. The Delhi and Kolkata checklist (C-3-9) asks for much the same, but 6 months of salary slips and a health condition report with a declaration form:

- Your original passport, valid for more than 6 months.
- 2 recent passport-size colour photos (35–40 mm wide, light background, taken in the last 6 months).
- The visa application form, filled in online on the Visa Portal.
- Proof of your job (a letter from your employer with their phone number and email).
- Salary slips for the last year.
- Income tax return acknowledgements for the last 2 years and your PAN card. If you can't give one, add a letter explaining why.
- Business owners: 3 years of company ITRs, the company registration and a business profile in the centre's format.
- Bank statements for the last 6 months, stamped and signed by the bank.
- If someone else pays for your trip: proof of your relation to them.

Lists can differ between centres, so download the latest checklist from your own centre before you go.

### Korea visa processing time

A normal application takes at least 15 working days in New Delhi and 17 in Kolkata, says their centre, which calls this "indicative" only. At the Chennai centre it takes at least 25 working days, not counting the day you submit it (27 at Bengaluru). Express takes at least 8–10 working days in Chennai and needs a confirmed air ticket and hotel booking. You can apply up to 89 days before you travel, not earlier. The consulate decides; it doesn't give reasons for a refusal.

### Korea without a visa: Jeju island and transit

- **Jeju island:** all visitors except nationals of 11 listed countries can enter Jeju without a Korean visa for up to 30 days, for tourism, says the Visa Application Centre's Jeju note. India isn't on that list. You must fly to Jeju directly from abroad and stay on Jeju; you can't go on to Seoul or Busan. There are no direct flights from India to Jeju, so you'd fly via another country (not via Seoul).
- **Transit with a US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand visa:** the same page says nationals who need a visa can stay up to 30 days without a Korean visa if they hold a visa for the US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand and are flying on to that country through Korea, or are coming straight back from it. You need an onward ticket within 30 days. This is the official answer to "Korea visa for Indians with a US visa": it works only on the way to or from those countries, not for a separate holiday.
- **K-ETA:** the online travel authorisation is only for visa-free countries. India isn't on the K-ETA list, so Indians can't use it; visa holders don't need one.

### Korea e-Arrival Card

Korean visa holders must fill in the free **e-Arrival Card** on the official site, [e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr/), from 3 days before you land (Korean time). It stops being valid 72 hours after you submit it, so don't do it too early. If you skip it, you fill in a paper arrival card on arrival. Indians on a group visa from a Korean mission don't need it. Beware of fake e-Arrival Card websites that charge a fee; the Korean Consulate in Chennai warns about them.

## Flights from India to South Korea

- **Direct flights go only from Delhi to Seoul (Incheon, ICN):** Air India and Korean Air, says Incheon Airport's airline list (Wikipedia, read 22 Nov 2026).
- **From Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai and other cities,** fly one-stop via Delhi, or through Singapore, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Kuala Lumpur, Hanoi or the Gulf.
- **Incheon is about 50 km from central Seoul.** The AREX airport railroad and airport limousine buses run into the city.

- **Time difference:** Korea is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India all year (Korea has no summer time).
- **Baggage:** check the limits by airline in the [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) and the [carry-on size by airline](../guides/carry-on-size-by-airline.html) guide.
- **Fares go up for Christmas, New Year, Korean holidays (Seollal and Chuseok) and Indian school holidays.** Book early.

## A 7-day South Korea itinerary: Seoul, Gyeongju and Busan

Most first trips from India spend 4 or 5 days in Seoul and 2 or 3 days in Busan, with Gyeongju on the way. The KTX fast train links Seoul and Busan in about 2½ hours.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land at Incheon, take the airport railroad into Seoul and rest | Myeongdong street food and shopping |
| 2 | Gyeongbokgung Palace (watch the changing of the guard, and try a hanbok from a rental shop nearby), Bukchon Hanok Village, Insadong | Cheonggyecheon stream walk |
| 3 | A day trip to the DMZ (book a tour), or Changdeokgung Palace and its Secret Garden | Hongdae: buskers, cafés and K-pop shops |
| 4 | N Seoul Tower on Namsan, Gangnam and COEX (the Starfield Library) | Han River park or a river cruise |
| 5 | KTX to Gyeongju: Bulguksa Temple and the royal tombs | Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond lit up at night |
| 6 | Busan: Haeundae beach, Gamcheon Culture Village, Jagalchi fish market | Gwangalli beach and the lit-up bridge |
| 7 | Haedong Yonggungsa, a temple by the sea, then fly home from Busan (Gimhae) or take the KTX back to Seoul for Incheon | |

- **Ten days?** Add Jeju island (a short flight from Seoul or Busan) for Hallasan, lava caves and the coast, or Nami Island and Seoraksan in autumn.
- **In winter,** add a ski day near Seoul or in Pyeongchang, or the Hwacheon ice fishing festival in January.
- **Many palaces close one day a week**. Check the palace's website before you go.

## South Korea weather by month, and the best time to go

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from the Korea Meteorological Administration, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Seoul (day / night) | Busan (day / night) | Jeju (day / night) |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | About 2 °C / −6 °C, the coldest | About 8 °C / 0 °C | About 9 °C / 4 °C |
| March | About 11 °C / 2 °C | About 14 °C / 5 °C | About 13 °C / 7 °C |
| April | About 18 °C / 8 °C | About 18 °C / 10 °C | About 18 °C / 11 °C |
| May | About 24 °C / 14 °C | About 22 °C / 15 °C | About 22 °C / 15 °C |
| July | About 29 °C / 22 °C, very rainy | About 28 °C / 22 °C | About 29 °C / 24 °C |
| October | About 20 °C / 11 °C | About 23 °C / 15 °C | About 22 °C / 16 °C |
| November | About 12 °C / 4 °C | About 17 °C / 8 °C | About 16 °C / 10 °C |
| December | About 4 °C / −3 °C | About 10 °C / 2 °C | About 11 °C / 6 °C |

- **Spring (April and May) is the favourite.** In 2026 the Korea Tourism Organization's forecast put the first cherry blossoms in Jeju and Busan around 25 March and in Seoul around 3 April, with full bloom in Seoul around 10 April.
- **Autumn (October and November) is just as good:** dry, clear days and red and gold leaves. The 2026 forecast put the peak colours at Seoraksan around 25 October and around Seoul (Bukhansan) around 4 November.
- **Winter (December to February) is cold and dry.** Seoul is below freezing at night, with some snow; it's the season for skiing, ice festivals and Christmas lights. Busan and Jeju are milder.
- **Summer (July and August) is hot and humid, with the monsoon rains.** Seoul gets about 414 mm of rain in July, the wettest month.

## Getting around South Korea

- **Buy a T-money card** at the airport or any convenience store, top it up with cash, and tap it on metros, buses and even taxis. A T-money card costs ₩3,000 to ₩5,000, says the Korea Tourism Organization. In Seoul, a Climate Card short-term pass gives unlimited metro and city bus rides (₩5,000 for 1 day up to ₩20,000 for 7 days, plus ₩3,000 for the card), says the Seoul city government; it doesn't cover airport buses, the Sinbundang Line or stations outside Seoul, and you can buy it only with cash or a Korean card.
- **Seoul's metro is easy:** signs and announcements are in English, and stations have numbers.
- **Between cities,** take the KTX fast train (Seoul to Busan in about 2½ hours) or intercity buses. Book KTX seats on the Korail website or app, especially at weekends.
- **Maps:** Google Maps works only partly in Korea. Locals use Naver Map or KakaoMap, which have English and give walking and transit directions.
- **Taxis** are metered. Kakao T is the main taxi app.
- **Learn a few words.** "Annyeonghaseyo" is hello and "kamsahamnida" is thank you. Fewer people speak English outside Seoul, so keep hotel addresses in Korean on your phone.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Korean food uses a lot of meat, fish sauce and seafood, so vegetarians need to plan:

- **Bibimbap:** rice with vegetables and chilli paste. Ask for it without meat and egg ("gogi bbaego").
- **Kimbap:** rice rolls; many have ham or tuna, so ask for vegetable kimbap ("yachae kimbap").
- **Tteokbokki:** spicy rice cakes, a street-food favourite, but the sauce often has fish stock.
- **Kimchi** usually contains fish sauce or salted shrimp. Temple food (sachal eumsik) is fully vegetarian and has no onion or garlic, which suits Jain travellers too.
- **Hotteok:** a sweet fried pancake with brown sugar and nuts, sold at street stalls in winter.
- **Indian restaurants:** Seoul (especially Itaewon) and Busan have Indian restaurants, and convenience stores sell fruit, bread, instant rice and snacks.
- **Jain travellers:** carry ready-to-eat food, khakhra or theplas, and check what you can take in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list. Don't pack meat, fruit, vegetables or plant products: Korea restricts them, and banned items are taken away at the airport.

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in South Korea

You have three ways to stay online in Korea:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card or a pocket Wi-Fi:** sold at Incheon airport counters. Shops ask for your passport.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply). Korea has fast free Wi-Fi in metro stations and many cafés.

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the South Korean won (₩, KRW).** Cards work almost everywhere, even in small shops and taxis. Carry some cash for street food, markets and topping up your T-money card.
- **Tipping isn't expected** in Korea.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Customs:** declare foreign currency and won over US$10,000 in total to [Korea Customs](https://www.customs.go.kr/english/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=10800&cntntsId=5500). The duty-free allowance is US$800 of goods per person, plus 2 bottles of alcohol (2 litres in total, up to US$400), 200 cigarettes and 100 ml of perfume, says the Korea Tourism Organization (updated 28 Sep 2026). Under-20s get no alcohol or tobacco allowance.
- **Tax refund:** many shops offer instant tax refunds on purchases for tourists. Show your passport when you pay.
- **Carry your passport** at all times. Police can ask to see it.

## What to wear in South Korea

- **Winter (December to February):** a warm padded jacket, thermals, gloves, a woolly hat and warm shoes. Seoul is often below freezing at night, and the wind makes it feel colder.
- **Spring and autumn:** layers, with a light jacket for the evenings.
- **Summer (July and August):** hot, humid and rainy. Pack light cotton clothes and an umbrella.
- **Temples:** dress modestly and take off your shoes where asked.
- **Comfortable walking shoes:** you'll walk a lot, with many stairs in metro stations and hilly areas.

## Help in an emergency

- **Emergency numbers in Korea:** police 112, fire and ambulance 119.
- **Korea Travel Helpline 1330:** a 24-hour tourist line in English. From a foreign mobile number, dial +82-2-1330. It can join a call with the emergency services to translate.
- **Embassy of India, Seoul:** contact details are on its [website](https://www.indembassyseoul.gov.in/).
- **Keep a photo of your passport and visa** and your insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address in Korean.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for South Korea?

Yes, for mainland Korea. Indians apply through the Korea Visa Portal and submit the application at a Korea Visa Application Centre (VFS) in India. The only exceptions are a 30-day visa-free stay on Jeju island (flying straight to Jeju from abroad) and a 30-day transit stay for holders of a US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand visa travelling to or from those countries.

### What is the South Korea visa fee for Indians?

₹3,600 for a short-term single-entry visa, plus a ₹1,600 VFS service charge, at the Korea Visa Application Centres in India (read 22 Nov 2026). Multiple entry costs ₹8,100, and express in Chennai ₹6,300.

### How long does a Korea visa take for Indians?

At least 15 working days in New Delhi, 17 in Kolkata and 25 in Chennai for a normal application. Express in Chennai takes 8–10 working days and needs a confirmed ticket and hotel. Apply no more than 89 days before you travel.

### What documents are required for a Korea tourist visa from India?

A passport valid for 6 months, 2 photos, the online application form, job proof, a year of salary slips, 2 years of ITR acknowledgements with PAN, and 6 months of bank statements stamped by the bank. Business owners add company papers. Check your centre's latest checklist.

### Can Indians visit South Korea with a US visa?

Only in transit. With a valid US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand visa, you can stay up to 30 days without a Korean visa if you're flying to or from that country through Korea, with an onward ticket. For a holiday on its own, you need a Korean visa.

### Is Jeju visa-free for Indians?

Yes. India isn't on the list of 11 countries excluded from Jeju's visa-free entry, so Indians can stay up to 30 days on Jeju if they fly there directly from abroad. You can't travel on to the mainland without a Korean visa.

### Are there direct flights from India to South Korea?

Yes, from Delhi only: Air India and Korean Air fly direct from Delhi to Seoul Incheon. From other Indian cities, fly one-stop via Delhi or another Asian hub.

### What is the best time to visit South Korea?

April and May for spring flowers, and late September to November for autumn colours. December to February is cold but dry, good for snow and skiing. July and August are hot and rainy.

### Is South Korea good for vegetarians?

It takes planning. Ask for bibimbap and kimbap without meat, egg or tuna, watch out for fish sauce in kimchi and soups, and look for temple food, which is fully vegetarian. Seoul has Indian restaurants.

### What is the time difference between India and South Korea?

Korea is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India, all year round.
