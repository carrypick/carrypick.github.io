Turkey, officially Türkiye, sits where Europe meets Asia: the mosques and bazaars of Istanbul on the Bosphorus, the hot air balloons and cave hotels of Cappadocia, and beaches on the Mediterranean. It's one of the few places in Europe with direct flights from India. Most Indians need a visa from a Turkish mission, but those with a valid US, UK, Schengen or Irish visa or residence permit can get an e-visa online. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day plan, Cappadocia's balloons, the weather by month, getting around and food for vegetarians.

## Turkey visa for Indians

Indians with an ordinary passport need a visa to enter Turkey, says Turkey's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (visa information page, read 21 Nov 2026). There are two ways to get one.

- **The usual way: a visa from a Turkish mission in India.** Apply at the Embassy of Türkiye in New Delhi or a Turkish consulate before you travel. Apply early, especially for the summer and the December holidays.
- **With a valid US, UK, Schengen or Irish visa or residence permit,** you can get a single-entry e-visa online instead, valid for one month, "provided that they meet certain conditions", says the foreign ministry.
- **Apply for the e-visa only on the official site, [evisa.gov.tr](https://www.evisa.gov.tr/en/).** Many other websites look official and charge extra. The conditions and the fee are shown on the official site when you apply.
- **Your US, UK, Schengen or Irish visa or permit must be valid.** Carry the passport that has it, and the e-visa printout or PDF.
- **Not sure?** Check with the Embassy of Türkiye in New Delhi before you book.

We couldn't open evisa.gov.tr's page for India when we checked, so the e-visa fee, the conditions and the sticker visa's fee and processing time aren't confirmed here. Check them on the official sites before you apply.

## Flights from India to Turkey

- **There are direct flights from India to Istanbul (IST).** IndiGo flies from Delhi and Mumbai, and Turkish Airlines flies from Delhi and Mumbai, says Istanbul Airport's airline list (Wikipedia, read 21 Nov 2026).
- **From other Indian cities,** fly one-stop via Delhi or Mumbai, or through the Gulf: Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha or Sharjah.
- **For Cappadocia,** take a domestic flight from Istanbul to Kayseri or Nevşehir (Kapadokya), then a road transfer.
- **Air India doesn't fly to Turkey,** so we don't show it below.
- **India is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of Turkey.** A flight that lands at 6 AM in Istanbul lands at 8:30 AM India time.
- **Baggage:** fares on low-cost airlines often include only a cabin bag. Check the limits by airline in the [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) and the [carry-on size by airline](../guides/carry-on-size-by-airline.html) guide.
- **Fares go up for Christmas, New Year and Indian school holidays.** Book early.

## A 7-day Turkey itinerary: Istanbul and Cappadocia

Most first trips from India spend a week: 3 or 4 days in Istanbul and 2 or 3 in Cappadocia. This plan keeps one flight inside Turkey.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Istanbul and rest. Walk around Sultanahmet Square | Dinner in Sultanahmet |
| 2 | The old city: Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque), the Basilica Cistern and Topkapı Palace | The Hippodrome area |
| 3 | The Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar, then a Bosphorus boat ride between Europe and Asia | Galata Tower and İstiklal Avenue |
| 4 | Fly to Kayseri or Nevşehir and transfer to Göreme. Walk to a valley viewpoint for sunset | A cave hotel in Göreme or Uçhisar |
| 5 | An early hot air balloon flight (weather allowing), then the Göreme Open-Air Museum and the fairy chimneys | Back in Göreme |
| 6 | An underground city (Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı), Uçhisar Castle and a valley walk | Fly back to Istanbul |
| 7 | Shopping and a last walk, then fly home | |

- **Balloon flights are weather-dependent.** They're often cancelled for wind, rain or snow, especially in winter. Book the balloon for your first morning in Cappadocia so you have a spare day if it's cancelled.
- **Ten days?** Add Pamukkale's white terraces and the ruins of Ephesus, or the beaches of Antalya in summer.
- **Mosques are open to visitors outside prayer times.** Take off your shoes, cover your shoulders and knees, and women should cover their hair. Many mosques lend scarves at the door.
- **Fridays at midday are busy** at the big mosques because of Friday prayers.

## Turkey weather in December, and the best time to go

Long-term averages from the Turkish State Meteorological Service, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Istanbul (day / night) | Istanbul rain | Cappadocia, Nevşehir (day / night) |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | About 15 °C / 9 °C | About 85 mm | About 12 °C / 2 °C |
| December | About 11 °C / 5 °C | About 107 mm, the wettest month | About 6 °C / −2 °C |
| January | About 9 °C / 3 °C | About 87 mm | About 4 °C / −4 °C, the coldest month |
| February | About 9 °C / 3 °C | About 71 mm | About 5 °C / −3 °C |
| April | About 17 °C / 8 °C | About 45 mm | About 16 °C / 5 °C |
| July | About 28 °C / 19 °C | About 24 mm | About 28 °C / 13 °C |
| October | About 20 °C / 12 °C | About 71 mm | About 18 °C / 6 °C |

- **April to June and September to October are best** for walking in Istanbul and for Cappadocia: mild days and fewer crowds than July and August.
- **December in Istanbul is cool and wet,** with rain on about 17 days of the month on average. Carry an umbrella and a warm, waterproof jacket.
- **Cappadocia is freezing at night in winter,** and snow on the fairy chimneys is possible. Balloon flights are cancelled more often in winter.
- **Antalya, on the Mediterranean coast,** stays milder: about 17 °C by day in December. But December and January are its wettest months.
- **Summer is hot,** about 28 °C in Istanbul and above 33 °C in Antalya in July.

## Getting around Turkey

- **In Istanbul, use the Istanbulkart,** the city's travel card for the metro, trams, buses and ferries. You can buy and top it up at machines in stations.
- **The T1 tram** runs past Sultanahmet, the Grand Bazaar and the ferry piers at Eminönü.
- **The ferries across the Bosphorus** are a cheap way to see the city from the water.
- **Taxis:** use the meter or a taxi app, and agree before you get in if there's no meter.
- **Between cities,** fly (Istanbul to Kayseri, Nevşehir, Antalya or Izmir) or take long-distance buses, which are comfortable but slow.
- **In Cappadocia,** the sights are spread out. Join a day tour or hire a car with a driver.
- **Learn a few words.** Turkish is spoken everywhere; English is common in hotels and tourist areas. A translation app helps.

## Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Turkish food has plenty for vegetarians, though many dishes use meat or meat stock, so ask first:

- **Mercimek çorbası:** red lentil soup, like a thin dal. Ask whether it's made with meat stock.
- **Menemen:** eggs cooked with tomatoes, peppers and onion, a common breakfast.
- **Gözleme:** a thin flatbread filled with spinach, cheese or potato, cooked on a griddle.
- **Börek and peynirli pide:** pastry and flatbread with cheese.
- **İmam bayıldı and zeytinyağlı dishes:** aubergine and vegetables cooked in olive oil, eaten cold.
- **Simit:** a sesame bread ring sold on the street.
- **Sweets:** baklava, künefe (with cheese) and lokum (Turkish delight).
- **Useful words:** "etsiz" means without meat. "Vejetaryen" is understood in tourist areas.
- **Indian restaurants:** Istanbul has Indian restaurants, many in the Sultanahmet and Taksim areas.
- **Jain travellers:** most dishes use garlic and onion. Carry ready-to-eat food or khakhra, and check what you can take in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in Turkey

You have three ways to stay online in Turkey:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops ask for your passport to register it.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

## Money and rules to know

- **The currency is the Turkish lira (₺, TRY).** Cards work in hotels, malls and most restaurants. Carry some cash for bazaars, small shops and tips.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Bargaining is normal** in the Grand Bazaar and markets, not in shops with marked prices.
- **Carry your passport** or a copy; police can ask for ID.
- **Photography:** don't take photos of military sites or police, and ask before photographing people.

## What to wear in Turkey

- **Winter (December to February):** a warm, waterproof jacket, a sweater or fleece, and waterproof shoes for Istanbul's rain. For Cappadocia add thermal innerwear, gloves and a woolly cap: an early balloon morning is very cold.
- **Spring and autumn:** layers, a light jacket and comfortable walking shoes for Istanbul's hills and cobbles.
- **Summer:** light, breathable clothes, sunglasses and sunscreen.
- **For mosques:** a scarf for women, and long trousers or a long skirt for everyone.

## Help in an emergency

- **Emergency number in Turkey:** 112.
- **Consulate General of India, Istanbul:** contact details are on its [website](https://www.cgiistanbul.gov.in/). India also has an embassy in Ankara.
- **Keep a photo of your passport, your visa** and your insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Turkey?

Yes. Indians with an ordinary passport need a visa to enter Turkey, says Turkey's foreign ministry. Most apply at a Turkish mission in India. Indians with a valid US, UK, Schengen or Irish visa or residence permit can get a single-entry e-visa online instead.

### Can Indians with a US, UK or Schengen visa get a Turkey e-visa?

Yes. Indians with a valid visa or residence permit of a Schengen country, the USA, the UK or Ireland can get a single-entry e-visa, valid for one month, on evisa.gov.tr, provided they meet certain conditions, says Turkey's foreign ministry. Apply only on the official site.

### What is the Turkey visa fee for Indians?

We couldn't confirm it on an official page when we checked. The e-visa fee is shown on evisa.gov.tr when you apply. For a visa from a Turkish mission, check with the Embassy of Türkiye in New Delhi.

### Are there direct flights from India to Turkey?

Yes. IndiGo and Turkish Airlines fly direct from Delhi and Mumbai to Istanbul. From other cities, fly one-stop via Delhi, Mumbai or the Gulf.

### How many days are enough for Turkey?

Seven days is enough for a first trip: 3 or 4 days in Istanbul and 2 or 3 days in Cappadocia. With 10 days, add Pamukkale and Ephesus, or Antalya's beaches in summer.

### Is December a good time to visit Turkey?

It's the low season. Istanbul averages about 11 °C by day and 5 °C at night in December and it's the city's wettest month, on the Turkish State Meteorological Service's averages. Cappadocia drops below freezing at night, and balloon flights are cancelled more often. Pack warm, waterproof clothes and keep a spare day.

### Is Turkey good for vegetarians?

Yes, with care. Lentil soup, gözleme, cheese börek and pide, menemen and vegetables cooked in olive oil are common, but ask whether soups use meat stock. Istanbul has Indian restaurants.

### What is the time difference between India and Turkey?

India is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of Turkey.
