## Bali visa for Indians

Indian passport holders can get Indonesia's tourist visa on arrival, the B1 visa, says Indonesia's official e-Visa website (checked 21 Nov 2026).

- **Fee:** Rp 500,000 per person.
- **Stay:** up to 30 days. You can extend it once, for another 30 days.
- **Apply online before you fly (e-VOA)** on the official site, [evisa.imigrasi.go.id](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/). Pay by Visa, Mastercard or JCB card. Once it's approved, a link to download the visa comes by email. One application can cover up to 5 people.
- **Use the visa within its validity.** The site shows a visa validity of 90 days for the e-VOA, so don't apply too early.
- **Or get it on arrival** at the airport. We couldn't confirm the counter's payment options on an official page, so the online e-VOA is the safer choice.
- **Show these to the immigration officer:** a passport valid for at least 6 months, and an onward ticket out of Indonesia.
- **No work on this visa.** You may not sell goods or services, or take wages, says the e-Visa site.
- **Use only the official site.** Many look-alike sites charge extra to apply for you.

## All Indonesia arrival card and the Bali tourist levy

Two more things to do before you land:

- **All Indonesia arrival card:** all travellers must submit an arrival card within 3 days before they arrive in Indonesia, says the e-Visa website. It covers immigration, customs, health and quarantine in one form. It's free on [allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id](https://allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id). Save the QR code on your phone.
- **Bali tourist levy: Rp 150,000 per person,** paid once per trip, says the Bali government's Love Bali site. Pay online before you fly on [lovebali.baliprov.go.id](https://lovebali.baliprov.go.id), or cashless at a counter at the airport or port. Love Bali strongly encourages paying online. You get a voucher with a QR code by email. Bali's rules say tourists who don't pay may be refused entry to attractions.

## A 6-day Bali itinerary

Most first trips from India spend 5 or 6 days. Bali's roads are slow, so this plan changes hotels once: 3 nights in Ubud, then 3 nights near the beach.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land in Denpasar and drive to Ubud. Rest | Walk around Ubud town and its market |
| 2 | Ubud: the Tegallalang rice terraces, the Tirta Empul water temple and a coffee plantation | Balinese dance show in Ubud |
| 3 | Ubud: the Sacred Monkey Forest, a cooking class or a spa, or a sunrise trek | Quiet dinner in Ubud |
| 4 | Move to Seminyak or Kuta. Beach and pool time | Sunset at the beach |
| 5 | Day trip by fast boat to Nusa Penida: Kelingking Beach and the west coast viewpoints | Back on the main island |
| 6 | Uluwatu: the cliff temple and beaches, then fly home, or stay one more night | The Kecak fire dance at Uluwatu at sunset |

- **Only 5 days?** Spend 2 nights in Ubud and skip day 3, or skip Nusa Penida.
- **Nusa Penida is a long day.** Boats leave from Sanur on the east coast, and the island's roads are rough. Skip it if the sea is rough or you get seasick.
- **Temples need a sarong.** Cover your shoulders and knees. Many temples give a sarong and sash at the gate, says Indonesia's official tourism site.
- **Sunrise trek?** It starts in the dark, and it's cooler up a volcano, so take a light layer.
- **In the wet months, plan mornings outdoors.** Rain often comes later in the day.

## Bali honeymoon tips

- **Split the stay.** Ubud is green and quiet, and good for a private pool villa. Seminyak and Uluwatu have the beaches, sunsets and restaurants.
- **Book a villa with a private pool** if you want privacy. Read recent reviews for the photos and the location.
- **Plan one special evening:** a sunset dinner, the Kecak dance at Uluwatu or a spa for two.
- **Ask about honeymoon extras** when you book, like a room decoration or a dinner. Get them in writing.
- **Don't over-plan.** Bali traffic is slow, so keep one or two sights a day.
- **For the photos,** pack one outfit for a temple and one for a nice dinner. See what to pack in our [honeymoon packing list](../guides/honeymoon-packing-list.html#beach-honeymoon-maldives-bali-goa-and-kerala).
- **Rules for photos:** Bali's rules for tourists ban climbing sacred trees or monuments and taking nude or improper photos at holy places.

## Bali weather in December and the best time to go

Long-term averages for Denpasar from Indonesia's weather agency BMKG, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Day | Night | Rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 179 mm, on about 16 days |
| December | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 276 mm, on about 22 days |
| January | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 345 mm, on about 27 days, the wettest month |
| February | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 274 mm, on about 22 days |
| July | About 30 °C | About 23 °C | About 55 mm, on about 4 days |
| August | About 30 °C | About 23 °C | About 25 mm, on about 4 days |

- **It's hot all year,** about 30–34 °C in the day and 22–25 °C at night in Denpasar.
- **December to February is the wettest time,** with rain on most days. You can still go: pack a poncho and keep indoor plans, like a spa or a cooking class, for wet afternoons.
- **June to September is the driest time,** with rain on only 4–8 days a month. It is also the busiest.
- **Is December a good time?** Yes, if you don't mind rain. Bali is green, and you get Christmas and New Year events, but prices are highest from Christmas to New Year.

## Getting around Bali

- **There is no metro or train.** Most visitors hire a car with a driver for the day, or use taxis and ride-hailing apps.
- **A car with a driver** is the easiest way for a couple or a family. Agree the hours, the stops and the price before you start.
- **Scooters:** Bali's rules for tourists say you need a valid driving licence and must wear a helmet. If you don't ride at home, don't learn in Bali traffic.
- **Fast boats to Nusa Penida** leave from Sanur. Wear a life jacket and keep your phone in a waterproof pouch.
- **Leave time for traffic,** especially from Ubud to the airport, and around Kuta and Seminyak in the evening.

## Food in Bali for Indian travellers

- **Many Balinese dishes use pork,** like babi guling (roast pig), and some use beef. Ask before you order.
- **Veg dishes to look for:** gado-gado (vegetables with peanut sauce), nasi goreng or mie goreng (fried rice or noodles) made without egg or meat, tempeh and tofu dishes, and fresh fruit.
- **Ask for no shrimp paste (terasi) or fish sauce** if you are strict veg or Jain. They are used in many sauces and sambals.
- **Indian restaurants** are easy to find in the tourist areas, like Kuta, Seminyak and Ubud.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water.** Bali's rules ban single-use plastic, so carry a refillable bottle.

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in Bali

You have three ways to stay online in Bali:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

## Money and rules to know in Bali

- **The currency is the Indonesian rupiah (Rp).** Cards work in many hotels and restaurants. Keep cash for small shops, warungs (small eateries) and tips.
- **Change money only at licensed money changers,** as Bali's rules for tourists ask. The Love Bali site lists authorised ones.
- **Carrying cash:** RBI lets a bank or money changer sell you up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can take up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Indonesia asks you to declare cash worth Rp 100 million or more. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Customs:** goods worth up to US$500 per person come in duty free. You may bring 1 litre of alcohol and 200 cigarettes; Indonesian Customs destroys the extra.
- **Temples:** dress modestly, and don't step on offerings. Bali's rules say women on their period may not enter the sacred areas of temples.
- **No single-use plastic.** Bags, straws, Styrofoam and plastic-packed drinks are banned under Bali's rules.
- **Medicines:** carry them in the original pack, with your prescription.

## What to wear in Bali

- **Light cotton or quick-dry clothes,** shorts, T-shirts and dresses for the heat.
- **One temple outfit** that covers shoulders and knees.
- **Swimwear,** and a kaftan or shirt to wear over it away from the beach.
- **Sandals that can get wet,** and walking shoes for Nusa Penida and treks.
- **A rain poncho,** sunscreen, sunglasses, a cap and mosquito repellent.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Bali?

Yes, but it's easy. Indians can get Indonesia's visa on arrival (B1) for Rp 500,000, for a stay of up to 30 days, says Indonesia's official e-Visa website. You can buy it online before you fly as an e-VOA on evisa.imigrasi.go.id.

### What is the Bali visa fee for Indians?

Rp 500,000 per person for the visa on arrival, for up to 30 days. The Bali tourist levy of Rp 150,000 per person is paid separately.

### Can the Bali visa on arrival be extended?

Yes, once, for another 30 days, says Indonesia's e-Visa website. You can start the extension on the same site.

### What documents do Indians need for Bali?

A passport valid for at least 6 months, an onward or return ticket, the visa on arrival or e-VOA, the All Indonesia arrival card (filed within 3 days before you land) and the Bali tourist levy voucher.

### What is the Bali tourist levy?

A fee of Rp 150,000 per foreign tourist, paid once per trip, says the Bali government's Love Bali site. Pay it online before you fly or at a counter at the airport.

### Which cities in India have direct flights to Bali?

Delhi (Air India and IndiGo) and Bengaluru (IndiGo). IndiGo's Mumbai flights are listed to start on 1 March 2027. From other cities, fly with one stop.

### How many days are enough for Bali?

Five or six days cover Ubud, a beach area, Uluwatu and a day trip to Nusa Penida. A week lets you go slower.

### Is Bali good for a honeymoon?

Yes. Pool villas in Ubud, beach sunsets in Seminyak and Uluwatu, spas and the Kecak dance make it one of the most popular honeymoon trips from India.

### Is December a good time to visit Bali?

It's the wet season, with rain on about 22 days in December, but it is still warm, about 33 °C by day, and showers are often in the afternoon. It is also the busiest and costliest time around Christmas and New Year, so book early.

### What is the time difference between India and Bali?

Bali is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India.

### Is veg food easy to find in Bali?

Yes, in the tourist areas. Look for gado-gado, tempeh and tofu dishes, and fried rice made without egg or meat, and ask for no shrimp paste. Indian restaurants are common in Kuta, Seminyak and Ubud.
