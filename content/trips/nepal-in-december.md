Nepal in December is dry, sunny and cold at night. The skies are mostly clear, so it is a good month for mountain views from Nagarkot and Pokhara. Days in Kathmandu are mild, about 20 °C, but nights fall to about 4 °C. Indians need no visa, only a passport or original voter ID. This guide covers the weather, where it snows, the festivals, a simple 6-day plan, the Everest mountain flight, trekking in winter, how to get there from India and what to wear.

## Nepal weather in December and January

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology (DHM), as tabled on Wikipedia:

| Place | Height | December day / night | January day / night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kathmandu | About 1,340 m | About 20 °C / 4 °C | About 19 °C / 3 °C |
| Pokhara | About 830 m | About 21 °C / 8 °C | About 20 °C / 7 °C |
| Nagarkot | About 2,150 m | About 14 °C / 4 °C | About 13 °C / 3 °C |
| Chitwan (Bharatpur) | About 190 m | About 24 °C / 9 °C | About 22 °C / 8 °C |

- **Days are sunny, nights are cold.** Dress in layers you can take off at noon. Many older hotels and homes have no central heating.
- **January is the coldest month,** but only by a degree or two. Both months are dry, with clear mornings.
- **Pokhara and Chitwan are milder.** They are much lower than Kathmandu, so nights are less cold.
- **Nagarkot is the cold one.** It is about 800 m higher than Kathmandu. Take a warm jacket, a cap and gloves for the sunrise.
- **The Terai gets fog.** Winter fog in the plains of southern Nepal usually starts in December, reports The Kathmandu Post. Morning flights and drives in the south can be delayed.

## Does it snow in Nepal in December?

- **Not in Kathmandu or Pokhara.** Snow in Kathmandu city is very rare: it fell in 1945 and 2007. Pokhara is even lower and warmer.
- **The hills around Kathmandu get snow only in some winters.** In February 2019, Phulchoki, Nagarkot, Chandragiri and Shivapuri had snow "after many years", reported The Kathmandu Post.
- **Snow falls on the high trekking routes.** In late January 2026, heavy snow in upper Manang stopped trekkers from crossing Thorong La on the Annapurna Circuit for a while, reported The Kathmandu Post.
- **Want to see snow without a trek?** The Everest mountain flight (see below) and the sunrise views from Nagarkot and Sarangkot show the snowy Himalaya on clear mornings.

## Festivals and holidays in December 2026 and January 2027

Dates are from Nepal's public holiday list for 2083 BS, published by the Ministry of Home Affairs (we read it in a law firm's summary). Festivals follow the lunar calendar, so check before you plan around one.

| Day | Festival or holiday | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 24 December 2026 | Yomari Punhi, Udhauli | A Newar harvest festival in the Kathmandu valley, known for yomari, a sweet rice dumpling |
| 25 December 2026 | Christmas | A public holiday. Hotels in Thamel and Lakeside are busy until New Year |
| 30 December 2026 | Tamu Lhosar | The Gurung New Year, celebrated most in Pokhara and the hills around it |
| 11 January 2027 | Prithvi Jayanti (National Unity Day) | A public holiday |
| 15 January 2027 | Maghe Sankranti | The start of the month of Magh, with holy dips and special food, the same day as Makar Sankranti in India |
| 30 January 2027 | Martyrs' Day | A public holiday |

- **Sonam Lhosar,** the Tamang New Year, falls in early February in 2027, so it is outside this guide.
- **On a public holiday,** government offices and some shops close, but temples, hotels and sights stay open.

## A simple 6-day plan for December

Most first trips from India cover the Kathmandu valley, Nagarkot and Pokhara. Add two days for Chitwan if you want a jungle safari.

| Day | Plan | Night |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fly into Kathmandu. Pashupatinath in the afternoon and the evening aarti, then Boudhanath stupa at sunset | Kathmandu |
| 2 | Kathmandu Durbar Square, Swayambhunath, and Patan Durbar Square in the afternoon | Kathmandu |
| 3 | Bhaktapur Durbar Square in the morning, then drive up to Nagarkot | Nagarkot |
| 4 | Sunrise over the Himalaya at Nagarkot. Back to Kathmandu, then fly or drive to Pokhara | Pokhara |
| 5 | Sunrise at Sarangkot, a boat on Phewa Lake, and the World Peace Pagoda | Pokhara |
| 6 | Fly back to Kathmandu and home, or stay one more night | Kathmandu |

- **Want the Everest mountain flight?** Add it on the morning of day 2 or day 3. It is an early flight, so keep a spare morning in case of bad weather.
- **Only 4 days?** Stay in the Kathmandu valley, with one night in Nagarkot.
- **Going to Chitwan?** Go from Pokhara by road and fly home from Bharatpur, or come back to Kathmandu. Mornings there can be foggy in December and January.
- **Kathmandu to Pokhara by road** takes most of a day on winding hill roads. A short flight saves time. Small planes have a 5 kg cabin bag limit, so see the bag table in our [Nepal packing list](../guides/nepal-packing-list.html#which-bag-to-take).
- **At Pashupatinath,** only Hindus may enter the main temple. Others can visit the ghats and watch the aarti from the east bank. Dress modestly at all temples.

## The Everest mountain flight

- **What it is:** a short sightseeing flight from Kathmandu along the Himalaya, past Everest and other high peaks, and back. You don't land anywhere.
- **Buddha Air** runs its "Everest Experience" daily from about 6:15 AM. The flight lasts about 50 minutes to an hour, and every passenger gets a window seat, says the airline.
- **Yeti Airlines** also runs a mountain flight of about an hour.
- **It depends on the weather.** If the weather is bad, Buddha Air cancels the flight and offers a refund or a new date. Winter mornings are often clear, but keep a spare morning just in case.
- **Book directly with the airline** or through your hotel or a registered travel agent.

## Trekking in Nepal in December and January

Winter treks are possible, but it is very cold up high, days are short, and some high passes close after snow. Book through a registered trekking agency, and ask it about the route each week before you go.

- **Lower, shorter treks are the usual winter choice.** Treks that stay lower, with lodges open all winter, are easier than crossing high passes. Your agency will tell you which routes are open.
- **High passes can close with snow.** Thorong La on the Annapurna Circuit was closed for a while after heavy snow in January 2026. Larkya La on the Manaslu route was blocked by snow in late September 2026, reported Onlinekhabar.
- **Avalanches are a risk on some routes** after heavy snow, such as the trail to Annapurna Base Camp in January 2020, reported The Kathmandu Post.
- **Guide and TIMS card:** Nepal Tourism Board says that since 31 March 2023, trekkers on many protected-area routes must go with a licensed guide and carry a TIMS card issued through a trekking agency. Its list includes Everest Base Camp, Langtang, the Annapurna Circuit, Poon Hill and Annapurna Base Camp, and Mardi Himal. The page lists no exemption for Indians.
- **The TIMS card costs NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals,** including Indians, says Nepal Tourism Board.
- **The Everest region has its own rules.** Its local municipality said in 2023 that it doesn't ask for TIMS there, which differs from the Tourism Board's list. We couldn't confirm which rule applies now, so check with your agency.
- **Park and conservation area fees for SAARC nationals:** NPR 1,000 for conservation areas such as Annapurna (NTNC permit office) and NPR 1,500 for Sagarmatha (Everest) National Park (Nepal Tourism Board).
- **Go up slowly.** Above 2,500 m, sleep no more than 300–500 m higher each day, and rest a day after every 1,000 m, says Nepal Tourism Board. If you feel worse, go down.
- **Rescue posts may be closed in winter.** The Himalayan Rescue Association's aid posts at Pheriche and Manang are staffed in spring and autumn.

Pack for the trek separately, with your agency's list. It is far colder up high than in Kathmandu.

## Getting to Nepal from India

- **Air India** flies direct from Delhi to Kathmandu, with about 6 flights a day. The flight takes about 1 hour 40 minutes, says the airline.
- **IndiGo** has two direct flights a day from Delhi to Kathmandu, and connecting flights from other cities through Delhi.
- **Nepal Airlines** lists flights from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru in its winter timetable.
- **Buddha Air** flies to Kathmandu from Varanasi and Kolkata.
- **Nepal is 15 minutes ahead of India** (GMT+5:45). Set your watch when you land.
- **Check your bags:** Buddha Air's cabin bag limit is only 5 kg. See the airline table in our [Nepal packing list](../guides/nepal-packing-list.html#which-bag-to-take), and the [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) for IndiGo and Air India.

### By road or train

- **Use an official border crossing.** Nepal Tourism Board lists these entry points from India: Kakarbhitta (from Siliguri), Birgunj (from Raxaul), Belahiya near Bhairahawa (from Sunauli), Nepalgunj (from Rupaidiha), Dhangadhi and Mahendranagar (from Banbasa).
- **Nearest railway stations:** New Jalpaiguri (NJP) for Kakarbhitta, Raxaul for Birgunj, and Gorakhpur for Sunauli.
- **Taking your own car?** Indian cars need a customs pass (bhansar) from Nepal Customs at the border, for the exact number of days. The Embassy of India's notice says the total limit is 30 days a year, and a car without a valid pass can be seized. The process may have moved online, so check at the border.
- **A train runs from Jaynagar in Bihar to Kurtha in Nepal,** the first broad-gauge passenger train between the two countries, opened in April 2022.
- **Carry your passport or original voter ID at the border too,** not in a bag on the roof of a bus.

## Documents and money

- **No visa for Indians.** For flights, carry a valid Indian passport or your original voter ID card with photo, says the Embassy of India in Kathmandu. A printed or downloaded voter ID isn't accepted.
- **Aadhaar, PAN card and driving licence aren't accepted** for travel to Nepal.
- **Children:** the safest ID for a child is a passport. See the [documents section of our Nepal packing list](../guides/nepal-packing-list.html#documents-for-nepal-from-india) for the rules on children and older travellers.
- **Indian rupees:** notes of ₹100 and smaller have no special limit. You can carry up to ₹25,000 per person in ₹200 and ₹500 notes, says Nepal Rastra Bank. Leave ₹2,000 notes at home.
- **Change some money to Nepali rupees** for taxis, small shops and entry tickets, and carry a card for hotels and ATMs.
- **A local SIM:** Ncell asks tourists for their original passport to register a tourist SIM. If you travel on a voter ID, ask your Indian mobile company about roaming before you go.

For the full list, see [Indian currency in Nepal](../guides/nepal-packing-list.html#indian-currency-in-nepal) and [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).

## What to wear in Nepal in December

What should I wear? Try the [what-to-wear helper](../tools/what-to-wear.html#nepal) for day and night temperatures and an outfit list by month.

- **Layers:** thermals, a full-sleeve top, a sweater or fleece, and a warm jacket for nights of 3–4 °C in Kathmandu. Take layers off in the midday sun.
- **For Nagarkot and Sarangkot sunrises:** your warmest jacket, a woollen cap, gloves and a neck warmer.
- **For temples:** cover your shoulders and knees. Wear shoes that slip off easily, because you take them off at every shrine.
- **Shoes:** walking shoes with a good grip for old city streets and stupa steps. Hiking shoes if you'll walk on hill trails.
- **Skin and eyes:** lip balm, moisturiser, sunscreen and sunglasses. The winter air is dry and the sun is strong.
- **A mask** for dusty roads and busy streets in Kathmandu.

## Health and safety

- **Never sleep with a gas heater or a fire burning in a closed room.** It can cause carbon monoxide poisoning. Keep a window slightly open and switch heaters off before you sleep.
- **Drink boiled, filtered or sealed water,** and carry something for an upset stomach.
- **Hill roads are slow and winding.** Carry something for motion sickness if you need it, and don't plan long drives after dark.
- **Trekking?** Read the altitude advice above, and make sure your travel insurance covers trekking and rescue.
- **Drones need permission** from Nepal's authorities before you fly one.

## Questions

### Is December a good time to visit Nepal?

Yes, if you're fine with cold nights. December is dry and mostly sunny, with clear mountain views. Kathmandu is about 20 °C by day and 4 °C at night, and Pokhara is a little warmer. High treks are colder and some passes can close after snow, so choose your trek carefully.

### What is the weather in Nepal in December?

Long-term DHM averages are about 20 °C by day and 4 °C at night in Kathmandu, 21 °C and 8 °C in Pokhara, 14 °C and 4 °C in Nagarkot, and 24 °C and 9 °C in Chitwan. It rarely rains in December.

### What is the temperature in Kathmandu in December?

About 20 °C in the afternoon and about 4 °C at night, on average. Mornings are cold, and old buildings often have no heating, so pack warm layers.

### Does it snow in Kathmandu in December?

Almost never. Snow fell in Kathmandu city in 1945 and 2007, and the hills around the valley, like Nagarkot and Phulchoki, get snow only in some winters. For snow, you need to go up into the mountains, or see it from the Everest mountain flight.

### What is the weather in Nepal in January?

January is the coldest month. Kathmandu averages about 19 °C by day and 3 °C at night, Pokhara about 20 °C and 7 °C, and Nagarkot about 13 °C and 3 °C. Days stay dry and sunny.

### Is Nepal good in the last week of December?

Yes. The weather stays dry and clear, and you can see Yomari Punhi on 24 December and Tamu Lhosar in Pokhara on 30 December. Christmas and New Year are busy with visitors from India, so book hotels and flights early.

### Can I trek in Nepal in December?

Yes, but it is very cold up high and some high passes, like Thorong La, can close after snow. Book through a registered agency. Nepal Tourism Board says many routes need a licensed guide and a TIMS card, which costs NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals.

### How many days are enough for Nepal?

Six days cover Kathmandu, Nagarkot and Pokhara. Four days are enough for the Kathmandu valley with one night in Nagarkot. Add two days for Chitwan, and more for any trek.

### What documents do Indians need for Nepal?

A valid Indian passport or your original voter ID card with photo. No visa is needed. Aadhaar, PAN card and driving licence aren't accepted. See our [Nepal packing list](../guides/nepal-packing-list.html#documents-for-nepal-from-india).

### What should I wear in Nepal in December?

Layers: thermals, a full-sleeve top, a sweater and a warm jacket, plus a cap, gloves and woollen socks. For temples, cover your shoulders and knees, and wear shoes that slip off easily.
