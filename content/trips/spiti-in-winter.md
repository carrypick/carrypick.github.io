Spiti in winter is white, silent and very cold. The mud-brick villages and monasteries stand in deep snow, the river freezes at the edges, and there are almost no other tourists. It's also hard: only one road is open, nights fall far below freezing, many hotels shut, and snow can close the road for days. This guide covers the road, Chandratal, the cold, snow leopards, where to stay, fuel, cash and phones, permits, altitude and a 7-day winter route from Shimla.

## Is Spiti open in winter?

Yes, but by one road only.

- **The open road:** Shimla – Narkanda – Rampur – Reckong Peo (Kinnaur) – Pooh – Nako – Sumdo – Tabo – Kaza, on NH-5 and then NH-505. It's the main road into Spiti, and the only one in winter.
- **The closed road:** Manali – Atal Tunnel – Gramphu – Batal – Kunzum La – Losar – Kaza. The Lahaul-Spiti district's road-status page lists Keylong to Kaza as **closed**, and news reports say about 100 km of the road from Gramphu towards Kaza is snowbound in winter. The district says Kunzum La (about 4,550 m) can be crossed only from about May or June to October or November.
- **Snow can close the open road too,** for a day or longer, especially on the stretches after Nako. We found no official winter road bulletin for the Kinnaur–Kaza road, so check with your homestay, driver or the district helpline (1077) the day before each drive.
- **Keep 2–3 spare days,** and travel with a local driver who knows the road in winter. A 4x4 or a car with snow chains is safer on ice.
- **We found no official winter bus timetable.** HP Tourism lists regular buses from Shimla to Kaza through Kinnaur for May to October. Ask HRTC at Shimla or Reckong Peo before you plan on a winter bus.

## Spiti weather in December, January and February

We couldn't find IMD averages for Kaza itself. The nearest IMD stations with published averages are Kalpa, on the way in through Kinnaur, and Keylong, in Lahaul on the other side of Kunzum La. These are IMD's long-term averages (1971–2000):

| Month | Kalpa (day / night) | Keylong (day / night) |
|---|---|---|
| December | About 8 °C / −1 °C | About 12 °C / −14 °C |
| January | About 4 °C / −4 °C | About 7 °C / −17 °C |
| February | About 6 °C / −3 °C | About 6 °C / −18 °C |

- **Kaza is higher than both,** at about 3,660 m, so expect nights well below these, especially in the high villages like Kibber.
- **January is the coldest month** at both stations. Keylong's coldest January night on record was −18.4 °C (2008), and Kalpa's was −15.4 °C (1985).
- **Sunny days feel much warmer** out of the wind, but the temperature drops fast after sunset.
- **Water freezes.** Pipes freeze in many homestays, so you may get hot water in buckets, and dry toilets are common.

## Chandratal in winter

- **You can't get there.** The Spiti Wildlife Division, which runs the Chandratal Wildlife Sanctuary, says the road is open from both sides for only about four months a year, typically July to mid-October. In winter, from about mid-November to March, the area is cut off by road.
- **The lake is about 4,300 m high.** Travellers describe it frozen in winter, but we found no official page on the ice, and no one can drive there to see it.
- **Kunzum La is closed too,** so Chandratal, Batal and Losar's road to Manali are all out of reach until summer.
- **For frozen water in winter,** look at the river below Kaza and the streams near Tabo and Nako, from the road or with a local guide. Don't walk on ice.
- **For Chandratal,** plan a summer trip with our [Spiti Valley trip guide](spiti-valley-trip-guide.html).

## Snow leopards in Spiti

Winter is when many people come to Spiti hoping to see a snow leopard. The animals come lower in the cold, following the ibex and blue sheep, though no official page we found gives a sighting season.

- **Where:** the Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary, about 2,220 sq km around Kibber, run by the Spiti Wildlife Division of the Himachal Forest Department. It lists the snow leopard, ibex and blue sheep among its main animals.
- **How many:** Himachal's second state survey, by the Forest Department's Wildlife Wing with the Nature Conservation Foundation, estimated **83 snow leopards** in the state (range 67 to 103), up from 51 in 2021. The highest numbers were in Spiti, Pin, upper Kinnaur and Tabo. The survey was released in October 2025, news reports said.
- **Sightings aren't guaranteed.** Snow leopards are shy and well hidden. Go with a local guide from Kibber or the villages around it, and plan several days.
- **Don't disturb them.** The Wildlife Division warns that winter snow-leopard tourism can disturb wildlife. Keep your distance, don't chase animals with vehicles, and never use baits or calls.
- **Camping, trekking and photography** in the sanctuary need a permit from the Wildlife Division in Kaza. Ask your guide or the DFO's office before you go.

## Where to stay and what's open in Kaza

- **Most hotels and camps close in winter.** Stay in a homestay or a small hotel that says it's open, and confirm a few days before you arrive.
- **Ask before you pay** about heating (a bukhari stove or a heater), hot water, backup power and meals. Many homestays serve simple home food at fixed times.
- **Kaza is the base.** HP Tourism's Spiti page says Kaza has a bank, an ATM, a post office and a petrol and diesel pump. Shops and cafés are fewer in winter, and many open late or close early.
- **Villages like Kibber, Langza and Key** have a few homestays that stay open in winter. They're higher and colder than Kaza, so sleep in Kaza first.
- **Never sleep with a stove, heater or coal fire burning in a closed room.** It gives off carbon monoxide, which can kill without warning. Keep a window open a little.

## Fuel, ATMs and phone network

- **Fill up at Reckong Peo,** and again in Kaza. HP Tourism lists a petrol and diesel pump in Kaza. Pumps between them are few, and diesel can thicken in deep cold, so ask your driver about winter diesel.
- **Carry enough cash** for the whole trip. HP Tourism lists an ATM in Kaza, but one machine can run out or stop working, and many homestays and taxis take only cash. Online payments need a phone signal.
- **Phone signal is patchy.** We found no official page on winter coverage. News reports say BSNL is adding 4G towers in Lahaul-Spiti. Expect signal in Kaza and some bigger villages, and none on long stretches of road. Tell someone your plan before you set off.
- **Cold drains batteries fast.** Keep your phone and power bank in an inside pocket.

## Permits for Spiti in winter

**Indian citizens:** the permit rules on the Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti district websites are for foreign nationals. We found no permit for Indians on the Shimla–Kinnaur–Kaza road. Carry your ID, as there are police checkposts.

**Foreigners** need an Inner Line Permit (a protected area permit) for the border stretch between Kinnaur and Spiti, which is the only way in during winter:

- **Which places:** the Kinnaur district lists places in the Pooh block, such as Nako, Khab, Pooh, Leo and Kaa, and in Spiti, such as Tabo, Dhankar, Poh, Lari and Sumdo. The Lahaul-Spiti district lists Khab, Samdo, Dhankar, Tabo, Kaza, Morang and Dubling.
- **Group size differs by page.** Kinnaur says groups of two or more, through a recognised Indian travel agency, for up to 30 days, at ₹200 a person (page updated July 2023). The Lahaul-Spiti page (updated 2018) says groups of four or more. Ask the issuing office before you plan.
- **Where to get it:** the SDM office at Reckong Peo (Kinnaur) on the way in, or the SDM at Kaza (01906-222302). Carry your passport, visa and photocopies.
- **Register where you stay.** The Lahaul-Spiti district says hotels must report foreign guests (Form C) within 24 hours.

## Altitude sickness in winter

- **Spiti is high.** Kaza is about 3,660 m, and the Lahaul-Spiti district puts Key monastery at about 13,500 ft (4,115 m) and Kibber at about 14,200 ft (4,330 m).
- **Go up slowly.** The district's travel advice is to gain height slowly. The Kinnaur road helps: sleep at Kalpa and Nako on the way, not straight in Kaza.
- **Cold makes it harder.** Breathing cold, dry air and sleeping badly add to the strain. Rest on your first day in Kaza, drink plenty of water (warm water is easier), and avoid alcohol.
- **Warning signs** are headache, nausea, poor sleep and breathlessness. If you're breathless at rest or confused, go down and get medical help.
- **Ask your doctor** before the trip, especially for children, older travellers and anyone with a heart or lung condition. Winter Spiti isn't a good idea for babies.

## Spiti winter trip plan: 7 days from Shimla

In and out the same way, through Kinnaur. Add 1–2 spare days for snow.

**Day 1: Shimla to Kalpa**

1. **Leave Shimla early,** through Narkanda and Rampur along the Sutlej, to Reckong Peo.
2. **Night in Kalpa** above Reckong Peo, at about the height of Kalpa's IMD station. Foreigners: get your Inner Line Permit at Reckong Peo.

**Day 2: Kalpa to Nako**

1. **Through Pooh** and up the hairpin bends to **Nako** village.
2. **Rest and walk slowly.** You're now well above 3,000 m.

**Day 3: Nako to Kaza, via Tabo**

1. **Tabo monastery** and its old mud-walled temples.
2. **On to Kaza,** with a stop at **Dhankar** if the road up is clear.

**Day 4: rest day in Kaza**

1. **Take it easy** around Kaza and its monastery.
2. **Ask about snow-leopard guides** in Kibber for the next days.

**Day 5: Key and Kibber**

1. **Key monastery** in the morning, in the snow.
2. **Kibber** in the afternoon. Spend the day with a local guide looking for snow leopards and ibex if you're well.

**Day 6: Kaza to Kalpa or Nako**

1. **Leave early** and drive back down through Tabo.
2. **Night at Nako or Kalpa,** depending on the road.

**Day 7: back to Shimla**

1. **Long drive** down the Sutlej to Shimla.

**For 8 days or more:** add a second Kibber day for snow leopards, or a day for Langza and Hikkim if the roads are clear. Keep the spare days for snow either way.

## Things to do in Spiti in winter

- **Monasteries in the snow:** Key, Tabo, Dhankar and Kaza. Dress modestly and ask before you take photos inside.
- **Snow leopards and ibex** around Kibber, with a local guide.
- **Village life:** winter is when families stay home, so a homestay shows a side of Spiti summer visitors don't see.
- **Stargazing:** the dry, clear nights are good for it, if you can bear the cold.
- **Not in winter:** Chandratal, Kunzum La and the drive over to Manali.

## What to wear in Spiti in winter

Dress for nights far below freezing, and for wind:

| Layer | What to wear |
|---|---|
| 1. Next to your skin | A thermal top and bottom, and a spare set |
| 2. Middle | A thick fleece, or two thin ones |
| 3. Outside | A heavy down jacket with a hood, windproof if possible |
| 4. Extras | Woollen cap, neck warmer, waterproof gloves, two pairs of woollen socks, sunglasses with UV protection, sunscreen and lip balm |

- **Shoes:** insulated, waterproof boots with deep grip. Paths and steps are icy.
- **Sun:** the sun on snow at this height is very strong. Wear sunglasses even on cloudy days.
- **At night:** sleep in your thermals and a cap. Homestays give heavy quilts, but bring warm socks for bed.

## Questions

### Is Spiti Valley open in winter?

Yes, by the Shimla–Kinnaur–Kaza road only. Kunzum La and the road from Manali are closed by snow, and the Kinnaur road itself can close for a day or more after snowfall, so keep spare days.

### Can I go to Chandratal in winter?

No. The Spiti Wildlife Division says the road to Chandratal is open only from about July to mid-October, and the area is cut off by road in winter. Plan it for summer.

### How cold is Spiti in January?

Very cold. There are no published IMD averages for Kaza, but at Kalpa, lower down on the way in, January nights average about −4 °C, and at Keylong in Lahaul about −17 °C. Kaza is higher than both, so expect nights far below freezing.

### Can you see snow leopards in Spiti in winter?

Sometimes, with luck and a good local guide, around Kibber. Himachal's 2025 survey estimated 83 snow leopards in the state, with the most in Spiti, Pin, upper Kinnaur and Tabo. Sightings aren't guaranteed, so plan a few days.

### Is there an ATM and petrol pump in Kaza?

HP Tourism lists a bank, an ATM and a petrol and diesel pump in Kaza. Still, carry enough cash for the whole trip and fill up at Reckong Peo, as the ATM can run out and many places take only cash.

### Do I need a permit for Spiti in winter?

Indians: we found no permit for Indian citizens. Foreigners: yes, an Inner Line Permit for the Kinnaur–Spiti border stretch, including Nako and Tabo, which is the only way in during winter. Get it at Reckong Peo on the way.

### How many days do I need for Spiti in winter?

About 7 days from Shimla and back, plus 1–2 spare days for snow. Add a day or two for snow leopards around Kibber.
