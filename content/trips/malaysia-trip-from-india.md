## Malaysia visa for Indians

Indians can visit Malaysia without a visa for now. The High Commission of Malaysia in New Delhi says the visa waiver for Indian nationals runs **until 31 December 2026, for stays of up to 30 days**, for business, tourism, social or transit visits (page updated 1 Nov 2026). Malaysia's Immigration Department also lists India with the note "visa exempts until 31st December 2026" (checked 21 Nov 2026).

At the airport you must show:

- **A passport valid for at least 6 months.**
- **A confirmed return flight ticket.**
- **A confirmed hotel booking.**
- **Your MDAC,** the arrival card you fill in online (see below).

The officer may also ask how you will pay for the trip, so carry a card or some cash.

- **Travelling after 31 December 2026?** On 21 Nov 2026 no official page said the waiver will be extended. If it isn't, Indians will need a visa again. Malaysia's eVISA is applied for online at [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/), says the High Commission. Check [the Immigration Department's visa page](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/en/main-services/visa/visa-requirement-by-country/) before you book a trip for January or later.
- **Working or studying?** The visa-free rule is only for short visits. Work and study need a visa.

### MDAC: the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card

- **All foreign visitors must fill in the MDAC,** says Malaysia's Immigration Department. Only a few groups are exempt, such as Singapore citizens and Malaysian residents. Indians are not exempt.
- **Submit it online within 3 days before you arrive,** on the official [MDAC website](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main).
- **At the airport,** show your MDAC registration and passport at the immigration counter.
- **Use only the official site.** It ends in imi.gov.my. Ignore sites that charge a fee to file it for you.
- **Carrying a lot of cash?** Cash or travellers' cheques worth more than USD 10,000 must be declared to customs, says the MDAC site. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html) for India's own rules.

## A simple 5-day Malaysia itinerary

This is a first trip that mixes the city, the hills and an island. It keeps travel to one domestic flight.

| Day | Plan | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land at KLIA and take the KLIA Ekspres train to KL Sentral. Rest, then walk around Brickfields (Little India) for lunch | The Petronas Twin Towers and KLCC park lights |
| 2 | Batu Caves in the morning, before the heat and the crowds, then Genting Highlands by road and the cable car | Cool evening in Genting, or back to KL |
| 3 | Fly from KL to Langkawi (or Penang) | Sunset at the beach |
| 4 | Langkawi: the SkyCab cable car and Sky Bridge, or an island-hopping boat trip. In Penang: George Town's street art and food streets, and Penang Hill | Night market or the beach |
| 5 | Fly back to KL and connect to your flight home | |

- **Langkawi or Penang?** Langkawi is for beaches, boat trips and a quiet holiday, and suits couples. Penang is for food, old streets and city life, and suits families who don't want beach days. Both are dry from December to February.
- **Only 4 days?** Skip the island. Do KL and Batu Caves on day 1 or 2, Genting on one day and a day trip to Malacca (Melaka) on another.
- **With kids?** Keep a slow day in KL for the aquarium and parks near KLCC, or Genting's theme parks. Plan indoor places for the hot afternoons.
- **A 7-day trip?** Add both Langkawi and Penang, or 2 days in Singapore (see the combo below).
- **Batu Caves has a long, steep staircase** up to the temple cave. Go early, carry water, and cover your shoulders and knees.

## Best time to visit Malaysia

Malaysia is warm all year, and the temperature changes by less than 2 °C over the year in most places, says the Malaysian Meteorological Department (MetMalaysia). Rain decides the best time, and it depends on where you go:

| Area | Wettest months | Driest months |
|---|---|---|
| Kuala Lumpur and most of the west | October–November (and April–May) | June–July and February |
| Langkawi and Penang (the northwest) | October–November | January–February |
| East coast islands (Perhentian, Redang, Tioman) | November–January, with heavy monsoon rain | June–July |

- **December to February** is the best time for Langkawi and Penang. Alor Setar, near Langkawi, gets about 8.7 hours of sunshine a day in January on average, among the highest in the country, says MetMalaysia.
- **Avoid the east coast islands from November to January.** The northeast monsoon brings heavy rain spells to the east coast of Peninsular Malaysia.
- **Kuala Lumpur works all year.** Expect afternoon showers, mostly short. Carry an umbrella.
- **Days are hot and humid; nights are milder,** usually about 21 °C to 24 °C. Genting Highlands is much cooler than KL, so carry a light jacket.

## Malaysia in December

- **Good for:** Langkawi and Penang (drier weather starts), Kuala Lumpur shopping and Christmas lights in the malls, and Genting.
- **Not good for:** the east coast islands, where the monsoon is at its peak.
- **Christmas and New Year are busy and costly.** Book flights and hotels early, especially in Langkawi.
- **The visa waiver ends on 31 December 2026,** unless it is extended. A trip that starts before the end date but runs into January is not clearly covered by any official page we read, so check with the High Commission if your dates cross into 2027.

## Singapore + Malaysia combo

Many Indians see both countries in one trip of 6 to 8 days. Singapore and Malaysia share a land border, so you can go by road, by train or by a short flight.

- **The visas are different.** Malaysia is visa-free for Indians until 31 December 2026, but Singapore needs a visa through an agent in India. See our [Singapore trip from India](singapore-trip-from-india.html) guide for the fee, documents and the SG Arrival Card.
- **Fly into one country and home from the other.** For example, fly Delhi to Kuala Lumpur and Singapore to Delhi. You don't go back the way you came, and you save a day.
- **Fill in both arrival cards:** the MDAC for Malaysia and the SG Arrival Card for Singapore. If you go back into Malaysia from Singapore, you need a new MDAC for that entry.
- **A simple 7-day plan:** 2 days in Kuala Lumpur, 1 day in Genting or Malacca, then go to Singapore for 3 days and fly home.
- **By road,** the crossing is at Johor Bahru (Malaysia) and Woodlands (Singapore). Queues can be long on weekends and holidays.

## Getting around Malaysia

- **From KLIA to the city:** the KLIA Ekspres train takes 28 minutes from KLIA Terminal 1 to KL Sentral, and runs every 20 minutes. The adult fare is RM55 one way (RM100 return), and children under 6 ride free, says KLIA Ekspres (checked 21 Nov 2026). Its website lists a fare change from 1 January 2027, so check the price before you go.
- **In Kuala Lumpur,** the LRT, MRT and monorail trains reach KLCC, Bukit Bintang, KL Sentral and Chinatown. Batu Caves has its own train station.
- **Ride-hailing apps and taxis** are easy to find and help with luggage and late nights. Agree on the fare or use the meter.
- **To Langkawi or Penang,** the quickest way is a domestic flight from KL. Penang can also be reached by train and road.
- **Genting Highlands** is about an hour's drive from KL, then a cable car to the top.
- **Walk early or late.** The midday heat is tiring, so keep malls and museums for the afternoon.

## Food for Indian travellers in Malaysia

- **Indian food is everywhere.** Malaysia has a large Indian (mostly Tamil) community. Brickfields in KL and Little India in Penang have many South Indian and North Indian restaurants.
- **Vegetarian food:** banana leaf rice, dosa, idli and thali are easy to find in Indian areas. In other places, ask for "vegetarian" and check if the dish has fish sauce, shrimp paste or egg.
- **Mamak stalls,** run by Indian Muslim families, serve roti canai, thosai, teh tarik (pulled tea) and murtabak, often late into the night.
- **Most food is halal.** Malaysia is a Muslim-majority country, so pork and alcohol are less common outside Chinese restaurants and hotels.
- **Jain food is harder to find.** If you are strict, carry some ready-to-eat food from home.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water.**

## Phone, SIM and eSIM in Malaysia

You have three ways to stay online in Malaysia:

- **Roaming on your own SIM:** the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
- **A local SIM card:** sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
- **An eSIM:** a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

## Money, rules and what to wear

- **The currency is the Malaysian ringgit (RM).** Cards work in malls and hotels. Keep cash for food stalls, night markets and small shops. Ask your Indian bank about foreign card charges before you go.
- **Dress modestly at mosques and temples.** Cover your shoulders and knees.
- **Medicines:** carry them in the original pack with your prescription.
- **What to wear:** light cotton clothes, walking shoes, sandals, a cap, sunglasses and an umbrella. Carry one light layer for cold malls, buses and Genting.

## Questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia?

Not until 31 December 2026. Indian passport holders can visit Malaysia without a visa for up to 30 days, for tourism, business, social or transit visits, says the High Commission of Malaysia in New Delhi. You must fill in the MDAC online and carry a return ticket and a hotel booking.

### Is Malaysia visa-free for Indians in 2027?

On 21 Nov 2026 no official page said the waiver will continue after 31 December 2026. If it isn't extended, Indians will need a visa again, applied for online as an eVISA. Check the Immigration Department of Malaysia's website before you book a 2027 trip.

### What is the MDAC for Malaysia?

The Malaysia Digital Arrival Card is an online form every foreign visitor must submit within 3 days before arriving. Indians must fill it in. Use only the official site, imigresen-online.imi.gov.my.

### How many days are enough for Malaysia?

Five days cover Kuala Lumpur, Batu Caves, Genting Highlands and one island, Langkawi or Penang. With 7 or 8 days you can add a second island or Singapore.

### Which is better, Langkawi or Penang?

Langkawi is better for beaches, boat trips and a quiet holiday. Penang is better for food, old streets and sightseeing. Both have their driest weather from December to February.

### Is December a good time to visit Malaysia?

Yes for Kuala Lumpur, Langkawi and Penang, where drier weather starts. No for the east coast islands like Perhentian and Redang, where the northeast monsoon brings heavy rain from November to January.

### Can I do Singapore and Malaysia in one trip?

Yes. Fly into one country and home from the other. Malaysia is visa-free for Indians until 31 December 2026, but you need a Singapore visa through an agent in India, and an arrival card for each country.

### Which cities in India have direct flights to Malaysia?

Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram, Ahmedabad and Amritsar have direct flights to Kuala Lumpur on Malaysia Airlines, AirAsia, Batik Air, IndiGo or Air India. IndiGo also flies Chennai to Penang and Bengaluru to Langkawi.

### Is vegetarian food easy to find in Malaysia?

Yes, in Indian areas like Brickfields in Kuala Lumpur and Little India in Penang, which have many South Indian vegetarian restaurants. Elsewhere, check for fish sauce, shrimp paste and egg.

### What is the time difference between India and Malaysia?

Malaysia is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India.
