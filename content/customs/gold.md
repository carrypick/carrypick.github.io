## Duty-free gold jewellery

| Who you are | Duty-free jewellery |
|---|---|
| Woman (Indian resident, NRI or OCI) who lived abroad **more than 1 year** | Up to 40 g |
| Man or other passenger (Indian resident, NRI or OCI) who lived abroad **more than 1 year** | Up to 20 g |
| Lived abroad for 1 year or less | Only the jewellery you wear every day |
| Foreign tourist | Only the jewellery you wear every day |

- **Weight only, no value limit.** Under the old 2016 rules the 20 g and 40 g also had value caps of ₹50,000 and ₹1 lakh. The Baggage Rules, 2026 removed them from 2 February 2026.
- **All precious metals count.** "Jewellery" in the rules means ornaments of gold, silver, platinum or other precious metals, with or without stones.
- **Above the limit?** Jewellery over your allowance is charged customs duty.

## Short trip to Dubai: can I bring gold back?

- **Jewellery you wear every day** (your used personal jewellery) is duty free, but Customs can still check it.
- **New jewellery you bought on the trip** isn't duty free: CBIC's master circular says jewellery that isn't part of your used personal things can be cleared on payment of duty. Declare it and pay at the Red Channel.
- **The 1 kg route below needs 6 months abroad**, and the 20 g and 40 g allowance needs more than a year. A one-week trip doesn't qualify for either.
- **Taking costly jewellery with you?** Before you leave India, get an **export certificate** on the Atithi app listing it. Then you can bring the same jewellery back duty free without questions. It's valid until you first return, or for 6 months.

## Up to 1 kg of gold on payment of duty

People who lived abroad for a while can bring more gold, in any form, by paying duty.

- **Who:** a passenger of Indian origin, or anyone with a valid Indian passport, coming to India after **at least 6 months abroad**. Short visits to India during those 6 months are ignored if they add up to 30 days or less.
- **How much:** up to **1 kg of gold** and up to **10 kg of silver** per eligible passenger.
- **What:** gold bars with the maker's or refiner's serial number and weight in grams or kg, tola bars, gold coins of at least 99.5% purity, and gold in other forms, including jewellery. **Jewellery studded with stones or pearls is not included.**
- **Pay duty in foreign currency,** such as US dollars, not rupees.
- **Carry it with you,** or declare on arrival that you'll collect it from a State Bank of India or MMTC customs bonded warehouse, and pay the duty before you clear Customs.

### Duty on this gold since 13 May 2026

| Gold brought by an eligible passenger | Basic customs duty | Agriculture cess (AIDC) | In all |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold bars (tola bars too) and coins | 10% | 5% | 15% |
| Gold jewellery | 10% | 4.35% | 14.35% |

Until 12 May 2026 the basic duty was 5%. The cess on bars and coins was 1%, making 6% in all. The officer works out the final amount, which can include other charges. The notification says this passenger rate runs until **31 March 2027** unless the government extends it.

## Don't skip the Red Channel

The customs form asks if you carry gold bullion and jewellery beyond your daily wear or above your allowance. Walking through the Green Channel with gold you should declare can mean losing the gold, a penalty and, in serious cases, arrest. Never carry gold for someone else.

## Questions

### How much gold can I bring from Dubai to India duty free?

Gold jewellery up to 40 g for a woman and 20 g for a man, if you're an Indian resident, NRI or OCI cardholder who lived abroad for more than one year. There's no value limit since 2 February 2026. Gold bars and coins are never duty free.

### How much gold can a man carry to India without duty?

Up to 20 g of jewellery, if he lived abroad for more than one year. Otherwise only the jewellery he wears every day is duty free.

### How much gold can a woman carry to India without duty?

Up to 40 g of jewellery, if she lived abroad for more than one year. Otherwise only the jewellery she wears every day is duty free.

### Is there a value limit on duty-free gold jewellery?

No. Since 2 February 2026 the allowance is by weight only. The old limits of ₹50,000 for men and ₹1 lakh for women were removed.

### Can I bring a gold bar or gold coins from Dubai?

Yes, but not duty free. If you have an Indian passport or are of Indian origin and lived abroad for at least 6 months, you can bring up to 1 kg by paying duty in foreign currency. Declare it at the Red Channel.

### What is the customs duty on gold for passengers in 2026?

For an eligible passenger, since 13 May 2026: 10% basic customs duty plus a 5% agriculture cess on gold bars and coins (15% in all), or a 4.35% cess on jewellery (14.35% in all). Until 12 May 2026 it was 6% on bars and coins.

### Can a foreign tourist bring gold jewellery for a wedding in India?

Yes. Declare it on arrival and ask Customs for a temporary baggage import certificate. Show the jewellery and the certificate again when you leave India.
