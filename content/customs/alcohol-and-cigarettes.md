## The 2-litre rule

Every passenger arriving in India by air or sea can bring **2 litres** of alcoholic drinks or wine without paying duty. It counts all your alcohol together, whatever the type.

| What you carry | Total | Within 2 litres? |
|---|---|---|
| 2 bottles of 1 litre | 2 litres | Yes |
| 2 bottles of 750 ml | 1.5 litres | Yes |
| 1 bottle of 1 litre and 1 of 750 ml | 1.75 litres | Yes |
| 3 bottles of 750 ml | 2.25 litres | No, 250 ml over |
| 2 bottles of wine (750 ml) and 1 litre of whisky | 2.5 litres | No, 500 ml over |

- **It's per passenger.** Each person's limit is their own, and there's no duty-free alcohol for anyone arriving by land.
- **Duty-free shop bottles count too.** Customs counts all the alcohol in your bags, wherever you bought it.
- **Flying on within India after you land?** Move sealed duty-free bottles into your check-in bag before the next security check. See our [alcohol rules for domestic flights](../can-i-carry/alcohol.html).

## More than 2 litres

- **The extra pays duty.** Alcohol over 2 litres can't use your ₹75,000 (or ₹25,000) allowance, and CBIC's lower 35% baggage rate doesn't apply to it. It pays the normal customs duty on alcohol, which is high.
- **Declare it** on the Atithi app and go to the Red Channel.
- **Don't want to pay?** You can ask Customs to keep the extra bottles. You get a detention receipt and can take them back when you leave India. Detained goods must be collected within 6 months (Customs can extend this), and storage charges may apply.
- **Many bottles of the same drink** can look like goods for sale. Goods in commercial quantity aren't allowed as baggage, even if you offer to pay duty.

## Cigarettes, cigars and tobacco

The duty-free limit is **100 cigarettes**, or **25 cigars**, or **125 g of tobacco**.

- A carton usually holds 200 cigarettes, so one full carton is double the limit.
- Like alcohol, extra cigarettes and tobacco don't get the 35% baggage rate. Declare them at the Red Channel.
- **E-cigarettes and vapes are banned.** The Prohibition of Electronic Cigarettes Act, 2019 doesn't allow anyone to import them, even one for personal use.

## Your state's liquor rules still apply

Customs clears your 2 litres at the airport, but your state's liquor law applies once you leave it. Dry states such as Bihar and Gujarat have their own strict rules, so check them before you carry bottles home.

## Packing bottles for the flight home

- Keep bottles sealed in their shop packing. Duty-free shops seal them in a clear bag.
- Wrap each bottle in clothes and pack it in the middle of a hard suitcase, away from the wheels.
- Glass is heavy. Weigh your bag before you leave for the airport.

## Questions

### How much alcohol can I bring into India from abroad?

Up to 2 litres of alcohol or wine per passenger, duty free, when you arrive by air or sea. This counts all types of alcohol together.

### Can I bring 3 bottles of alcohol to India?

Three 750 ml bottles make 2.25 litres, which is 250 ml over the duty-free limit, so the extra pays duty. Two 1-litre bottles, or two 750 ml bottles, are within the limit.

### How much alcohol can I bring from duty free to India?

The same 2 litres in total. Bottles from duty-free shops count towards the limit, together with any other alcohol in your bags.

### How many cigarettes can I bring to India?

Up to 100 cigarettes, or 25 cigars, or 125 g of tobacco. A full carton of 200 cigarettes is over the limit.

### What happens if I bring more than 2 litres of alcohol?

Declare it at the Red Channel. The extra pays the normal customs duty on alcohol, not the 35% baggage rate. You can also ask Customs to keep the extra bottles and collect them when you leave India.

### Can I bring a vape or e-cigarette into India?

No. The Prohibition of Electronic Cigarettes Act, 2019 bans importing e-cigarettes.
