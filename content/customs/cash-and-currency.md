## Indian rupees: the ₹25,000 limit

| Who | Taking rupees out of India | Bringing rupees into India |
|---|---|---|
| Indian resident | Up to ₹25,000 | Up to ₹25,000, coming back from a trip |
| Foreign visitor (not a citizen of Pakistan or Bangladesh) | Up to ₹25,000 | Up to ₹25,000, entering through an airport |
| Anyone going to or coming from Nepal or Bhutan (not a citizen of Pakistan or Bangladesh) | ₹100 notes and smaller; notes above ₹100 up to ₹25,000 in all | Same rule |

- **It's per person.** Each traveller has their own ₹25,000 limit.
- **More than ₹25,000 in rupees** needs the Reserve Bank of India's permission. Coming into India with more, you must declare it at the Red Channel.

## Foreign currency for your trip

Buy foreign currency from a bank or an authorised money changer. RBI limits how much of it you can take as cash:

| Where you're going | Foreign currency notes and coins per trip |
|---|---|
| Most countries, including the UAE, USA, UK, Thailand and Singapore | Up to US$3,000, or the same value in another currency |
| Iraq and Libya | Up to US$5,000 |
| Iran, Russia and other CIS countries | All your foreign exchange can be cash |
| Haj or Umrah | All your foreign exchange, or the cash limit set by the Haj Committee of India |

- **The rest** goes on a forex card (prepaid travel card), travellers' cheques or a bank draft.
- **Yearly limit:** all your foreign exchange for travel counts towards RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit of US$250,000 per financial year.
- **Paying for it:** you can pay in rupee cash if the amount is under ₹50,000. From ₹50,000 up, pay by cheque, draft, debit or credit card, or a prepaid card.

## Bringing foreign currency into India

- **There's no limit** on how much foreign currency you bring in.
- **Declare it** on the Currency Declaration Form if your foreign currency notes are worth more than **US$5,000**, or if notes and travellers' cheques together are worth more than **US$10,000**. Fill it in with your customs declaration on the Atithi app, and go to the Red Channel.
- **Foreign visitors:** keep the stamped form. You need it to take unspent money back out when you leave India. Show it to the bank or money changer when you change money, too.

## After you come back

- **Unspent foreign notes and travellers' cheques** must be sold to a bank or money changer, or put into a Resident Foreign Currency (Domestic) account, within **180 days** of your return.
- **You can keep up to US$2,000** in foreign notes or travellers' cheques for your next trip.
- **Foreign coins:** you can keep any amount.

## Carrying cash safely

- Keep cash, your forex card and your passport in your cabin bag or on you, never in a checked bag.
- Split cash between two places, so one lost wallet doesn't take all of it.
- Carry the bank's receipt for the foreign currency you bought.

## Questions

### How much cash can I carry abroad from India?

Up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes per person. For foreign currency, a bank or money changer can sell you notes worth up to US$3,000 per trip for most countries (US$5,000 for Iraq and Libya). Take the rest on a forex card.

### How much cash can I carry from India to Dubai?

The same: up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes, and foreign currency notes worth up to US$3,000 per trip, bought from a bank or money changer. Dubai's own customs rules also apply when you land.

### How much cash can I bring into India without declaring it?

Foreign currency notes worth up to US$5,000, or up to US$10,000 if you include travellers' cheques. Indian notes up to ₹25,000. Above these, declare at the Red Channel.

### How much Indian currency can I bring to India?

Up to ₹25,000 per person, for Indian residents coming back from a trip and foreign visitors entering through an airport.

### What do I do with leftover dollars after my trip?

Sell them to a bank or money changer, or deposit them in a Resident Foreign Currency (Domestic) account, within 180 days. You can keep up to US$2,000 in notes for your next trip, and any coins.

### How much cash can I carry to Nepal?

Indian notes of ₹100 and smaller are allowed. ₹200 and ₹500 notes are allowed up to ₹25,000 in all. For documents and what to pack, see our [Nepal packing list](../../guides/nepal-packing-list.html).
