The school summer holidays, from about April to June, are when most Indian families travel: to the hills to escape the heat, to the beach, or abroad. Kids need more than a smaller version of your bag: sun protection, something for car sickness, things to do on a long drive, their medicines, and the right ID at the airport. This list covers all three kinds of trip, for children from about 3 to 12. For a baby or toddler, use our [travelling with a baby checklist](travelling-with-baby-checklist.html) instead.

## The short list

For any summer trip, pack these for each child:

1. A sun hat or cap, and sunscreen
2. A water bottle they can carry, and ORS sachets
3. Light cotton clothes, plus one warm layer (even for the beach: planes, trains and hotels are cold)
4. Comfortable shoes and a pair of sandals or flip-flops
5. Their own small backpack, with a snack, a book and a game
6. Headphones with a volume limit, and a charged device or two
7. Something for travel sickness, if your doctor suggests it, and sick bags
8. Their regular medicines and a small first-aid kit (ask your doctor what to add)
9. Wet wipes, tissues and hand sanitiser
10. Their ID: a passport for trips abroad, and for domestic flights the documents your airline asks for (see below)
11. A spare set of clothes in your cabin bag
12. A card in their pocket with your name and phone number

## Sun and heat

April to June is the hottest time of year in much of India, and heat waves are common in May. Our [May guide](../trips/where-to-go-in-may.html) suggests keeping children out of the midday sun on travel days, and carrying water and ORS.

- **A sun hat or cap** with a brim. A hat with a neck flap covers the ears and neck too.
- **Sunscreen:** use one made for children, and ask your doctor or pharmacist which suits your child's skin. Put it on before going out, and again after swimming.
- **Kids' sunglasses** with UV protection for the beach, snow at Rohtang and Gulmarg, and Ladakh.
- **Light, loose cotton clothes** in light colours, with full sleeves for long days outside.
- **Water, often.** Give them their own bottle, and refill it at every stop.
- **Plan the day:** sightseeing in the morning and evening, rest indoors at noon.

## Motion sickness on hill roads, flights and boats

The roads to Manali, Shimla, Mussoorie, Darjeeling and Sikkim are long and winding, and many children get car sick. These tips help:

- **Sit them in the middle or front seat** (with the right seat belt or child seat), where they can see the road ahead.
- **Keep the window open a little** for fresh air, and avoid strong smells in the car.
- **A light meal before the drive,** not a heavy one, and dry snacks like biscuits on the way.
- **Look out, not down.** Reading and screens make it worse; audiobooks and songs are better on hill roads.
- **Stop every hour or two** for a short walk.
- **Ask your doctor about travel-sickness medicine** for your child's age and weight before the trip. Don't give any without advice.
- **Carry sick bags, wet wipes, a towel and a spare set of clothes** where you can reach them.

## Entertainment for long journeys

A 12-hour drive to Manali or a long-haul flight goes faster with a bag of things to do. Pack a small backpack for each child, and let them choose some of it.

- **Headphones with a volume limit,** for the seat screen, a tablet or audiobooks. Kids' headphones keep the volume down.
- **A travel activity book, colouring book or travel journal.** Our [audiobooks for long flights](audiobooks-for-long-flights.html) guide has ideas for listening.
- **Card and board games** for the hotel and train.
- **A drawing kit** with washable pens.
- **Downloaded shows, games and music,** since the network drops on hill roads.
- **A power bank,** so devices last the journey. See our [power bank rules on flights](power-bank-rules-on-flights.html): power banks go in the cabin bag, never in checked luggage.
- **A travel pillow** for sleeping in the car, train or plane.

## A kids' medicine kit

**Ask your doctor** what to pack for your child, and how much to give. A basic kit often includes:

- **Your child's regular medicines,** with spares and the prescription
- **Fever and pain medicine** in a form and dose your doctor has told you for your child
- **ORS sachets,** for heat and upset stomachs
- **Travel-sickness medicine,** only if your doctor suggests it
- **Plasters, antiseptic cream and a small crepe bandage**
- **Insect repellent** suitable for children, for the hills and beaches
- **A thermometer**
- **Your doctor's phone number** and your child's allergies written down

Keep it in your cabin bag or day bag, not in the suitcase in the boot or the hold.

## ID for kids on flights

Airlines set their own rules for children's documents, so **check with your airline** before you fly. Here's what we could confirm on the airlines' own pages (checked 26 Nov 2026):

- **Trips abroad:** every child needs their own passport, and a visa where the country asks for one. Our [trips abroad from India](../trips/international-trips-from-india.html) page links each country's visa rules.
- **Babies under 2:** carry age proof such as a birth certificate. Air India also lists a vaccination certificate, and a passport for international travel. More in our [flying with a baby](flying-with-baby-or-toddler.html) guide.
- **Children flying with a relative, not a parent (Air India):** Air India asks for a consent letter signed by the parent or legal guardian, and photocopies of a valid government-issued ID of the child and the adult on domestic flights (passport pages on international flights). It asks for these at an Air India ticketing office at least six hours before the flight.
- **Children flying alone:** Air India treats children aged 5 to 11 travelling alone as unaccompanied minors; SpiceJet's age band is 5 to 12. Both need forms and the airline's paid assistance, and SpiceJet asks for valid photo ID for the child and the parent at check-in. Book it with the airline in advance.
- **Children with their parents on domestic flights:** we couldn't find a clear rule on the airlines' pages for which ID a child of 2 to 12 must show. Carry an Aadhaar card, school ID or birth certificate for each child to be safe, and **check with your airline.**
- **DigiYatra:** we couldn't confirm on DigiYatra's official pages whether children can use it. Plan to use the regular entry gate with your family's IDs ready, and check with your airline or the DigiYatra app.

## Packing for the hills: Manali, Shimla, Kashmir, Ladakh

Summer days in the hills are pleasant, but evenings are cool and the snow points are cold.

- **A warm jacket, a sweater or fleece, a cap and gloves** for evenings and snow points like Rohtang, Gulmarg and Sonamarg
- **A thermal set** for Ladakh, or for Kashmir and Manali in April
- **Waterproof shoes** for snow and rain, and warm socks
- **A rain jacket or poncho,** since showers come often in the afternoons
- **Sun protection:** the sun is strong at height, even when it feels cool

**Ladakh with kids:** Leh is at about 3,256 m, and our [Ladakh trip guide](../trips/ladakh-trip-guide.html) says to ask your doctor before the trip if you're travelling with small children. Rest for the first two days. See our [Ladakh packing list](ladakh-packing-list.html) for the full list.

## Packing for a beach trip

- **Swimwear,** two sets so one can dry
- **A rash guard or a full-sleeved T-shirt** for the water, and a sun hat
- **Sunscreen,** put on again after every swim
- **Flip-flops or water shoes,** since sand gets very hot by noon
- **A quick-dry towel,** and a plastic bag for wet clothes
- **Mosquito repellent** for the evenings
- **Sand toys,** if they're small

The monsoon usually reaches Kerala around the end of May or early June, and the west coast is hot and humid before that. Our [Goa packing list](goa-packing-list.html) and [Andaman packing list](andaman-packing-list.html) have more, and our [beach checklist](../packing/index.html#beach) is ready to tick.

## Packing for a trip abroad

- **Every child's passport and visa,** and copies on your phone
- **A family passport holder** for all the documents
- **A kids' cabin bag or backpack** they can pull or carry themselves
- **A travel adapter** for the country: see our [power bank and travel adapter guide](power-bank-and-travel-adapter-guide.html)
- **Their medicines in the original packs,** with the prescription
- **Snacks** for the flight and the first day, but check the country's food rules
- **A warm layer** for cold planes and air-conditioned malls

## Kids' packing list for a 7-day summer trip

- 5–6 T-shirts and 3–4 shorts or trousers (they'll get dirty)
- 1 or 2 nightwear sets
- 7 sets of underwear and socks
- 1 sweater or fleece; add a warm jacket, cap and gloves for the hills
- 1 sun hat, 1 pair of sunglasses, sunscreen
- 1 pair of comfortable shoes, 1 pair of sandals or flip-flops
- Swimwear for the beach or a hotel pool
- Water bottle, snacks, ORS
- Headphones, a book, a game, a drawing kit
- Medicine kit (ask your doctor)
- Toothbrush, toothpaste, a small towel, wet wipes, sanitiser
- ID and documents, and a contact card in their pocket

## Questions

### What should I pack for kids on a summer vacation?

A sun hat and sunscreen, a water bottle and ORS, light cotton clothes and one warm layer, comfortable shoes and sandals, headphones and a few books or games, their regular medicines and a small first-aid kit (ask your doctor what to add), wet wipes, and their ID.

### What should kids wear in Manali or Kashmir in May?

Light clothes for the day, with a sweater or fleece and a warm jacket for evenings and snow points like Rohtang and Gulmarg. Add waterproof shoes, warm socks, a cap and gloves.

### How do I stop my child getting car sick on hill roads?

Sit them where they can see the road ahead, keep a window open a little, give a light meal before the drive, avoid reading and screens, and stop every hour or two. Ask your doctor about travel-sickness medicine before the trip, and carry sick bags and a spare set of clothes.

### What ID does a child need on a domestic flight in India?

It depends on the airline, so check with yours before you fly. Babies need age proof such as a birth certificate. Air India asks for a copy of a government-issued ID for a child flying with a relative instead of a parent. We couldn't find a clear rule for children flying with their parents, so carry an Aadhaar card, school ID or birth certificate to be safe.

### Do kids need a passport to travel abroad?

Yes. Every child, including a baby, needs their own passport for a trip abroad, and a visa where the country asks for one.
