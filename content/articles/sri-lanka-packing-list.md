Sri Lanka is small, but its weather changes a lot from the coast to the hills. The beaches are hot all year, the hill country around Nuwara Eliya and Ella is cool at night, and the two monsoons bring rain to different coasts at different times. Sri Lanka Tourism's own advice is to pack lightweight clothing, rain protection, comfortable footwear and an extra layer for the hills. This list tells you what to pack and why, for a trip from India.

## Sri Lanka weather by place

Long-term averages from Sri Lanka's National Meteorological Centre, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Place | December day | December night | December rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colombo | About 30 °C | About 23 °C | About 175 mm, on about 10 days |
| Galle | About 29 °C | About 23 °C | About 177 mm, on about 12 days |
| Kandy | About 28 °C | About 20 °C | About 196 mm, on about 14 days |
| Nuwara Eliya | About 19 °C | About 11 °C | About 196 mm, on about 15 days |
| Trincomalee | About 28 °C | About 24 °C | About 365 mm, on about 16 days |

- **The west and south coasts are best from January to March.** Colombo averages only about 58 mm of rain on 5 days in January, and about 73 mm on 5 days in February. Galle is drier then too.
- **The east coast is the other way round.** Trincomalee gets about 343 mm in November and 365 mm in December, but only about 25 mm in June and 64 mm in July. Go east from May to September.
- **The hill country is cool.** Nuwara Eliya is about 18–23 °C in the day all year, and about 9–11 °C at night from December to March. Pack a warm layer.
- **October and November are wet almost everywhere.** Sri Lanka's Climate Change Secretariat says this season brings widespread rain to the whole country, sometimes with floods and landslides. Colombo gets about 365 mm in October and 414 mm in November.
- **The monsoons, by month:** Sri Lanka's Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the south-west monsoon from May to September, an inter-monsoon in October and November, the north-east monsoon from December to February, and another inter-monsoon in March and April.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Passport, printed tickets and hotel bookings, and a copy of your passport
2. Light cotton or linen clothes, one outfit for each day
3. A light jacket or sweater for Nuwara Eliya, Ella and early starts
4. One or two outfits that cover shoulders and legs, for temples
5. Swimwear, and a shirt or kaftan to wear over it
6. Walking shoes for Sigiriya and hill walks, plus sandals or flip-flops you can slip off
7. A rain poncho or small umbrella
8. Sunscreen, sunglasses, a cap and mosquito repellent
9. Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription
10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

## Documents and money

- **Passport.** Keep it on you, not in your check-in bag. Carry a photocopy and a photo of it on your phone, kept apart from the passport.
- **Print or save your bookings:** return ticket, hotel bookings and travel insurance. Check the entry rules for Indian passport holders on Sri Lanka's official immigration website before you fly.
- **Cash.** RBI lets a bank or money changer sell you up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can take up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Declare large amounts of cash.** Sri Lanka Customs says foreign currency worth more than US$15,000 must be declared when you arrive, and more than US$10,000 when you leave. You can bring in or take out up to 20,000 Sri Lankan rupees.
- **Gold:** only your own personal jewellery, in a reasonable quantity. Gold bars and biscuits aren't allowed as passenger baggage.
- **Keep a pen in your cabin bag.** The airport operator says foreign visitors fill in a paper disembarkation card at immigration.
- **Carry your passport to Sigiriya, Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa.** The Central Cultural Fund gives cheaper tickets to tourists from SAARC countries, including India, only when you show your passport.

## What to wear to temples in Sri Lanka

The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic in Kandy asks visitors to be respectfully dressed:

- **No shorts, sleeveless tops or revealing clothes.** The temple says visitors wearing them aren't allowed in. You can get suitable clothes at the entrances.
- **Hand over your shoes** at the reception booths and collect a token before you go in. There's a separate booth for foreign visitors.
- **Leave bulky bags at the booth too.** Everyone is screened at the entrance.
- **Don't bring polythene or plastic bags,** even for offerings.

For other temples, Sri Lanka Tourism says to dress respectfully, cover your shoulders and legs at Buddhist temples, and take off shoes and hats where required:

- **Wear long trousers or a long skirt and a top with sleeves.** A light scarf or stole covers your shoulders when you need it.
- **Avoid disrespectful poses with Buddha images,** and follow flash rules.
- **Ask before you photograph people,** and follow signs and staff at religious sites.
- **Socks** help on hot stone floors.

## Sun, rain and health

- **A rain poncho or small umbrella.** Showers can come at any time, and the hills are wet in most months.
- **Sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher.** Put it on again every 2 hours and after swimming. Read our [sunscreen guide](travel-sunscreen-guide.html).
- **Sunglasses and a cap or hat** for the beaches and the climb up Sigiriya.
- **Mosquito repellent,** especially in the evening.
- **A small first-aid kit:** something for an upset stomach, ORS and plasters.
- **Pills for motion sickness** if winding hill roads upset you.
- **Drink sealed bottled or treated water,** as Sri Lanka Tourism advises, including for ice and brushing your teeth.
- **Your medicines:** see the medicine rules below.

## Phone, plugs and power bank

- **Indian chargers work on Sri Lanka's 230 V supply,** the same voltage as India, says Sri Lanka Tourism. Carry a universal travel adapter in case your room's sockets take a different plug. Plug types by country are in our [travel adapter by country](travel-adapter-by-country.html) list.
- **Drones need approval.** Sri Lanka's Civil Aviation Authority asks visiting tourists to apply online for flying approval, and Sigiriya and the other Central Cultural Fund sites don't allow drone filming without written approval.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **A cable organiser** keeps chargers, cables and the adapter in one place.

## Medicines

Sri Lanka's National Medicines Regulatory Authority lets travellers bring medicines for their own use without approval, with these rules:

- **Up to 90 days' supply,** or enough for your stay, whichever is less. More than that needs approval first.
- **In the original container,** or labelled with the name, strength and how to use it.
- **Carry your prescription or medical records,** to show when asked.
- **No intramuscular or intravenous injections,** and no narcotic medicines (Schedule III).

## Sigiriya, Adam's Peak and the hills

- **Sigiriya:** walking shoes, water, a cap and your passport for the SAARC ticket. Ticket counters open from 7 AM to 5 PM, says the Central Cultural Fund, and you must follow staff instructions about wild elephants and wasps.
- **Adam's Peak (Sri Pada):** the pilgrimage season runs from Unduvap Poya, on 23 December 2026, to Vesak Poya in May. Most people climb at night for the sunrise, and the summit is very cold, so take a warm jacket, a cap and a torch. Sri Lanka Tourism's pilgrimage list adds a shawl for the evening and a water bottle.
- **Nuwara Eliya and Ella:** a warm layer for evenings and early trains, and a rain jacket.

## Which bag to take

- **About 5 nights:** a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack is enough.
- **About a week or more:** a medium trolley. Leave space for tea and spices.
- **Taking the train to Ella or Kandy?** A backpack or a light trolley is easier on station steps and crowded carriages.

## What you can't bring into Sri Lanka

- **Cigarettes, cigars, beedis and snuff.** Sri Lanka Customs says they aren't allowed into the country as personal baggage. Smokeless tobacco such as gutka, khaini or zarda, and e-cigarettes that contain tobacco, are banned imports too. Leave vapes at home.
- **Fresh fruit, including mangoes.** Customs says no fruit may be brought in by air in personal baggage. It's sent to plant quarantine at the airport and destroyed. Plants, seeds, shells and animal parts are restricted too.
- **Narcotics,** pornographic material, and religious materials that are derogatory to any religion.
- **Toy guns and dummy firearms,** and weapons.
- **Kesari dal.** It's on Sri Lanka's list of banned imports.
- **A drone without approval.**

What you can bring duty free as a tourist, says Sri Lanka Customs: your personal clothing and essentials, 1.5 litres of spirits and two regular bottles of wine, a quarter litre of toilet water and a little perfume, and souvenirs worth up to US$250. Alcohol must come with you on arrival or be bought at Sri Lanka's duty-free shops.

Also leave out:

- **A power bank in your check-in bag.** It goes only in your cabin bag. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **Big bottles in your cabin bag.** Each liquid must be 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. Bigger bottles go in check-in. See [can I carry liquids?](../baggage/can-i-carry/liquids.html)
- **Plastic bags for temple offerings.** The Temple of the Tooth asks visitors not to bring polythene or plastic, so carry a cloth bag.

Not sure about an item on the flight? Search our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.

## Questions

### What should I pack for Sri Lanka from India?

Light cotton clothes, a light jacket or sweater for Nuwara Eliya and Ella, one or two outfits that cover shoulders and legs for temples, swimwear, walking shoes and slip-on sandals, a rain poncho, sunscreen, mosquito repellent, your medicines in their original packs with the prescription, and a power bank in your cabin bag.

### What should I wear in Sri Lanka in December?

On the west and south coasts, light cotton clothes and swimwear: Colombo is about 30 °C by day. Take a rain jacket, because December still gets about 175 mm of rain in Colombo and more on the east coast. In Nuwara Eliya, nights are about 11 °C, so pack a warm layer.

### What should women wear in Sri Lanka?

Dresses, skirts and shorts are fine at the beaches. For temples, wear a long skirt or trousers and a top with sleeves: the Temple of the Tooth doesn't allow shorts, sleeveless tops or revealing clothes. A light stole helps for temples and cool hill evenings.

### What should men wear in Sri Lanka?

Shorts and T-shirts on the coast, and long trousers with a shirt with sleeves for temples. Take a light jacket for the hills and a warm one if you climb Adam's Peak at night.

### What should I wear to the Temple of the Tooth in Kandy?

Clothes that cover your shoulders and legs. The temple doesn't allow shorts, sleeveless tops or revealing clothes, though you can get suitable clothes at the entrance. You hand over your shoes and bulky bags at a booth, and everyone is screened.

### Can I take cigarettes to Sri Lanka?

No. Sri Lanka Customs says cigarettes, cigars, beedis and snuff aren't allowed into the country as personal baggage. Smokeless tobacco such as gutka and e-cigarettes that contain tobacco are banned imports too.

### Can I take fruit or mangoes to Sri Lanka?

No. Sri Lanka Customs says no fruit, including mangoes, may be brought in by air in personal baggage. It's destroyed at the airport.

### How much baggage can I take from India to Sri Lanka?

IndiGo gives 30 kg to Colombo on regular fares (35 kg on UpFront) but only 15 kg to Jaffna. Air India gives 30 kg on Value and Classic fares and 40 kg on Flex. Both allow a 7 kg cabin bag.
