Singapore uses the **13 A type G** plug, three rectangular pins, says its Consumer Product Safety Office, and lists Malaysia, Hong Kong, Brunei and the UK as using the same plug. The supply is **230 V and 50 Hz**, like India's. An Indian plug doesn't fit, so take an adapter.

## Quick answer

- **Plug type:** G (13 A, three rectangular pins). Type M (15 A, three round pins) exists, but only for air conditioners and other heavy appliances.
- **Type C two-pin:** used for small appliances such as TVs. Singapore's safety office says to use a proper adaptor, not to force a 2-pin plug into a 3-pin socket.
- **Voltage:** 230 V and 50 Hz. No converter needed from India.
- **From India:** a type G adapter, or a universal one.
- **From the USA:** an adapter, and chargers marked **100-240V**.
- **From Europe:** an adapter. Singapore's safety office says German Schuko plugs are not suitable for its sockets.

**Isn't type M the Indian 3-pin plug?** Type M is the big round-pin plug used in India for heavy appliances. Singapore has type M sockets only for air conditioners and similar appliances, installed by electricians, so you won't find one by the hotel bed.

## Questions

### Which plug is used in Singapore?

Type G, the 13 A three-pin plug with rectangular pins, at 230 V and 50 Hz.

### Do Indian plugs work in Singapore?

No. A thin Indian 2-pin plug is a type C, and Singapore's safety office says to use a proper adaptor rather than force one into a 3-pin socket. Indian 3-pin plugs don't fit either.

### Do I need a converter for Singapore from India?

No. Both use 230 V and 50 Hz.

### Is the Singapore plug the same as the UK and Malaysia?

Yes. Singapore's Consumer Product Safety Office lists Malaysia, Hong Kong, Brunei and the UK as using the same type of plug.

### Is the Singapore plug the same as Dubai?

Yes, Dubai and the UAE also use type G. See [travel adapter for Dubai](travel-adapter-for-dubai.html).
