Bhutan is a mountain country, so it's cool most of the year and cold in winter. Paro and Thimphu go below freezing on December and January nights, but the days are often sunny. Indians don't need a visa, but you do need the right ID and you pay a daily fee. This list tells you what documents to carry, what to wear, and which bag to take, for a trip from India. ## Bhutan weather in December and January

Long-term averages from Bhutan's National Center for Hydrology and Meteorology (NCHM), as tabled on Wikipedia:

| Place | December day | December night | January day | January night |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paro | About 15 °C | About 0.5 °C | About 13 °C | About −1 °C |
| Thimphu | About 17 °C | About −1 °C | About 15 °C | About −2 °C |
| Punakha | About 20 °C | About 7 °C | About 19 °C | About 6 °C |
| Bumthang (Jakar) | About 13 °C | About −3 °C | About 11 °C | About −4 °C |

- **Winter is December to February,** says Bhutan's Department of Tourism. Days are mild and sunny, but nights are freezing in Paro, Thimphu and Bumthang.
- **Punakha is warmer.** It's lower down, so you'll be fine in a sweater by day.
- **Snow is possible but not certain.** Thimphu gets light snow showers in some winters. Ask your guide about the roads over high passes after snow.
- **The monsoon runs from late June or July to late September,** mostly in the south. Pack a rain jacket for those months.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Passport (valid for 6 months) or original voter ID card, plus 2 passport-size photos
2. Your SDF payment, hotel bookings and guide booking, printed or on your phone
3. Thermals, a warm jacket, a cap and gloves for December to February
4. Clothes with long sleeves and long trousers or a long skirt, for dzongs and temples
5. Walking or hiking shoes with grip for Tiger's Nest
6. Indian rupees in ₹500 notes or smaller, and a card
7. Adapters for British, European and Indian plugs
8. Lip balm, moisturiser, sunscreen and sunglasses: the air is dry and the sun is strong
9. Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription
10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

## Documents for Bhutan from India

The Embassy of India in Thimphu and Bhutan's Department of Tourism list what Indian citizens need:

- **No visa.** But you need an entry permit, which you get with one of these:
  - **An Indian passport** valid for at least 6 months, or
  - **Your original voter ID card** (EPIC), from the Election Commission of India.
- **Carry recent passport-size photos.** The embassy asks for them with your ID.
- **Children under 18** can use a passport or their original birth certificate, and must travel with a parent or legal guardian.
- **Aadhaar and driving licence aren't on the list.** Carry a passport or voter ID.
- **Where you get the permit:** at Paro airport, or at the immigration offices at Phuentsholing, Gelephu, Samdrup Jongkhar and Samtse on the land border. You can apply online first; at the border there may be a long wait. A certified hotel can also apply for you.
- **A guide.** The Department of Tourism says you must be accompanied by a guide at all times in Bhutan, including if you enter by road and go beyond the border towns, and guides are needed at dzongs and monuments (rechecked 21 Nov 2026). Book one with your hotel or a licensed tour operator before you go.
- **Route permits are no longer needed** to move between places, if you travel with a certified guide. National parks still need a permit.
- **Travel insurance** isn't required since April 2024, but the Department of Tourism strongly recommends it.

## SDF for Indians and money

- **SDF (Sustainable Development Fee):** Nu 1,200 per person per night for Indians who show a valid passport or voter ID, payable in ngultrum or the same in Indian rupees, says the Department of Tourism.
- **Children aged 6 to 11** pay Nu 600 a night. Children under 6 pay no SDF.
- **Day visits to border towns:** no SDF for visits of up to 24 hours to Phuentsholing, Samtse, Gelephu and Samdrup Jongkhar, since 2 June 2026.
- **The SDF is refunded** if you cancel or cut short your trip, minus bank charges.
- **Indian rupees work in Bhutan.** The ngultrum is fixed 1:1 with the rupee, and rupees are widely accepted.
- **Carry ₹500 notes or smaller.** The Indian Embassy says you can carry Indian notes of up to ₹500 each, up to ₹25,000 in total. Any amount above ₹25,000 must be in ₹100 notes or smaller.
- **Cards:** ATMs and banks accept Visa and Mastercard, mostly in towns. Carry cash for small shops and villages.
- **Cash limit:** you can bring cash worth up to US$10,000 into Bhutan. Declare it at customs. For rules on leaving India with cash, see [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).

## What to wear in Bhutan

The Department of Tourism says there are no rules on what visitors wear in general. For sacred places, it asks for more:

- **At dzongs, monasteries and temples:** wear long sleeves and cover your legs. Smart-casual clothes that cover you from shoulders to knees are appreciated.
- **Take off your shoes** when you go inside a temple, and **take off caps and hats** at sacred sites.
- **Don't take photos inside** sacred sites.
- **Pack one or two "dzong outfits":** a shirt or top with sleeves, and long trousers or a long skirt. Many tourists carry a light shawl or stole too.

## What to wear in Bhutan in December

- **Layers.** Thermals, a full-sleeve T-shirt or shirt, a sweater or fleece, and a warm jacket on top. Take off layers when the midday sun is out.
- **A warm jacket** for Paro, Thimphu and Bumthang, where nights are below 0 °C.
- **A cap, gloves and a neck warmer** for early mornings, the Tiger's Nest start and evenings.
- **Woollen socks,** two or three pairs.
- **A sweater for Punakha,** where days are about 20 °C.
- **Your dzong outfit can go under the jacket.** Long sleeves and long trousers work for both.

## Shoes for Tiger's Nest

- **Tiger's Nest (Paro Taktsang)** is a hike of about 5 to 6 hours there and back, says the Department of Tourism. The path climbs steeply most of the way.
- **Wear hiking or walking shoes with a good grip.** Not new shoes, and not sandals.
- **Carry water, a snack, a cap and a light jacket.** It's cold at the start and warm on the climb.
- **Wear shoes that are easy to take off,** or carry socks: you remove them inside the temples.
- **Horse rides** for part of the way must be booked in advance.
- Also pack a pair of comfortable shoes for towns. Two pairs are enough. See our [travel shoes guide](travel-shoes-guide.html).

## Skin, sun and health

- **Lip balm and a moisturiser.** Winter air in Bhutan is dry and cold.
- **Sunscreen and sunglasses.** The sun is strong at this height, even when the air is cold. Read our [sunscreen guide](travel-sunscreen-guide.html).
- **Your medicines,** in their original packs, with the prescription. See [can I carry medicines on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry/medicines.html)
- **Altitude:** Paro is at about 2,400 m and Tiger's Nest is higher. Go slowly on the first day, drink water and ask a doctor before you go if you have heart or lung problems.
- **A small first-aid kit:** something for an upset stomach, plasters and pain relief.
- **A steel bottle.** Hot water stays warm for the cold mornings.

## Phone, plugs and power bank

- **Bhutan uses three kinds of plugs:** British (type G), European (type C) and Indian (type D). The Department of Tourism says to bring adapters for all three.
- **A universal travel adapter** covers all three.
- **Drones:** tourists generally can't bring one. You need a permit in advance.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **A cable organiser** keeps chargers, cables and the adapter together.

## Which bag to take

- **About 5 nights:** a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack. Winter clothes are bulky, so use packing cubes.
- **About a week or more:** a medium trolley.
- **Going by road through Phuentsholing?** A backpack or a light trolley is easier at the immigration office and on hill roads. For the permit, route and own-car rules, see [Bhutan by road from India](../trips/bhutan-by-road.html).

Economy bag limits on flights between India and Bhutan, from the airlines' own websites:

| Airline | Cabin bag | Check-in bag | From India |

| Drukair | 5 kg, 45 x 35 x 20 cm | 30 kg | Delhi, Kolkata, Guwahati, Bagdogra; Bodh Gaya in winter |
| Bhutan Airlines | 7 kg, 55 x 40 x 20 cm | 30 kg (20 kg on Gaya–Paro) | Delhi, Kolkata |

- **No single bag may weigh more than 32 kg** on Drukair.

## What you can't bring into Bhutan

Bhutan's customs rules (2023 edition) list what's banned and what needs a permit:

- **Narcotics, pornography and endangered animals or plants** are banned.
- **Tobacco is restricted.** The rules list tobacco products under the Tobacco Control Rules, and the limits we could find are only in old news reports. Check with Bhutan customs before you carry cigarettes, and don't bring gutka or khaini.
- **Drones** need a permit from Bhutan's civil aviation authority before you travel.
- **Alcohol is restricted.** Tourists can bring a duty-free allowance that includes up to 2 litres of spirits and 100 ml of perfume. Declare anything more.
- **Your own used things** (camera, laptop, clothes) come in duty free, as long as you take them back out.
- **Antiques can't be taken out of Bhutan.** Craft souvenirs need an export permit, so keep your shop receipts.
- **Smoking in public places** such as hotels, bars and shops is banned outside smoking areas.

Also leave out:

- **A power bank in your check-in bag.** It goes only in your cabin bag.
- **Big bottles in your cabin bag.** Each liquid must be 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. See [can I carry liquids?](../baggage/can-i-carry/liquids.html)

## Questions

### What are the things to carry for a Bhutan trip from India?

A valid passport or your original voter ID card, passport-size photos, your SDF, hotel and guide bookings, warm layers, clothes with long sleeves and long trousers for dzongs, hiking shoes for Tiger's Nest, Indian rupees in ₹500 notes or smaller, adapters for British, European and Indian plugs, lip balm, sunscreen and your medicines.

### What documents are required for Bhutan from India?

An Indian passport valid for at least 6 months, or your original voter ID card, plus recent passport-size photos. Children under 18 without a passport need their original birth certificate. No visa is needed, but you get an entry permit at Paro airport or a land border, or online first.

### What is the SDF for Indians in Bhutan?

Nu 1,200 per person per night, which you can pay in Indian rupees. Children aged 6 to 11 pay Nu 600 a night, and children under 6 pay nothing. Day visits of up to 24 hours to the border towns, such as Phuentsholing, have no SDF.

### What should I wear in Bhutan in December?

Layers: thermals, a full-sleeve top, a sweater and a warm jacket. Nights are about 0 °C in Paro and below freezing in Thimphu, while days are about 15–17 °C and sunny. Add a cap, gloves and woollen socks, and long trousers or a long skirt for dzongs.

### What not to wear in Bhutan?

At dzongs, monasteries and temples, don't wear shorts, short skirts or sleeveless tops: the Department of Tourism asks for long sleeves and covered legs. Take off caps and hats at sacred sites, and your shoes inside temples.

### Can I use Indian rupees in Bhutan?

Yes. The ngultrum is fixed 1:1 with the rupee, and rupees are widely accepted. The Indian Embassy says you can carry Indian notes of up to ₹500 each, up to ₹25,000 in total; any amount above that must be in ₹100 notes or smaller.

### Does it snow in Bhutan in December?

It can, but it isn't certain. Thimphu gets light snow showers in some winters. Nights are below freezing in Thimphu and Bumthang even without snow.

### How much baggage is allowed on Drukair?

On international flights, 30 kg of checked baggage in economy, and a 5 kg cabin bag of up to 45 x 35 x 20 cm. No single bag may weigh more than 32 kg. Bhutan Airlines allows a 7 kg cabin bag of 55 x 40 x 20 cm and 30 kg checked.
