Canada in winter is much colder than anything most of us know from India. Toronto's nights are about −4 °C in December, Banff's are about −13 °C, and the wind makes it feel colder still. But homes, malls, trains and cars are heated, so the trick is layers you can take off indoors, not one huge coat. This list covers what to wear, what to plug in, what to leave at home and which bag to take, whether you're visiting family in Toronto, seeing the snow in Banff or spending Christmas in Vancouver.

## Canada weather in December and January

Long-term averages (1971–2000) from the Meteorological Service of Canada (Environment and Climate Change Canada), published by the World Meteorological Organization, as in our [Canada trip guide](../trips/canada-trip-from-india.html) (read 23 Nov 2026):

| Place | December, day / night | January, day / night |
|---|---|---|
| Toronto | About 2 °C / −4 °C | About −1 °C / −7 °C, snow |
| Vancouver | About 6 °C / 1 °C, wet | About 6 °C / 1 °C, wet |
| Banff (Rocky Mountains) | About −5 °C / −13 °C | About −5 °C / −14 °C |

- **Toronto, Montreal and Ottawa are below freezing** at night all winter, often by day too in January. Snow and slush are normal.
- **Banff and the Rockies are much colder,** well below freezing by day. Pack for real snow: a warm jacket, waterproof shoes, gloves and a cap that covers your ears.
- **Vancouver is mild but rainy.** Pack a waterproof jacket and shoes more than a heavy coat.
- **Wind makes it feel colder.** A windproof outer layer and a scarf or neck warmer matter as much as thickness.
- **Indoors is warm,** often 20 °C or more. Wear layers you can take off.
- **Check the forecast** for your dates. These are averages.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Your passport with the Canadian visitor visa, and your tickets and bookings
2. Travel insurance with medical cover: healthcare in Canada is expensive for visitors
3. A warm, windproof jacket with a hood
4. Two thermal sets, two sweaters or fleeces, and warm trousers
5. Gloves, a woolly cap that covers your ears, and a scarf or neck warmer
6. Waterproof shoes or boots with a good grip for ice and slush
7. Warm socks, more than one pair per day if your shoes get wet
8. A travel adapter for Canada's flat-pin sockets, and chargers marked 100–240 V
9. Lip balm and a rich moisturiser: cold air and heaters dry your skin
10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

## Documents and money

- **Indians need a visitor visa,** applied for online, with biometrics at a VFS centre in India, says Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. See the [Canada trip guide](../trips/canada-trip-from-india.html#canada-visitor-visa-for-indians) for the fee and the steps.
- **CAN$10,000 or more? Declare it.** You must declare currency and monetary instruments of CAN$10,000 or more when you enter or leave Canada, says the Canada Border Services Agency.
- **Cash from India:** RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip. Cards, including contactless, work almost everywhere in Canada. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **Carrying sweets or snacks for relatives? Declare them.** Canada limits the food you can bring for personal use, says the Canada Border Services Agency. Check what you can pack in our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.
- **Keep a photo of your passport, visa and insurance papers** on your phone.

## How to dress for a Canadian winter: three layers

1. **Base layer:** thermals next to your skin, top and bottom. For days outdoors in Banff, or if you walk a lot, a synthetic set dries faster than cotton.
2. **Middle layer:** a sweater or fleece. This is what holds in most of the heat. Take two.
3. **Outer layer:** a warm, windproof jacket with a hood, long enough to cover your hips. For Toronto and Banff, a down or thick puffer jacket. For Vancouver, a waterproof jacket over a fleece.

Then add a cap that covers your ears, a neck warmer or scarf, and gloves. Take a layer off before you start to sweat: damp clothes make you cold once you stop.

Not sure which jacket? Down is the warmest for its weight but loses its warmth when wet; synthetic fill copes better with damp. Our [winter jacket for travel guide](winter-jacket-for-travel.html) explains down vs synthetic, and how to read "water repellent" and "waterproof" on a listing.

## Shoes and boots for snow and ice

- **Waterproof, with a deep, grippy sole.** Pavements are icy and slushy, and salt on the roads stains shoes.
- **Room for thick socks.** Buy half a size up if you'll wear two pairs.
- **Wear your boots on the flight,** to save weight and space in your bag.
- **A pair of indoor shoes or slippers.** In Canadian homes, people take their shoes off at the door.

## Skin, health and medicines

- **Lip balm and a rich moisturiser.** Cold wind outside and heaters inside dry your skin and lips quickly.
- **Sunglasses and sunscreen for snow.** Sun on snow is bright, especially in the mountains.
- **A small first-aid kit:** plasters, pain relief, cold and flu tablets.
- **Your medicines** go in your cabin bag, in their original packs, with the prescription. See [can I carry medicines on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry/medicines.html)

## Phone, plugs and power bank

- **Canada uses 120 V and flat two-pin or three-pin plugs,** different from India's. You need an adapter. See our [power bank and travel adapter guide](power-bank-and-travel-adapter-guide.html) or our [travel adapter for the USA and Canada](travel-adapter-for-usa.html) page.
- **Check your charger's label.** Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work with just an adapter. 230 V-only hair dryers and straighteners won't work properly.
- **Cold drains batteries fast.** Keep your phone and power bank in an inside pocket when you're outdoors.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **Phone data:** see the eSIM and SIM options in the [Canada trip guide](../trips/canada-trip-from-india.html#phone-sim-and-esim-in-canada).

## Which bag to take

- **About 10 days or more,** which is usual for a long flight to Canada: a medium or large trolley for checked baggage, plus a cabin bag. Winter clothes are bulky.
- **Wear your bulkiest jacket and boots on the plane.** They don't count towards your bag's weight.
- **Packing cubes** keep thermals, sweaters and socks apart, and squeeze bulky clothes smaller.
- **Weigh your bag before you leave.** Gifts for relatives add up. Check your fare's limit with our [excess baggage calculator](../tools/bag-weight.html).

## Questions

### What should I pack for Canada in winter?

Two thermal sets, two sweaters or fleeces, a warm windproof jacket with a hood, warm trousers, gloves, a cap that covers your ears, a scarf, and waterproof shoes or boots with a good grip. Add a travel adapter for flat-pin sockets, lip balm, a moisturiser and your visa and insurance papers.

### What should I wear in Canada in December?

Layers: thermals, a sweater or fleece, and a warm windproof jacket, plus a cap, gloves and waterproof boots. Toronto averages about 2 °C by day and −4 °C at night in December, and Banff about −5 °C and −13 °C. Vancouver is milder, about 6 °C, but rainy, so pack a waterproof jacket.

### How cold is Canada in winter?

Very cold away from the west coast. Environment Canada averages put Toronto at about −1 °C by day and −7 °C at night in January, and Banff at about −5 °C and −14 °C. Vancouver stays around 6 °C by day and 1 °C at night, with a lot of rain.

### Is a down jacket or a synthetic jacket better for Canada?

For dry cold in Toronto or Banff, down is the warmest for its weight and packs small. For Vancouver's rain or wet snow, synthetic fill keeps more warmth when damp, or wear a waterproof shell over down. See our [winter jacket for travel guide](winter-jacket-for-travel.html).

### What shoes should I take to Canada in winter?

Waterproof shoes or boots with a deep, grippy sole for ice and slush, with room for thick socks. Wear them on the flight to save space. Take slippers or indoor shoes too, because people take their shoes off at home.

### Can I buy winter clothes in Canada instead?

Yes, Canadian shops have a wide range of winter clothes. But you'll need warm clothes the moment you land, so carry at least a jacket, thermals, a cap and gloves in your cabin bag or wear them on the flight.

### Do I need an adapter for Canada from India?

Yes. Canada uses 120 V and flat two-pin or three-pin plugs. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work with just an adapter, but 230 V-only appliances won't work properly.

### How much cash can I carry to Canada?

Any amount, but you must declare CAN$10,000 or more when you enter or leave Canada, says the Canada Border Services Agency. RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, so carry a card or a forex card too.
