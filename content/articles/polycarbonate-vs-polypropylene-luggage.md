Most hard trolleys in India are polycarbonate (PC) or polypropylene (PP). Luggage sellers usually say PC is tougher and PP is lighter. We checked the weight part against real listings, and the difference was small.

## Quick answer

- **Choose polycarbonate** if your bag will be checked in often and you want a tougher shell. Expect to pay a little more.
- **Choose polypropylene** if you want a light, good-value bag for domestic trips.
- **Don't choose by material alone.** The lightest cabin bag we read was polycarbonate, and the model matters more than the material.

## How they compare

Here's what luggage sellers say about each one:

| What | Polycarbonate (PC) | Polypropylene (PP) | ABS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toughness | Described as very tough and resistant to cracks | Crack-resistant, less rigid than PC | Rigid; can be damaged by rough handling |
| Weight | A little heavier than PP | Called the lightest hard shell | Varies by source |
| Flex | Flexes under a hit | Flexes too | Rigid |
| Cost | Usually more than PP | Usually the lower-cost choice | Varies by source |

**A warning:** these come from companies that sell luggage. We didn't find independent drop tests of these materials, and we haven't tested them ourselves. Words like "unbreakable" on a listing are marketing. A long warranty tells you more.

## What the listings really weigh

On 6 Nov 2026 we read trolley listings on Amazon.in that named one of these two materials:

| Size | PP: typical weight | PP: listings | PC: typical weight | PC: listings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cabin | 2.53 kg | 9 | 2.4 kg | 4 |
| Medium | 3.3 kg | 21 | 3.5 kg | 7 |
| Large | 4.25 kg | 12 | 4.5 kg | 9 |

So PP was only 0.2–0.25 kg lighter in medium and large bags. In cabin bags, PC was lighter here, though we read only 4 of them. The range inside each material is much wider:

- **Lightest PC cabin bag:** [American Tourister Splash](../picks/B0B6BVXCCD.html), 2 kg.
- **Lightest PP medium bag:** [Kamiliant Rock-Lite](../picks/B0GNSKLF5Z.html), 2.52 kg.
- **Lightest PP large bag:** Kamiliant Rock-Lite 79 cm, 3.48 kg.
- **Lightest PC large bag:** Kamiliant Ather 78 cm, 3.96 kg.

Check the weight of the bag itself, not the material. A difference of 0.2 kg won't change your trip. A difference of 1 kg might.

## Which one for your trip?

| Your trip | Good choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin bag only | Either | You keep it with you, so weight and full size matter more |
| Check-in bag, a few trips a year | PP | Lighter on average, and usually lower cost |
| Check-in bag, frequent flights | PC | Sellers describe it as tougher against rough handling |
| Long sunny trips with bright colours | Check the listing | One seller warns that PP colours can fade in strong sun |
| Trains and car boots | Consider soft | A hard shell can't squeeze into a tight space |

## Hard or soft luggage?

Hard shells handle rain well, wipe clean and protect what's inside better. Soft bags can squeeze into train berths and car boots, and often have outside pockets, but protect fragile things less.

For flights, most people choose hard. For trains and road trips, soft or a duffle can be easier. See [our luggage size guide](luggage-size-guide.html) and the [train luggage rules](../baggage/train-luggage-rules.html).

## Questions

### Which is better, polycarbonate or polypropylene?

Sellers usually describe polycarbonate as tougher and polypropylene as lighter and lower in cost. PC suits frequent check-ins, and PP suits most domestic trips. Check the weight and warranty of the bag you're choosing, because they vary by model.

### Is polycarbonate stronger than polypropylene?

Luggage sellers generally say so, and describe polycarbonate as very tough and resistant to cracks. We didn't find independent tests, and we haven't tested them ourselves.

### Is polypropylene lighter than polycarbonate?

Usually, but not by much. In the listings we read, it was 0.2–0.25 kg lighter in medium and large sizes. In cabin bags, polycarbonate was slightly lighter, from a small sample.

### Is polypropylene good for check-in luggage?

Yes, for most trips. Many check-in bags we read were polypropylene, including American Tourister's Liftoff+. Look for 8 wheels, a lock and a good warranty.

### What about ABS luggage?

ABS is another hard plastic. Travelpro, a luggage maker, describes it as rigid and says it can be damaged by rough handling or high heat. Only 2 of the listings we read named ABS, so we can't say much about it.

### Hard or soft luggage: which is better?

Hard is usually better for flights, because it protects your things and handles rain well. Soft is often easier on trains and road trips, because it can squeeze into tight spaces.
