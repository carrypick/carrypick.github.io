An "8 wheel" trolley bag doesn't have eight wheels spread around it. It has **four double spinner wheels**, one pair at each corner. A "4 wheel" bag has four single spinner wheels, and a "2 wheel" bag has two fixed wheels that you pull behind you. Here's which suits which trip.

## Quick answer

- **8 wheels (4 double spinners):** smooth and steady on airport and mall floors. Most trolleys sold in India now have them: 38 of our 44 trolleys and sets list 8 wheels.
- **4 wheels (4 single spinners):** they still roll upright beside you. Fine for most trips.
- **2 wheels (fixed):** you tilt and pull the bag. Better on rough roads, kerbs and station platforms.
- **Wheels didn't decide weight.** Our lightest 4-wheel and 8-wheel cabin bags weigh about the same (2 kg and 2.03 kg).

## 8, 4 and 2 wheels compared

| What | 8 wheels (double spinners) | 4 wheels (single spinners) | 2 wheels (fixed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| How you move it | Push or roll upright, any direction | Push or roll upright, any direction | Tilt and pull behind you |
| Smooth floors | Smoothest | Smooth | Fine |
| Rough roads, kerbs, gravel | Small wheels can catch | Small wheels can catch | Best: wheels are larger and set into the bag |
| Slopes and moving trains | Can roll away | Can roll away | Stays put when stood up |
| Weight on each corner | Shared by two wheels | One wheel | Two wheels when tilted |
| Where you'll find it | Most hard trolleys | Some cabin and soft bags | Duffle trolleys, some soft bags |

Spinner wheels stick out below the bag. Airlines measure cabin bags **with wheels and handles**, so the wheels are part of the listed size you need to check. See [carry-on size by airline](carry-on-size-by-airline.html) or test your bag in the [cabin bag checker](../baggage/checker.html).

## Which should you choose?

| Your trip | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Flights, mostly airports | 8 wheels | Smoothest on long terminal floors |
| Flights, light packer on a budget | 4 or 8 wheels | Either works; check size and weight first |
| Trains, old stations, villages | 2 wheels or a duffle | Handles kerbs, steps and rough ground |
| Cobbled streets abroad | 2 wheels, or 8 wheels carried | Small spinners rattle on cobbles |
| Heavy, large bag | 8 wheels | Two wheels share the load at each corner |

## Looking after the wheels

- **Lift, don't drag, up kerbs and steps.** Spinner wheels are the part most likely to break.
- **Check what the warranty covers.** Most of our picks list 3 or 5 years, but the terms differ, so read the warranty card and ask whether wheels are included.
- **Weigh the packed bag.** An overfull bag is hard on wheels, and on your fare: use the [excess baggage calculator](../tools/bag-weight.html).

## Questions

### Which is better, a 4 wheel or 8 wheel trolley bag?

For airports, 8 wheels are usually smoother and steadier, because two wheels share the load at each corner. A 4-wheel bag works well too. Choose by size, weight and lock first.

### Does an 8 wheel trolley really have 8 wheels?

Yes, but in four pairs. Each corner has a double spinner wheel, so it rolls like a 4-wheel bag with two wheels at each corner.

### Are 8 wheel bags heavier?

Not in our picks. Our lightest 8-wheel cabin bag is 2.03 kg, and our lightest 4-wheel one is 2 kg.

### Are 2 wheel trolleys better?

On rough ground, yes. Fixed wheels handle kerbs, gravel and steps better, and the bag doesn't roll away on a slope. On smooth floors, spinners are easier.

### Do wheels count in the cabin bag size?

Yes. Airlines such as IndiGo say their cabin size includes wheels and handles. Check your bag's listed size, which should include them, against your airline.
