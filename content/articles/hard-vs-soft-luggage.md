Hard or soft? It depends less on which is "better" and more on how you travel. Hard shells suit flights where bags get thrown about. Soft bags suit trains, cars and people who like outside pockets. We also checked a common belief: that soft bags are always lighter.

## Quick answer

- **Choose hard** for checked bags on flights, monsoon trips and anything fragile.
- **Choose soft** for trains, road trips and buses, where a bag that squeezes into a berth or boot helps.
- **Soft isn't always lighter.** In our picks the lightest large bag is soft, but the lightest cabin and medium bags are hard.
- **For the cabin,** size matters more than material. Check your airline with the [cabin bag checker](../baggage/checker.html).

## Hard vs soft at a glance

| What | Hard shell | Soft (fabric) |
|---|---|---|
| Protects fragile things | Better | Less |
| Rain | Shell keeps water out; zips can still leak | Fabric can soak through in heavy rain |
| Outside pockets | Rare | Common, handy for papers and chargers |
| Squeezes into tight spaces | No | Yes, a little |
| Expandable zip | Some | Common |
| Scuffs and marks | Show on the shell | Hide better |
| Cleaning | Wipe clean | Harder to clean |
| Lock | Usually built in | Built in on some, zip pullers on others |

## Which one for your trip?

| Trip | Better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Flights, checked bag | Hard | Protects your things when bags are stacked and thrown |
| Flights, cabin bag | Either | The airline's size limit matters more: see [carry-on size by airline](carry-on-size-by-airline.html) |
| Train journeys | Soft or a duffle | Fits under a berth more easily |
| Road trips | Soft | Squeezes into a full car boot |
| Monsoon or snow | Hard | Water stays out of the shell |
| Gifts, bottles, fragile things | Hard | Less likely to crush |

On Indian trains, the size limits are tighter in AC 3-tier and AC Chair Car. See our [train luggage rules](../baggage/train-luggage-rules.html).

## Hard shell materials

Most hard trolleys in India are polypropylene (PP) or polycarbonate (PC), and a few are ABS. The weight gap between them is small, and the model matters more than the material. See [polycarbonate vs polypropylene vs ABS](polycarbonate-vs-polypropylene-luggage.html).

## Questions

### Is hard or soft luggage better?

Hard is usually better for checked bags on flights, because it protects your things and keeps rain out. Soft is often better for trains and road trips, because it squeezes into tight spaces and has outside pockets.

### Is hard luggage heavier than soft luggage?

Not always. Among our picks, the lightest cabin and medium bags are hard, but the lightest large bag is soft.

### Is soft luggage good for check-in?

It can be, if you don't pack anything fragile. Choose one with a lock and strong zips, and put liquids in a sealed bag.

### Hard or soft for a cabin bag?

Either works. What matters is the size: many 55 cm hard bags are too wide or deep for Indian airlines. Use our [cabin bag checker](../baggage/checker.html).

### Is soft luggage waterproof?

Usually not fully. Many soft bags are water-resistant fabric, so heavy rain can soak through. A hard shell keeps water out better, though zips can still let some in.
