## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Your passport, the one linked to your eVisa
2. Your CAS number and university letters, and any other documents your visa needed, in your hand bag
3. Your medicines in their original packs, with the prescription
4. A warm, waterproof jacket with a hood, thermals and two sweaters
5. Waterproof shoes with a good grip
6. A laptop and its charger, a travel adapter with a Type G plug, and a power bank in your cabin bag
7. A card that works abroad, and some pounds for the first days
8. A check-in trolley within 158 cm and 23 kg, and a luggage scale

## Documents to carry in your hand bag

GOV.UK's Student visa pages (read 23 Nov 2026) say you need **a current passport** and a **Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)** from your course provider to apply. Depending on your case you may also need:

- **Proof that you have enough money**
- **An ATAS certificate,** if your course and nationality need one
- **Tuberculosis (TB) test results**
- **Written consent from your parents and a birth certificate,** if you're under 18

Keep these papers, or copies, in your hand bag when you fly. Your university may ask to see them when you enrol.

- **Your eVisa:** students get an eVisa linked to a UKVI account, and the passport you travel on must be linked to it, says GOV.UK.
- **At the border,** your passport (and visa) are checked, and eVisa holders may have their fingerprints checked. Take your passport out of any cover.
- **When you can arrive:** up to 1 week before your course starts if it lasts 6 months or less, and up to 1 month before if it lasts longer. You must not travel before the start date on your visa.

## Baggage allowance for students

Students on these airlines can get more checked baggage than a normal ticket, as each airline's own page says (read 23 Nov 2026):

- **Air India student fare:** on routes where bags are counted by piece, Economy and Premium Economy get **2 bags of 23 kg each**. Choose "Student" as the concession type when you book. International student fares are for full-time students aged 12 to 30, and you must show a student ID, student visa or acceptance letter at the airport if asked.
- **Virgin Atlantic student offer:** from Delhi, Mumbai or Bengaluru to London, Economy Classic and Delight and Premium get **3 bags of 23 kg**, 69 kg in all. It isn't valid on Economy Light, it's for the outbound flight only, and you show your student visa at check-in. Virgin says to book it through its India contact centre.

On a normal fare, check what your ticket includes. From our airline data: [British Airways](../baggage/british-airways.html) gives 1 bag of 23 kg in Economy (none on Basic), [Lufthansa](../baggage/lufthansa.html) 1 bag of 23 kg (none on Economy Light), and Air India 1 bag of 23 kg on Value and 2 on Classic and Flex to Europe. Emirates and Qatar Airways depend on your fare. See our [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html) and, if you'll pay for extra bags, the [excess baggage calculator](../tools/bag-weight.html).

- **Each bag can usually be up to 158 cm in total** (length + width + height). Air India's page also limits both Economy bags together to 271 cm. See [trolley bag size for international travel](trolley-bag-size-for-international-travel.html).
- **Cabin bag:** British Airways allows one bag of 56 x 45 x 25 cm plus a handbag or backpack; Air India 7 kg and 55 x 40 x 20 cm. Check yours with the [cabin bag checker](../baggage/checker.html).

## Which bags to take

- **Large bag:** one of 75–79 cm that's listed within 158 cm. Lighter bags leave more of the 23 kg for your things.
- **Second bag:** a medium bag of 65–69 cm. On Air India, keep both bags together within 271 cm, so pair it with a large bag of about 152 cm or less.
- **Buying a set?** Check the large bag's listed total in our [trolley bag set of 3 guide](trolley-bag-set-of-3.html), and read the [28 inch trolley guide](28-inch-trolley-bag-size.html).
- **Lots of stairs:** many UK student houses and older buildings have no lift. Four wheels and a light bag help.

## What to wear in the UK

The UK is cool and damp more than freezing. London averages about 8 °C by day and 3 °C at night in December and January, and Edinburgh about 7 °C and 1 °C (Met Office averages on the WMO site, as in our [UK trip guide](../trips/uk-trip-from-india.html)). It rains often, in small amounts, and winter days are short.

- **A waterproof jacket with a hood** matters more than a very thick one. Wind and rain make it feel colder.
- **Layers:** thermals, two sweaters or fleeces, and warm trousers. Rooms, buses and libraries are heated.
- **Waterproof shoes with a good grip,** worn in before you fly, and plenty of socks.
- **A cap, gloves and a scarf** for winter mornings.
- **One formal outfit** for presentations and events.

## Indian food: what the UK allows

GOV.UK's rules on bringing food into Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland has its own rules) say that from a country like India (read 23 Nov 2026):

- **No meat or meat products.**
- **No milk or milk-based products.** That means ghee, paneer, khoya and milk sweets stay at home. Powdered infant milk, infant food and special medical food are the exception.
- **Fish:** up to 20 kg per person.
- **Honey:** up to 2 kg per person.
- **Most fresh fruit and vegetables** need a plant health ("phytosanitary") certificate. Some are allowed without one, including mango, banana, dates, guava, curry leaves, coconut, citrus fruit and pineapple, and certain grains such as rice.
- **Processed and packaged plant products** are allowed, so sealed packs of spices, masalas and dry snacks without meat or milk are usually fine.

## Medicines

GOV.UK's page on taking medicine into the UK covers medicines that contain a **controlled drug**:

- **Carry them in your hand luggage.**
- **Bring a letter** with your name, travel dates, the list of medicines with amounts, doses and strengths, and the prescriber's signature.
- **Up to 3 months' supply.** More can be taken away.
- **For any medicine,** keep it in its original pack with the prescription, in your cabin bag. See [can I carry medicines on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry/medicines.html)

## Laptop, phone, plugs and power bank

- **The UK uses Type G plugs** (three square pins) and 230 V, like India's voltage, so Indian chargers and hair dryers work with just an adapter. See our [power bank and travel adapter guide](power-bank-and-travel-adapter-guide.html) or the full [travel adapter by country](travel-adapter-by-country.html) list.
- **An adapter changes only the plug,** not the voltage.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **Take two adapters** if you can: one for your room and one for the library.

## What not to pack

- **Ghee, paneer, milk sweets and anything with meat**
- **Most fresh fruit and vegetables** (except those listed above)
- **A 32 inch bag** that's over 158 cm: see the [32 inch trolley guide](32-inch-trolley-bag-size.html)
- **Too many clothes.** You can buy everyday things in the UK.
- **Power banks in check-in.**

## Customs allowances and cash

From GOV.UK (read 23 Nov 2026):

- **Other goods,** such as gifts and electronics bought abroad: up to £390 when you arrive by air. Over that, you pay duty and tax on the full value.
- **Alcohol:** 42 litres of beer, 18 litres of wine, and 4 litres of spirits (or 9 litres of fortified wine). **Tobacco:** 200 cigarettes or 250 g of tobacco. No alcohol or tobacco allowance for under-17s.
- **Cash of £10,000 or more** must be declared when you arrive from outside the UK. The total of a family or group counts. Not declaring can mean a fine of up to £5,000.
- **Cash from India:** see [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).

## Questions

### What should Indian students pack for the UK?

Your passport linked to your eVisa, your CAS and university letters, your medicines with the prescription, a waterproof jacket, thermals and sweaters, waterproof shoes, a laptop with a Type G adapter, a power bank in your cabin bag, and a card that works abroad. Pack a large trolley within 158 cm and 23 kg and a medium bag.

### What documents do I need to carry to the UK as a student?

GOV.UK lists a current passport and your CAS for the Student visa, and depending on your case proof of money, an ATAS certificate and TB test results. Your passport must be linked to your eVisa. Keep these papers or copies in your hand bag.

### Can I take ghee or Indian sweets to the UK?

No, not if they contain milk. GOV.UK says you can't bring meat or milk-based products into Great Britain from countries like India. Sealed spices and dry snacks without meat or milk are usually fine.

### Can I take rice and spices to the UK?

Rice is among the grains GOV.UK allows without a plant health certificate, and processed, packaged plant products are allowed, so sealed spice packs are usually fine.

### How much baggage can students carry to the UK?

Air India's student fare gives 2 bags of 23 kg in Economy on piece-based routes, and Virgin Atlantic's student offer from India to London gives 3 bags of 23 kg, as their pages said on 23 Nov 2026. On a normal British Airways Economy fare, it's 1 bag of 23 kg.

### When can I travel to the UK on a Student visa?

Up to 1 week before your course starts if it lasts 6 months or less, and up to 1 month before if it's longer, says GOV.UK. Not before the start date on your visa.

### Do Indian plugs work in the UK?

No. The UK uses three-pin Type G plugs. The voltage is 230 V, like India, so you need only a plug adapter.
