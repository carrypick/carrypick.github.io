A good everyday bag holds what you carry, sits comfortably, and keeps your phone and wallet safe in a crowd. Start with what you need to carry, then choose the shape.

## Which type of bag?

| Type | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Tote | Office, laptop, daily use | Heavy on one shoulder; some have no zip |
| Satchel | Work, smart occasions | Holds less than a tote |
| Crossbody sling | Sightseeing, markets, airports | Small; rarely fits a tablet |

## Three things to check

1. **How it closes.** In crowded markets and trains, a zip is safer than a magnet or an open top. Wear a sling in front of you.
2. **Its weight when empty.** Listed weights on this page go from about 140 g to 800 g, and a heavy bag gets tiring by the end of a long day.
3. **Your laptop's size.** If you carry one, check for a padded sleeve and the size it fits.

## Your bag on a flight

Most Indian airlines limit the second bag you carry on board, and the rules differ:

| Airline | Personal bag rule |
|---|---|
| IndiGo | One personal item up to 3 kg, like a ladies' purse or a small laptop bag |
| Air India | One small bag up to 3 kg and 40 x 30 x 20 cm, under the seat |
| Akasa Air | One personal item up to 3 kg, under the seat |
| Air India Express | A laptop bag up to 30 x 40 x 10 cm, but it shares your 7 kg |
| SpiceJet | A laptop bag or ladies' purse, but it counts inside your 7 kg |

These rules were checked on 3 Nov 2026. Fares and rules change, so see our [airline baggage rules](../baggage/index.html) before you fly.
