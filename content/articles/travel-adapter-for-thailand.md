Thailand's sockets take both **flat parallel pins** (like the USA) and **two round pins** (like most of Europe and Asia), says the Tourism Authority of Thailand. The supply is **220 V, 50 Hz**. A thin two-pin Indian phone charger usually fits; a thick three-pin Indian plug doesn't.

## Quick answer

- **Plug types:** A, B, C and O. Most sockets are hybrids that take US flat pins and thin round pins.
- **Voltage:** 220 V at 50 Hz (Wikipedia's table gives the nominal 230 V). Close enough to India's 230 V for every Indian appliance.
- **From India with only phone chargers:** you may not need an adapter at all.
- **From India with a laptop or trimmer (three-pin plug):** take an adapter.
- **From the USA:** your plug often fits, but the voltage is 220 V. The TAT advises not to bring 110 V-only appliances.
- **From the UK or Australia:** take an adapter. Your plugs don't fit.

- **Some hotels and offices have three-pin outlets,** says the TAT. Beach huts and guest houses on the islands may have only one or two sockets, so a universal adapter with USB ports helps.

## Questions

### Do I need an adapter for Thailand from India?

Not for a thin two-pin phone charger: it usually fits. You do for a three-pin Indian plug, such as many laptop chargers and trimmers.

### What is the voltage in Thailand?

220 V at 50 Hz, according to the Tourism Authority of Thailand. Indian appliances work there.

### Can I use a US plug in Thailand?

Most Thai sockets take flat US pins. The voltage is 220 V, so only use chargers marked 100-240V. The TAT advises against bringing 110 V-only appliances.

### Do UK plugs work in Thailand?

No. Type G is not used in Thailand. Take an adapter.

### Can I buy an adapter in Thailand?

Yes, at convenience stores and electronics shops. Bringing one from home means you can charge at the airport on arrival.
