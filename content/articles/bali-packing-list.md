Bali is hot all year. November to March are the wet months, with rain on most days in December and January, so pack light clothes that dry fast, a rain poncho and sandals that can get wet. Add one temple outfit and good sun care. This list tells you what to pack and why, for a trip from India.

## Bali weather by month

Long-term averages for Denpasar from Indonesia's weather agency BMKG, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

| Month | Day | Night | Rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 179 mm, on about 16 days |
| December | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 276 mm, on about 22 days |
| January | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 345 mm, on about 27 days |
| February | About 33 °C | About 24 °C | About 274 mm, on about 22 days |
| July | About 30 °C | About 23 °C | About 55 mm, on about 4 days |
| August | About 30 °C | About 23 °C | About 25 mm, on about 4 days |

- **It's hot all year,** about 30–34 °C in the day and 22–25 °C at night in Denpasar.
- **December to February is the wettest time.** Expect rain on most days. Pack a poncho, quick-dry clothes and a waterproof pouch.
- **June to September is the driest time,** with rain on only 4–8 days a month.
- **Sunrise treks start in the dark.** If you plan one, take a light layer: early mornings up a volcano are cooler than the beach.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Passport, printed tickets, hotel bookings and travel insurance
2. Light cotton or quick-dry clothes, one outfit for each day
3. One outfit that covers shoulders and knees, for temples
4. Swimwear, and a shirt or kaftan to wear over it
5. A rain poncho, and a rain cover or dry bag for your backpack
6. Sandals or flip-flops that can get wet, plus walking shoes
7. Sunscreen, sunglasses, a cap or hat and mosquito repellent
8. A waterproof pouch for your phone
9. A universal travel adapter
10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

## Documents and money

- **Passport.** Keep it on you, not in your check-in bag. Carry a photocopy and a photo of it on your phone.
- **Print or save your bookings:** return ticket, hotel bookings and travel insurance. Check the entry rules for Indian passport holders on Indonesia's official immigration or embassy websites before you fly.
- **Cash.** RBI lets a bank or money changer sell you up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can take up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).
- **All Indonesia arrival form.** Indonesian Customs says every passenger must fill in the All Indonesia declaration, which covers immigration, customs, health and quarantine. It's free on the official site, [allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id](https://allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id), from 3 days before you land. Save the QR code.
- **Bali tourist levy: Rp 150,000 per person,** paid once per trip, says the Bali government's Love Bali site. Pay online before you fly on [lovebali.baliprov.go.id](https://lovebali.baliprov.go.id), or cashless at a counter at the airport. Use only the official site.

## Clothes

- **Cotton, linen or quick-dry fabric.** In the rainy season, clothes that dry overnight save your trip.
- **Shorts, T-shirts, dresses and skirts** are normal at the beaches and in Seminyak, Kuta and Canggu.
- **One temple outfit.** See the temple rules below.
- **Swimwear for the beach and the pool.** Put a shirt or kaftan over it when you walk to shops and cafes.
- **One light layer** for flights, AC cafes and a sunrise trek.
- **One nicer outfit** for a beach club or a good dinner.

## What to wear to temples in Bali

- **Cover your shoulders and knees.** Indonesia's official tourism site says this for Bali's temples, and adds that sarongs and sashes are usually given at the entrance.
- **Some temples require a sarong and sash.** At Besakih, all visitors must wear a sarong (kamen) and sash. At Tirta Empul, sarongs can be rented for a small donation. Your own sarong saves time.
- **Stay out of the inner sacred areas.** Bali's 2025 rules for tourists say only worshippers in Balinese dress may enter them, and menstruating women may not enter them.
- **Don't climb sacred trees or monuments,** and don't step on offerings.
- **Dress modestly in public places too,** the same rules say, not only at temples.

## Shoes

- **Sandals or flip-flops that can get wet,** for the beach, rain and waterfalls.
- **Walking shoes or sandals with a good grip** for rice terraces, waterfalls and temple steps. Paths are slippery in the rain.
- **Trekking shoes** only if you plan a volcano sunrise trek.
- Two pairs are enough for most trips. See our [travel shoes guide](travel-shoes-guide.html).

## Sun, rain and health

- **Sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher.** Put it on again every 2 hours and after swimming. Read our [sunscreen guide](travel-sunscreen-guide.html).
- **Sunglasses and a cap or hat.**
- **Mosquito repellent,** especially in the evening and in the rainy season.
- **A rain poncho.** It covers you and your backpack. On a scooter, a poncho flaps in the wind, so a rain jacket is better.
- **Your regular medicines** in their original packs, with your prescription, plus something for an upset stomach, ORS and plasters.
- **Indonesia's medicine rules.** Its drug authority, BPOM, says medicines from abroad need a doctor's prescription or hospital letter, for up to 90 days. Over-the-counter medicines can come without one for up to 3 days' use. Narcotic medicines aren't allowed. Say you carry medicines on your arrival form, or Customs can seize them.
- **A refillable water bottle.**

## Phone, plugs and power bank

- **A universal travel adapter.** Socket types can differ from India's, and from one hotel to another, so one adapter that takes many plug types is the simple answer.
- **Check your charger's label.** Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work abroad with just an adapter. Check hair dryers and straighteners before you pack them.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in, and you can't use or charge it during IndiGo flights. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html)
- **A cable organiser** keeps chargers, cables and the adapter in one place.

- **A waterproof pouch** for your phone, cash and room key on boat trips to Nusa Penida and at waterfalls.
- **A backpack with a rain cover,** or a dry bag, for scooter rides and day trips.
- **A quick-dry towel or sarong,** and a plastic or zip bag for wet swimwear.

## What to pack for women

- Three or four cotton dresses, or tops with shorts and skirts
- Swimwear and a kaftan or sarong to wear over it
- A top with sleeves and a long skirt or sarong, for temples
- A light scarf or stole for temples, cool evenings and the flight
- Flip-flops and one pair of sandals with a good grip
- A crossbody bag that zips shut

## What to pack for men

- Shorts and T-shirts, plus one or two cotton shirts
- Swim shorts
- One pair of long trousers, or a sarong, for temples
- One light layer for evenings and a sunrise trek
- Flip-flops and sandals with straps or walking shoes
- A cap

## What to pack for kids

- More clothes than days: kids get wet, and clothes dry slowly in the rain
- A swimsuit, a sun hat and kids' sunscreen
- A light rain jacket
- Slip-on sandals that can get wet
- Snacks and a water bottle for the long journey
- Their medicines and a small first-aid kit

## Which bag to take

- **About 5 nights:** a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack is enough.
- **About a week or more:** a medium trolley. Leave space for shopping.
- **Staying in more than one area,** like Ubud and the beach? A backpack or a light trolley is easier on stairs and narrow paths.

## What not to pack

- **More than 1 litre of alcohol or 200 cigarettes** (or 25 cigars or 100 g of tobacco). Indonesian Customs destroys the extra at the airport. Alcohol is only for travellers aged 21 or older, and tobacco 18 or older.
- **Single-use plastic.** Bali's rules for tourists ban single-use plastic bags, straws, Styrofoam and plastic-packed drinks. Carry a cloth bag and a refillable bottle.
- **Narcotic medicines,** and medicines without a prescription beyond 3 days' use.
- **A power bank in your check-in bag.** It goes only in your cabin bag.
- **Big bottles in your cabin bag.** Each liquid must be 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. Bigger bottles go in check-in.
- **Expensive jewellery.** You won't need it on the beach.

Indonesian Customs lets in goods worth up to US$500 per person duty free, and charges 10% duty on the amount above that. Cash worth Rp 100 million or more must be declared. Not sure about an item on the flight? Search our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.

## Flying home to India

- **Indian Customs lets you bring goods worth ₹75,000 per person duty free**, plus up to 2 litres of alcohol. See our [Indian customs rules](../baggage/customs.html).
- **Duty-free alcohol and cigarettes** count towards your limits. See [alcohol and cigarette limits](../baggage/customs/alcohol-and-cigarettes.html).
- **Weigh your bag before you leave the hotel.** Shopping adds up fast, and 20 kg fares fill quickly.

## Questions

### What should I pack for Bali from India?

Light cotton or quick-dry clothes, swimwear, one outfit that covers shoulders and knees for temples, a rain poncho, sandals that can get wet, walking shoes, sunscreen, mosquito repellent, a waterproof phone pouch, your medicines with the prescription, and a power bank in your cabin bag.

### What should women wear in Bali?

Dresses, shorts, skirts and tops are normal at the beaches and in the beach towns. For temples, cover your shoulders and knees and wear a sarong and sash, which many temples give or rent at the entrance. Bali's rules ask tourists to dress modestly in public places too.

### What should men wear in Bali?

Shorts, T-shirts and cotton shirts for most days. For temples, cover your shoulders and knees and wear a sarong and sash, which many temples give or rent at the entrance.

### What should I wear to temples in Bali?

A top that covers your shoulders, and a sarong (kamen) with a sash over your legs. Besakih requires a sarong and sash for all visitors, and other temples usually lend or rent them at the entrance.

### Does it rain in Bali in December?

Yes. Denpasar averages about 276 mm of rain on about 22 days in December, and January is even wetter. Pack a poncho, quick-dry clothes and a waterproof pouch. June to September is the driest time.

### Do I need to pay a tourist tax in Bali?

Yes. Foreign tourists pay a Bali levy of Rp 150,000 per person, once per trip. Pay online before you fly on the official Love Bali site, or cashless at a counter at the airport.

### How much alcohol can I take to Bali?

Up to 1 litre per person, only if you're 21 or older. Indonesian Customs destroys anything over the limit at the airport.

### How much baggage can I take from India to Bali?

On IndiGo, 20 kg on regular fares (25 kg on UpFront), plus a 7 kg cabin bag. With a stop on the way, Singapore Airlines gives 25–30 kg, Malaysia Airlines 20–35 kg and Thai Airways one or two 23 kg bags, by fare.
