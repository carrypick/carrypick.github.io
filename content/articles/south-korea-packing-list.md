South Korea in winter is cold, dry and often sunny. Seoul's nights are below freezing from December to February, and a wind off the river makes it feel colder, but the metro, cafés and shops are warm. So you need a properly warm jacket for outdoors, and thin layers underneath that you can take off. You'll also walk a lot, up and down many metro stairs, so your shoes matter as much as your coat. This list covers what to wear, what to plug in, what to leave at home and which bag to take.

## South Korea weather in December and January

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from the Korea Meteorological Administration, published by the World Meteorological Organization, as in our [South Korea trip guide](../trips/south-korea-trip-from-india.html) (read 22 Nov 2026):

| Place | December, day / night | January, day / night |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul | About 4 °C / −3 °C | About 2 °C / −6 °C, the coldest month |
| Busan | About 10 °C / 2 °C | About 8 °C / 0 °C |
| Jeju | About 11 °C / 6 °C | About 9 °C / 4 °C |

- **Seoul is below freezing at night** all winter, with some snow, and January is the coldest month. The wind makes it feel colder.
- **Busan and Jeju are milder,** like a cool Delhi winter by day, but still cold at night and by the sea.
- **Winter is dry.** You rarely need an umbrella, but a light rain-proof layer helps on snowy days.
- **Ski resorts and the mountains are far colder,** with snow. Pack waterproof gloves and shoes if you're going skiing or to a winter festival.
- **Indoors is warm:** metro, cafés, shops and hotels are heated, and many Korean floors are heated too. Wear layers you can take off.
- **Check the forecast** for your dates. These are averages.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Your passport with the Korean visa, printed and on your phone
2. Your tickets and hotel bookings, with hotel addresses in Korean on your phone
3. Travel insurance papers
4. A warm, windproof jacket with a hood
5. Thermals, two sweaters or fleeces, and warm trousers
6. Gloves, a cap that covers your ears, and a scarf or neck warmer
7. Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes, easy to slip off at temples and some restaurants
8. Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription
9. A card that works abroad, and some won for street food and your T-money card
10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

## Documents and money

- **Indians need a visa** for mainland Korea, applied for in India through the Korea Visa Portal and a VFS Korea Visa Application Centre. Jeju has a visa-free rule for direct flights from abroad. See the [South Korea trip guide](../trips/south-korea-trip-from-india.html#south-korea-visa-for-indians) for the fee, the documents and the dates.
- **Declare foreign currency and won over US$10,000 in total** to Korea Customs, as our trip guide explains.
- **Cards work almost everywhere,** even in small shops and taxis. Carry some cash for street food, markets and topping up your T-money card.
- **No tipping.** It isn't expected in Korea.
- **Carry your passport at all times.** Police can ask to see it.

## What to wear in South Korea in winter

- **Base layer:** thermals under your clothes from December to February. A thin synthetic or blended set is best, because the metro and shops are warm and you'll sweat in thick cotton.
- **Middle layer:** a sweater, a fleece or a thin hoodie. Take two.
- **Outer layer:** a warm, windproof jacket with a hood, long enough to cover your hips. Long padded coats are what most people in Seoul wear in winter.
- **Cap, gloves and neck warmer or scarf** for evenings, night markets, palace visits and the Han River.
- **Warm trousers or jeans** over thermals. Leggings under dresses for women.
- **Warm socks,** and socks without holes: you take your shoes off at temples, in some restaurants and in traditional guesthouses.
- **Wear the bulky jacket on the plane** to save weight in your bag.

## Shoes for Korea

- **Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes.** You'll walk a lot, with many stairs in metro stations and on hilly streets in Seoul and Busan.
- **Easy to slip on and off.** You remove them at temples, in some restaurants and in hanok guesthouses.
- **Waterproof shoes** only if you're going to the mountains, skiing or it snows heavily. City pavements are cleared quickly.

## Skin, health and medicines

- **Lip balm and a moisturiser.** Korean winter air is very dry, and heated rooms dry your skin even more.
- **Sunglasses and sunscreen** if you go to the snow.
- **A small first-aid kit:** plasters, pain relief, cold tablets.
- **Your medicines** go in your cabin bag, in their original packs, with the prescription. See [can I carry medicines on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry/medicines.html)

## Food from home

- **Korean food uses a lot of meat, fish sauce and seafood,** so vegetarian and Jain travellers often carry ready-to-eat food, khakhra or theplas.
- **Don't pack meat, fruit, vegetables or plant products.** Korea restricts them, and banned items are taken away at the airport, says the Korea Tourism Organization (via our trip guide).
- See our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list, and the veg food tips in our [South Korea trip guide](../trips/south-korea-trip-from-india.html#food-for-indian-travellers-veg-and-jain-tips).

## Phone, plugs and power bank

- **Korea uses 220 V and sockets with two round pins** (Types C and F), says Wikipedia's mains electricity table. Slim two-pin Indian plugs often fit; three-pin Indian plugs don't. A universal adapter is the simple answer. See our [power bank and travel adapter guide](power-bank-and-travel-adapter-guide.html) or the full [travel adapter by country](travel-adapter-by-country.html) list.
- **Cold drains batteries faster.** Keep your phone and power bank in an inside pocket.
- **Power bank:** in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See [can I carry a power bank?](../baggage/can-i-carry/power-bank.html) and [power bank rules on flights worldwide](power-bank-rules-on-flights.html).
- **Maps:** Google Maps works only partly in Korea; locals use Naver Map or KakaoMap. Download one before you go.
- **Phone data:** see the eSIM and SIM options in the [South Korea trip guide](../trips/south-korea-trip-from-india.html#phone-sim-and-esim-in-south-korea).

## What to pack for women

- Two thermal sets, and leggings
- Two or three full-sleeve tops, and two sweaters or a sweater and a fleece
- A warm hooded jacket or long padded coat
- Warm trousers, jeans, or a long skirt or dress with leggings

## Which bag to take

- **About 7 days, moving between Seoul, Gyeongju and Busan:** a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack, plus a small day bag. The KTX train has luggage racks, but metro stations have many stairs.
- **Leave space for shopping.** Korean skincare and clothes are a big draw. Weigh your bag before you fly home.
- **Packing cubes** keep thermals, sweaters and socks apart, and squeeze bulky clothes smaller.

Direct flights from India to Korea go only from Delhi to Seoul, on Air India and Korean Air (see our [South Korea trip guide](../trips/south-korea-trip-from-india.html)). ## Questions

### What should I pack for South Korea in winter?

Thermals, two sweaters or fleeces, a warm windproof jacket with a hood, warm trousers, a cap that covers your ears, gloves and a scarf. Add comfortable walking shoes that slip on and off, lip balm and moisturiser for the dry air, a universal adapter, a power bank in your cabin bag, and your visa and insurance papers.

### What should I wear in Korea in December?

Layers. Seoul averages about 4 °C by day and −3 °C at night in December, so wear thermals, a sweater and a warm padded or down jacket, with a cap and gloves in the evening. Busan is milder, about 10 °C by day and 2 °C at night. Indoors is heated, so wear layers you can take off.

### How cold is Seoul in January?

Seoul averages about 2 °C by day and −6 °C at night in January, the coldest month, says the Korea Meteorological Administration. Wind makes it feel colder. A warm hooded jacket, thermals, gloves and a cap that covers your ears are the basics.

### Does it snow in South Korea in winter?

Yes, Seoul gets some snow, and the mountains and ski areas get a lot. Busan and Jeju rarely see snow. Winter is mostly dry and sunny.

### What shoes should I wear in Korea in winter?

Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes that are easy to slip off, because you'll walk a lot and take them off at temples and some restaurants. Take waterproof shoes only for skiing, the mountains or heavy snow.

### Do I need an adapter for South Korea from India?

Korea uses 220 V and round two-pin sockets (Types C and F). Slim two-pin Indian plugs often fit, but three-pin Indian plugs don't, so carry a universal adapter to be safe.

### Can I take Indian food to South Korea?

Packed, ready-to-eat snacks are common, but don't pack meat, fruit, vegetables or plant products: Korea restricts them and takes banned items away at the airport. Check our [can I carry it on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry.html) list.
