Going to Australia to study? Australia has some of the strictest border rules in the world for food, so what you pack matters more than usual. This list covers your documents, what to declare (almost all Indian food), medicines, which bags to take, and clothes for seasons that are the opposite of India's.

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Your passport, the one your Student visa is linked to
2. Your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) and proof of your Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), in your hand bag
3. Your medicines in their original packs, with a prescription or doctor's letter in English
4. Clothes for the season you arrive in (Australia's seasons are the opposite of India's)
5. A laptop and its charger, a travel adapter with a Type I plug, and a power bank in your cabin bag
6. Sunscreen, sunglasses and a cap
7. A card that works abroad
8. A filled-in Incoming Passenger Card, with every food item declared

## Documents to carry in your hand bag

Australia's Department of Home Affairs page for the Student visa (subclass 500, last updated 9 Nov 2026, read 23 Nov 2026) says you must be enrolled in a course and hold a **Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)**, and hold **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** unless you're exempt.

- **Your OSHC must start on the day you arrive,** not the day your course starts. Home Affairs says you may be refused entry if you can't prove you have health insurance when you arrive.
- **When to arrive:** Home Affairs says students generally arrive at least 1 week to 1 month before their course starts.
- **Keep your passport, CoE and OSHC papers in your hand bag,** with copies on your phone.
- **Under 18?** You also need approved welfare arrangements.

## Food: declare it, or leave it at home

The Australian Border Force (ABF) says **food brought into Australia must be declared on your Incoming Passenger Card**, even small amounts or ingredients for cooking, and biosecurity officers may inspect it (page last updated 9 Oct 2026, read 23 Nov 2026). Its list of common foods:

| What | ABF says |
|---|---|
| Biscuits, bread, cakes, chocolate, sweets, oil | Bring it |
| Spices, pepper, rice, noodles, tea, coffee, dried herbs | Declare it |
| Dairy products, meat items, nuts, eggs, honey | Declare it |
| Pickles, chutney, jam, preserved fruit and vegetables, sauces | Declare it |
| Fresh fruit | Don't bring |
| Homemade food (meals, cakes, anything you made yourself) | Don't bring |
| Food from the plane | Don't bring |

"Declare it" means you tick yes on the card and show the food to an officer, who decides whether it can come in. Some items may be taken away. Keep food in sealed shop packs with the label, all in one bag, so it's quick to show. If you're unsure, declare it.

Not sure about a specific item? The ABF points to the Department of Agriculture's import conditions system (BICON).

## Medicines

The ABF says most prescription medicines can come in under a traveller exemption, without a permit, if (page last updated 9 Oct 2026):

- **They're in your accompanied baggage** (with you on the flight)
- **You carry a letter or a copy of your prescription in English** from your doctor
- **You bring no more than three months' supply**
- **You leave them in their original packaging and declare them** when you arrive

Some substances aren't covered and need permission first, and vaping goods aren't part of this exemption. See [can I carry medicines on a flight?](../baggage/can-i-carry/medicines.html)

## Baggage allowance for students

- **Air India student fare:** Air India's student page lists **10 kg more** in Economy on routes where bags are weighed (up to 40 kg), and **2 bags of 23 kg** on routes where they're counted by piece. Which applies depends on your destination, so check when you book. International student fares are for full-time students aged 12 to 30, with a student ID, visa or acceptance letter to show if asked (read 23 Nov 2026).
- **Other airlines** that fly from India to Australia with a stop include Singapore Airlines (25 kg on Lite and Value, 30 kg on Standard and Flexi), Malaysia Airlines, Thai Airways, Cathay Pacific, Emirates and Qatar Airways. Each depends on your fare: see our [international baggage rules](../baggage/international.html), and the [excess baggage calculator](../tools/bag-weight.html) if you'll pay for more.
- **Each bag can usually be up to 158 cm in total.** See [trolley bag size for international travel](trolley-bag-size-for-international-travel.html).
- **Cabin bags are small:** 7 kg on most of these airlines, and Qatar Airways allows only 50 x 37 x 25 cm. Check yours with the [cabin bag checker](../baggage/checker.html).

## What to wear in Australia

Australia's seasons are the opposite of India's. Long-term averages from the Bureau of Meteorology, published by the WMO (as in our [Australia trip guide](../trips/australia-trip-from-india.html)):

| Month | Sydney day / night | Melbourne day / night |
|---|---|---|
| January | About 27 °C / 20 °C | About 26 °C / 16 °C |
| March | About 25 °C / 18 °C | About 24 °C / 15 °C |
| July | About 17 °C / 9 °C | About 14 °C / 7 °C |

- **Arriving for a February or March start:** summer clothes, a light jacket for cool evenings, and an umbrella.
- **Arriving for a July start:** it's winter. Melbourne is about 14 °C by day and 7 °C at night, and colder with wind. Take a warm jacket, sweaters and thermals.
- **Melbourne's weather changes fast,** so layers work best all year.
- **The sun is strong.** Take sunscreen, sunglasses and a cap. See our [sunscreen guide](travel-sunscreen-guide.html).

## What not to bring to Australia

- **Fresh fruit**
- **Homemade food,** including sweets and pickles your family made
- **Food from the plane**
- **Undeclared food of any kind.** Declare it all on your Incoming Passenger Card.
- **More than three months of medicines,** or medicines without an English prescription or letter
- **Power banks in check-in**

## Duty-free limits

From the ABF's duty-free page (last updated 17 Nov 2026):

- **Personal clothing, shoes and toiletries** you bring with you are free of duty and tax.
- **General goods** bought abroad, such as gifts, cameras, electronics and jewellery: up to AUD 900 if you're 18 or over, and AUD 450 if you're under 18.
- **Alcohol:** 2.25 litres if you're 18 or over. No alcohol or tobacco allowance for under-18s.
- **Cash from India:** see [how much cash you can carry abroad](../baggage/customs/cash-and-currency.html).

## Questions

### What should Indian students pack for Australia?

Your passport, CoE and OSHC papers in your hand bag, your medicines with an English prescription, clothes for the season you arrive in, sunscreen and sunglasses, a laptop with a Type I adapter, and a power bank in your cabin bag. Declare every food item on your Incoming Passenger Card.

### What can I not bring to Australia from India?

The Australian Border Force lists fresh fruit, homemade food and food from the plane as items not to bring. Most other foods, including spices, rice, dairy, meat items, nuts and pickles, must be declared and may be inspected.

### Can I take Indian spices to Australia?

Yes, but declare them. The ABF lists spices, pepper, rice, tea and dried herbs as items to declare on your Incoming Passenger Card. An officer checks them and decides.

### Can I bring medicines to Australia from India?

Yes, for personal use. The ABF says carry them with you, with a letter or prescription in English from your doctor, no more than three months' supply, in their original packaging, and declare them.

### Do I need health insurance before arriving in Australia?

Yes. Home Affairs says your OSHC must start on the day you arrive, and you may be refused entry if you can't prove you have health insurance.

### What is the duty-free limit in Australia?

AUD 900 of general goods for travellers aged 18 or over (AUD 450 under 18), and 2.25 litres of alcohol for adults, says the ABF.

### Do Indian plugs work in Australia?

No. Australia uses Type I plugs. The voltage is 230 V, like India, so you need only a plug adapter.
