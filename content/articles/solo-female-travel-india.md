Thousands of women travel around India on their own every year, for work, for study, for a pilgrimage or just for the joy of it. A solo trip here is very doable with a bit of planning: save the right numbers, book your first nights and your trains ahead, and know the help that Indian Railways and the police offer. This guide is practical, not scary. Every helpline and railway rule on it comes from an official Government of India page, with the date it was published.

## Quick answer

- **Save 112** on your phone. It's India's single emergency number for police, medical and fire help, and it also works through the **112 India app**.
- **Save 181,** the Women Helpline, and **139,** the Railway helpline for security or medical help on a train.
- **Book a women's quota berth** on long-distance trains, and know that Indian Railways' **Meri Saheli** teams look out for women travelling alone.
- **Book your first night's stay before you arrive,** and reach new places in daylight when you can.
- **Use app cabs or prepaid taxi counters,** and share your ride with someone you trust.
- **Coming from abroad?** Read our [visiting India guide](../trips/visiting-india.html) and your own government's travel advice too.

## Is India safe for solo female travellers?

It depends on where you go, how you travel and the time of day, as it does in most big countries. Many women travel alone in India without trouble, and many also deal with staring, unwanted attention or touts, mostly in crowded tourist spots and on public transport. We don't rank places by safety, and we don't quote crime figures without an official source. What helps most is practical: plan your arrivals, keep your phone charged, save the helplines below, and trust your instincts.

Foreign women should also read their own government's travel advice for India, for example the [UK's](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/india) or the [US's](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/india.html).

## Helplines to save before you go

| Number | What it is | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| **112** | Emergency Response Support System: police, medical and fire help. Reach it by call, SMS, e-mail, the panic button or the 112 India app. | PIB, Ministry of Home Affairs (4 Feb 2020); india.gov.in |
| **181** | Women Helpline, toll free | Ministry of Women and Child Development; india.gov.in |
| **139** | Railway helpline for security or medical help on a train, round the clock | Ministry of Railways on PIB (17 Dec 2021, 27 Nov 2024); india.gov.in |

| **1930** | Cyber crime helpline, for online fraud or harassment | india.gov.in |

The Ministry of Railways says 139 is now integrated with 112, and that you can also complain on the [Rail Madad](https://railmadad.indianrailways.gov.in/) website. Many state police forces also run their own women's safety apps and numbers: get these only from the state police's own website or app store page.

## On trains

Trains are one of the best ways to see India, and Indian Railways has several things in place for women. These come from Ministry of Railways answers in Parliament, published on PIB:

- **A women's berth quota.** Six berths in sleeper class on long-distance Mail and Express trains, and six berths in 3AC on Garib Rath, Rajdhani, Duronto and fully air-conditioned Express trains, are kept for women of any age travelling alone or in a group of women (10 Feb 2021, 17 Dec 2021).
- **Lower berths** are set aside for women aged 45 and above, senior citizens and pregnant women (same releases).
- **Meri Saheli.** The Railway Protection Force (RPF) rolled it out to all zones on 17 October 2020. It focuses on women travelling alone on long-distance trains, for the whole journey from the starting station to the destination, with teams of women RPF staff (10 Feb 2021, 27 Nov 2024).
- **Women-only coaches** on suburban and local trains (EMU, DMU and MMTS), depending on demand, and **Ladies Special** trains in big cities, escorted by women RPF staff (10 Feb 2021).
- **CCTV** in a number of coaches and stations (27 Nov 2024).

Practical tips for the journey:

- **Book early,** and choose the women's quota when you book, if there's a seat.
- **3AC and 2AC coaches** are calmer than general or sleeper coaches, and an upper or side upper berth gives you more privacy at night.
- **Lock your suitcase** to the berth with a chain, and keep your sling or handbag with you while you sleep.
- **Don't take food or drinks from strangers,** however friendly. Railways warn passengers against drugging and theft in their announcements.
- **Need help on board?** Call 139, or tell the TTE or the RPF staff on the train.

## Getting around in cities

- **Use app cabs or prepaid taxi counters** at airports and stations. Check that the car number matches the app before you get in, and share the ride with a friend.
- **Agree the fare first** if an auto-rickshaw has no meter.
- **Keep your map offline** and your phone charged: carry a power bank.
- **Sit in the back seat** of a cab, and keep your bag on the seat next to you.

## Where to stay

- **Book your first night** before you arrive, so you don't have to look for a room at night.
- **Read recent reviews from women** on the booking site, and check the room has a lock and the hotel a 24-hour front desk.
- **Hostels with women-only dorms** are easy to find in tourist cities, and a good way to meet other travellers.
- **Don't share your room number** out loud at the front desk, and keep the door locked and chained.
- **Some women carry a door stop alarm** for extra peace of mind. It's a small wedge that works on doors that open into the room.

## Tips for foreign women visiting India

- **Get your e-Visa only from the official site** and fill in the e-Arrival card before you land. See our [India e-Visa guide](../trips/india-e-visa-for-tourists.html).
- **Get an Indian SIM card** at the airport, so you can use maps, app cabs and the 112 India app. See [SIM, UPI and money for tourists](../trips/india-sim-card-upi-and-money-for-tourists.html).
- **Dress to cover shoulders and knees** in temples, old cities and small towns. A long, loose kurta or a maxi dress with a scarf works almost everywhere. See [what to pack for India](what-to-pack-for-india.html#what-to-wear-in-india-as-a-woman).
- **Keep a copy of your passport and visa** on your phone and on paper, apart from the passport.
- **Register with your embassy** if your country offers it.

## Everyday tips

- **Tell someone your plan:** where you're staying, and your train or flight numbers. Share your live location on longer journeys.
- **Arrive in daylight** when you can, above all in a new city.
- **A firm "no" and walking away** works with most touts. Go into a shop, hotel or café if someone won't leave you alone.
- **If there's a women's queue or a women's coach,** use it.
- **Trust your instincts.** If a ride, a room or a plan feels wrong, change it.
- **Keep your valuables split:** a card and some cash in your day bag, another card in your suitcase or room safe.

## Questions

### Is India safe for solo female travellers?

Many women travel alone in India without trouble, but unwanted attention and touts are common in crowded places. Plan your arrivals for daylight, book stays ahead, use app cabs or prepaid taxis, and save 112, the Women Helpline 181 and the Railway helpline 139.

### What is the women's helpline number in India?

181 is the Women Helpline, toll free, according to the Ministry of Women and Child Development. In an emergency, call 112, India's single emergency number.

### What number do I call for help on a train?

139, the Railway helpline, for security or medical help. The Ministry of Railways says it's integrated with 112, and you can also complain on the Rail Madad website.

### Is there a ladies quota in Indian trains?

Yes. Six berths in sleeper class on long-distance Mail and Express trains, and six in 3AC on Garib Rath, Rajdhani, Duronto and fully air-conditioned Express trains, are kept for women travelling alone or in a group of women.

### What is Meri Saheli?

An initiative of the Railway Protection Force, rolled out across Indian Railways on 17 October 2020. Teams of women RPF staff focus on women travelling alone on long-distance trains, from the starting station to the destination.

### Which is the safest place in India for solo female travellers?

There's no official ranking, and we don't make one. For a first solo trip, choose a well-connected place with lots of visitors and stays, like Jaipur, Udaipur, Rishikesh, Kerala or Goa, and follow the tips above.

### What should a woman pack for a solo trip in India?

A zip sling worn in front, a power bank, a scarf or stole, clothes that cover shoulders and knees, your medicines, and a lock for your suitcase on trains. Some women also carry a door stop alarm for hotel rooms.
