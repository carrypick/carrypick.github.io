Getting stopped at the airport gate because your cabin bag is too heavy or too big is stressful and can cost money. This guide explains the rules and how to pick a trolley bag that fits them. Looking at a bag sold as "20 inch"? See [what 20 inch really means](20-inch-trolley-bag-size.html).

## The cabin bag rule on most Indian domestic flights

Take IndiGo, India's biggest airline, as an example. Its official baggage page allows each economy passenger:

- **One cabin bag** up to **7 kg**, with a total size of **115 cm** (length + width + height).
- **One personal item** up to **3 kg**, such as a ladies' purse or a small laptop bag.
- **15 kg of check-in baggage** on domestic flights for standard fares.

Other airlines have similar rules, but they can differ by fare type and they change from time to time. Air India, for example, limits the cabin bag to **55 x 40 x 20 cm**, so a deep trolley can be too thick even if the total is fine. **Always check your airline's baggage page before you fly.**

## Why "55 cm cabin size" isn't the full story

Most cabin trolleys are sold as "55 cm". That's only the **height**. Airlines usually check the **total** of length + width + height, wheels and handles included.

[[diagram:measure-bag]]

Here's how three popular 55 cm bags add up, using the dimensions listed by the sellers:

- Safari Pentagon Pro 55 cm: 55 + 38 + 23 = **116 cm**
- American Tourister Liftoff 55 cm: 55.5 + 39.5 + 22 = **117 cm**
- Safari Thorium Royale 55 cm: 55 + 40 + 23 = **118 cm**

All three are a little over 115 cm on paper. Airline staff don't measure every bag, but they can. For zero risk, look for a total of **115 cm or less**, check each side too, and don't overfill expandable zips.

- [American Tourister Liftoff+ Small](../picks/B0F5HGLC5Q.html): **55 x 40 x 20 cm**, exactly Air India's limit, and only 2.2 kg.

No single hard trolley we found fits every Indian airline, because IndiGo wants it narrow (35 cm) and Air India wants it thin (20 cm). ## Weight matters as much as size

An empty hard-shell cabin trolley usually weighs **2.5–3 kg**. With a 7 kg limit, that leaves only about **4–4.5 kg** for your things.

- Choose a lighter bag if you pack heavy.
- Weigh your packed bag at home with a **luggage scale**. It's a small, cheap tool that can save you excess-baggage charges.

## Hard shell: polypropylene or polycarbonate?

- **Polypropylene (PP)** is light and good value. It's fine for most domestic trips.
- **Polycarbonate (PC)** flexes under impact instead of cracking. It's tougher, and usually costs a bit more.

See what the listings really weigh in [polycarbonate vs polypropylene](polycarbonate-vs-polypropylene-luggage.html).

## Wheels and locks

- **8 spinner wheels** (4 double wheels) roll more smoothly than 4 wheels, especially on airport floors.
- A **3-digit combination lock** is enough for domestic travel.
- A **TSA lock** lets US airport security open the bag without breaking the lock. It matters if you fly to or through the USA.

## Choosing a check-in bag

For check-in luggage, size depends on trip length:

- **Medium (about 65 cm):** about a week of clothes. Easy to keep under a 15 kg domestic limit.
- **Large (75 cm and above):** long holidays and international trips with bigger allowances. A full large bag can easily cross 15 kg, so weigh it.

## Before you fly: checklist

1. Check your airline's current cabin and check-in limits.
2. Measure your bag's total size (L + W + H).
3. Weigh the packed bag at home.
4. Keep power banks, laptops and medicines in your cabin bag. Power banks are not allowed in checked baggage.
