Seeing snow for the first time is the best part of a winter trip, and the coldest. Most snow points in India sit far above the town you sleep in, and the cold that matters is wet: snow in your gloves, your socks and your jeans. This list is for any snow trip in India, from a day at Solang or Kufri to a week in Gulmarg or Auli, with a list for kids and what you can rent on the spot.

## How cold is it at the snow?

IMD's long-term averages for the towns you'll stay in, as in our trip guides (read 25 Nov 2026). The snow points above them are colder and windier:

| Place | January, day / night | Snow point above it |
|---|---|---|
| Manali | About 10.5 °C / −1 °C | Solang, Sissu, Atal Tunnel |
| Shimla | About 9 °C / 2 °C | Kufri, Narkanda |
| Srinagar | About 7 °C / −2 °C | Gulmarg (about 0.5 °C / −8 °C), Sonamarg |
| Pahalgam | About 4 °C / −7 °C | Aru, Betaab Valley |
| Joshimath | About 11 °C / 2 °C | Auli, about 700–1,100 m higher |

- **Snow isn't certain in December** in most places. January and February are usually the snowiest months. Uttarakhand Tourism names January to March as Auli's snow season.
- **A sunny day at the snow can feel warm** for an hour, then very cold once the sun goes behind a ridge. Carry the layer you took off.
- **Check the forecast** for your dates on [IMD](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/) and in our guides: [Manali](../trips/manali.html), [Shimla](../trips/shimla-in-december.html), [Kashmir](../trips/kashmir-in-january.html), [Gulmarg](../trips/gulmarg-in-january.html) and [Auli](../trips/auli-in-december.html).

## The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

1. Two thermal sets
2. Two sweaters or fleeces
3. A warm, hooded jacket
4. Waterproof trousers, or plan to rent a snow suit
5. **Waterproof gloves,** plus a knit pair for the town
6. A woollen cap that covers your ears, and a neck warmer
7. **Woollen socks,** and a dry pair in your day bag
8. Waterproof shoes or boots with a deep, grippy sole
9. Sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm
10. A power bank and a small towel
11. A plastic bag for wet clothes on the drive back

## The layers that keep you warm in snow

1. **Thermals,** top and bottom. A synthetic set dries faster if you get sweaty playing in the snow.
2. **A sweater or fleece.** Two thin ones are better than one thick one.
3. **A warm jacket with a hood,** long enough to cover your hips when you sit in the snow.
4. **Waterproof on the outside.** Snow melts on warm clothes. Waterproof trousers, or a rented snow suit, keep the wet out. Jeans are the worst thing to wear: they soak up snow and stay cold.

## Hands, feet and head

- **Gloves get wet first.** Waterproof ski gloves keep your hands warm through snowball fights and sledge rides. Carry a spare pair for kids.
- **Woollen socks, not cotton.** Wool stays warm when damp. Pack a dry pair in your day bag for after the snow.
- **A cap that covers your ears, and a neck warmer.** Most heat goes from your head and neck when it's windy.

## Shoes for snow

- **Waterproof, with a deep sole.** Packed snow and morning ice are slippery.
- **Not mesh sports shoes.** They soak through in minutes and stay wet for the rest of the trip.
- **Half a size up** if you'll wear thick socks.
- **Rented gumboots** are fine for a few hours at the snow point. Wear your own woollen socks inside.

## What you can rent at the snow point

At Solang, Kufri, Gulmarg, Pahalgam, Auli and most snow points, shops rent snow suits or coats, gumboots and gloves by the day. Renting is fine for a few hours in the snow. Still carry your own:

- Thermals and woollen socks (you wear them under the rented clothes)
- A cap, neck warmer and your own gloves
- Sunglasses and sunscreen
- A warm jacket for the drive and the evening

Check what you're renting before you pay: zips that close, boots without holes, and gloves that are dry inside.

## Sun, skin and health

- **Sunglasses.** Snow reflects most of the sunlight, and glare can hurt your eyes after a few hours. Polarized lenses help.
- **Sunscreen and lip balm with SPF.** The mountain sun burns even when it's cold.
- **Moisturiser and cold cream.** Cold air and room heaters dry skin fast.
- **Your medicines,** plus cold, headache and motion sickness tablets for the hill roads.
- **Drink water.** You won't feel thirsty in the cold, but you still need it.

## Phone and power in the cold

- **Cold drains phone batteries fast.** Keep your phone in an inside pocket, and carry a power bank.
- **Power cuts are common after heavy snow.** A small torch helps.
- **Keep your phone in a waterproof pouch** when you play in the snow, and save maps and bookings offline.

## Your day bag for the snow point

- A spare pair of woollen socks and gloves
- The layer you took off
- Water and snacks: food at snow points costs more and runs out
- Power bank, sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm
- A small towel, and a plastic bag for wet clothes
- Some cash for rentals, sledges and ponies

## Packing for kids and toddlers in snow

- **Thermals, two sweaters and a warm hooded jacket,** and a cap that covers their ears
- **Two pairs of waterproof gloves.** Kids always get one wet in the first ten minutes
- **More socks than days,** and waterproof shoes or boots
- **Change of clothes in the day bag,** including thermals
- **Cold cream, lip balm and kids' sunscreen**
- **Snacks, a flask of something warm and a water bottle**
- **Toddlers:** keep snow time short, and watch for cold hands and feet. Small children get cold faster than adults and may not say so.

## Snow trip packing list for 4 days

- 2 thermal sets
- 2 sweaters or fleeces
- 1 warm hooded jacket
- 2 pairs of warm trousers, plus waterproof trousers or a rented snow suit
- 3 T-shirts or tops
- 6 pairs of socks, at least 3 of them woollen
- 1 cap, 1 neck warmer, 1 pair of waterproof gloves and 1 knit pair
- 1 pair of waterproof shoes, 1 pair of slippers
- Sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, moisturiser
- Power bank, charger, torch, ID, cash and your medicines

## What not to pack

- **Only jeans.** Bring warm trousers, and waterproof ones for the snow.
- **Mesh sports shoes, canvas shoes or heels.**
- **Cotton socks only.** They stay cold and wet.
- **A single huge coat.** Layers you can add and take off work better.

## Driving to the snow?

After snow, police in Himachal and Kashmir often allow only 4x4s or cars with snow chains on hill roads, such as Manali–Solang and Tangmarg–Gulmarg. Carry warm clothes, blankets, water, food, a first-aid kit, a torch and a power bank in the car. See [driving in snow](../trips/manali.html#driving-in-snow) in our Manali guide. If you're not used to mountain driving, take a local taxi from an authorised stand.

## Questions

### What should I pack for a snow trip in India?

Two thermal sets, two sweaters or fleeces, a warm hooded jacket, waterproof trousers or a rented snow suit, waterproof gloves, a woollen cap, a neck warmer, woollen socks and waterproof shoes with a good grip. Add sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, moisturiser and a power bank.

### What should I wear to play in the snow?

Thermals, a fleece and a warm jacket, with waterproof trousers or a rented snow suit on top, waterproof gloves, woollen socks and waterproof boots or rented gumboots. Avoid jeans and mesh shoes: they soak up snow and stay cold.

### Can I rent snow clothes instead of buying them?

Yes, at most snow points in India, such as Solang, Kufri, Gulmarg and Pahalgam, you can rent snow suits or coats, gumboots and gloves by the day. Still carry your own thermals, socks, cap and a warm jacket.

### What should kids wear in the snow?

The same layers as adults: thermals, a sweater, a warm hooded jacket, a cap, waterproof gloves (and a spare pair), woollen socks and waterproof boots. Pack a full change of clothes in the day bag, and keep snow time short for toddlers.

### Do I need sunglasses in the snow?

Yes. Snow reflects most of the sunlight, and the glare can hurt your eyes after a few hours. Polarized sunglasses and sunscreen are worth packing even in December.

### When is the best time to see snow in India?

Usually January and February. December snow isn't certain in Manali, Shimla or Auli, and some early winters are dry. Gulmarg and Pahalgam get the most snow. See our [where to go in December and January](../trips/where-to-go-in-december-january.html) guide.
