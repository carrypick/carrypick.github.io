Japan's voltage is **100 V** everywhere, the lowest of any major country, says the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO). The frequency is **50 Hz in the east** (Tokyo) and **60 Hz in the west** (Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka). Most sockets take **type A**, two flat pins. Indian plugs don't fit.

## Quick answer

- **Plug type:** A (two flat pins), and some B (with a round earth pin). Many sockets only take two pins.
- **Voltage:** 100 V. Frequency 50 Hz in eastern Japan, 60 Hz in western Japan.
- **Phone, laptop and camera chargers marked 100-240V:** work with a plug adapter.
- **230 V-only appliances from India:** need a step-down transformer, says JNTO. It's easier to leave them at home.
- **From the USA:** two-pin US plugs fit. Three-pin ones may not, so pack a 3-to-2 adapter.

## Questions

### Which plug is used in Japan?

Type A, two flat pins, and some type B. The voltage is 100 V.

### Do Indian plugs work in Japan?

No. Neither the 2-pin nor the 3-pin Indian plug fits. Indian chargers marked 100-240V work with an adapter.

### Do I need a converter for Japan from India?

Not for 100-240V chargers. You do for 230 V-only appliances such as many Indian hair dryers and straighteners. JNTO calls it a step-down transformer.

### Is Japan 50 Hz or 60 Hz?

Both. Eastern Japan, including Tokyo, uses 50 Hz; western Japan, including Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka, uses 60 Hz. Phone and laptop chargers don't mind.

### Does a US plug work in Japan?

A two-pin US plug fits. A three-pin US plug often doesn't, because many Japanese sockets have no earth hole.
