**The short answer:** yes, you can take sunscreen on a flight. In your cabin bag, each tube or spray must be **100 ml or less**, packed with your other liquids in **one clear, resealable 1-litre bag**. A bigger bottle goes in your check-in bag. For the label: choose **SPF 30 or higher**, "broad spectrum" or a PA rating for UVA, and pack more than you think. One full-body coat takes about 30 ml.

## Where your sunscreen can go on a flight

Both Air India and IndiGo count sunscreen as a liquid. Air India's list names creams, lotions, sprays and lip balm. IndiGo's names suntan lotions, creams and sprays.

| What you're carrying | Cabin bag | Check-in bag |
|---|---|---|
| Cream, lotion or gel, 100 ml or less | Yes, in your 1-litre liquids bag | Yes |
| Cream, lotion or gel, over 100 ml | No, even if it's half empty | Yes |
| Spray, 100 ml or less | Yes, in your 1-litre liquids bag | Yes, with the cap on |
| Spray, over 100 ml | No | IndiGo allows toiletry sprays such as perfume and deo up to 0.5 litre each and 2 litres in all. Sunscreen spray isn't named, so check with your airline |
| Lip balm with SPF | Yes. Air India counts lip balm as a liquid, so put it in the 1-litre bag | Yes |
| Stick or solid sunscreen | Not named on the lists we read. Put it in the 1-litre bag to be safe | Yes |

The rule is about the size printed on the container, not how much is left in it. Air India says a container over 100 ml "will not be accepted even if" it's partly full. The full list of what counts is on our [100 ml liquids page](../baggage/can-i-carry/liquids.html).

## What SPF and PA mean

| On the label | What it means |
|---|---|
| SPF | Protection from UVB, the rays that burn. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says SPF 15 filters about 93% of UVB and SPF 30 about 97% |
| Broad spectrum | Also protects from UVA, the rays that age skin. This is a US test |
| PA+ to PA++++ | A UVA rating used in Japan and much of Asia. It started as a Japanese cosmetics industry standard. More plus signs mean more UVA protection, and PA++++ is the highest |
| Water resistant | The AAD says these stay effective for 40 minutes in water. "Very water resistant" means 80 minutes |
| Waterproof | Doesn't exist, says the AAD. Sweat and water always wash sunscreen off |

**SPF 30 or 50?** The AAD recommends SPF 30 or higher, and no sunscreen blocks all UVB. The bigger difference on a trip is using enough and putting it on again: most sunburn happens when people use too little or don't reapply, the AAD says.

## How much sunscreen to pack

The AAD says most adults need **about 1 ounce (roughly 30 ml, a shot glass full)** for the skin clothes don't cover, and **at least 1 teaspoon (about 5 ml)** for the face, about the length of two fingers. Put it on about 15 minutes before you go out, and again every 2 hours, and after swimming or sweating.

From those amounts, here's roughly how long a tube lasts. These are our estimates. You'll need more if you're tall or out all day.

| Your day | Coats | Sunscreen a day | A 50 g face tube lasts | A 100 ml bottle lasts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| City sightseeing, face and neck | 2 | About 10 ml | About 5 days | About 10 days |
| Snow day, face only, out for 6 hours | 3 | About 15 ml | About 3 days | About 6 days |
| Beach day, full body, out for 4–6 hours | 3 | About 90 ml | Less than a day | About 1 day |

So a 50 g face tube is plenty for a city trip, but a beach holiday needs a big bottle in your check-in bag, or a plan to buy more when you get there. With only a cabin bag, take one 100 ml body sunscreen in your liquids bag.

## Sunscreen in the snow

The World Health Organization says fresh snow can reflect as much as 80% of UV rays, and UV goes up by about 10% for every 1,000 m of height. Manali is about 2,050 m up, so you can burn there on a cold, clear day. Use sunscreen on your face, and a lip balm with SPF: the AAD suggests at least SPF 30 for lips. ## Questions

### Is sunscreen allowed in a flight?

Yes. In your cabin bag, each container must be 100 ml or less and go in one clear, resealable 1-litre bag with your other liquids. Bigger bottles go in your check-in bag.

### Can I carry sunscreen in cabin baggage on IndiGo?

Yes, in a container of 100 ml or less, inside a clear 1-litre bag. IndiGo's list names suntan lotions among the liquids that must go in that bag.

### Can I carry sunscreen spray in a flight?

In the cabin, only in a can of 100 ml or less, in your 1-litre liquids bag. A bigger spray goes in check-in with its cap on. IndiGo allows toiletry sprays up to 0.5 litre each and 2 litres in all in check-in, but its list doesn't name sunscreen spray, so ask your airline if yours is large.

### Can I carry sunscreen in checked luggage?

Yes. The 100 ml limit is for cabin bags. Close the cap tightly and put the bottle in a zip bag in case it leaks.

### What do SPF and PA mean on sunscreen?

SPF measures protection from UVB, the rays that burn. PA, from PA+ to PA++++, rates protection from UVA, the rays that age skin. More plus signs mean more UVA protection.

### What does PA+++ mean?

It's a UVA protection grade that started as a Japanese cosmetics industry standard. PA+++ is the second highest grade, and PA++++ is the highest.

### SPF 30 or 50: which is better?

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends SPF 30 or higher, so both are fine. Using enough and reapplying every 2 hours matters more than the number.

### How much sunscreen should I apply on my face?

At least 1 teaspoon, about 5 ml, says the American Academy of Dermatology. That's about the length of your index and middle fingers.
