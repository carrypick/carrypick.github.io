How to choose clothing

Layers beat one thick jacket

Nights in Manali can fall below −5 °C while a sunny afternoon feels mild, so you need clothes you can add and take off. Wear three layers: a thermal next to the skin, then a sweater or fleece, then a jacket that stops the wind. Add a cap, a neck warmer and gloves, because ears and fingers get cold first.

Pick the fabric for what you'll do

  • Sightseeing and sleeping: cotton thermals are soft and fine.
  • Treks and long walks: cotton holds sweat and stays wet. Outdoor guides suggest wool or synthetic base layers instead.
  • Snow and high passes: a light padded jacket is enough for a hill town, but deep snow or a high-altitude trek needs a much warmer down or synthetic jacket. See winter jackets for travel.
  • Monsoon trips: a rain jacket or poncho that packs small, and quick-dry clothes.

Before you pack

Take a layer off before you start to sweat: wet clothes make you cold once you stop moving. For what each place is like in your month, see what to wear, and for the full list see winter clothes for hill trips.
