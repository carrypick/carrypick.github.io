Travelling with a baby: the checklist

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Your first trip with a baby is mostly about remembering the small things: the birth certificate, enough diapers for a delay, a spare top for you. This checklist covers what to pack for a baby or toddler on a flight, a train or a road trip, in India or abroad. Want it as a list you can tick? The diaper bag is your cabin bag for the baby. Keep everything for the journey in it, and pack the rest in your check-in bag.

Documents for the baby

On Indian airlines an infant is a child under 2 years on the day of travel. You need proof of the baby's age at check-in, or the airline can charge a full fare:

  • IndiGo accepts a birth certificate, the mother's hospital discharge summary, a vaccination certificate or a passport.
  • Air India asks for the birth certificate and vaccination certificate, and a passport on international flights.
  • Akasa Air accepts a digital copy of the age proof.
  • Abroad, every baby needs their own passport and, where the country asks for one, a visa.

Keep a photo of each document on your phone too. Flying soon? Read our guide to flying with a baby or toddler for each airline's rules on age, baggage, strollers and bassinets.

Feeding on the way

  • Milk and baby food for the journey can go in your cabin bag above the 100 ml liquids limit. Security may ask to check them. See our liquids rules.
  • Formula: pre-measure each feed into a container, so you only add water. Carry a flask of hot water or ask the crew: Emirates says its crew will help warm bottles.
  • Bottles: take one more than you think, and a small brush to clean them.
  • On trains, clean drinking water can be hard to find: carry your own.

Clothes and sleep

  • One set of clothes a day, plus 2 spare in the diaper bag: babies spill, and so do the people holding them.
  • A warm layer and socks for the plane or an AC train coach, even in summer.
  • A cap or sun hat for the beach and the hills. Going somewhere cold? See winter clothes for hill trips.
  • A blanket and a favourite toy help a baby sleep in a new place.
  • For older kids, check their size before you buy with our clothing size tool.

Health: ask your paediatrician

We don't give medical advice. Before you travel, ask your paediatrician what to pack in a small medicine kit, whether your baby is ready to fly, and about any vaccines for where you're going. Take the medicines they suggest, a thermometer and the doctor's phone number. Medicines can go in your cabin bag: see carrying medicines on a flight.

Getting around: a stroller or a carrier

A stroller is best in airports, malls and flat cities. A carrier is better on stairs, crowded stations, hill towns and beaches, and it keeps the baby close in queues. Many parents take both.

Questions

What should I pack for a baby on a trip?

Diapers (about 6 a day, plus a day's extra), wipes, a changing mat, milk or formula and bottles, one set of clothes a day plus 2 spare, a blanket, medicines your paediatrician suggests, the baby's age proof, and a stroller or carrier.

What should be in my baby's carry-on bag on a flight?

Everything for the journey plus one day: diapers, wipes, a changing mat, milk or formula, bottles, baby food, 2 spare sets of clothes, a blanket, medicines, a spare top for you and the baby's documents.

Can I carry baby food and milk in the cabin?

Yes. Baby food and milk for the journey are allowed above the 100 ml liquids limit, and security may ask to check them.

How many diapers should I pack?

About 6 a day for a baby, fewer for a toddler. Keep a day's extra in the diaper bag in case of a delay, and buy more where you're going if the trip is long.

Stroller or baby carrier for travel?

A stroller for airports and flat cities, a carrier for stairs, crowds, hills and beaches. A light travel stroller that folds small is easiest to take to the gate.

At what age can a baby travel?

It depends on the airline. IndiGo's infant page says from 3 days old; Air India, Akasa Air and SpiceJet say over 7 days. For whether your baby is ready, ask your paediatrician.
