Neck pillow for flights: memory foam or inflatable?

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

A neck pillow stops your head from falling sideways when you sleep sitting up. That matters most on a long flight, a night train or an overnight bus. The big choice is the fill: memory foam is comfier but takes space, and an inflatable one packs flat but feels firmer. Here's how to choose, and three pillows we picked by their listed specs.

Quick answer

  • Long flight or night train: a memory foam pillow. It keeps its shape for hours.
  • Short trips and a small cabin bag: an inflatable pillow. Let the air out and it packs flat.
  • Want to sleep on a lit flight? A set with an eye mask and ear plugs covers the light and noise too.
  • Check the packed size, not just the open size. That's what goes in your bag.

If you carry only a small backpack, the space matters more than you'd think. A foam pillow clipped to the outside of your bag is fine, but some airlines count what you carry on, so check your cabin bag rules.

How to choose a neck pillow

  • Fill. Memory foam for long sleeps, air for short trips. Microbead and cotton pillows exist too, but they flatten over time.
  • Packed size. Our Mokobara pick folds to 14.5 x 14.5 x 11 cm. An inflatable one packs much smaller.
  • A fastener at the front. A snap or clip stops the pillow from sliding off when you nod.
  • A washable cover. A pillow touches your neck for hours, on planes, trains and buses. A cover you can take off and wash keeps it fresh.
  • Extras. An eye mask and ear plugs help on night flights, when the cabin lights or a crying baby keep you awake.

How to wear a neck pillow

  • Thick part at the back if your head falls back, or at the front if your chin drops onto your chest.
  • Sit up straight with your lower back against the seat. A rolled jacket behind your lower back helps on a long flight.
  • Recline your seat a little at night, and do it slowly so the person behind you isn't surprised.
  • Take a window seat if you can: you can lean on the side of the plane.

Questions

Which neck pillow is good for a long flight?

A memory foam one, as it keeps its shape for hours. Our Mokobara and Amazon Basics picks are both memory foam with washable covers. The Amazon Basics set adds an eye mask and ear plugs.

Is an inflatable neck pillow good?

Yes, for short trips and light packing. It packs flat and you choose how firm it is. It feels less soft than foam, and our pick's cover is hand wash only.

Can I take a neck pillow on a flight?

Yes. Most people wear it or clip it to their bag. Some airlines count everything you carry on towards your cabin allowance, so check your airline's rules on our baggage rules page.

How do I wash a travel neck pillow?

Take the cover off and wash it as its label says. Our Mokobara pick's cover is machine washable, and so is the Amazon Basics one. The Travel Blue inflatable cover is hand wash only. Don't put the foam itself in the machine unless the label says you can.

Which side of a neck pillow goes in front?

The thick part goes where your head falls. Wear it at the back if your head drops back, or turn it round if your chin drops forward.

Is a neck pillow good for the train?

Yes, on a sleeper or chair car. On an overnight train with a berth you can lie down, so a neck pillow helps most in chair car and sitting seats. See our train packing list and things to carry in a train journey.
