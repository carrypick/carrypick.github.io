Malaysia packing list: what to pack and wear for a trip from India

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Malaysia is hot and humid all year, and it rains often, usually in short, heavy afternoon showers. So pack light cotton clothes, an umbrella, walking shoes that cope with rain, and one modest outfit with a scarf for mosques and temples. Malaysia uses UK-style type G plugs, so an Indian charger needs an adapter. This list tells you what to pack and why, for a trip from India.

Malaysia weather by month

Malaysia is warm every month, and the temperature changes by less than 2 °C over the year in most places, says the Malaysian Meteorological Department (MetMalaysia). Rain decides what to pack. Long-term monthly averages from MetMalaysia:

MonthKuala LumpurLangkawiPenang
November288 mm, 18 rain days193 mm, 13 days216 mm, 15 days
December246 mm, 15 days56 mm, 5 days103 mm, 9 days
January170 mm, 11 days19 mm, 2 days61 mm, 5 days
February165 mm, 12 days34 mm, 3 days97 mm, 7 days
June–JulyAbout 125 mm, 9–10 daysAbout 250–270 mm, 14–15 daysAbout 170–185 mm, 11 days
  • It's hot every day. Kuala Lumpur averages about 32–33 °C by day and 22–24 °C at night all year. Langkawi and Penang are about the same.
  • The hills are cool. Cameron Highlands averages about 21–23 °C by day and 15–16 °C at night. Genting Highlands is also much cooler than KL, so take a light jacket or sweatshirt.
  • Langkawi and Penang are driest from December to February. That's their best beach time.
  • The east coast is the opposite. Kota Bharu gets about 690 mm of rain in November and 580 mm in December, when the northeast monsoon hits. The islands there (Perhentian, Redang) are best from about March to September.
  • Kuala Lumpur gets rain all year, most in October–November and March–April. Showers are usually in the afternoon. Carry an umbrella every day.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Passport valid for at least 6 months, return ticket and hotel booking
  2. Your MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card), submitted online in the 3 days before you land
  3. Light cotton or linen clothes, one outfit for each day
  4. One modest outfit and a scarf or stole for mosques, Batu Caves and temples
  5. A light jacket for cold malls, flights and Genting or Cameron Highlands
  6. Walking shoes, plus sandals or flip-flops that can get wet
  7. A small umbrella or rain poncho
  8. Sunscreen, sunglasses, a cap and mosquito repellent
  9. A type G (UK-style) plug adapter
  10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

Documents and money

  • Visa. Indians don't need a visa for Malaysia for stays of up to 30 days until 31 December 2026, says the High Commission of Malaysia in New Delhi. Travelling in January 2027 or later? On 21 Nov 2026 no official page said the waiver will be extended, so check before you book. Our visa guide for Indian passport holders shows the rule, the source and the date we checked it.
  • What the immigration officer can ask for: a passport valid for at least 6 months, a confirmed return ticket, a confirmed hotel booking and your MDAC. Print them or keep them on your phone.
  • MDAC. All foreign visitors, Indians included, fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card online within 3 days before arrival, on the official site that ends in imi.gov.my. It's free, so ignore sites that charge a fee.
  • Passport. Keep it on you, not in your check-in bag. Carry a photocopy and a photo of it on your phone.
  • Cash. RBI lets a bank or money changer sell you up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can take up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Cards work in malls, hotels and most restaurants, but carry some ringgit for street food, markets and taxis. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Carrying more than US$10,000 in cash? Declare it to Malaysian customs on Customs Form No. 7 when you arrive.

What to wear in Malaysia

Malaysia is a Muslim-majority country, but Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi are used to tourists. In malls, cities and beach areas, shorts, T-shirts, dresses and skirts are normal. Dress a little more modestly in small towns and on the east coast.

  • Women: cotton dresses or skirts to the knee or longer, loose trousers or palazzos, kurtas and light tops. Shorts are fine in KL malls, Langkawi and Penang's beaches. A light stole is the most useful thing you can pack.
  • Men: shorts or chinos with T-shirts, polos or cotton shirts. One pair of long trousers for dinners, mosques and the flight.
  • Beaches: swimwear is fine at hotel pools and beaches in Langkawi and Penang. Cover up with a kaftan, shorts or a T-shirt when you walk into town.
  • One nicer outfit for a rooftop bar in KL, a show or a good dinner.
  • Buying clothes online for the trip? Check your size first with our clothing size converter, then the brand's own size chart.

Mosques, Batu Caves and temples

  • Cover your shoulders, arms and knees. Long trousers or a long skirt, and a top with sleeves.
  • Women: carry a scarf to cover your hair at a mosque.
  • Take your shoes off before you go in. Sandals or slip-on shoes make this easy.
  • Robes: travel guides say big mosques like the Putra Mosque in Putrajaya lend robes to visitors who aren't covered. We couldn't confirm this on an official page, so carry your own scarf and long clothes.
  • Mosques may close to visitors at prayer times, especially around Friday midday prayers. Plan visits for the morning or late afternoon, and check the mosque's hours.
  • Batu Caves: cover your shoulders and knees, go early before the heat, and carry water for the long staircase.

Rain gear

Rain in Malaysia is usually short and heavy, often in the afternoon. You don't need a raincoat, just:

  • A small folding umbrella in your day bag, every day. It also gives shade.
  • A poncho for days at Langkawi's beaches, theme parks or hill walks, when you need both hands.
  • Shoes that dry fast, or sandals with straps for wet days.
  • A waterproof phone pouch for boat trips and heavy showers.
  • A zip or plastic bag for a wet umbrella or clothes.

Sun, rain and health

  • Sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher. Put it on again every 2 hours and after swimming. Read our sunscreen guide.

Phone, plugs and power bank

  • Malaysia uses type G plugs (three rectangular pins, like the UK and Singapore) at 230 V. Indian plugs don't fit, so pack an adapter. See which travel adapter for Singapore and Malaysia.
  • Check your charger's label. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work in Malaysia with just an adapter. Most Indian appliances are 230 V too, so the voltage is close.
  • Power bank: in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See can I carry a power bank?
  • One USB-C cable for phone, laptop and power bank saves space. See which USB-C cable you need.

Which bag to take

  • About 5 nights: a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack is enough. Summer clothes are light.
  • About a week or more, or a Singapore + Malaysia trip: a medium trolley. Leave space for shopping.
  • Internal flights to Langkawi or Penang on low-cost airlines may have a smaller check-in allowance than your flight from India. Check both tickets.
  • AirAsia and Batik Air weigh your cabin bag and small item together: 7 kg in all. AirAsia's Low Fare and Batik Air's Super Saver include no check-in bag, so add one when you book.

Malaysia customs: what you can bring in

The Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM) lets travellers arriving by air bring these duty free:

GoodsDuty-free amount
Wine, spirits, beer or malt liquorUp to 1 litre in total
New clothesUp to 3 pieces
New footwear1 pair
FoodUp to RM150 in value
New personal care appliances (like a hair dryer or trimmer)1 of each
All other goodsUp to RM1,000 in value
  • No duty-free cigarettes or tobacco. JKDM leaves cigarettes, tobacco products, e-cigarettes, vapes and vape liquids out of the allowance. You pay duty on them.
  • Coming in by road or sea (for example from Singapore by bus), the "all other goods" limit is RM500. The rest is the same.
  • Over the limit? Use the Red Lane and declare. Duty is charged on the extra. The Green Lane is only for "nothing to declare". JKDM says not declaring, or a false declaration, is an offence.
  • Drugs: importing drugs is strictly prohibited, and trafficking can carry the death penalty under Malaysian law.
  • Cash over US$10,000: declare it on Customs Form No. 7.

What not to pack

  • More than 1 litre of alcohol, if you don't want to pay duty.
  • Cigarettes or vapes, if you don't want to pay duty. There's no duty-free allowance for them.
  • Any drugs, or medicines that aren't yours. Carry your own medicines in their original packs with the prescription.
  • A power bank in your check-in bag. It goes only in your cabin bag.
  • Big bottles in your cabin bag. Each liquid must be 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. Bigger bottles go in check-in.
  • Expensive jewellery you don't need on the beach.

Questions

What should I pack for a Malaysia trip from India?

Light cotton clothes, one modest outfit and a scarf for mosques, a light jacket for cold AC and the hills, walking shoes and sandals, an umbrella or poncho, sunscreen and mosquito repellent, a type G plug adapter, your medicines with the prescription and a power bank in your cabin bag. Don't forget the MDAC.

What should I wear in Malaysia as a tourist?

Light summer clothes are fine in Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi: shorts, T-shirts, dresses and skirts. Cover your shoulders and knees at mosques and temples, and women should carry a scarf for their hair. Dress more modestly in small towns.

What should women wear in Malaysia?

Cotton dresses, skirts, loose trousers, kurtas and light tops. Shorts are fine in malls and beach areas. Carry a stole or scarf for mosques, temples and cold AC.

What to wear in Malaysia in December?

Summer clothes, because Kuala Lumpur is about 31–32 °C by day. Pack an umbrella: KL averages about 246 mm of rain on 15 days in December. Langkawi and Penang are much drier from December. Take a jacket for Genting or Cameron Highlands.

What to pack for Malaysia in November?

The same summer clothes, plus good rain gear. November is Kuala Lumpur's wettest month, with about 288 mm on 18 days, and the east coast gets heavy monsoon rain. Shoes that dry fast help.

Is the Malaysia plug the same as India?

No. Malaysia uses type G plugs with three rectangular pins, like the UK and Singapore. Indian plugs need an adapter. The voltage, 230 V, is about the same as India.

Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia?

Not until 31 December 2026, for stays of up to 30 days. You need a passport valid for 6 months, a return ticket, a hotel booking and the MDAC. For trips from January 2027, check the official rule before you book.

How much alcohol and how many cigarettes can I take to Malaysia?

By air, up to 1 litre of wine, spirits or beer duty free. There's no duty-free allowance for cigarettes, tobacco or vapes. Other goods are duty free up to RM1,000.
