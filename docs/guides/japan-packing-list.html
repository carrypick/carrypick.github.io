Japan packing list: what to pack for a winter trip from India

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Japan in winter is cold but dry and sunny in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, and trains and shops are warm. That makes it a trip about layers. Three things catch travellers from India out: Japan's sockets and voltage are different from India's, some everyday medicines are restricted, and you take your shoes off in many places. This list covers what to wear, what to plug in, what to leave at home and which bag to take.

Japan weather in December and January

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from the Japan Meteorological Agency, as in our Japan trip guide (read 21 Nov 2026):

PlaceDayNight
Tokyo, DecemberAbout 12 °CAbout 4 °C
Tokyo, JanuaryAbout 10 °C, the coldest monthAbout 1 °C
Kyoto, DecemberAbout 12 °CAbout 4 °C
Osaka, DecemberAbout 12 °CAbout 5 °C
Sapporo (Hokkaido), DecemberAbout 2 °CBelow 0 °C
  • December is cold but mostly dry and sunny in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka. Tokyo gets about 58 mm of rain in December, much less than in autumn.
  • January is the coldest month. Tokyo's nights are about 1 °C.
  • Hokkaido and the mountains are far colder, with snow. If you go to Sapporo or a ski area, pack a proper snow jacket, waterproof shoes and gloves.
  • Indoors, trains and shops are heated. You'll be hot in a thick coat, so wear layers you can take off.
  • Check the forecast for your dates. These are averages.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Your passport and your Japan eVISA, printed and on your phone
  2. Your tickets and hotel bookings, with the hotel's address in Japanese on your phone
  3. Travel insurance papers (the Embassy of Japan asks visitors to have insurance)
  4. A warm jacket, thermals, a sweater, a cap and gloves
  5. Socks without holes, because you take your shoes off often
  6. Comfortable walking shoes that are easy to slip on and off
  7. A travel adapter with Type A pins, and chargers marked 100–240 V
  8. Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription, after checking Japan's rules below
  9. A card that works abroad, and some Japanese yen for small shops
  10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

Documents and money

  • Indians need a visa. It's a Japan eVISA applied for through VFS, says the Embassy of Japan in India. See the Japan trip guide for the fee, the documents and the dates.
  • Fill in Visit Japan Web before you land. It's Japan's free official site for the immigration and customs forms. Japan Customs recommends the electronic declaration.
  • Cash and means of payment above ¥1 million must be declared to Customs when you enter or leave Japan, says Japan Customs (read 21 Nov 2026).
  • Cards work in hotels, shops and many restaurants, but carry some yen for small restaurants, temples and shrines. Convenience-store ATMs often take foreign cards. Ask your bank about foreign charges before you go.
  • No tipping. It isn't expected in Japan.
  • Keep a photo of your passport and insurance papers on your phone.

What to wear in Japan in winter

  • Base layer: thermals under your clothes from December to February. Choose a thin set, because shops, trains and hotels are warm.
  • Middle layer: a sweater or fleece. Take two, so one can dry.
  • Outer layer: a warm, windproof jacket with a hood. You can put it in a locker or on your lap indoors.
  • Cap, gloves and scarf or neck warmer for early mornings and evenings, especially at shrines, temples and lights displays.
  • Socks: four or five pairs of warm, hole-free socks. You take your shoes off in temples, some restaurants and traditional inns.
  • Trousers: warm trousers or jeans over thermals.
  • Dress neatly. You'll often sit on tatami floors or in small seats, and Japan is a quiet, tidy place. Wear what you're happy to sit down in.
  • Wear the bulky jacket on the plane to save weight in your bag.

Shoes for Japan

  • Shoes that slip on and off easily. You remove them at temples, some restaurants, many hotels and traditional inns (ryokan). Laces take time and tie up the queue.
  • Comfortable and broken in. You walk a lot in big train stations and on temple paths. New shoes mean blisters.
  • Waterproof if you go to the mountains or Hokkaido, where there's snow. For cities in December, dry-weather walking shoes are fine.
  • A second pair for the hotel, plus socks you can change.

Japan's rules for medicines

Japan controls some medicines that are normal in India. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) runs a Narcotics Control Department site for travellers (read 21 Nov 2026). Its main points:

  • If your medicine contains a controlled substance, you need permission in advance before you enter Japan. The categories are narcotics, psychotropics, stimulants, stimulants' raw materials, cannabis and opium.
  • Narcotics include morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone and codeine. Codeine is in some cough medicines and painkillers.
  • Stimulants' raw materials include pseudoephedrine (found in some cold and allergy medicines) and lisdexamfetamine, used for ADHD (sold as Vyvanse or Elvanse). You need advance permission.
  • Amphetamine (the ingredient in Adderall) can't be imported into Japan, even for treatment, says MHLW.
  • Some medicines can't come in at all, even if they're prescription medicines at home.
  • Check the ingredients on the pack, or ask the doctor who prescribed it. Apply to MHLW's regional bureau well before you travel, because permission takes time. Don't post your medicines to Japan or ask someone else to carry them.
  • Other medicines: carry them in the original packs with the prescription, in your cabin bag, and bring only what you need for your trip. We couldn't read MHLW's quantity limits for ordinary medicines from an official page, so if you carry a large supply, or you're unsure, ask the MHLW regional bureau for your port of entry before you fly.

Not sure about a medicine? Read our can I carry medicines on a flight? page too, and apply on MHLW's site.

Phone, plugs and power bank

  • Japan uses 100 V and Type A plugs, with two flat pins (it also uses Type B, with a third pin). Indian chargers have round pins, so you need an adapter. See our power bank and travel adapter guide or our travel adapter for Japan page for the plug table.
  • A universal adapter with a socket for Type A pins, ideally with USB ports, is the easiest choice. Ones that are US/Japan type fit Japanese sockets.
  • Check your charger's label. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work in Japan with just an adapter.
  • Check hair dryers, straighteners and kettles. Indian ones are often 230 V only, and they won't work properly in Japan at 100 V. Leave them at home, or buy a dual-voltage one.
  • An adapter does not change voltage. It only changes the plug's shape.
  • Power bank: in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See can I carry a power bank? and power bank rules on flights worldwide.
  • Cold drains batteries faster. Keep your phone and power bank in an inside pocket.
  • Phone data: see the eSIM and SIM options in the Japan trip guide.

Which bag to take

  • About 7 days, moving between cities: a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack plus a small bag. Japanese trains and hotel rooms are compact, and station stairs are many.
  • About 10 days or more: a medium trolley, and send big bags ahead. Hotels and convenience stores offer luggage delivery (takkyubin) to your next hotel, so you can travel light on the bullet train.
  • Keep a day bag light. You'll carry your jacket and a water bottle all day.

What you can bring into Japan

Japan Customs (read 21 Nov 2026) lets an adult bring in duty-free for personal use:

  • Alcohol: 3 bottles of about 760 ml each.
  • Tobacco: 200 cigarettes, or 50 cigars, or 250 g of other tobacco. Heated tobacco has its own limits.
  • Perfume: 2 ounces (about 56 ml).
  • Other goods: up to ¥200,000 in total overseas market value. Items under ¥10,000 each aren't counted.
  • Cash and means of payment above ¥1 million must be declared.
  • Meat, vegetables and fruit: don't bring them, says the Embassy of Japan in India. Check our can I carry it on a flight? list for food.
  • Stimulant drugs, cannabis and narcotics are strictly controlled. See the medicines section above.
  • Liquids in your cabin bag: each container 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. See can I carry liquids?
  • A power bank in your check-in bag. It goes only in your cabin bag.

If you have goods above these limits, use the red channel and pay at the bank in the Customs zone. If you have nothing to declare, take the green channel.

Questions

What should I pack for Japan in winter?

Thermals, two sweaters or a fleece, a warm windproof jacket, warm trousers, a cap, gloves and four or five pairs of warm socks. Add comfortable shoes that slip on and off, a travel adapter with Type A pins, a power bank in your cabin bag, and your visa and insurance papers.

What should I wear in Japan in December?

Layers. Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka are about 12 °C by day and 4 to 5 °C at night, and mostly dry and sunny. Wear thermals, a sweater and a warm jacket, and add a cap and gloves. Shops and trains are heated, so you'll want layers you can take off.

Is Japan cold in December?

Yes, but dry. Tokyo averages about 12 °C by day and 4 °C at night in December, and about 10 °C and 1 °C in January. Sapporo is about 2 °C by day and below freezing at night, with snow.

What shoes should I wear in Japan?

Comfortable walking shoes that slip on and off easily, because you take them off at temples, some restaurants and traditional inns. Wear them in before the trip, and carry socks without holes. For snow or mountains, take waterproof shoes.

Do I need an adapter for Japan from India?

Yes. Japan uses 100 V and Type A plugs with two flat pins, and sometimes Type B. Indian chargers have round pins. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work with just an adapter, but 230 V-only hair dryers and kettles won't work properly.

Can I take my medicines to Japan?

Carry them in your cabin bag, in their original packs, with the prescription, and bring only what you need. Some need advance permission from Japan's health ministry: medicines with codeine, pseudoephedrine (some cold and allergy medicines) or lisdexamfetamine. Amphetamine can't be imported at all.

Can I carry cold medicine to Japan?

Check the ingredients. Cold and allergy medicines with pseudoephedrine count as stimulants' raw materials in Japan and need permission in advance. Medicines with codeine are narcotics. Ask your doctor or the MHLW regional bureau before you fly.

How much cash can I carry to Japan?

You can carry any amount, but cash or other means of payment above ¥1 million must be declared to Japan Customs when you enter or leave. Carry a card too, and some yen for small shops, temples and shrines.

How much alcohol and tobacco can I bring into Japan?

Duty free, an adult can bring 3 bottles of alcohol (about 760 ml each), 200 cigarettes, and 2 ounces of perfume, plus other goods worth up to ¥200,000 in total. It's for your own use, not for sale.

What cabin bag can I take to Japan?

It depends on your airline and fare. Singapore Airlines, Thai Airways and Cathay Pacific each allow 7 kg in the cabin in Economy. For Air India, ANA and JAL, check the baggage shown for your fare before you pay.
