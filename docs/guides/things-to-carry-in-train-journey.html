Things to carry in a train journey: a packing list for Indian trains

Updated 24 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

A long train journey in India is easy if a few things are within reach: your ticket and ID, water and food, a way to lock your bag, something warm for the AC coach and a charged phone. This list tells you what to carry and why, for a day train or an overnight one, with the Indian Railways luggage rules checked on official pages.

Want to tick things off as you pack? Use our train journey checklist. Need the full luggage rules, with sizes and charges? Read Indian Railways luggage rules. Travelling with kids or a baby, or need a list for your exact dates? Make one with the packing list maker.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Your e-ticket or ticket SMS, and the photo ID you booked with
  2. A water bottle, and dry snacks or a home-cooked meal
  3. A chain or cable lock to tie your bag to the berth
  4. A small sling bag for your phone, wallet and ID, kept on you
  5. A neck pillow, an eye mask and earplugs
  6. A shawl, sweater or light jacket for the AC coach
  7. A bedsheet and light blanket if you travel in Sleeper or Second Class
  8. A power bank and a charging cable
  9. Wet wipes, tissues, hand sanitiser and a small toiletry kit
  10. Your regular medicines, and something for an upset stomach

Documents and money

  • Your ticket. An e-ticket on your phone, or the ticket SMS, is enough on most reserved journeys. Keep a screenshot in case the network drops.
  • Photo ID. Carry the original ID you entered while booking, such as Aadhaar, a driving licence, a voter ID or a passport. The ticket checker can ask for it.
  • Some cash, plus UPI. Station stalls and vendors on the train usually take UPI, but the signal drops in hills and forests.
  • Your PNR saved somewhere. You'll need it to check the train's running status, order food or make a complaint.

Keep your bag safe

  • A chain or cable lock. Thread it through your bag's handle and around the berth or seat frame below you, then lock it. Most coaches have a ring or bar under the lower berth you can use.
  • Valuables in a small bag on you. Keep your phone, wallet, ID and charger in a sling bag that zips shut. Sleep with it, not on the floor.
  • Small locks on the bag zips. A number lock stops quick zip theft at stations.
  • Hard to steal, easy to find. A bright luggage tag or ribbon helps you spot your bag at once.

Food and water

  • A refillable water bottle. Fill it before you board. Stations have drinking water taps, and packaged water is sold on most trains.
  • Dry snacks. Biscuits, namkeen, dry fruits, khakhra, thepla and fruit travel well.
  • A home-cooked meal for the first day. Pack dry food such as parathas, puri-sabzi or lemon rice in a steel or leakproof box. Avoid curries that spill or spoil.
  • Order on the way. On many trains you can order a meal to your seat through IRCTC's e-catering with your PNR, or buy from the pantry car if the train has one.
  • A small cloth or newspaper, spoons, and a bag for your rubbish.

Sleep kit for an overnight train

  • A neck pillow. Useful on a side berth or in a chair car, where you sit up.
  • An eye mask and earplugs. Coach lights and announcements go on at night.
  • Bedding. AC coaches usually come with a bedroll of sheets, a pillow and a blanket. If you travel in Sleeper or Second Class, carry your own bedsheet, a small pillow or air pillow and a light blanket or shawl.
  • Slippers. Easier than shoes on the ladder and in the washroom.

Clothes: what to wear on a train journey

  • Loose, comfortable clothes. Cotton trousers or a track pant with a T-shirt or kurta, or a salwar kameez. You'll sit, sleep and climb in them.
  • A warm layer for the AC coach. AC coaches can be cold at night, even in summer. A shawl, hoodie or light jacket is enough.
  • Socks. For cold feet at night and the berth ladder.
  • One change of clothes and fresh innerwear for a journey longer than a day.
  • Slip-on shoes or sandals, plus slippers for inside the coach.
  • In winter in north India, carry a proper jacket, a cap and thermals. Sleeper coaches get very cold at night. See winter clothes for hill trips.

Toiletries and health

  • Wet wipes, tissues and hand sanitiser. You'll use them more than anything else.
  • A small toiletry kit. Toothbrush, toothpaste, a small soap or face wash, a comb and a small towel. A hanging toiletry bag can hook on the washroom door.
  • Toilet paper or tissue packs.
  • Your regular medicines, with the prescription, plus something for acidity, an upset stomach and motion sickness, a pain reliever, band-aids and ORS.
  • Mosquito repellent for Sleeper coaches and stations at night.
  • A mask, if you're sensitive to dust.

Phone and power

  • A power bank. Charging points in coaches are few, and some don't work. A 10,000 mAh power bank charges most phones twice.
  • A charging cable long enough to reach from the socket to your berth.
  • Earphones, so you don't disturb others with videos or calls.
  • Download films, music and maps before you leave. The signal drops on long stretches.
  • The official apps. Indian Railways' RailOne app and the IRCTC site show your PNR and running status.

Which bag to take on a train

  • For most trips, one trolley or backpack per person, plus a small bag. It must fit under the lower berth or on the luggage rack.
  • AC 3-tier and AC Chair Car have a smaller size limit. Indian Railways says suitcases in these coaches can be at most 55 x 45 x 22.5 cm. In other classes the limit is 100 x 60 x 25 cm. Bigger luggage must be booked in the brake van.
  • Soft bags squeeze into tight spaces better than hard shells. A backpack is easiest on crowded platforms and stairs.

How much luggage you can carry on Indian trains

Indian Railways' luggage rules, checked on 24 Nov 2026:

ClassFree luggageMost you can carry in the coach
AC First Class70 kg150 kg
AC 2-tier / First Class50 kg100 kg
AC 3-tier / AC Chair Car40 kg40 kg
Sleeper40 kg80 kg
Second Class35 kg70 kg
  • Above the free allowance, book the extra weight at the station's luggage office at least 30 minutes before departure. It costs 1.5 times the luggage rate (minimum ₹30).
  • Unbooked extra luggage found on the train is charged at six times the luggage rate (minimum ₹50).
  • Children aged 5 to 11 get half the adult free allowance, up to 50 kg.

All the details, with sizes, pets and two-wheelers, are on our train luggage rules page.

What you can't carry on a train

Indian Railways doesn't allow these as luggage:

  • Explosive, dangerous or inflammable things, such as firecrackers, kerosene, petrol and gas
  • Gas cylinders, even empty ones
  • Acids and other corrosive substances
  • Offensive articles that could harm or disturb other passengers
  • Dead poultry and game
  • Goods for trade: book them as parcels, not personal luggage

An oxygen cylinder with its stand is allowed in all classes for a patient with a medical certificate.

Train travel essentials for women

  • A shawl or stole: it works as a blanket, a cover and a warm layer
  • A sling bag that zips shut, worn across the body
  • Sanitary pads or a menstrual cup, and a small pouch for used ones
  • Wet wipes, a small mirror and a hair tie
  • A safety pin and a small torch

Keep the 139 helpline saved. If you feel unsafe on the train, call 139 for security help.

Train travel essentials for kids and babies

  • More snacks than you think, and a sipper bottle
  • A change of clothes for each day, plus one extra
  • Books, colouring and a downloaded film with kids' headphones
  • For babies: diapers, wipes, a changing mat, formula or food, and a feeding bottle
  • A small blanket and a cap, because AC coaches are cold

Help on the train: helpline 139

Indian Railways' RailMadad helpline is 139, for security and medical help during the journey. You can also report problems with the coach, cleaning, food or staff on the RailMadad website with your PNR.

Questions

What should I carry on a long train journey?

Your ticket and photo ID, a water bottle, dry snacks or a home-cooked meal, a chain lock for your bag, a neck pillow and eye mask, a warm layer for the AC coach, a power bank, wet wipes and sanitiser, a small toiletry kit and your medicines. In Sleeper or Second Class, add a bedsheet and a light blanket.

What food should I carry on a train journey?

Dry food that doesn't spill or spoil: parathas, thepla, puri-sabzi, lemon rice, sandwiches for the first day, and biscuits, namkeen, dry fruits and fruit after that. On many trains you can also order meals to your seat through IRCTC's e-catering with your PNR.

What should I wear on a train journey?

Loose cotton clothes you can sit and sleep in, such as a track pant and T-shirt, a kurta or a salwar kameez, with socks and slip-on shoes. Carry a shawl, hoodie or light jacket, because AC coaches get cold at night.

How much luggage can I carry on an Indian train for free?

70 kg in AC First Class, 50 kg in AC 2-tier and First Class, 40 kg in AC 3-tier, AC Chair Car and Sleeper, and 35 kg in Second Class, according to Indian Railways. Book anything more at the luggage office.

Can I take a big suitcase in an AC 3-tier coach?

Only up to 55 x 45 x 22.5 cm. Bigger suitcases must be booked in the brake van. In other classes, the limit is 100 x 60 x 25 cm.

Do I need to carry a bedsheet on the train?

AC coaches usually come with a bedroll. In Sleeper and Second Class, carry your own bedsheet, a small pillow and a light blanket or shawl.

How do I keep my luggage safe on a train?

Lock your bag to the berth frame with a chain or cable lock, put small locks on the zips and keep your phone, wallet and ID in a sling bag on your body. If something goes wrong, call the 139 helpline.

Can I carry a gas cylinder or firecrackers on a train?

No. Explosive, inflammable and dangerous goods, including gas cylinders, even empty ones, are not allowed as luggage.
