Power bank rules on flights worldwide: limits, how many and in-flight use

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 9 min read

Almost every airline in the world lets you carry a power bank in your cabin bag, up to 100 Wh. The part that is changing fast is how many you can take and whether you can use it in flight. Since late 2025, Emirates, Singapore Airlines, Etihad and the Lufthansa Group have banned using one on board, and most big airlines now say two at most. This guide puts the rules side by side, each read on the regulator's or airline's own page.

The rules in short

  • Cabin bag only. Every regulator and airline below keeps power banks out of checked bags. If your cabin bag is checked at the gate, take the power bank out first.
  • 100 Wh or less is the safe size for every airline in the table. Between 100 and 160 Wh needs the airline's approval on most airlines, and some (Emirates, Qatar Airways, Etihad, American, Delta, BA) don't take it at all.
  • Two power banks is the most common limit. Emirates and Etihad allow only one.
  • Using one in flight is banned on Emirates, Singapore Airlines, Etihad, the Lufthansa Group and all Indian airlines. Qatar Airways, American, Delta and BA still allow it with conditions. Charging the power bank itself from the seat is banned almost everywhere.
  • Never in the overhead bin. Keep it in the seat pocket, under the seat or on you, so the crew can reach it fast.

How to read it: "Banned" means you can carry the power bank but not switch it on to charge anything, and not plug it in. Sometimes a regulator says "should not" and the airline says "must not": follow the airline, because its rule is the one the crew enforces.

How to turn mAh into Wh

Airlines count watt-hours (Wh), but power banks are sold by mAh. The sum is:

Wh = mAh x V ÷ 1,000

The voltage (V) is the cell voltage, usually 3.6 to 3.7 V, and it is often printed on the label. We use 3.7 V.

Where to keep it on the plane

Put it in your seat pocket, in a bag under the seat in front, or in your pocket. Not in the overhead bin: Emirates, Singapore Airlines, Lufthansa, BA, American and Delta all say so, and so does DGCA. Etihad advises avoiding it. Keep it in its box, a pouch or a plastic bag so the ends can't touch metal (FAA PackSafe asks for this too). Don't take a swollen, cracked or very hot one on any flight.

Questions

Can I bring a power bank on a plane?

Yes, in your cabin bag. The FAA, EASA and every airline in the table above keep power banks out of checked bags. Up to 100 Wh is allowed everywhere in the table.

What is the power bank limit on a flight?

100 Wh per power bank on the airlines we checked. The FAA allows 101 to 160 Wh with airline approval (up to two), and Singapore Airlines and the Lufthansa Group accept that range with approval, but Emirates, Etihad, Qatar Airways, American, Delta and BA say 100 Wh at most.

Can I take a 20,000 mAh power bank on a plane?

Yes. 20,000 mAh is about 74 Wh at 3.7 V, which is under the 100 Wh limit on every airline in the table. A 27,000 mAh power bank is right at the limit, so check the label.

How many power banks can I take on a plane?

Two on most airlines in the table (Singapore Airlines, Qatar Airways, Lufthansa Group, BA, American, Delta, and Air India and Akasa Air). Emirates and Etihad allow only one. The FAA itself sets no number for ordinary power banks, but says airlines may be stricter.

Can I use my power bank during the flight?

Not on Emirates, Singapore Airlines, Etihad, the Lufthansa Group or Indian airlines. Qatar Airways, American, Delta and BA still allow it with conditions: for example American wants it in sight and within reach, and Delta says not during taxi, take-off and landing. Charge your phone before you fly if you aren't sure.

Can I charge my power bank from the seat socket or USB port?

No, on every airline in the table that mentions it. Emirates, Singapore Airlines, Etihad, Lufthansa, Qatar Airways, BA, American and Delta all say so, and EASA and DGCA advise or require the same.

Can I put a power bank in checked luggage?

No. Even switched off, power banks must go in the cabin. If your cabin bag is checked at the gate, take the power bank out and keep it with you (FAA, BA and United say this).

Does the 100 Wh limit apply to my 10,000 mAh power bank?

Yes, but it is far under: 10,000 mAh is about 37 Wh. Only power banks above roughly 27,000 mAh get close to the limit.

Which airlines allow 100 to 160 Wh power banks?

Only with approval, and only on some: the FAA allows it with airline approval, and Singapore Airlines and the Lufthansa Group say they need prior approval. Qatar Airways, Emirates, Etihad, American and Delta list 100 Wh as the top. Ask the airline well before you travel.

What we couldn't confirm

  • EASA gives no Wh figures for power banks on its passenger page, so the 100 Wh limit in Europe comes from the airlines (Lufthansa Group says its rule matches EASA, FAA, IATA and ICAO advice).
  • United's page lists up to two spare lithium batteries of 160 Wh each and doesn't set a separate power bank number or in-flight rule. Check with United if yours is over 100 Wh.
  • Qatar Airways and Etihad show slightly different wording on some country versions of their sites. We used the international English pages.
