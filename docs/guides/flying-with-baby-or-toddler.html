Flying with a baby or toddler: airline rules and tips

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 10 min read

Flying with a baby is easier when you know the airline's rules before you reach the counter: how old the baby must be, what the infant ticket includes, whether the stroller goes to the gate, and which document proves the baby's age. Here are the rules on IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet, Air India Express and Emirates, read on each airline's own pages, and what to pack in the cabin bag.

Quick answer

  • An infant is a baby under 2 years on the day of travel. The baby flies on your lap, with no seat of its own, and each adult can take only one infant.
  • The minimum age is 3 days on IndiGo and over 7 days on Air India, Akasa Air and SpiceJet.
  • Carry the baby's age proof: a birth certificate is accepted by every airline here. Without it, the airline can charge a full fare.
  • The infant ticket is a fixed fee per flight on Indian airlines, not a seat. The airline shows it when you add the infant to the booking.
  • Strollers: IndiGo and Akasa carry one stroller or pram per infant free. SpiceJet doesn't allow push chairs in the cabin, and Air India says strollers aren't allowed on the flight, so expect to hand it over.
  • Your child is 2 or older? They need their own seat and fare, and get the normal baggage allowance.

Our baggage pages for each airline have the adult allowances too. Flying abroad on IndiGo or Air India? See IndiGo international and Air India international, and all airlines in our international baggage rules.

IndiGo

  • Age: IndiGo's infant page says children above 3 days and under 2 years on the date of travel can fly as infants.
  • Age proof at check-in: a birth certificate, the mother's hospital discharge summary, a vaccination certificate or a passport. Without one, IndiGo charges the full fare and the baby flies only if a seat is free.
  • Seat: no extra seat can be booked; the infant sits on an adult's lap. Up to 12 infants fly on an A320 and 6 on an ATR, and only one per adult, so book early.
  • Baggage: one hand bag up to 7 kg and 115 cm (L + W + H), domestic and international. No check-in allowance. One stroller or baby pram per infant goes free.
  • Fee: a fixed infant fee per flight, cheaper when you add it while booking than at the airport.

Air India

  • Age: over 7 days and under 2 years. A younger baby can fly only for medical reasons, with a paediatrician's certificate and clearance from Air India's medical department.
  • Documents: the birth certificate and vaccination certificate, and a passport on international flights. If someone other than a parent travels with the baby, Air India lists a signed consent letter from the parents and copies of their IDs.
  • Seat: the baby sits on your lap. One infant per adult, booked in the same cabin and fare as that adult. Child seat extenders aren't allowed.
  • Baggage: 10 kg of check-in baggage for the baby in Economy, 12 kg in Premium Economy and 15 kg in Business and First. On board you can carry food for the baby, feeding bottles and a small tote for them.
  • Bassinet: free, subject to availability. Ask when you book, at check-in, or through Air India's support portal.
  • Stroller: Air India's infant page says strollers aren't allowed on the flight.

Akasa Air

  • Age: babies up to 7 days old can't fly. The baby must travel with an adult.
  • Age proof: a digital copy of the document is accepted. Without proof, the full fare applies.
  • Seat: on an adult's lap, no extra seat. Up to 10 infants per flight and one per adult.
  • Baggage: an extra 7 kg hand bag (115 cm) for the infant, and no check-in allowance. One stroller or pram per infant goes free. You may also carry baby food or formula, feeding bottles and a small tote for them.

SpiceJet

  • Age: above 7 days and below 2 years on the date of travel.
  • Booking: tell SpiceJet about the infant when you book. A fee per flight applies, the baby travels on your lap, and only one infant per adult is allowed. Infant places are limited.
  • Cabin: SpiceJet's FAQ says baby or car seats and push chairs aren't allowed in the aircraft cabin.
  • Documents: carry the baby's passport or birth certificate for the age check, on domestic and international flights.

Emirates

  • Seat: an infant under 2 flies on your lap with a special seat belt, or in a bassinet. A child of 2 or older can sit in their own car seat or next to an adult.
  • Bassinet: for babies up to 2 years. Ask for one when you book online or later through Manage a Booking. Bassinets are limited and have set sizes, and you can't ask for one on a normal seat booked at a child fare.
  • Baggage: the infant ticket includes up to 10 kg of checked baggage, a 5 kg handbag for baby things and a fully folding stroller or carrycot.
  • On board: changing tables in the bathrooms, an infant kit, baby meals and formula on request, and the crew helps warm food and bottles.

At the airport

  • Arrive early. Baby counters, a stroller tag and the age check all take time. Akasa doesn't let adults travelling with infants use its self baggage drop.
  • DigiYatra: the DigiYatra Foundation's own FAQ asks "why are passengers with infants not allowed to use DigiYatra?", so plan to use the regular entry gate, with your ID and the baby's age proof ready.
  • Security: baby food and milk for the journey can go above 100 ml in your cabin bag; security may check them. See our liquids rules and baby food in our can I carry search.
  • Stroller at the gate: ask at check-in whether your stroller goes to the aircraft door or into the hold, and where you collect it.

On the flight

  • Ears: many parents feed the baby or give a bottle during take-off and landing. For any worry about ears, a cold or whether your baby is ready to fly, ask your paediatrician. We don't give medical advice.
  • Sleep: book a night flight if you can, and ask about a bassinet when you book (Air India and Emirates offer them).
  • Diapers: change the baby just before boarding. Most planes have a small changing table in one toilet; a mat of your own helps.
  • Toddlers: a new toy or book, snacks and headphones for a tablet or the seat screen.

Questions

What is the age limit for an infant on a flight?

Under 2 years on the day of travel. The minimum age is 3 days on IndiGo, and over 7 days on Air India, Akasa Air and SpiceJet.

Do I need a ticket for my baby?

Yes. On Indian airlines an infant pays a fixed infant fee per flight and sits on your lap. From age 2, the child needs a normal ticket and their own seat.

What is IndiGo's infant baggage allowance?

One hand bag up to 7 kg and 115 cm, and no check-in bag, on domestic and international flights. One stroller or baby pram per infant goes free.

Is a stroller allowed in the flight?

Not in the cabin on most airlines. IndiGo and Akasa carry one stroller or pram per infant free, SpiceJet says push chairs aren't allowed in the cabin, and Air India says strollers aren't allowed on the flight. Ask at check-in whether you can use it up to the aircraft door.

What documents does an infant need to fly in India?

Proof of age, such as a birth certificate. IndiGo also accepts the mother's hospital discharge summary, a vaccination certificate or a passport. On international flights the baby needs a passport.

Can I get a bassinet for my baby?

Air India offers free bassinets if available; ask when you book, at check-in or through its support portal. Emirates has bassinets for babies up to 2, requested when you book or through Manage a Booking.
