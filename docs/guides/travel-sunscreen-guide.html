Sunscreen and skincare for trips: what to pack and what fits your cabin bag

Updated 4 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Sunburn can spoil a beach holiday, and so can having your full-size bottle taken away at airport security. This guide covers the airline rule, how to read a sunscreen label, and how much to pack.

The 100 ml rule for your cabin bag

Sunscreen, lotion, face wash and gel all count as liquids:

  • Each container must be 100 ml or less. A half-empty 200 ml bottle still isn't allowed, because the rule is about the size of the container.
  • All of them go in one clear, resealable bag of up to 1 litre, and you show it separately at security.
  • Anything bigger goes in your check-in bag.

See the full list on our can I carry it on a flight? page.

What SPF and PA mean

  • SPF is mainly about UVB, the rays that burn. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends SPF 30 or higher.
  • Broad spectrum means the sunscreen also protects against UVA.
  • PA is a rating for UVA protection, from PA+ to PA++++. More plus signs mean more protection.
  • "Waterproof" sunscreen doesn't exist, says the Academy. Water-resistant ones are tested for 40 or 80 minutes in water.

How much to pack

This is where most people go wrong. The American Academy of Dermatology says most adults need about 1 ounce (roughly 30 ml, a shot glass full) to cover the skin that clothes don't cover. Put it on about 15 minutes before you go out, and put it on again every 2 hours, and after swimming or sweating.

So a 50 g tube gives you less than two full-body coats. It's fine for your face, but not for a beach day.

  • Face only, short trip: a 50 g tube is plenty.
  • Beach trip with only a cabin bag: take a 100 ml body sunscreen, and buy more when you get there if you need it.
  • Long beach trip: put a big bottle in your check-in bag.

Don't forget sunscreen in the snow

The World Health Organization says fresh snow can reflect up to 80% of UV rays, and UV goes up by about 10% for every 1,000 m of height. So you can burn in Manali even on a cold day. Use sunscreen on your face, and a lip balm with SPF: the Academy suggests at least SPF 30 for lips.
