Kedarnath packing list: what to wear and carry for the trek

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 10 min read

Kedarnath is about 3,580 m high, at the end of a walk of about 16–17 km from Gaurikund. You'll walk up in sun, rain or both, sleep one cold night near the temple and walk down the next day. So you pack light: rain gear, warm layers, good shoes, a stick, your medicines and your yatra papers, all in one backpack you can carry for six to eight hours. This list works for the rest of the Char Dham Yatra too: Yamunotri, Gangotri and Badrinath.

Kedarnath weather: how cold is it?

There's no long-term IMD table for Kedarnath, so here is what the official and news reports say:

  • Nights are cold even in summer. IMD recorded a low of 4.7 °C at Kedarnath on 6 June 2026, as its daily bulletin reported.
  • Snow is likely by late October. Kedarnath had snow on 29 and 30 October 2026, news reports said.
  • It rains often from July to September, and the yatra is stopped on heavy-rain days. Afternoon showers can come in any month.
  • Above 3,000 m it's very cold and the air is thin, Uttarakhand's health advisory warns.
  • Check the forecast on IMD and the district updates in the days before you go.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. A rain jacket or poncho, and a rain cover or plastic bags for your backpack
  2. A thermal top and bottom
  3. A sweater or fleece and a warm jacket
  4. Quick-dry trousers or track pants (not jeans) and full-sleeved T-shirts
  5. A woollen cap, gloves and two or three pairs of warm socks
  6. Waterproof walking or trekking shoes with a good grip, already worn in
  7. A walking stick or trekking pole
  8. A power bank and a small torch
  9. Your regular medicines, prescriptions and a small first-aid pouch
  10. Your yatra registration letter (printed and on your phone), photo ID and some cash
  11. Sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm
  12. A water bottle and some dry snacks

What to wear for the Kedarnath trek

Dress in layers. You'll be warm while walking uphill and cold the moment you stop, and colder still at night.

  1. Next to your skin: a thermal top, or a quick-dry full-sleeved T-shirt on warm days.
  2. Middle: a sweater or fleece you can take off and tie to your bag.
  3. Outside: a rain jacket or poncho in your bag all day, and a warm jacket for the evening and the early morning darshan.
  4. Legs: track pants or trekking trousers that dry fast. Jeans get heavy and cold when wet.
  5. Head and hands: a cap that covers your ears, and gloves.

Take a layer off before you start to sweat, and put it back on as soon as you stop.

What to wear in Kedarnath in May and June

Mornings and nights near the temple can be close to freezing, and afternoons on the trail can be warm and sunny. Carry a thermal, a fleece, a warm jacket and a rain layer. Sunscreen and sunglasses matter: the sun is strong at this height.

What to wear in Kedarnath in October

Colder. Plan for nights below freezing and snow in the last weeks before the temple closes. Wear thermals under everything, your warmest jacket, a cap, gloves and two pairs of socks at night.

Which shoes to wear in Kedarnath

  • Waterproof walking or trekking shoes with a deep grip. The path is steep, and wet and muddy after rain, with ponies using it too.
  • Wear them in before the trip. New shoes on a 16 km climb mean blisters.
  • Take two or three pairs of socks and change into dry ones at the top.
  • Slip-on sandals or flip-flops for the hotel and the temple, where your shoes come off. Keep them in a plastic bag in your backpack.
  • Don't trek in flip-flops or slippers.

A walking stick or trekking pole

A stick helps most on the way down, when your knees take the strain on the steep path. A folding pole goes in your backpack when you don't need it and adjusts to your height. Set it so your elbow is bent at a right angle on flat ground.

Power, light and your phone

  • A power bank. Sockets are few on the trail and at the top, and the cold drains phone batteries fast. Keep the phone in an inner pocket.
  • A small torch. The walk down often starts before dawn, and power cuts happen.
  • A waterproof pouch for your phone, cash and registration letter in the rain.
  • Phones and cameras are not allowed inside the temple, news reports say. Leave them in your bag or at your stay.

Medicines and health

From Uttarakhand's health advisory for the yatra (as reported in April 2026) and AIIMS Rishikesh's medical advisory:

  • Enough of your regular medicines, with spares, your prescriptions and your doctor's phone number. Older pilgrims and anyone with heart or lung problems should get a medical check-up well before the trip.
  • Spare inhalers, insulin and syringes if you use them; a glucometer if you're diabetic and a BP machine if you have high blood pressure.
  • Ask your doctor about altitude-sickness medicine before you go. Don't take any without advice.
  • A small first-aid pouch: plasters for blisters, a crepe bandage, and the pain, stomach and travel-sickness tablets your doctor suggests.
  • Eye drops and saline nose drops for the dry air, SPF 50 sunscreen and UV sunglasses.
  • Drink at least 2 litres a day, and carry dry snacks: don't walk on an empty stomach.
  • Headache, dizziness, breathlessness, chest tightness or vomiting are warning signs. Stop, go to the nearest medical post or call 104 or 108, and come down if you get worse.

Documents to carry

  • Your yatra registration letter with its QR code, printed and saved on your phone. It's checked at Sonprayag and at the temple. Registration is free on the official portal, registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in.
  • The photo ID you registered with, and a copy. If you've booked a helicopter, the ID must match your booking.
  • Your helicopter ticket, if any, from IRCTC's official site only.
  • Hotel bookings and some cash. Card and UPI payments may not work where the phone signal is weak.
  • Your medicines list and an emergency contact on a card in your pocket.

Keep them together in a zip pouch or a plastic folder in the top of your backpack.

Which bag to take

  • One backpack of about 30 to 45 litres for the trek, with a rain cover. Pack only one night's things and leave your big bag at your hotel in Guptkashi or Sonprayag.
  • Line it with a plastic bag so your warm clothes stay dry even if the rain cover slips.
  • Hiring a pony or a porter? Still carry your rain gear, a warm layer, water, medicines and documents yourself, in case you get separated.

What to pack for older pilgrims

  • Everything on the short list, with an extra warm layer and an extra pair of socks
  • All medicines, in their strips with the prescription, and a list of them
  • A walking stick, even if they're riding a pony or using a palki: the walk inside the temple area is on wet stone
  • A small bag they can carry themselves, with water, a snack, a warm layer and their ID
  • Slip-on shoes or sandals for the temple

Kedarnath packing list for 2 days (one night at the top)

  • 1 rain jacket or poncho, and a backpack rain cover
  • 1 thermal set
  • 1 sweater or fleece, 1 warm jacket
  • 1 pair of quick-dry trousers, 2 full-sleeved T-shirts
  • 1 woollen cap, 1 pair of gloves, 3 pairs of socks
  • 1 pair of waterproof walking shoes, 1 pair of sandals or flip-flops
  • A walking stick or pole
  • Power bank, torch, waterproof phone pouch
  • Medicines, first-aid pouch, sunscreen, lip balm, sunglasses
  • Water bottle and dry snacks
  • Registration letter, ID, cash and bookings

Doing the full Char Dham Yatra? Pack for about 5 days and wash clothes on the way. Add a set of clothes for the road days and a towel. Yamunotri has a walk of about 6 km, so the same shoes, stick and rain gear work there.

Questions

What should I pack for Kedarnath?

A rain jacket or poncho, a thermal set, a fleece and a warm jacket, a cap and gloves, warm socks, waterproof walking shoes, a walking stick, a power bank and torch, your medicines, sunscreen and sunglasses, and your yatra registration letter and photo ID.

What should I wear in Kedarnath in May?

Layers: a thermal or full-sleeved T-shirt, a fleece and a warm jacket for nights and early mornings, quick-dry trousers, and a rain jacket or poncho. Nights near the temple can be close to freezing even in May and June.

What should I wear in Kedarnath in October?

Your warmest clothes: thermals, a fleece, a warm jacket, a cap, gloves and two pairs of socks at night. Snow is possible by late October; it snowed on 29 and 30 October 2026.

Which shoes are best for the Kedarnath trek?

Waterproof walking or trekking shoes with a deep grip, already worn in. Carry sandals or flip-flops for the hotel and the temple. Don't trek in slippers.

Do I need a walking stick for Kedarnath?

It helps a lot, mostly on the steep way down. A folding trekking pole adjusts to your height and fits in your backpack when you don't need it.

Which documents do I need for Kedarnath?

Your Char Dham registration letter with the QR code, the photo ID you registered with, your helicopter ticket if you booked one on IRCTC, your hotel bookings and some cash.
