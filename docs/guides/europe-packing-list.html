Europe packing list: what to pack for a winter trip from India

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Europe in December means Christmas markets, lights and snow in the Alps, and also cold, damp days that surprise many first-time travellers from India. This list tells you what to wear, which shoes and plug to take, what the EU lets you carry in, and which bag to choose. It works for the classic Switzerland, Paris and Italy trip, and for most of western Europe.

Europe weather in December and January

Long-term averages from MeteoSwiss, Météo-France and the Italian Air Force Meteorological Service, published by the World Meteorological Organization (the same figures as in our Europe trip guide, read 21 Nov 2026):

MonthZurich day / nightParis day / nightRome day / night
DecemberAbout 3 °C / −2 °CAbout 8 °C / 4 °CAbout 13 °C / 4 °C
JanuaryAbout 2 °C / −3 °CAbout 7 °C / 3 °CAbout 12 °C / 3 °C
  • Switzerland is the coldest. Zurich stays near freezing all day in December and January, and the Alps are colder and snowier. Pack for snow, not just cold.
  • Paris is cold and damp, around 7 to 8 °C by day. It's the wind and rain, more than the number, that make it feel colder.
  • Rome is milder, about 12 to 13 °C by day, but its nights are about 3 to 4 °C, and November is its wettest month.
  • Days are short. Plan fewer sights per day, and keep a warm layer in your day bag for the evening.
  • Check the forecast for your dates before you pack. These are averages, and cold snaps happen.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Your passport (valid for at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area) and your visa, with printouts of your insurance and bookings
  2. Travel medical insurance papers: the Schengen visa needs them
  3. A warm, windproof jacket with a hood
  4. Thermals, two sweaters or fleeces, and warm trousers
  5. A cap, gloves, a scarf or neck warmer, and woollen socks
  6. Waterproof shoes with a good grip, already worn in
  7. A travel adapter with a Type C plug (and a Type J if you go to Switzerland: a universal adapter has both)
  8. Your medicines in their original packs, with the prescription
  9. A card that works abroad and some euros (or Swiss francs for Switzerland)
  10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

Documents and money

  • A Schengen visa is needed by Indian passport holders. See the Europe trip guide for which country to apply to, the documents and the processing time.
  • Carry your visa printout, travel insurance, flight and hotel bookings, on paper and on your phone. Border officers can ask for them.
  • Cash of €10,000 or more (or the same value in other currency) must be declared to customs when you enter or leave the EU, says the EU's Your Europe site (read 21 Nov 2026). Cash includes notes, coins, traveller's cheques and some gold. Switzerland isn't in the EU, so it has its own rule: check the Swiss customs site if you carry a lot.
  • For ordinary spending, take a debit or credit card that works internationally, and a small amount of euros. Ask your bank about foreign charges before you go. Our cash and currency rules cover what you can carry out of India.
  • Switzerland uses the Swiss franc, not the euro, even though it's in the Schengen area.

What to wear in Europe in winter

You'll be outside a lot, walking from stations to sights and standing in market queues. Layers beat one thick coat, because trains, shops and hotels are heated and warm.

  • Base layer: thermals under your clothes, from December to February. Cotton thermals are fine for walking, but thick ones can feel hot indoors, so choose a thin set.
  • Middle layer: a sweater or fleece. Take two, so one can dry.
  • Outer layer: a warm, windproof jacket with a hood. In Switzerland and for the Jungfrau region, make it a proper winter jacket, not a light one.
  • Cap, gloves and scarf: cold hands and ears spoil a day faster than anything else. A neck warmer is easy to pack.
  • Woollen socks: three or four pairs, plus one spare pair in your day bag.
  • Trousers: warm trousers or jeans over thermals. Avoid thin cotton trousers in the Alps.
  • Wear the bulky jacket on the plane. It frees up space and weight in your bag.

Shoes for wet, cold streets

  • Waterproof shoes with a good grip are the one thing to get right. Old European towns have cobbles, and snow and wet leaves are slippery.
  • Wear them in before you go. New shoes mean blisters in the first two days.
  • Ankle height keeps slush out. Take shoes that fit over thick socks.
  • A second pair of comfortable shoes or sneakers for the hotel, trains and dry days.
  • Walk a lot? City breaks mean many hours on your feet, so comfort matters more than looks. See our travel shoes guide.

Skin, health and rain

  • Lip balm and a moisturiser. Cold wind and heated rooms dry your skin and lips fast.
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen if you go up to the snow. Sun on snow is strong, even in winter. Read our sunscreen guide.
  • A small umbrella or a hooded jacket for rain in Paris and Rome.
  • Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription. See can I carry medicines on a flight?
  • A small first-aid kit: plasters, pain relief and something for an upset stomach.
  • A steel bottle. Tap water is safe to drink in most of western Europe, and in Switzerland there are fountains in many towns.
  • Travel insurance papers. They're needed for the visa, and useful if you fall ill.

Phone, plugs and power bank

  • Most of Europe uses 230 V, and Type C, E or F plugs. Switzerland also uses Type J and Italy Type L. Indian two-pin chargers often fit Type C, but not always the recessed sockets, so take an adapter. See our power bank and travel adapter guide for the plug table.
  • A universal adapter with a Type C plug, ideally with USB ports, covers most hotels. One with slide-out plugs also works in Switzerland and Italy.
  • Check your charger's label. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work with just an adapter. Indian hair dryers and straighteners are usually 230 V, so they work in Europe, but they need a plug that fits.
  • An adapter does not change voltage. It only changes the plug's shape.
  • Power bank: in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See can I carry a power bank? and power bank rules on flights worldwide.
  • A cable organiser keeps chargers and cables together, so none stays plugged in at the hotel.
  • Cold drains batteries faster. Keep your phone and power bank in an inside pocket.

Which bag to take

  • About 10 days: a medium check-in trolley and a cabin bag or backpack. Winter clothes are bulky, so use packing cubes.
  • Several trains and hotel changes? Pack light. You will carry the bag up station stairs and over cobbles, and many old hotels have no lift.
  • Flying inside Europe on a low-cost airline? Free cabin allowance is small. Ryanair's free bag is one small bag of 40 x 30 x 20 cm, and easyJet's free bag is 45 x 36 x 20 cm and 15 kg (both from our airline data). Larger cabin bags and checked bags cost extra.

What you can bring into the EU

The EU's Your Europe site (read 21 Nov 2026) lists what you can bring in free of tax when you arrive from outside the EU, for personal use and not for sale. Each EU country can apply its own rules too, and Switzerland has its own customs.

  • Alcohol: 4 litres of still wine and 16 litres of beer, plus 1 litre of spirits over 22%, or 2 litres of fortified or sparkling wine. Under 17s get no alcohol or tobacco allowance.
  • Tobacco: 200 cigarettes, 100 cigarillos, 50 cigars or 250 g of tobacco. A few countries, including Poland, Hungary and Greece for land and sea travellers, and Estonia and Romania for everyone, allow only 40 cigarettes or 50 g.
  • Other goods, including perfume: up to €430 per traveller by air or sea, or €300 by land.
  • No meat or dairy products from outside the EU. That includes cheese and milk-based sweets from India, so leave them at home. You can bring a limited amount of fruit, vegetables, eggs, honey and fish. Powdered baby milk, baby food and special medical food (under 10 kg in sealed packaging) are allowed.
  • Cash: €10,000 or more must be declared.
  • Liquids in your cabin bag: each container 100 ml or less, all in one clear plastic bag of up to 1 litre, say the EU rules. Bigger bottles go in check-in. Medicines and baby food are exempt.

Questions

What should I pack for Europe in winter?

Thermals, two sweaters or a fleece, a warm windproof jacket with a hood, warm trousers, a cap, gloves, a scarf and woollen socks, and waterproof shoes with a good grip. Add a travel adapter with a Type C plug, a power bank in your cabin bag, your medicines and your visa and insurance papers.

What should I wear in Europe in December?

Layers. In Zurich it's about 3 °C by day and −2 °C at night, in Paris about 8 °C and 4 °C, and in Rome about 13 °C and 4 °C. Wear thermals, a sweater and a warm, windproof jacket. In the Alps, add a cap, gloves and a neck warmer.

Is Europe cold in December?

Yes. Zurich averages about 3 °C by day and −2 °C at night, and Paris about 8 °C and 4 °C. Rome is milder, at about 13 °C by day. Days are short, and rain and wind make Paris feel colder than the numbers.

What shoes should I wear in Europe in winter?

Waterproof shoes or ankle boots with a good grip, worn in before the trip. Old towns have cobbles that get slippery in rain and snow. Take a second pair that is comfortable for long days of walking.

Do I need an adapter for Europe from India?

Yes, usually. Most of Europe uses 230 V, the same as India, and Type C, E or F plugs. Switzerland also uses Type J and Italy Type L. A travel adapter changes only the plug's shape, not the voltage, so check that your charger says 100–240 V.

Can I take Indian food or sweets to Europe?

Not meat or dairy products. The EU does not let travellers from outside the EU bring them in, which covers cheese and milk-based sweets. You can bring a limited amount of fruit, vegetables, eggs and honey. Dry snacks without meat or dairy are usually fine, but check the rules of the country where you land.

How much cash can I carry to Europe?

For the EU, cash of €10,000 or more (or the same value in other currencies) must be declared to customs when you enter or leave. Below that you need not declare it. See our cash and currency rules for India's own limits.

How much alcohol and tobacco can I bring into the EU?

Free of tax: 4 litres of still wine and 16 litres of beer, plus 1 litre of spirits or 2 litres of fortified or sparkling wine, and 200 cigarettes (40 in a few countries). Other goods up to €430 by air. It's for your own use, not for sale.

What cabin bag can I take to Europe?

On long-haul economy fares from India, Lufthansa allows 8 kg and 55 x 40 x 23 cm, and Air France and KLM 12 kg in total with a 55 x 35 x 25 cm bag. British Airways gives a 56 x 45 x 25 cm bag with no weight limit. Low-cost airlines inside Europe allow only a small bag free.
