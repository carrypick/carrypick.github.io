Rajasthan packing list: what to pack and wear for Jaipur, Udaipur and Jaisalmer in winter

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 10 min read

Rajasthan in winter is two trips in one day. The sun is warm and strong at noon, and the nights are cold, colder still in a desert camp outside Jaisalmer. So you pack light cotton for the forts and markets, a real warm layer for the evenings, good walking shoes, and serious sun care. This list covers a 5- to 7-day trip to Jaipur, Udaipur, Jodhpur and Jaisalmer from October to March.

Rajasthan weather in winter

IMD's long-term averages (1981–2010), as in our trip guides (read 25 Nov 2026):

CityDecember, day / nightJanuary, day / night
JaipurAbout 24 °C / 9 °CAbout 22.5 °C / 8 °C
UdaipurAbout 26 °C / 8 °CAbout 25 °C / 7 °C
JaisalmerAbout 25 °C / 10–11 °CAbout 24 °C / 9 °C
Bikaner–About 23 °C / 7 °C
  • It gets about 15 °C colder after dark. Midday is T-shirt weather, but 7 AM and 9 PM need a warm jacket.
  • Desert camps are colder than town. Nights on the open dunes at Sam and Khuri feel colder than Jaisalmer, and can drop close to freezing. IMD's lowest January reading for Jaisalmer is −5.9 °C (1967).
  • It almost never rains in December and January: less than one rainy day a month on average.
  • Mornings are the coldest. In Jaipur the lowest temperature comes around 7–8 AM, and a few nights each winter drop to 2–3 °C.
  • Fog in January can delay early trains and flights from Delhi. Pack your warm layer in your hand bag, not the checked bag.
  • Check the forecast on IMD Jaipur in the week before you go. IMD issues cold-wave warnings for Rajasthan in January.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Light cotton or linen tops and trousers, one set a day
  2. One warm jacket or thick fleece, plus a sweater or cardigan
  3. Thermals or a warm layer for a desert camp night
  4. A big scarf, stole or dupatta: for sun, sand and temples
  5. A cap or sun hat, and sunglasses
  6. Sunscreen and lip balm with SPF, and a moisturiser for the dry air
  7. Comfortable walking shoes that are quick to take off, plus sandals
  8. A small crossbody bag or anti-theft backpack for the bazaars
  9. A power bank, a refillable bottle and some cash
  10. Your photo ID, tickets and hotel bookings

Clothes: hot days, cold nights

  • By day: light cotton or linen, loose and full-sleeved if you burn easily. Long sleeves and long trousers or skirts are cooler in the sun than they sound, and right for temples too.
  • Evenings: a sweater or cardigan in Udaipur and Jaipur, a warm jacket after sunset anywhere in January.
  • A night in the dunes: your warmest jacket over a thermal, a woollen cap and warm socks. Camps should give you heavy quilts, but ask before you book.
  • Layer, don't pack heavy. A thermal and a fleece under a light jacket do the work of one thick coat, and fit your bag better.

Sun, sand and the desert camp

  • A cap or a wide-brimmed hat. Fort ramps and stepwells have little shade.
  • Sunglasses. The light off sand and pale stone is strong.
  • A scarf or buff for the dunes. Wind blows sand at sunset; cover your nose and mouth on a jeep ride.
  • A zip pouch for your phone on the dunes, so sand doesn't get into the ports.
  • A small torch. Camp paths are dark at night.
  • Closed shoes for the sand. It's hot by day and very cold at night.

Shoes

  • Walking shoes with a good grip. Amer, Mehrangarh and Jaisalmer Fort mean long, polished stone ramps and steps.
  • Easy on, easy off. Shoes come off at every temple, and at many havelis and palaces. Slip-ons save time.
  • Sandals or flip-flops for the hotel and short walks.

Skin, health and medicines

  • Sunscreen, every day. The dry winter sun burns easily, even when it feels cool. See our travel sunscreen guide.
  • Lip balm with SPF and a moisturiser. The desert air dries skin and lips fast.
  • Drink plenty of water in the day, even when you don't feel hot. Carry a refillable bottle and fill it with bottled or filtered water.
  • Your own medicines, with the prescription, plus stomach, cold and headache tablets.
  • Never sleep with a coal brazier or heater in a closed room.

At temples, mosques and dargahs

  • Cover your shoulders and knees. At the Ranakpur Jain temple near Udaipur, Incredible India advises covering shoulders and knees and taking off your shoes at the entrance.
  • Carry a scarf or stole. Some places ask visitors to cover their head, so it's handy to have one in your bag.
  • Wear socks you don't mind showing, and remember that stone floors are cold in the morning.
  • Read the notice at the gate. Rules differ from temple to temple, and the one at the gate is the one that counts.

Bags and money

  • A crossbody bag that zips shut, or an anti-theft backpack. The bazaars of Jaipur and Jodhpur get crowded.
  • Carry some cash for autos, camel rides, tips and small shops, and agree prices first.
  • A power bank. Long days of photos and maps drain your phone.
  • Book monuments on the official sites. Jaipur's composite ticket is sold on Rajasthan's official ticket site, and at the counters.

Rajasthan packing list for 7 days

  • 6 light tops or shirts, 3 pairs of trousers or skirts
  • 1 sweater or cardigan, 1 warm jacket
  • 1 thermal set, 1 woollen cap, 3 pairs of warm socks for the desert night
  • 7 pairs of everyday socks and underwear
  • 2 scarves or stoles
  • 1 cap or sun hat, 1 pair of sunglasses
  • 1 pair of walking shoes, 1 pair of sandals
  • Sunscreen, lip balm, moisturiser and your medicines
  • Power bank, charger, phone pouch, small torch, refillable bottle
  • ID, cash, tickets and bookings

Which bag to take

  • 5 to 7 days, flying: a cabin trolley can do it if you wear your jacket and pack light cotton. Check your fare's cabin bag size and weight on our domestic baggage rules page.
  • Moving every two days? A medium trolley or duffle, plus a day backpack. Packing cubes make the quick hotel changes easier.
  • On the train? Keep to a bag you can lift onto the rack yourself. See the train luggage rules.
  • Buying textiles, blue pottery or quilts? Leave room, or pack a folding bag for the way home.

Questions

What should I pack for a Rajasthan trip in winter?

Light cotton clothes for the day, a sweater and a warm jacket for the evenings, a thermal set for a desert camp, a scarf or stole, a cap, sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, and comfortable walking shoes that are quick to take off. IMD's January averages are about 22–25 °C by day and 7–9 °C at night in Jaipur, Udaipur and Jaisalmer.

What should I wear in Rajasthan in December?

Light, loose cotton by day, with a cap and sunglasses, and a sweater or warm jacket after sunset. IMD's December averages are about 24–26 °C by day and 8–11 °C at night. Cover your shoulders and knees at temples.

How cold is a desert camp in Jaisalmer at night?

Colder than the town. Jaisalmer's IMD averages are about 10–11 °C at night in December and 9 °C in January, and the open dunes feel colder, sometimes close to freezing. Take your warmest jacket, a thermal, a cap and warm socks.

What should women wear in Rajasthan?

Loose cotton kurtas, tops and long skirts or trousers by day, with a stole or scarf for sun, sand and temples. Add a warm jacket or long cardigan and leggings for the cold evenings.

What shoes should I wear in Rajasthan?

Comfortable walking shoes with a good grip for the fort ramps and old-city lanes, ideally slip-ons, because you take them off at every temple. Add sandals or flip-flops for the hotel.

Do I need a heavy jacket for Rajasthan?

Not a snow jacket, but a proper warm one for December and January evenings, early starts and desert nights. A puffer or quilted jacket over a sweater is enough for most people.

How many clothes should I pack for 7 days in Rajasthan?

Six light tops, three trousers or skirts, a sweater, a warm jacket, a thermal set and two scarves, plus walking shoes and sandals. See the 7-day list above.
