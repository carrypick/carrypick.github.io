Nepal packing list: things to carry for a trip from India

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Nepal is easy for Indians: no visa, and Indian rupees work in many places. But two things catch people out. Only two IDs are accepted on flights, and Indian notes above ₹100 have a limit. This list tells you what documents to carry, what to wear, and which bag to take, for a trip from India.

Nepal weather in November, December and January

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology, as tabled on Wikipedia:

MonthKathmandu dayKathmandu nightPokhara dayPokhara night
NovemberAbout 24 °CAbout 8 °CAbout 24 °CAbout 12 °C
DecemberAbout 20 °CAbout 4 °CAbout 21 °CAbout 8 °C
JanuaryAbout 19 °CAbout 3 °CAbout 20 °CAbout 7 °C
  • Days are mild and sunny in Kathmandu and Pokhara from November to January. Nights are cold, and old houses and some hotels have no heating.
  • Pokhara is warmer at night. It's lower than Kathmandu, so a sweater and a light jacket are enough.
  • Hill viewpoints like Nagarkot and Sarangkot are colder at sunrise. Take a warm jacket, a cap and gloves.
  • Treks go much higher, where it is far colder. Pack for the trek separately, with your guide's list.
  • Planning the trip? See Nepal in December and January for a day-by-day plan, festivals and where it snows.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Your Indian passport or original voter ID card with photo (Aadhaar won't do on a flight)
  2. Your tickets and hotel bookings, printed or on your phone
  3. Indian rupees, with no more than ₹25,000 in ₹200 and ₹500 notes, plus a card
  4. A warm jacket, thermals, a cap and gloves for December and January
  5. Clothes that cover your shoulders and knees, for temples
  6. Comfortable walking shoes that are easy to take off
  7. A rain jacket or poncho from June to September
  8. Lip balm, sunscreen and sunglasses
  9. Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription
  10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

Documents for Nepal from India

The Embassy of India in Kathmandu says only two documents are valid for Indians flying between India and Nepal:

  • A valid Indian passport, or
  • Your original voter ID card with photo, issued by the Election Commission of India.
  • A downloaded or printed voter ID isn't accepted. Carry the original card.
  • Aadhaar, PAN card and driving licence aren't accepted for travel to Nepal. Nor is an Indian Embassy registration.
  • No visa is needed for Indian citizens.
  • Children and older travellers: India's Home Ministry rules allow people under 15 and over 65 to travel with photo ID that shows their age, and ages 15 to 18 with an identity certificate from their school principal. Rules at airports can be stricter, so the safest choice for a child is a passport.
  • Going by road? The same passport or voter ID is the safest choice at the border too. Keep it on you, not in a bag on the roof of a bus.

Indian currency in Nepal

  • ₹100 notes and smaller: no special limit.
  • ₹200 and ₹500 notes: you can take up to ₹25,000 per person into or out of Nepal, says Nepal Rastra Bank (notice reissued in 2026). India's own rule for Nepal and Bhutan is the same. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Old ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes from before November 2016 are not legal money in India. Don't carry them.
  • Leave ₹2,000 notes at home. RBI has withdrawn them, and they aren't part of Nepal's allowance.
  • Bigger amounts: carry cash worth up to US$5,000 into Nepal without declaring it. Above that, declare it at customs.
  • Cards and ATMs: bring an international debit or credit card for hotels and ATMs, and ask your bank about charges before you go.
  • Change some money to Nepali rupees for taxis, small shops and temple fees. Keep the receipt.

What to wear in Nepal

  • At temples: cover your shoulders and knees. Pashupatinath's own website asks for modest, traditional dress, with no shorts or sleeveless tops.
  • Take off your shoes before you enter any shrine. Wear shoes or sandals that slip off easily.
  • Inside Pashupatinath's main temple, only Hindus may enter. Others can visit the ghats, gardens and other shrines, and watch the evening aarti from the east bank.
  • No photos inside the main temple or during ceremonies at Pashupatinath.
  • Pack one or two "temple outfits": a top with sleeves, and long trousers or a long skirt. A light shawl or stole helps too.

What to wear in Nepal in November, December and January

What should I wear? Try the what-to-wear helper for day and night temperatures and an outfit list by month.

  • November: days are about 24 °C. Light clothes by day, and a sweater or fleece for nights near 8 °C in Kathmandu.
  • December and January: layers. Thermals, a full-sleeve top, a sweater, and a warm jacket for nights of 3–4 °C in Kathmandu.
  • A cap, gloves and a neck warmer for early starts, like a Nagarkot or Sarangkot sunrise.
  • Woollen socks, two or three pairs.
  • Sunglasses. The winter sun is bright.

Skin, health and rain

  • Lip balm and a moisturiser. Winter air in Kathmandu is dry.
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses. The sun is strong, even when the air is cold. Read our sunscreen guide.
  • A mask for dusty roads and busy streets in Kathmandu.
  • Your medicines, in their original packs, with the prescription. See can I carry medicines on a flight?
  • A small first-aid kit: something for an upset stomach, plasters and pain relief.
  • A steel bottle. Drink boiled, filtered or sealed water.
  • Monsoon trips (June to September): pack a rain jacket or poncho.

Phone, plugs and power bank

  • Nepal uses 230 V and the same round-pin plugs as India (types C, D and M). Your Indian chargers will work, so you don't need an adapter.
  • A power bank helps on long road trips and treks.
  • Power bank: in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. See can I carry a power bank?
  • Drones need permission from Nepal's authorities before you fly one. Pashupatinath asks for written permission from its trust too.
  • A cable organiser keeps chargers and cables together.

What to pack for kids

  • Their passport, the safest ID for a child
  • Thermals, a warm jacket, a cap and gloves
  • More socks than days
  • Full-sleeve tops and long trousers for temples
  • Snacks and a water bottle for long drives on hill roads
  • Their medicines, and something for motion sickness if winding roads upset them

Which bag to take

  • About 5 nights: a cabin trolley or a 30–40 litre backpack. Winter clothes are bulky, so use packing cubes.
  • About a week or more: a medium trolley.
  • Flying inside Nepal (Kathmandu to Pokhara, for example)? Small planes allow a 5 kg cabin bag and 15–25 kg checked, by fare. Pack light, or leave a bag at your Kathmandu hotel.
  • Going by road? A backpack or a light trolley is easier on bus roofs, hill roads and at the border.

What you can't bring into or out of Nepal

  • Duty-free limits: about 1 litre of liquor (Nepal Tourism Board lists one 1.15-litre bottle) and 200 cigarettes or 50 cigars. Declare anything more.
  • Antiques can't be taken out. Objects over 100 years old, such as sacred images, paintings and manuscripts, can't be exported. Keep your shop receipts for crafts.
  • Drones need permission in advance.
  • A power bank in your check-in bag. It goes only in your cabin bag.
  • Big bottles in your cabin bag. Each liquid must be 100 ml or less, in one clear 1-litre bag. See can I carry liquids?

Trekking in Nepal

  • Annapurna Conservation Area entry costs NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals (including Indians), says the permit office of Nepal's National Trust for Nature Conservation.
  • Since April 2023, Nepal asks foreign trekkers to trek with a licensed guide in many areas. Rules for Indians on guides and TIMS cards have changed more than once, so ask a registered trekking agency or the Nepal Tourism Board before you book.
  • Pack for the trek separately. It's far colder up high than in Kathmandu.

Coming back to India

  • Weigh your bag before you leave the hotel. Woollens, tea and singing bowls add up fast. A luggage scale helps.
  • For what you can bring into India, see our Indian customs rules and alcohol and cigarette limits.
  • Also going to Bhutan? Indians need a passport or voter ID there too, and pay a daily fee. See our Bhutan packing list.

Questions

What documents are required for Nepal from India?

For flights, only a valid Indian passport or your original voter ID card with photo, says the Embassy of India in Kathmandu. A downloaded or printed voter ID isn't accepted. Aadhaar, PAN card and driving licence aren't accepted either. No visa is needed.

Is Aadhaar card valid for Nepal?

Not for flights. The Embassy of India in Kathmandu lists only a passport or the original voter ID card. Older Home Ministry rules let children under 15 and people over 65 use other photo ID, but carry a passport to be safe.

Can I carry Indian currency to Nepal?

Yes. Notes of ₹100 and smaller have no special limit. ₹200 and ₹500 notes are allowed up to ₹25,000 per person, says Nepal Rastra Bank. Don't carry ₹2,000 notes or old pre-2016 ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes.

Can I use ₹500 notes in Nepal?

Yes, new ₹500 notes (issued after November 2016) are allowed, up to ₹25,000 per person together with ₹200 notes. Carry the rest of your money in ₹100 notes or on a card.

What are the things to carry for a Nepal trip from India?

A passport or original voter ID, your tickets and hotel bookings, Indian rupees within the ₹25,000 limit for big notes, a card, warm layers for winter, clothes that cover shoulders and knees for temples, shoes that slip off easily, sunscreen, lip balm and your medicines.

What should I wear in Nepal in December?

Layers: thermals, a full-sleeve top, a sweater and a warm jacket. Kathmandu days are about 20 °C and sunny, but nights are about 4 °C. Add a cap, gloves and woollen socks, and long trousers or a long skirt for temples.

What not to wear in Nepal?

At temples, don't wear shorts or sleeveless tops. Pashupatinath asks for modest, traditional dress. Take off your shoes before you enter a shrine.

Do I need an adapter for Nepal?

No. Nepal uses 230 V and the same round-pin plugs as India (types C, D and M), so your Indian chargers work.

How much baggage is allowed on Buddha Air?

A 5 kg cabin bag of up to 55 x 40 x 20 cm, and 15 to 25 kg of checked baggage, depending on your fare. This covers its flights from Varanasi and Kolkata and its flights inside Nepal.
