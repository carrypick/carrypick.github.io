Maldives packing list: what to pack and wear for a trip from India

Updated 18 Nov 2026 · 15 min read

The Maldives is hot all year, at about 30 °C, so you won't need a single sweater. What matters more is where you stay: resorts are relaxed, but Malé and the local islands expect modest clothes. There are also strict rules on what you can bring in, and seaplanes have tight bag limits. This list tells you what to pack and why, for a trip from India.

Maldives weather by month

Long-term averages from the Maldives Meteorological Service, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

JanuaryAbout 30 °CAbout 26 °CAbout 114 mm, on about 6 days
FebruaryAbout 31 °CAbout 26 °CAbout 38 mm, on about 3 days
MarchAbout 31 °CAbout 26 °CAbout 74 mm, on about 5 days
MayAbout 31 °CAbout 26 °CAbout 219 mm, on about 15 days
  • It's hot all year. Days are about 30–32 °C and nights about 24–27 °C on every atoll. Pack only summer clothes, plus one light layer for the flight.
  • February to April is the driest time around Malé. November and December still get rain on about 12–13 days a month, often as short, heavy showers.
  • The north is drier and the far south wetter in winter. In December, Hanimaadhoo in the north averages about 117 mm on 7 days, and Gan in Addu Atoll about 236 mm on 15 days.
  • May to November is wetter, with rain on about 12–15 days a month in Malé. A light rain jacket or umbrella helps then.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Passport, return ticket, resort or guesthouse booking, and a copy of your passport
  2. Light cotton or linen clothes, one outfit for each day
  3. Swimwear, and a kaftan, sarong or shirt to wear over it
  4. Modest clothes that cover shoulders and knees, for Malé and local islands
  5. One or two nicer outfits for dinners and evenings
  6. Flip-flops or sandals, and water shoes if you snorkel from the beach
  7. Sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat and lip balm with SPF
  8. A waterproof pouch for your phone and cash
  9. Your medicines in their original packs, with a recent prescription
  10. Phone charger and a power bank, in your cabin bag

Documents and money

  • Passport. Keep it on you, not in your check-in bag. Carry a photocopy and a photo of it on your phone.
  • Print or save your bookings: return ticket, resort or guesthouse booking, transfer details and travel insurance. Check the entry rules for Indian passport holders on Maldives Immigration's official website before you fly.
  • Fill in the Traveller Declaration on IMUGA. Maldives Immigration says all travellers must submit it within the 96 hours before their flight to the Maldives, and it's needed again before you leave. It's free and takes about 5 minutes on the official site, imuga.immigration.gov.mv. Avoid other websites that charge a fee.
  • Cash. RBI lets a bank or money changer sell you up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can take up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. The Maldives asks anyone carrying US$10,000 or more in cash to report it to Maldives Customs. See how much cash you can carry abroad.

What to wear in the Maldives

  • At resorts: dress is relaxed. Shorts, T-shirts, dresses and swimwear are normal. The state airline Maldivian says T-shirts and cotton clothes are most suitable.
  • In Malé and on local islands: dress modestly. Maldivian recommends that women respect local customs and wear modest clothing in Malé and on other inhabited islands. Cover your shoulders and knees, and put a T-shirt and shorts or a kaftan over your swimwear away from the beach.
  • Staying in a guesthouse on a local island? Ask your guesthouse where you can swim in swimwear.
  • For boat trips and snorkelling: a rash guard or quick-dry T-shirt keeps the sun off your back.
  • On the flight: one light layer. Planes and airport lounges are cold, even when Malé is 30 °C.

What to wear in the Maldives at night

Evenings are warm, at about 25–26 °C, so night outfits can be light:

  • Women: a maxi or midi dress, a kaftan, or a skirt or palazzo with a light top. Flat sandals are easier than heels on sand and wooden jetties.
  • Men: linen or cotton trousers or smart shorts with a linen shirt or polo.
  • A light stole or shirt for windy jetty walks and air-conditioned restaurants.
  • Mosquito repellent for dinners on the beach.

What to pack for couples and a honeymoon

  • Two or three outfits each for photos. Bright or white clothes stand out against the blue water.
  • One special-dinner outfit each, for a beach or overwater dinner.
  • Matching swimwear or kaftans, if you like coordinated photos.
  • Share one light check-in bag if you're taking a seaplane. See the seaplane limits below.
  • One power bank and a charger with two ports for both phones.
  • More ideas, including hill honeymoons, in our honeymoon packing list.

Medicines

The Maldives has clear rules for medicines you bring for yourself:

  • Bring a recent prescription. The Maldives Food and Drug Authority's guideline says personal medicines should come with a valid prescription from a registered doctor, not older than 3 months.
  • The prescription should show your full name and address, the date, what the medicine is for, its name, strength and dose, how long you take it, and the doctor's name, registration number, signature and seal.
  • Bring no more than 90 days' supply. The same guideline limits personal medicines to 90 days.
  • Controlled medicines need a valid prescription, for personal use only, says Velana International Airport, the country's main airport.
  • Keep medicines in your cabin bag, in their original packs, and have the prescription ready. If you're not sure about a medicine, declare it when you arrive.

See can I carry medicines on a flight?

Beach and boat bag

  • A waterproof pouch for your phone, cash and room key on boats and snorkelling trips.
  • A small dry bag or zip bag for wet swimwear.
  • Leave the reef as it is: don't stand on coral or touch marine life. Black coral, conches, giant clams and turtles may not be collected, and black and stony corals, Triton shells and turtle shells may not be taken out of the country.

Phone, plugs and power bank

  • Indian chargers work on the Maldives' 230 V supply, the same voltage as India, says Velana International Airport. Carry a universal travel adapter in case your room's sockets take a different plug.
  • Power bank: in your cabin bag only, never in check-in. The state airline Maldivian allows up to 2 power banks, each up to 100 Wh, and you can't use them to charge anything during the flight. See can I carry a power bank?
  • Drones need approval. The Maldives Civil Aviation Authority reviews every drone flight case by case. You may need approval from the Maldives National Defence Force, and from your resort or the island council.

Which bag to take

  • About 4–5 nights: a cabin trolley or a soft bag is enough. Beach clothes are light.
  • About a week: a light medium trolley, or one shared between two people.
  • Taking a seaplane? Pack light. Seaplane limits are lower than flight limits.

Flights from India, from the airlines' own websites:

IndiGo says it flies direct to the Maldives from Bengaluru, Mumbai and Kochi. Check the baggage shown for your fare before you pay.

Seaplanes and domestic flights to resorts have their own limits:

  • Trans Maldivian Airways allows 25 kg per person: 20 kg of check-in luggage and 5 kg of hand luggage. Extra weight costs US$5 per kg, plus taxes, and no single bag may weigh over 32 kg. Seaplanes fly only between sunrise and sunset.
  • Maldivian's seaplanes allow 15 kg per seat, including carry-on items.
  • Maldivian's domestic flights allow 20 kg of check-in baggage, and 5 kg of carry-on on its smaller planes.

Your resort books the transfer, so ask it which operator and allowance you'll have. Measure your bag with the cabin bag checker, and see extra baggage charges or our international baggage rules.

What you can't bring into the Maldives

Velana International Airport lists these as banned completely:

  • Idols for worship. Leave idols and murtis at home, even small ones for your own puja.
  • Religious materials offensive to Islam.
  • Pornographic material, including sex toys.
  • Narcotics and psychotropic substances.
  • Live pigs, and dogs.

These need a permit, so don't bring them:

  • Alcohol. Importing alcohol without approval is against Maldivian law, and alcohol bought on the flight or at duty-free shops on the way is confiscated when you land. Resorts and hotels sell alcohol under a special licence.
  • Pork and pork products.
  • Spear guns and harpoons, fireworks, and weapons.

Also leave out:

  • Vapes and e-cigarettes. The Maldives bans their import, sale, possession and use, for everyone, says the Ministry of Health.
  • Tobacco, if you were born on or after 1 January 2007. The Maldives bans selling tobacco to them, and their buying and using it. Others can bring up to 200 cigarettes, 25 cigars and 250 g of tobacco duty free.
  • A power bank in your check-in bag.
  • Big bottles in your cabin bag. Liquids must be in containers of 100 ml or less, up to 10 containers and 1 litre in total, says Velana International Airport.

When in doubt, declare it when you arrive, as Velana advises. Not sure about an item on the flight? Search our can I carry it on a flight? list.

Flying home to India

  • Fill in the IMUGA declaration again before your flight home.
  • Don't take coral or shells home. Corals, Triton shells and turtle shells may not be exported.
  • Indian Customs lets you bring goods worth ₹75,000 per person duty free, plus up to 2 litres of alcohol. See our Indian customs rules and alcohol and cigarette limits.
  • Weigh your bag before you leave the resort. Most fares to India give only 20 kg.

Questions

What should I pack for the Maldives from India?

Light cotton clothes, two swimsuits and a cover-up, modest clothes for Malé and local islands, one or two evening outfits, flip-flops, sunscreen, polarized sunglasses, a hat, a waterproof pouch, your medicines with a recent prescription and a power bank in your cabin bag. Don't pack alcohol, idols for worship or a vape.

What should couples pack for a Maldives honeymoon?

Two or three photo outfits each, one special-dinner outfit each, swimwear and kaftans, sunscreen, polarized sunglasses and a waterproof pouch. Share one light check-in bag if you take a seaplane, because Trans Maldivian Airways allows 20 kg of check-in luggage and 5 kg of hand luggage per person.

What should women wear in the Maldives?

At resorts, dresses, shorts and swimwear are fine. In Malé and on local islands, wear modest clothes that cover shoulders and knees, as the state airline Maldivian recommends, and put a kaftan or T-shirt and shorts over your swimwear away from the beach.

What should I wear in the Maldives at night?

Evenings are about 25–26 °C, so wear light clothes: a maxi dress, kaftan or skirt for women, and linen trousers or smart shorts with a linen shirt for men. Flat sandals are easier on sand and jetties. Take mosquito repellent for beach dinners.

What should I wear on local islands in the Maldives?

Dress modestly. Cover your shoulders and knees in Malé and on local islands, and wear swimwear only where your guesthouse says it's allowed.

Can I take alcohol to the Maldives?

No. Importing alcohol without approval is against Maldivian law, and alcohol bought on the flight or at duty-free shops on the way is confiscated at the airport. Resorts and hotels sell alcohol under a special licence.

Can I carry idols or puja items to the Maldives?

Idols for worship are on the Maldives' list of banned imports, so leave them at home. Religious materials offensive to Islam are banned too.

How much baggage is allowed on a Maldives seaplane?

Trans Maldivian Airways allows 25 kg per person: 20 kg of check-in luggage and 5 kg of hand luggage, with extra weight at US$5 per kg. Maldivian's seaplanes allow 15 kg per seat, including carry-on items. Ask your resort which one you'll fly.

Is it rainy in the Maldives in December?

Sometimes. Malé averages about 217 mm of rain on about 12 days in December, usually as short showers, and about 30 °C in the day. February to April is the driest time.
