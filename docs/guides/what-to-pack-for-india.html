What to pack for India: what to wear, by season and region, plus plugs and a checklist

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Packing for India is mostly about two things: the season (cold northern winter mornings, hot summers, monsoon rain) and dress that covers your shoulders and knees, which you'll need for temples, mosques and gurdwaras, and which keeps you comfortable in small towns. This list is for visitors flying in from abroad, written in plain English.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Passport (valid for 6 months), your e-Visa email printed and on your phone, and a return ticket
  2. Loose, light cotton or linen clothes that cover shoulders and knees
  3. A scarf or shawl: for temples, sun, dust and cold mornings
  4. A warm layer (fleece or light down) for December to February in the north
  5. Comfortable walking shoes, plus sandals or flip-flops you can slip off
  6. Sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat and mosquito repellent
  7. Your prescription medicines, in their original boxes, with a copy of the prescription
  8. A phone, charger and power bank, and a travel adapter if your plugs aren't round-pin
  9. Hand sanitiser and tissues, since not every public toilet has soap or paper
  10. A small lock for your bag on trains and in hostels

  • Winter nights are colder than people expect. IMD's averages for Jaipur are about 9 °C at night in December, and Varanasi about 10 °C, with some nights near freezing.
  • The Himalayas are different: Manali, Shimla and Kashmir need real winter clothes from December to February. See our winter clothes for hill trips.
  • The monsoon reaches Kerala around 1 June and covers all of India around 15 July, says the India Meteorological Department.

What to wear in India as a woman

There's no national dress code, but loose clothes that cover your shoulders and knees are the easy choice. You'll feel comfortable and blend in, and you can walk straight into any temple.

  • Good everyday clothes: loose trousers or long skirts, tunics or long shirts, cotton dresses that reach below the knee, and a light scarf.
  • Shorts and strappy tops are fine on Goa's beaches, in big-city malls and at many resorts. In small towns, old cities and holy places, they draw stares, so cover up there.
  • A kurta (a long Indian tunic) is cheap to buy in any Indian market, cool in the heat and good for temples. Many visitors buy one or two on their first day.
  • Swimwear is for the beach and the pool. Wear a cover-up or a kaftan when you walk to a café or a shop.
  • Carry a scarf in your day bag: you can cover your head in a gurdwara or a mosque, your shoulders in a temple, and your face from dust and sun.

What to wear in India as a man

  • Long trousers or knee-length shorts, and shirts with sleeves. Sleeveless vests are fine on the beach but not in temples.
  • Cover your head in a gurdwara (Sikh temple) such as the Golden Temple. A scarf or a bandana works, and many give you one at the door.

Temples, mosques and gurdwaras

  • Take your shoes off before you enter. Many places have a free or cheap shoe counter. Socks help on hot or cold stone floors.
  • Cover shoulders and knees, and in gurdwaras and some mosques, your head.
  • Leather (belts, bags) isn't allowed in some Hindu and Jain temples.
  • Photos: some temples don't allow cameras or phones inside, and you may need to leave them at a counter. Read the signs.

Shoes

  • Comfortable walking shoes for forts, old cities and long days on your feet.
  • Sandals or flip-flops that you can slip off fast at temples, and for the beach and the monsoon.

Medicines, liquids and toiletries

  • Bring your prescription medicines for the whole trip, in their original boxes, with a copy of the prescription. Keep them in your cabin bag.
  • Stomach upsets are common on a first trip. Ask your own doctor or pharmacist what to bring, such as oral rehydration salts.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water, and carry a refillable bottle where your hotel offers filtered water.
  • Liquids in your cabin bag: Indian airports follow the 100 ml rule for international flights. See can I carry liquids on a flight? and medicines on a flight.

Plugs, voltage and your phone

  • India's sockets take round-pin plugs. You'll most often see type D (three round pins) and type C (two thin round pins), and type M (three big round pins) for heavy appliances. We couldn't confirm this on an official page: the IEC retired its world plugs guide, so check your adapter's own list.
  • The voltage is 230 V (some places still 240 V), at 50 Hz. India's Central Electricity Authority is standardising household supply at 230 V.
  • Coming from the US or Canada (110–120 V)? Phone and laptop chargers marked 100–240 V work with just an adapter. Hair dryers and straighteners made only for 110 V need a converter, or leave them at home. Most hotels have a hair dryer.
  • Power cuts happen in some places, so carry a power bank, in your cabin bag only. See can I carry a power bank?

Documents and money

  • Passport and e-Visa: keep your passport on you, and the e-Visa email printed and on your phone. Get the e-Visa only from the official site. See our India e-Visa guide.
  • Cash and cards: declare foreign currency notes over US$5,000 (or over US$10,000 with travellers' cheques) when you land. See SIM, UPI and money for tourists.
  • Keep it all in one place you always carry, like a passport holder.

Which bag to take

  • A wheeled trolley is fine for hotels and airports. Old-city lanes, train platforms and steps are easier with a backpack or a light trolley.
  • Domestic flights in India have strict limits: most Indian airlines allow one cabin bag of 7 kg. Check yours with the cabin bag checker and our Indian airline baggage rules.
  • Trains: see the Indian Railways luggage rules.
  • Leave room for shopping: textiles, spices and tea are popular. Check the customs rules of your home country before you buy.

What not to pack

  • Valuables you don't need: expensive jewellery and extra gadgets are one more thing to watch.
  • Too many clothes: laundry is cheap and quick in most hotels, and you'll want to buy a kurta or two.

Questions

What should I wear in India as a woman tourist?

Loose, light clothes that cover your shoulders and knees, such as long skirts, loose trousers, tunics and cotton dresses, plus a scarf. Shorts and strappy tops are fine on Goa's beaches and at resorts, but cover up in temples, old cities and small towns.

Can women wear shorts in India?

Yes, on beaches, at resorts and in big-city malls. In temples, holy towns and villages, wear clothes that cover your knees.

What plug type does India use?

Round-pin sockets: mostly type D and type C, and type M for heavy appliances, at 230 V and 50 Hz. Take an adapter if your plugs have flat pins.

What should I pack for India in December?

Layers for the north: a warm jacket or fleece, a sweater and a scarf for cold, foggy mornings, and light clothes for sunny afternoons. For Goa and Kerala, light summer clothes and sun care.

What should I pack for India in October or November?

Light cotton clothes, one light sweater for nights in the north, sun care and comfortable shoes. Carry a small umbrella in early October.
