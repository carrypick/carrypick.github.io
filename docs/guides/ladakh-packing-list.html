Ladakh packing list: what to wear and carry for Leh, Nubra and Pangong

Updated 26 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Ladakh is a high, dry desert. Leh's airport is at about 3,256 m, the sun burns in minutes, and the same day can be hot at noon and cold at night, even in July. On the passes and at Pangong it's colder still. So you pack for two climates at once: sun protection and light clothes for the day, warm layers for the evening, plus cash, a power bank and your permit papers for places with no ATM and little network. This list works for a 6- to 8-day trip to Leh, Nubra and Pangong, by plane, car or bike.

Ladakh weather: how hot and how cold?

Long-term averages for Leh from the India Meteorological Department (IMD), from our Ladakh trip guide:

MonthDayNight
MayAbout 20 °CAbout 6 °C
JuneAbout 24 °CAbout 11 °C
JulyAbout 28.5 °CAbout 16 °C
AugustAbout 28 °CAbout 15 °C
SeptemberAbout 23 °CAbout 9 °C
OctoberAbout 17 °CAbout 0 °C
  • These are Leh's numbers. The passes (Khardung La, Chang La) and the lakes (Pangong, Tso Moriri) are much higher and much colder, even in July.
  • It hardly rains in Leh: IMD's average is only about 35 mm in a whole year. But you can get rain or snow on the passes, and on the Manali road.
  • Winter is another trip. Ladakh Tourism says winter temperatures can drop to −30 °C. This list is for May to October.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. A sun hat or cap, UV-protection sunglasses, SPF 50 sunscreen and an SPF lip balm
  2. Light full-sleeved T-shirts (they protect your arms from the sun) and comfortable trousers
  3. A thermal top and bottom, for nights and the passes
  4. A fleece or sweater, and a warm jacket that packs small
  5. A woollen cap, gloves and warm socks
  6. Comfortable walking shoes with a good grip, and sandals or flip-flops
  7. A power bank and your charging cables
  8. Cash, enough for Nubra and Pangong
  9. Your permit printout (and a copy on your phone), photo ID, and a postpaid SIM
  10. Your regular medicines and a small first-aid pouch
  11. A water bottle you can refill
  12. A backpack for day trips, with a rain cover

Sun and UV: the most important part

At Ladakh's height, the air is thin and dry and the sun is very strong. Our Ladakh guide says to wear a cap, sunglasses and sunscreen every day.

  • Sunglasses with UV protection, not just dark lenses. The glare off snow on the passes and off the lakes is strong. Read our sunglasses guide for what to look for.
  • A wide hat or a cap. A hat with a neck flap covers your ears and neck too.
  • Sunscreen on your face, ears, neck and hands, and again after a few hours. An SPF lip balm stops your lips from cracking in the dry air.
  • Full sleeves, even when it's warm. They stop sunburn on your arms on a long day outside.
  • Drink water often. The dry air dries you out without you noticing.

What to wear in Ladakh: how to layer

Leh in summer is hot in the sun and cold in the shade, at night and on the passes. Dress in layers, so you can take one off at noon and put it back on at Khardung La.

  1. Next to your skin: a cotton or quick-dry T-shirt by day, and a thermal set for nights and the passes.
  2. Middle: a fleece or a warm sweater.
  3. Outside: a windproof jacket; a light down or puffer jacket for Pangong, Tso Moriri and October.
  4. Head and hands: a woollen cap, gloves and warm socks for the evenings.

What to wear in Ladakh in June, July and August

Days in Leh are warm (about 24–28 °C on average) and nights are cool (about 11–16 °C). T-shirts and trousers are fine in Leh by day. Carry a fleece and a warm jacket every time you go higher: the camps at Pangong and in Nubra get cold at night.

What to wear in Ladakh in May, September and October

Colder. Leh's average night is about 6 °C in May, about 9 °C in September and about 0 °C in October. Pack a thermal set, a fleece and your warmest jacket, a cap and gloves. In October plan for freezing nights at the lakes.

Packing for a Ladakh bike trip

On a bike you're out in the sun, wind, dust, cold and the odd shower all day. Our Ladakh guide says bikers should add riding gear, rain covers and a good pair of gloves. Here's what that means:

  • Riding gloves, and a pair of warm or waterproof gloves for the passes and early mornings. Cold hands make it hard to brake and steer.
  • A balaclava or a neck warmer under your helmet. It keeps the wind, dust and sun off your face and neck.
  • A riding jacket with armour, and a warm layer under it. Add riding pants and knee guards if you have them.
  • Riding boots or high-ankle shoes with a good grip, for water crossings and loose gravel.
  • Rain covers: a rain suit or poncho for you, and a waterproof cover or dry bags for your luggage. Line your bag with a plastic bag too.
  • Bungee cords or straps to tie your bag to the bike, and a small tool kit and puncture kit (ask your rental shop what's included).
  • Your licence, the bike's papers and your permit, in a waterproof pouch.
  • Sunglasses or a clear visor, and sunscreen on the parts of your face the helmet doesn't cover.
  • Ride only after you've rested for 48 hours in Leh. Riding doesn't help you adjust to the height (see below).

Altitude sickness: what to pack and what to know

Altitude sickness is the biggest risk on a Ladakh trip. Read the full altitude sickness and the first 48 hours section of our trip guide before you go. In short:

  • Rest completely for the first 48 hours in Leh. The Leh administration asks all tourists to do this before going higher, even if you drove in.
  • Drink 2–3 litres of water a day, and avoid alcohol, cigarettes and sleeping pills, says Ladakh's official advice.
  • Headache, nausea, tiredness, poor sleep or breathlessness are warning signs. Rest, and don't go higher until you feel better.
  • Breathless at rest, confused or a wet cough? Go down at once and get medical help.
  • Ask your doctor about altitude-sickness medicine before the trip, especially if you have a heart or lung condition, are pregnant or are travelling with small children. Don't take any without advice.
  • A pulse oximeter helps you keep an eye on your oxygen level. Ladakh Tourism gives above 90% as normal.

What to pack for your health:

  • Your regular medicines, with spares and your prescriptions
  • The altitude medicine your doctor suggests, if any
  • A small first-aid pouch: plasters, a crepe bandage, and the headache, stomach and travel-sickness tablets your doctor suggests
  • Eye drops and saline nose drops for the dry air, a moisturiser and lip balm
  • A water bottle you can refill, so you keep drinking

Cash, phone and power

  • Carry cash. Ladakh Police says ATMs are available in Leh but sparse elsewhere, so take enough for Nubra and Pangong. Cards and UPI may not work where there's no network.
  • Use a postpaid SIM. "Only post-paid mobile connections work in Ladakh due to security restrictions," says Ladakh Police. Prepaid SIMs from other states are unreliable.
  • A power bank, or two. Camps at Pangong and in Nubra may have power only for a few hours, and long drives have no sockets. The cold also drains batteries faster. Keep the power bank in your cabin bag on the flight: see our power bank rules on flights.
  • A car charger if you're on a road trip, and a short cable.
  • Download offline maps before you leave Leh.
  • A small torch or a headlamp for camps and power cuts.

Permit and documents to carry

  • Your permit, printed and saved on your phone. Indians pay the environment fee, Red Cross Fund and wildlife fee on the official portal, lahdclehpermit.in, and show the permit at checkpoints on the way to Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle. Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit. Our trip guide's permit section has the details.
  • A few photocopies of your photo ID for checkpoints and hotels, and the original.
  • Foreigners: your passport and visa, and copies. Apply for the e-Tourist Visa only at indianvisaonline.gov.in.
  • Driving or riding? Your driving licence and the vehicle's papers.
  • Hotel and camp bookings, flight tickets and an emergency contact on a card.

Keep them together in a zip pouch or a plastic folder, in the top of your day bag.

Shoes

  • Comfortable walking shoes with a good grip, already worn in, for monastery steps, gravel and short walks.
  • Waterproof shoes if you'll walk by streams or ride a bike.
  • Warm socks for the lakes and the nights.
  • Sandals or flip-flops for the hotel and monasteries, where your shoes come off.

What to pack for women

  • Everything on the short list
  • Loose, full-sleeved tops and trousers: cool by day, and modest for monasteries
  • A light scarf or stole for the sun, dust and monasteries
  • A thermal set and a warm jacket that packs small
  • Sanitary supplies for the whole trip: shops are few outside Leh
  • A moisturiser, sunscreen and lip balm for the dry air

What to pack for men

  • Everything on the short list
  • Two or three full-sleeved T-shirts and two pairs of trousers
  • A thermal set, a fleece and a warm jacket
  • A cap and sunglasses for every day outside
  • A trimmer or razor and a small toiletry bag

What to pack for kids

Ladakh is very high for small children, so ask your doctor before you book. If you go:

  • Plenty of warm layers: a thermal set, a fleece, a warm jacket, a cap and gloves
  • A sun hat, sunscreen made for children and kids' sunglasses
  • Their regular medicines, and whatever your doctor suggests for the height
  • Water bottles and snacks for long drives
  • Keep day one and two quiet in Leh, and watch them for headache, vomiting, unusual tiredness or poor sleep

Questions

What should I pack for a Ladakh trip?

Sun protection (a hat, UV sunglasses, SPF 50 sunscreen and lip balm), light full-sleeved clothes for the day, a thermal set, a fleece and a warm jacket for the nights and passes, a cap and gloves, walking shoes, a power bank, cash, your permit and ID, and your medicines.

Is Ladakh cold in summer?

Leh's days are warm in June, July and August (about 24–28 °C on average, IMD), but nights are about 11–16 °C, and the passes and lakes are much colder. Carry a warm jacket even in July.

What should I wear in Ladakh in June?

T-shirts and trousers in Leh by day, with full sleeves for the sun, and a fleece and warm jacket for the evenings and for Khardung La, Chang La and Pangong. Leh averages about 24 °C by day and 11 °C at night in June.

What should I pack for a Ladakh bike trip?

Riding gloves and a pair of warm gloves, a balaclava or neck warmer, a riding jacket with a warm layer under it, riding boots, rain covers for you and your luggage, straps, a tool kit, sunscreen, and your licence, bike papers and permit in a waterproof pouch.

How much cash should I carry to Ladakh?

Enough for the days outside Leh. Ladakh Police says ATMs are available in Leh but sparse elsewhere, and card or UPI payments may not work without network. Take cash for Nubra and Pangong before you leave Leh.

Which documents do I need for Ladakh?

Your permit from lahdclehpermit.in (printed and on your phone), photo ID and copies, and for foreigners a passport, visa and Protected Area Permit. Add your driving licence and vehicle papers if you're driving or riding.
