How to choose a laptop backpack for college or office

Updated 23 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

A good backpack can last you four years of college or many years of office commutes. A bad one hurts your shoulders in a week. Here's what actually matters, in order.

1. Start with your laptop size

Check your laptop's screen size. It's written in the product details, or you can measure the screen diagonally. Most laptops in India are 14 or 15.6 inches, and gaming laptops are often 16 or 17 inches.

  • Pick a bag whose laptop sleeve is the same size or bigger than your laptop.
  • A 15.6 inch sleeve fits most 14 and 15.6 inch laptops.
  • If you have a 17 inch laptop, many "15.6 inch" bags will not close properly. Look for a sleeve made for 17 inches.

2. Choose the right size in litres

Backpack size is measured in litres. More litres means more space, but also a bigger, heavier bag.

  • 15–21 litres: a slim office bag for a laptop, charger and a few small things.
  • 22–30 litres: an everyday college bag with room for a laptop, books, lunch box and water bottle.
  • 30–35 litres: good for long days, gym clothes or a 2-day trip.

Most students are happy with 25–30 litres. Office workers who travel light often prefer 20–25 litres.

3. Compartments and pockets

Two or three main compartments keep your laptop away from your lunch box and water bottle. Look for:

  • A padded laptop sleeve, ideally one that doesn't reach the bottom of the bag, so your laptop is protected when you put the bag down.
  • A front organiser for pens, keys, earphones and cables.
  • Side pockets for a water bottle or umbrella.

4. Comfort: padding and straps

If you carry your bag for an hour or more every day, comfort matters more than looks.

  • Thick shoulder padding and S-shaped straps spread the weight better.
  • A padded back panel stops the laptop from pressing into your back.
  • Always wear both straps. One strap puts all the weight on one shoulder.

5. Monsoon protection

Indian monsoons are tough on bags. Some bags come with a rain cover: a stretchy cover hidden in a bottom pocket that you pull over the whole bag. This protects your laptop much better than "water-resistant" fabric alone.

6. Useful extras

  • Trolley sleeve: a strap on the back that slides over your suitcase handle. Great for travel.
  • Anti-theft pocket: a hidden zip against your back for your wallet or phone. Handy in crowded buses and trains.
  • Reflective strips: help drivers see you at night.

7. Check the warranty

Branded bags usually come with a 1-year warranty on zips and stitching, and sometimes longer. Zips are the part that breaks first, so a warranty is worth having.

Quick checklist before you buy

  1. Does the laptop sleeve fit my laptop size?
  2. Is it 20–30 litres for daily use?
  3. Does it have padded straps and a padded back?
  4. Is there a rain cover, or will I carry an umbrella?
  5. Does it have at least a 1-year warranty?
