Which USB-C cable do I need? 3 A vs 5 A, 60 W vs 100 W and 240 W

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Most USB-C cables look the same, but they are not. Some carry 3 A (60 W), some 5 A (100 W or 240 W), some move data at 480 Mbps and some at 10 Gbps or more. The short answer: a 3 A cable is enough for every iPhone, a Pixel 10 and a base Galaxy S25. You need a 5 A cable only for Samsung's 45 W mode (S25 Ultra, S25+, S25 FE) and for laptops that take more than 60 W.

Quick answer

  • iPhone 15, 16, 17 and Air: the USB-C cable in the box is enough. Apple says wired fast charging works with a 60 W-capable USB-C cable, which every normal 3 A cable is.
  • iPhone 14 or older (Lightning): a USB-C to Lightning cable and a USB-C charger of 18 W or more.
  • Galaxy S25 Ultra, S25+ and S25 FE: a 5 A cable and a 45 W PPS charger. With a 3 A cable they stay at about 25 W.
  • Base Galaxy S25 and Pixel 10: any 3 A USB-C cable. The charger matters more: it needs PPS.
  • Laptop over 60 W: a 5 A (100 W) cable. A 240 W cable is only for big gaming laptops and new 140 W+ chargers.
  • Copying videos or using a monitor: a USB 2.0 cable won't do it. Look for USB 3, 10 Gbps, USB4 or Thunderbolt on the cable.

3 A vs 5 A: what the numbers mean

USB-C chargers that use USB Power Delivery (USB PD) send power at a few fixed voltages. The top normal one is 20 V. Watts = volts x amps, so:

Cable ratingMost it can carryGood for
3 A (often sold as 60 W)20 V x 3 A = 60 WEvery iPhone, Pixel 10, base Galaxy S25, power banks, earbuds, small laptops
5 A, 100 W20 V x 5 A = 100 WSamsung 45 W mode, laptops up to 100 W
5 A, 240 W (EPR)48 V x 5 A = 240 WBig laptops and new chargers above 100 W
  • 240 W is newer. USB-IF says USB PD Revision 3.1 (2021) added 28 V, 36 V and 48 V, for 140 W, 180 W and 240 W. Before that, USB PD stopped at 100 W: 20 V on a 5 A cable. This top range is called Extended Power Range (EPR).
  • A bigger cable is always safe. A 5 A or 240 W cable works with a 20 W phone charger. The charger and phone agree on the power first. The cable only sets the top limit.
  • A 65 W charger on a 3 A cable gives up to 60 W. Fine for a phone; a laptop that wants the full 65 W needs a 5 A cable.

3A vs 5A cable for Samsung

This is where it matters most. Samsung's Super Fast Charging 2.0 (45 W) on the S25 Ultra, S25+ and S25 FE runs at more than 3 A, so it needs a 5 A cable. Samsung's own help page says 45 W needs a 45 W charger and a 5 A cable. With the 3 A cable from the phone box, you get about 25 W. See which charger for Samsung S25 Ultra.

What is an e-marker chip?

An e-marker (electronically marked cable) is a tiny chip inside the plug of a USB-C cable. When you plug in, the charger asks the cable what it can carry, and the chip answers: "5 A", "240 W", "10 Gbps" and so on.

  • Why 5 A cables need one. Under the USB Type-C rules, a charger may send more than 3 A only if the cable says it can take it. A cable with no e-marker is treated as 3 A, so the charger stops at 60 W, even if the cable is thick and the box says "100 W".
  • So "100 W" without an e-marker is a warning sign. Good 5 A cables say "5A" and "e-marker" or "E-Mark chip" on the listing, or carry the USB-IF 100 W or 240 W logo.
  • 3 A cables don't need one. Most short phone cables have none, and that's fine.
  • Fast data cables have one too. USB 3 and USB4 cables use the chip to tell the devices their speed.

USB-IF certified: what the logo tells you

USB-IF is the group that writes the USB rules. Cables that pass its tests can carry its logos:

  • Power: USB-IF now requires every certified USB-C to USB-C cable to be labelled 60W or 240W.
  • Speed: every certified cable except plain USB 2.0 ones must also show its data rate, like 20Gbps or 40Gbps.
  • "Certified" or "compliant"? "USB-IF certified" means the cable passed the tests and is on USB-IF's list. "USB-IF compliant" or "meets USB-IF specs" is the maker's own claim. Both can be fine from a known brand; certified is stronger.
  • A certified cable isn't faster. It's just more likely to do what the box says, and to be safe at high power.

Our Belkin 5 A pick lists USB-IF certified. Our Anker 3 A pick lists USB-IF compliant.

Data speed: USB 2.0 vs USB 3 and USB4

Charging speed and data speed are separate. A 100 W cable can still be a slow USB 2.0 cable, like our Belkin pick.

What the cable saysData speedVideo to a monitor?
USB 2.0480 MbpsNo
USB 3 / USB 3.2, 5 Gbps5 GbpsYes, on most
USB 3 / USB 3.2, 10 Gbps10 GbpsYes, on most
USB 3.2, 20 Gbps20 GbpsYes
USB4 / Thunderbolt 440 GbpsYes
USB4 80 Gbps / Thunderbolt 580 GbpsYes
  • Most phone cables are USB 2.0. Apple's iPhone 17 page lists USB 2 (up to 480 Mbps) on the base iPhone 17, and Google says the Pixel 10's box cable is USB 2.0. That's fine for charging.
  • iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max support USB 3 (up to 10 Gb/s), but Apple says you need a USB 3 cable with 10Gb/s speed for it. Shooting ProRes video or copying big files to a laptop? Buy one.
  • Monitor or projector from a laptop or phone: use a USB 3, USB4 or Thunderbolt cable. A USB 2.0 cable charges but shows no picture.
  • USB-C vs Thunderbolt: Thunderbolt uses the same USB-C plug. A Thunderbolt 4 or 5 cable works as a fast USB-C cable too, but costs more. You only need it for Thunderbolt docks and drives.

USB-C to Lightning for older iPhones

The iPhone 15 and later have USB-C. The iPhone 14 and older use Lightning.

  • To fast charge an older iPhone, Apple says to use a USB-C to Lightning cable with an Apple 18 W or greater USB-C adapter, or a third-party USB-C charger that supports USB PD.
  • An old USB-A charger (the flat plug) with a USB-A to Lightning cable still works, just slowly.
  • Moved to an iPhone 15 or later? Your old Lightning cables won't fit. You can keep them for an iPad or AirPods that still use Lightning.
  • USB-C to Lightning adapters (a small plug that turns a Lightning cable into USB-C, or the other way) exist, but a proper cable is simpler and sturdier on a trip.

One cable for a trip

On a trip you want fewer cables, not more. If your phone, laptop and power bank all use USB-C:

  • One 5 A, 100 W cable can charge all three from one 65 W charger: the phone at full speed, the laptop at full power and the power bank.
  • Add one short 3 A spare. Cables get left in hotel sockets. A short one also connects your phone to the power bank in a bag or a pocket.
  • Length: 1 m is enough in a plane seat or a train berth, with the power bank in your lap or the bag at your feet. 2 m helps in hotel rooms, where the socket is often far from the bed.
  • Braided cables last longer in a backpack, but they're bulkier to coil.
  • Abroad: a cable works in any country. Only the charger's plug changes, so you need a travel adapter, not new cables.
  • Flights: cables and chargers are fine in both bags. Power banks go in the cabin only. See power bank rules on flights and our power bank and travel adapter guide.
  • Keep them together in a small pouch: see our gadget organiser guide.

Questions

Which USB-C cable is best for fast charging?

For an iPhone, a Pixel or a base Galaxy S25, any 3 A (60 W) USB-C cable from a known brand. For the Galaxy S25 Ultra, S25+ or S25 FE at 45 W, and for laptops over 60 W, a 5 A (100 W) cable with an e-marker.

Which USB-C cable comes with the iPhone 17?

Apple's box has a 1 m USB-C Charge Cable. Apple says wired fast charging works with a 60 W-capable USB-C cable, so the box cable is enough. For USB 3 speeds on the iPhone 17 Pro, Apple says you need a USB 3 cable with 10Gb/s speed.

What is the difference between a 3A and a 5A cable?

A 3 A cable carries up to 60 W (20 V x 3 A). A 5 A cable carries up to 100 W, or 240 W if it's an EPR cable. A 5 A cable has an e-marker chip that tells the charger it can take the extra current.

USB-C cable 100W vs 240W: which do I need?

100 W is enough for phones and almost all laptops. 240 W is only for chargers and laptops above 100 W, like big gaming laptops. A 240 W cable also works on smaller chargers.

USB-C cable 100W vs 60W: is 100W faster for my phone?

Only for phones that go above 3 A, like Samsung's 45 W mode. An iPhone, a Pixel 10 or a base Galaxy S25 charges at the same speed on either.

What does an e-marker chip do?

It's a small chip in the plug that tells the charger and devices what the cable can carry: current, power and data speed. Without one, a charger treats the cable as 3 A and stops at 60 W.

Which USB-C cable supports video or a display?

A USB 3 (5 Gbps or more), USB4 or Thunderbolt cable. USB 2.0 cables, which most phone cables are, can charge but can't send video to a monitor.

Which USB-C cable is best for data transfer?

A USB 3 cable rated 10 Gbps or more, or USB4. Check that your phone or laptop supports it too: the base iPhone 17 is USB 2 only, while the 17 Pro supports 10 Gb/s.

Can I use a USB-C cable with an older iPhone?

The iPhone 14 and earlier use Lightning, so you need a USB-C to Lightning cable. With an 18 W or higher USB-C charger it fast charges, Apple says.

Is USB-C the same as Thunderbolt?

They share the same plug. Thunderbolt is a faster standard that runs over USB-C. A Thunderbolt cable works as a USB-C cable, but a normal USB-C cable may not do Thunderbolt speeds.
