Sunglasses buying guide: UV protection, polarized lenses and the right size

Sunglasses are for your eyes first and your look second. A cheap-looking label can hide poor lenses, and a dark lens can still let UV through. Here's what to check, in plain words, before you buy a pair for Goa, Manali or a long drive.

Check this first: 100% UV protection

The American Academy of Ophthalmology says to look for a label that promises 100% UV protection. A label that says "UV absorption up to 400 nm" (often written UV400) means the same thing and is fine too.

  • Darker isn't safer. The Academy warns not to judge protection by how dark a lens is. Only the UV label tells you.
  • Lens colour is mostly style. Coloured lenses don't necessarily block more UV. Grey keeps colours natural; brown adds a little contrast.
  • Big lenses and wrap shapes cover more of your eye, so less sun gets in from the sides.

Polarized or not?

Polarized lenses cut the glare that bounces off water, wet roads, cars and snow. The Academy points out that polarization itself doesn't block UV, so you still need the UV label.

Where you'll wear themPolarized?
Beaches, boats and water sports (Goa, Andaman, Maldives)Yes, it helps a lot
Snow (Gulmarg, Manali, Auli)Yes, and choose big or wrap lenses
Long drives and bike ridesYes
City walks and shoppingEither is fine

One catch: polarized lenses can make some phone screens and car dashboard displays look dark when you tilt your head. Most people get used to it.

How to read the frame size

Sunglasses list three numbers in millimetres, for example 56-19-140:

  1. Lens width (56 mm): how wide each lens is.
  2. Bridge (19 mm): the gap between the lenses, over your nose.
  3. Arm length (140 mm): from the hinge to the end of the arm.

The easiest way to choose: find the three numbers printed on the inside arm of glasses that fit you well, and pick a pair within 2 or 3 mm.

Which shape suits your face?

There are no strict rules, but these are good places to start:

  • Round face: square or rectangular frames add some angles.
  • Square face: round, oval or aviator frames soften a strong jaw.
  • Oval face: most shapes work.
  • Heart-shaped face (wide forehead, narrow chin): aviators and cat-eye frames balance it.

Before you buy

  • Check the colour and size you click on. One Amazon page often sells several frames, lens colours and sizes.
  • We haven't tested these lenses. UV, polarization and sizes come from each Amazon listing. If you wear prescription glasses or have an eye problem, ask an eye doctor.
  • Test the lenses when they arrive: the Academy suggests looking at a tiled floor through one lens and moving the glasses slowly. If straight lines wobble, return them.
  • Carry them in a hard case in your cabin bag, so they don't get crushed under the seat.

Questions

Are polarized sunglasses better?

They are better at cutting glare from water, roads and snow, which makes beaches, boats and long drives more comfortable. They don't block more UV on their own, so check for a 100% UV or UV400 label too.

What does UV400 mean on sunglasses?

It means the lenses block UV light up to 400 nm, which covers UVA and UVB. The American Academy of Ophthalmology treats "UV absorption up to 400 nm" the same as 100% UV protection.

What do the numbers 56-19-140 mean on sunglasses?

Lens width 56 mm, bridge 19 mm and arm length 140 mm. Compare them with the numbers on the inside arm of glasses that already fit you.

Which sunglasses are best for snow in Manali or Gulmarg?

Polarized sunglasses with 100% UV protection, in a large or wrap-around shape. Snow reflects a lot of sunlight, and UV is stronger at height, so don't skip them on a cold day.

Are cheap sunglasses safe?

They can be, if they say 100% UV protection or UV400. Price and lens darkness don't tell you how much UV a lens blocks; the label does.
