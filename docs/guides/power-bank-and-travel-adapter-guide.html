Power banks for flights and travel adapters: how to choose

Power banks on flights: the rules in short

  • Cabin bag only. IndiGo, Air India and Akasa Air all keep power banks out of check-in bags, because a lithium battery fire in the hold can't be seen or put out.
  • Up to 100 Wh on Air India and Akasa Air. IndiGo's list allows up to 160 Wh.
  • The rating must be printed on it, says IndiGo's list. If the label has worn off, take a different one.
  • Two spare batteries per person on Air India and Akasa Air, and a power bank counts as one.

Most power banks in India are sold by mAh, not Wh. Work it out like this: Wh = mAh x 3.7 ÷ 1,000.

How to choose a power bank

  • Capacity. 10,000 mAh charges most phones about one and a half to two times. Take 20,000 mAh for long trips, treks and places with little power, like Ladakh or Spiti.
  • Output in watts (W). 18 to 22.5 W is fast for phones. Only take 30 W or more if you want to charge a tablet or a small laptop.
  • Ports. Type C in and out is the most useful: one cable charges your phone and the power bank.
  • Weight. A 10,000 mAh power bank weighs about 230 to 280 g; a 20,000 mAh one about 440 g. That counts towards your 7 kg cabin bag.
  • A built-in cable means one less thing to forget, but check it matches your phone (Type C or Lightning).

Type C is the thin two-pin plug that most phone chargers in India use. A thick three-pin Indian plug (type D or M) is the one that usually won't fit. Hotels can also differ from the national list, so one universal adapter is the simple answer.

Adapter or converter?

A travel adapter only changes the shape of the plug. It does not change the voltage. Our adapter picks all say so on their listings.

  • Phone, laptop and camera chargers usually say "100-240V" on the label. They work everywhere with just an adapter.
  • Hair dryers, straighteners and kettles from India are often 230 V only. They're fine in Dubai, Thailand or the UK, but in the US (120 V) or Japan (100 V) they won't work properly. Check the label, or buy a dual-voltage one.

Questions

Which power bank is allowed on flights in India?

One rated at 100 Wh or less is allowed on Air India, Akasa Air and IndiGo, in your cabin bag only. That covers every 10,000 and 20,000 mAh power bank. IndiGo allows up to 160 Wh.

Is a 20,000 mAh power bank allowed on a flight?

Yes, in your cabin bag. 20,000 mAh is about 74 Wh, which is under the 100 Wh limit on Air India and Akasa Air (IndiGo allows up to 160 Wh). It is not allowed in your check-in bag.

Can I put a power bank in my check-in bag?

No. IndiGo, Air India and Akasa Air don't allow power banks in check-in bags. Take it out before you drop your bag, or it may be pulled out at security.

Which travel adapter do I need for Thailand, Dubai or the UK?

Thailand uses types A, B, C and O, so a two-pin Indian charger often fits, but a three-pin plug won't. Dubai and the UK use type G (three square pins), so you need an adapter. A universal adapter covers all three.

Does a travel adapter change the voltage?

No. It only changes the shape of the plug. Phone and laptop chargers marked 100-240V work anywhere. A 230 V-only hair dryer won't work properly in the US or Japan.

Can I use my Indian charger in Nepal?

Yes. Nepal uses the same round-pin plugs as India (types C, D and M) at 230 V, so you don't need an adapter.
