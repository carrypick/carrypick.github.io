What size luggage do you need? A simple guide by trip length

Updated 6 Nov 2026 · 2 min read

Buying the wrong size suitcase is a common mistake. Too small, and you sit on it to close it. Too big, and you pay for extra baggage or drag a half-empty bag around. Here's how to pick the right size.

Quick answer: size by trip length

These are rough guides. Every brand's sizes differ a little, so always check the litres and dimensions on the listing. Seeing sizes in inches? Our luggage size chart converts them to cm, and we explain 20 inch, 24 inch and 28 inch trolleys in detail.

  • Weekend (1–3 days): a duffle or a backpack, about 30–40 litres.
  • Short trip (2–4 days): a cabin trolley, about 55 cm tall and 35–45 litres.
  • One week (5–8 days): a medium check-in trolley, about 65–69 cm tall.
  • Long holiday or family trip (10+ days): a large trolley, about 75–79 cm tall. Check the total size first: most large bags we checked are listed over the 158 cm airline limit.

Check your airline first

Bag size only matters if the airline accepts it. For example, IndiGo's official baggage page allows a 7 kg cabin bag with a total size of 115 cm (length + width + height), and 15 kg of check-in baggage on domestic flights for standard fares. Rules vary by airline and fare, so check before you fly. Our cabin bag guide explains this in detail.

Remember that the bag's own weight counts. A hard cabin trolley often weighs 2.5–3 kg, and a medium one around 3.5 kg.

Hard or soft luggage?

  • Hard shell (polypropylene or polycarbonate): protects things inside better, handles rain well and is easy to wipe clean. It doesn't stretch, so you can't overpack it. Most popular for flights.
  • Soft (fabric or leatherette): lighter, can squeeze into tight spaces like train berths or car boots, and often has outside pockets. It protects fragile items less.
  • Duffle bags: the lightest and most flexible, but you carry them by hand or on your shoulder. Great for weekends, trains and road trips.

Before you buy

  1. Decide your usual trip length.
  2. Check your airline's cabin and check-in limits.
  3. Compare the bag's own weight. Lighter leaves more room for your things.
  4. Pick hard shell for flights, soft or duffle for trains and road trips.
  5. Weigh the packed bag at home with a luggage scale.
