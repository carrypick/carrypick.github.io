Winter jacket for travel: what to buy for Kashmir, Manali, Gulmarg or a Europe winter trip

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 12 min read

The jacket is the most expensive thing you'll pack for a winter trip, and the easiest to get wrong. A thick coat that's perfect for a Delhi winter morning won't keep you warm in Gulmarg's snow at −5 °C, and a heavy, bulky one is a nuisance on a flight to Europe, where trains, shops and museums are heated. This guide explains how warm a jacket you need for Kashmir, Manali, Gulmarg, Shimla and a Europe winter trip, the difference between down and synthetic fill, and how to read the "water repellent" and "waterproof" words on a listing. Then it covers what goes under the jacket, because layers do half the work.

What that means for your jacket:

  • Gulmarg, Sonamarg, Solang Valley or any snow play: below freezing, often wet snow. You want your warmest jacket, a hood, and waterproof gloves and shoes. A down jacket, or a thick synthetic one listed for 0 °C or below.
  • Srinagar, Manali and Pahalgam town: cold mornings and nights around or below freezing, milder sunny afternoons. A warm puffer over thermals and a fleece.
  • Shimla, Mussoorie and Darjeeling: cool, rarely below freezing in December. A medium padded jacket over a sweater is enough for most days.
  • Switzerland, Austria, Germany and Prague: around freezing, sometimes snow, often grey and damp. A warm jacket that copes with drizzle.
  • Paris, London, Amsterdam: cool and damp rather than freezing. Rain and wind matter more than thickness: a water-resistant, hooded jacket over a sweater.
  • Rome and southern Europe: mild by day. A light padded jacket or a sweater and a rain jacket.

Check the forecast for your dates: these are averages, and a cold wave can bring snow to places that usually don't get it.

Layers first: the jacket is only the top one

  1. Thermals next to your skin. They keep warm air close to your body.
  2. A sweater or fleece in the middle. This holds in most of the heat.
  3. The jacket on the outside. It stops wind, snow and rain, and adds warmth.

With good layers, you can buy a lighter jacket than you think, and take a layer off in a heated train, café or museum. In Europe and Seoul, you'll be indoors for half the day. In Kashmir, hotels and houseboats aren't always fully heated, so pack warmer thermals for the night.

Down or synthetic fill?

Puffer and padded jackets are filled with either down (the soft feathers of ducks or geese) or a synthetic fibre, usually polyester. The listing says which: look for the fill or "material composition".

DownSynthetic (polyester) fill
Warmth for its weightWarmest for its weightNeeds more bulk for the same warmth
Packs small for a flightVery smallBigger
When wetClumps and loses most of its warmthKeeps some warmth when damp
WashingOften hand wash or special careOften machine washable
Best forDry cold: Gulmarg, Ladakh, Switzerland, CanadaWet snow, rain, drizzle, kids, everyday use
  • A listing that says "down" should name it in the fill. Some jackets are called "down jacket" in the title, but the listing says "down-like warmth" and the fill is polyester. That's a synthetic jacket, which is fine, but know what you're buying.
  • Fill power (for example 650-fill or 700-fill) appears only on real down jackets. The higher the number, the more loft (warmth) each gram of down gives. It doesn't tell you how much down is inside, so a thin 800-fill jacket can be less warm than a thick 600-fill one.
  • "Listed for 0 °C" or "warm down to 0 °C" is the seller's own rating, not a test standard. Treat it as a guide, and add layers below that.
  • For wet snow or rain over a down jacket, wear a thin waterproof shell or a poncho on top.

Water repellent, water resistant or waterproof? Reading the listing

Listings use these words loosely. Here's how to read them, from least to most protection:

  • "Water repellent": the fabric has a coating that makes light drizzle and snowflakes roll off. It will soak through in steady rain or if you sit in wet snow.
  • "Water resistant": usually the same as water repellent, or a little better. Fine for a short shower in Paris, not for an afternoon of snow play in Gulmarg.
  • "Waterproof": the fabric is meant to keep water out. A real waterproof jacket usually mentions a membrane or coating, and taped or sealed seams (water gets in through stitching otherwise).
  • A number in mm (for example 5,000 mm or 10,000 mm) is a waterproof rating some listings give: the higher it is, the more water pressure the fabric takes before it leaks. Many listings give no number. If there's no number and no mention of sealed seams, treat the jacket as water repellent.
  • Brand names such as Omni-Shield or DWR are water-repellent coatings. Names such as Omni-Tech or OutDry are waterproof systems. The listing should say which.

What to look for, besides the fill

  • A hood, ideally adjustable or detachable. Wind in Kashmir and Switzerland is cold.
  • Length: a jacket that covers your hips is warmer than a short bomber.
  • Cuffs and hem that close: elastic or adjustable, so cold air doesn't blow in.
  • Zip pockets for your phone and hands.
  • Weight: check the listed weight if you're flying with a 7 kg cabin limit. Wear the jacket on the plane anyway: it doesn't count towards your bag's weight.
  • Packable: some jackets pack into a pouch, handy for warm Europe trains or a Kashmir day that turns sunny.
  • Care label: some jackets are dry clean or hand wash only. Synthetic ones are often machine washable.
  • Size: you'll wear a sweater and thermals under it, so check the brand's size chart and allow room. Our clothing size converter helps with UK, US and EU sizes.

Thermals

  • Cotton thermals are soft and fine for sightseeing, cold nights and sleeping.
  • Synthetic or blended thermals dry faster and suit walking, snow play and heated trains, where you'll sweat and then cool down.
  • Take two sets, so one can dry.

Questions

Which winter jacket is best for a Kashmir trip?

For Gulmarg and snow play in December or January, a down jacket or a thick synthetic puffer with a hood, worn over thermals and a fleece. Gulmarg averages about −5 °C at night in December, says IMD. For Srinagar alone, a warm puffer over layers is enough. Add waterproof gloves and shoes for snow.

What should I wear in Kashmir in December?

Two thermal sets, a sweater or fleece, a warm hooded jacket, warm trousers, a cap that covers your ears, waterproof gloves and waterproof shoes with grip. Srinagar averages about 10 °C by day and −1 °C at night, and Gulmarg about 4 °C and −5 °C.

Which winter jacket is best for a Europe trip?

A warm, hooded, packable jacket that copes with drizzle, worn over a sweater and thermals. Paris averages about 8 °C by day in December and Zurich about 3 °C, and museums, trains and shops are heated, so a lighter jacket with layers beats a heavy coat.

Down jacket or synthetic jacket: which is better for travel?

Down is the warmest for its weight and packs smallest, so it suits dry cold such as Gulmarg, Switzerland or Canada. Synthetic fill keeps more warmth when damp and is often machine washable, so it suits wet snow, rain and everyday use. Over down, wear a waterproof shell in wet weather.

What does 650-fill or 700-fill mean on a down jacket?

Fill power measures how much loft (warmth) each gram of down gives: the higher the number, the warmer for its weight. It doesn't say how much down is inside, so compare the listed weight and thickness too.

Is a water-repellent jacket waterproof?

No. "Water repellent" means light rain and snow roll off, but it will soak through in steady rain or wet snow. A waterproof jacket should mention a membrane and sealed or taped seams. If the listing doesn't, treat it as water repellent.

Which thermal wear is best for travel?

Cotton thermals are soft and fine for sightseeing and sleeping. Synthetic or blended ones dry faster and suit walking, snow play and heated trains. Take two sets so one can dry.

Do I need waterproof gloves for Gulmarg?

For snow play or skiing, yes. Knit gloves soak through quickly in wet snow. Take gloves listed as waterproof, and a pair of knit ones for the town.
