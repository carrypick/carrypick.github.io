Polycarbonate vs polypropylene trolley bags: which is better?

Updated 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Most hard trolleys in India are polycarbonate (PC) or polypropylene (PP). Luggage sellers usually say PC is tougher and PP is lighter. We checked the weight part against real listings, and the difference was small.

Quick answer

  • Choose polycarbonate if your bag will be checked in often and you want a tougher shell. Expect to pay a little more.
  • Choose polypropylene if you want a light, good-value bag for domestic trips.
  • Don't choose by material alone. The lightest cabin bag we read was polycarbonate, and the model matters more than the material.

Hard or soft luggage?

Hard shells handle rain well, wipe clean and protect what's inside better. Soft bags can squeeze into train berths and car boots, and often have outside pockets, but protect fragile things less.

For flights, most people choose hard. For trains and road trips, soft or a duffle can be easier. See our luggage size guide and the train luggage rules.

Questions

Which is better, polycarbonate or polypropylene?

Sellers usually describe polycarbonate as tougher and polypropylene as lighter and lower in cost. PC suits frequent check-ins, and PP suits most domestic trips. Check the weight and warranty of the bag you're choosing, because they vary by model.

Is polycarbonate stronger than polypropylene?

Luggage sellers generally say so, and describe polycarbonate as very tough and resistant to cracks. We didn't find independent tests, and we haven't tested them ourselves.

Is polypropylene lighter than polycarbonate?

Usually, but not by much. In the listings we read, it was 0.2–0.25 kg lighter in medium and large sizes. In cabin bags, polycarbonate was slightly lighter, from a small sample.

Is polypropylene good for check-in luggage?

Yes, for most trips. Many check-in bags we read were polypropylene, including American Tourister's Liftoff+. Look for 8 wheels, a lock and a good warranty.

What about ABS luggage?

ABS is another hard plastic. Travelpro, a luggage maker, describes it as rigid and says it can be damaged by rough handling or high heat. Only 2 of the listings we read named ABS, so we can't say much about it.

Hard or soft luggage: which is better?

Hard is usually better for flights, because it protects your things and handles rain well. Soft is often easier on trains and road trips, because it can squeeze into tight spaces.
