Kenya safari packing list: what to wear and pack for the Masai Mara, for women and men

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

A Kenya safari is easier to pack for than a winter trip, but it has its own rules. Game drives start before sunrise, when the open vehicle is cold, and by noon the equator sun is strong. Bright colours stand out to animals, dark blue and black attract tsetse flies, and the small planes into the Masai Mara take only a soft bag of about 15 kg. Kenya has also banned plastic carrier bags. This list follows the Kenya Wildlife Service's own packing advice and covers what to wear, what to carry on a game drive and which bag to take.

What Kenya Wildlife Service says to pack

Kenya Wildlife Service's visitor FAQ (dated 11 Feb 2026, read 23 Nov 2026) gives this advice for its parks and reserves:

  • Layers: many parks have warm-to-hot days and cool-to-cold nights. Start the day in layers and take them off as it warms up.
  • Loose long trousers and shirts or T-shirts.
  • A hat and sunglasses for the sun.
  • Lace-up shoes.
  • Natural, earth-toned or khaki colours.
  • In your bag: a torch, insect repellent, binoculars and a small medical kit with antacids, painkillers, antihistamines and cold remedies.

The same FAQ lists vaccines and malaria tablets in its health advice. Ask your doctor or a government vaccination centre well before you travel, and read the health section of our Kenya safari guide.

Kenya weather: cold mornings, hot afternoons

Long-term averages from the Kenya Meteorological Department, published by the World Meteorological Organization, as in our Kenya safari guide (read 23 Nov 2026). Narok is the nearest town to the Masai Mara:

JanuaryAbout 25 °C / 12 °C, dryAbout 27 °C / 9 °C
AprilAbout 24 °C / 14 °C, the wettest monthAbout 25 °C / 12 °C, wet
JulyAbout 21 °C / 10 °C, cool and dryAbout 22 °C / 9 °C, dry
OctoberAbout 25 °C / 13 °CAbout 26 °C / 10 °C, dry
DecemberAbout 23 °C / 13 °CAbout 26 °C / 9 °C
  • Mornings are cold all year. At about 9 °C, an open safari vehicle at 6 am needs a fleece or a light jacket, and a cap.
  • Afternoons are warm, and the sun is strong on the equator. Wear a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • July to October is the main safari season and the coolest time. April and May are the long rains: pack a rain jacket. November has the short rains, usually showers.
  • The coast (Mombasa) is hot and humid all year, about 30 °C. Pack beach clothes if you add it on.

The short list

If you pack only these, you'll be fine:

  1. Your passport with the approved Kenya eTA, printed and on your phone
  2. Your vaccination papers, and travel insurance with medical evacuation cover
  3. Two or three long-sleeved shirts and light trousers in khaki, olive, beige or brown
  4. A fleece or light jacket for cold early-morning drives
  5. A wide-brim hat with a chin strap, sunglasses and sunscreen
  6. Comfortable closed lace-up shoes
  7. Binoculars, a torch and insect repellent
  8. Your medicines and a small first-aid kit
  9. Clean US dollar notes for tips, and a card
  10. A soft bag of about 15 kg if you're flying into the reserve, with no plastic carrier bags

Documents, money and rules

  • Indians need an eTA before they travel, applied for only on the official site etakenya.go.ke or the official app, says Kenya's Directorate of Immigration Services. See the Kenya safari guide for the fee and the documents.
  • Plastic carrier bags are banned. Kenya banned the manufacture, import and use of single-use plastic carrier and flat bags in 2017, says Kenya's National Environment Management Authority (NEMA), whose plastic bag page says "It is illegal to use, import or manufacture plastic carrier bags in Kenya" (read 23 Nov 2026). Pack shoes and dirty clothes in cloth bags or packing cubes, not shopping bags.
  • Carry some US dollars in clean, newer notes for tips on safari, and shillings for small purchases.
  • Ask before you photograph people, including the Maasai.

What to wear on a Kenya safari

  • Colours: khaki, beige, olive and brown blend in and hide dust. Avoid bright white, which shows dirt, and dark blue and black, which attract tsetse flies.
  • Long sleeves and long trousers: they protect you from sun, dust, thorny bushes and mosquitoes in the evening. Loose and light, as KWS advises.
  • Layers for the morning: a T-shirt, a long-sleeved shirt and a fleece or light jacket that you take off by mid-morning.
  • A hat with a brim and a chin strap. Open-roof vehicles are windy.
  • A buff or scarf against dust on dirt roads.
  • Something smarter for the lodge in the evening, with long sleeves against mosquitoes.
  • A swimsuit if your lodge has a pool.

For the game drive

  • Binoculars: the animals are often far away. Each person in the vehicle having their own pair helps a lot. 8x42 is a good size to hold steady.
  • A torch or headlamp for the camp at night.
  • Insect repellent for evenings, and long sleeves.
  • Sunscreen, lip balm with SPF and a water bottle.
  • A power bank for your phone and camera, and spare memory cards. Some camps have power only at set hours.
  • A small day bag or crossbody to keep things handy in the vehicle.

Which bag to take

  • Flying into the Masai Mara? Pack a soft bag of 15 kg or less. Safarilink, which flies from Nairobi's Wilson Airport to the reserve, says its baggage allowance is strictly limited to 15 kg per person, including hand baggage, in soft bags, for most of its destinations (20 kg for some coast and lake routes, read 23 Nov 2026). Its small planes have small, odd-shaped holds that rigid suitcases may not fit. Check your own airline's rule: other operators have their own.
  • Leave your big trolley in Nairobi. Safarilink offers a free secure store at its Wilson Airport office, and many Nairobi hotels store bags too.
  • Driving to the Mara instead? A trolley is fine, but a duffle is still easier in a safari van.
  • Weigh your bag before you leave home. See our excess baggage calculator for the flight from India.

Kenya Airways and IndiGo fly direct from Mumbai to Nairobi (see our Kenya safari guide). Check the baggage shown for your fare before you pay, and see our international baggage rules.

Questions

What should I pack for a Kenya safari?

Long-sleeved shirts and light trousers in khaki, olive, beige or brown, a fleece for cold mornings, a wide-brim hat, sunglasses, sunscreen and closed lace-up shoes. Add binoculars, a torch, insect repellent and a small medical kit, as Kenya Wildlife Service advises, plus your eTA, vaccination and insurance papers.

What should I wear on safari in Kenya?

Loose, light layers in natural, earth-toned or khaki colours, as Kenya Wildlife Service advises: a T-shirt, a long-sleeved shirt and a fleece in the early morning, with a hat and sunglasses. Avoid bright white, which shows dust, and dark blue and black, which attract tsetse flies.

What should women wear on a safari?

Loose long trousers and long-sleeved tops in khaki, olive or beige, a fleece for mornings, a wide-brim hat and closed shoes. A sports bra helps on bumpy roads. Take a dress or smart top for dinner at the lodge, and a swimsuit if there's a pool.

What colours should I not wear on safari?

Avoid dark blue and black, which attract tsetse flies, and bright white, which shows dust. Bright colours also stand out. Kenya Wildlife Service advises natural, earth-toned or khaki colours.

How much luggage can I take on a safari flight in Kenya?

Safarilink's limit is 15 kg per person, including hand baggage, in soft bags, for most destinations, and 20 kg for some coast and lake routes. Other operators set their own limits, so check yours. Leave a big suitcase in Nairobi.

Can I take plastic bags to Kenya?

No. Kenya banned single-use plastic carrier and flat bags in 2017, says NEMA, and it is illegal to use or import them. Pack shoes and laundry in cloth bags or packing cubes.

Is it cold on safari in Kenya?

Early mornings and nights are, all year. The Masai Mara averages about 9 °C at night, and an open vehicle at sunrise feels colder. Afternoons are warm, around 22 to 27 °C. Pack a fleece or light jacket and a cap.

Do I need binoculars for a Kenya safari?

Yes, Kenya Wildlife Service lists binoculars among its safari essentials. Animals are often far from the road, and each person having a pair saves passing one around. An 8x42 pair is easy to hold steady.
