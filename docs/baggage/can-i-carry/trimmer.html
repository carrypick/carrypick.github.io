Can I carry a trimmer on a flight in India?

Checked on 16 Nov 2026 against IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express and Akasa Air rules

Quick answer

Cabin bagWith limits Yes, if it has a built-in battery. A trimmer without a battery goes in check-in only (IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express).
Check-in bagWith limits Yes, switched fully off and protected so it can't turn on in the bag.

Checking more than one thing? See all 47 items on our Can I carry it on a flight? list.

The rule

A trimmer with a built-in battery is fine in the cabin. In check-in it must be fully switched off and protected from turning on. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express put a trimmer without a battery in check-in only. Source

Checked on 16 Nov 2026. Security staff can still refuse any item, and airlines can be stricter or change their rules, so check your airline before you fly.

A beard trimmer or rechargeable trimmer can go in your cabin bag, and in check-in if it is fully switched off. Which bag it goes in depends on whether it has a built-in battery.

What the airline lists say

Three airline lists name trimmers: IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express. All three have the same two rows:

TrimmerCabin bagCheck-in
With a built-in batteryYesYes, fully switched off and protected from turning on (IndiGo)
Without a batteryNoYes

A rechargeable trimmer has a built-in battery, so it can go in your cabin bag. None of the three explains the second row. If your trimmer has no battery, for example one that only works when plugged in, pack it in check-in. Air India's and Akasa Air's pages don't name trimmers.

The battery rules behind it

A rechargeable trimmer has a small lithium battery inside, so it follows the rules for battery devices. Akasa Air allows up to 15 such devices per person, each with a battery of 100 Wh or less. A trimmer's battery is far smaller than that, so the limit won't matter.

In a check-in bag, Akasa Air says any battery device must be completely switched off, protected so it can't turn on, and packed securely. That's the same as IndiGo's rule for trimmers.

How to stop it turning on in your bag

  • Turn on the travel lock if your trimmer has one. Your manual shows how.
  • Put the cap or comb on and keep it in its pouch or box, so nothing can press the button.
  • If the battery comes out, carry it in your cabin bag as a spare battery, with the ends covered. Spare batteries can't go in check-in.

Blades, scissors and grooming kits

  • Scissors are banned in the cabin bag (airport security list). Many grooming kits have a small pair, so take them out or pack the whole kit in check-in. See our nail cutter and scissors rules.
  • Loose razor blades are banned in the cabin. A safety razor with the blades in a cartridge is fine. See our razor rules.
  • Trimmer oil is a liquid, so in the cabin it goes in your clear 1-litre liquids bag, in a bottle of 100 ml or less.

Electric shavers

Electric shavers aren't named on any of the airline pages we checked (IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet and Air India Express), or on the airport security lists based on BCAS rules. BCAS's own list of items banned in the cabin doesn't name them either (we read an archived copy on 21 November 2026, because the link on BCAS's website is broken). A rechargeable shaver is a battery device, so:

  • Cabin bag: every airline we checked allows phones and other small battery devices in the cabin, so this is the safe place for a shaver.
  • Check-in: IndiGo's dangerous goods table and Akasa Air allow battery devices if they're fully switched off and can't turn on. Air India's page keeps phones and laptops with batteries out of check-in, and doesn't mention shavers.

If you're not sure, ask your airline before you fly.

What to do instead

  • Short trip with only a cabin bag: carry a rechargeable trimmer with the travel lock on.
  • Charge it fully at home, so you don't need the charger on a short trip.
  • Corded trimmer: pack it in check-in (IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India Express).
  • Take the scissors out of your grooming kit before you pack it in your cabin bag.

Questions

Trimmer in check-in or cabin bag: which is better?

Yes, if it has a built-in battery. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express allow a trimmer with a battery in both bags. They put a trimmer without a battery in check-in only.

Can I carry a trimmer in my check-in bag?

Yes. It must be fully switched off and protected so it can't turn on, for example with the travel lock on and the cap fitted.

Is a beard trimmer a sharp object at airport security?

None of the airline pages we checked treats it as one, and IndiGo, SpiceJet and Air India Express allow a trimmer with a battery in the cabin. Loose razor blades and scissors are different: they are banned in the cabin.

Can I carry an electric shaver on a flight?

The airline pages and airport lists we checked don't name shavers. A rechargeable shaver is a battery device, and small battery devices are allowed in the cabin, so pack it in your cabin bag. Ask your airline if you want to put it in check-in.
