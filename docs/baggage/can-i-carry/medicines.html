Can I carry medicines on a flight in India?

Checked on 10 Nov 2026 against IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air and airport security rules

Quick answer

Cabin bagYes Keep the medicines you need with you. Liquid medicines over 100 ml are allowed if you need them on the journey and carry the prescription.
Check-in bagWith limits Yes, with a prescription on IndiGo. Akasa Air advises not to put medicines in check-in, because a bag can be late or lost.

The rule

Keep the medicines you need in your cabin bag. Liquid medicines and inhalers over 100 ml are allowed with a prescription. IndiGo accepts medicines in check-in only with a prescription. Source

Checked on 10 Nov 2026. Security staff can still refuse any item, and airlines can be stricter or change their rules, so check your airline before you fly.

Keep medicines in your cabin bag

A check-in bag can be late or lost. Akasa Air advises not to put medicines in check-in, and says it carries them there at your own risk. Keep the medicines you need for the journey, and for the first days of your trip, in your cabin bag or handbag.

On Akasa Air, small medicine packs and inhalers you need on board can also go in your one personal item, on top of your cabin bag.

Do I need a prescription?

IndiGo's list has two rows for medicines:

MedicinesCabin bagCheck-in
With a prescriptionYesYes
Without a prescriptionYesNo

So everyday tablets without a prescription are fine in your cabin bag. In an IndiGo check-in bag, medicines need a prescription.

Syrups and liquid medicines over 100 ml

Medicines are an exception to the 100 ml liquids rule. The airport rule allows liquid medicines and inhalers over 100 ml in the cabin when you carry the prescription. Air India adds a few conditions:

  • You need the medicine during the journey, and you carry no more than the journey needs.
  • The name on the prescription label should match the name on your boarding pass.
  • For prescribed medicine, be ready to show a note from your doctor.
  • Over-the-counter liquid medicine is fine in a quantity just enough for the journey.

Security can ask you what a liquid is, so keep it in its original bottle with the label, and tell the staff about it at the security check.

Insulin, syringes and ice packs

  • Insulin over 100 ml is allowed in the cabin with the prescription. Expect a check at security for the needles. See the insulin rule.
  • Ice and other things used for medical needs, such as keeping insulin cold, are allowed even though they aren't medicines (Air India).
  • Medical oxygen needs the airline's approval first. Declare it at check-in (airport security list).

Before you pack

  • Keep tablets in their strips or bottles with the label. Loose tablets are harder to explain at security.
  • Carry the prescription, or a photo of it on your phone as a backup.
  • Pack a few extra days of any medicine you take every day, in case your return is delayed.
  • Flying abroad? Some medicines that are sold freely in India are controlled in other countries. Check the rules of the country you're visiting before you fly.

What to do instead

  • If your medicines are in your check-in bag, move the ones you need on the journey to your cabin bag.
  • On IndiGo, keep medicines without a prescription in the cabin, or carry the prescription.
  • Big bottles of syrup: carry only what the journey needs in the cabin, and the rest in check-in with the prescription.

Questions

Can I carry medicines in my cabin bag?

Yes. Keep the medicines you need with you. Liquid medicines and inhalers over 100 ml are allowed with a prescription.

Do I need a prescription to carry tablets on a flight?

Not for the cabin: IndiGo's list allows medicines without a prescription in the cabin bag. In an IndiGo check-in bag, medicines need a prescription.

Can I carry cough syrup on a flight?

Yes. A bottle of 100 ml or less goes in your clear liquids bag. A bigger bottle is allowed in the cabin if you need it on the journey and carry the prescription.

Can I carry insulin and syringes on a flight?

Yes, in your cabin bag with the prescription. Expect a check at security for the needles. Ice to keep insulin cold is allowed too (Air India).

Should I put medicines in my check-in bag?

Better not. Akasa Air advises against it, because a check-in bag can be late or lost, and IndiGo accepts medicines in check-in only with a prescription.
