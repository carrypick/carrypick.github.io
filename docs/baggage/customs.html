Indian customs rules for international passengers (2026)

Checked on 15 Nov 2026 against the Baggage Rules, 2026 and other official CBIC and RBI documents

Coming home from Dubai, the US or anywhere abroad? Here's what Indian Customs lets you bring in without paying duty, what you must declare, and how much duty you pay on the rest. India's new Baggage Rules, 2026 apply since 2 February 2026.

Quick answer

  • Duty-free allowance: goods worth ₹75,000 per person for Indian residents, NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreigners living in India; ₹25,000 for foreign tourists. Only when you arrive by air or sea.
  • Also duty free: your used personal things, and one new laptop if you're 18 or older.
  • Alcohol and cigarettes: up to 2 litres of alcohol, and 100 cigarettes (or 25 cigars or 125 g of tobacco).
  • Gold jewellery: 20 g for men and 40 g for women, only if you lived abroad for more than one year.
  • Over the limit? Duty is 35% of the value above your allowance. Declare it on the Atithi app and use the Red Channel.
  • Cash: declare foreign currency notes over US$5,000, or more than US$10,000 in all. Indian notes: up to ₹25,000. See cash limits.

This page explains the rules in simple words. It is not legal advice. Customs officers make the final decision, and rules and duty rates can change, so check with Indian Customs (CBIC) or the Customs help desk at the airport if you're unsure.

Duty-free allowance by passenger

This is the value of new goods (gifts, shopping, electronics) you can bring in free of duty, on top of your used clothes and personal things.

Tourist of Indian origin, including NRIs and OCI cardholders₹75,000
Foreigner with a visa other than a tourist visa, staying in India more than 6 months₹75,000

  • It's per person. A family can't add up allowances to clear one costly item.
  • Duty is only on the extra. If you bring goods worth more than your allowance, you pay duty on the value above it.
  • Keep your shop bills with you. Customs uses the value of the goods.

Not covered by the allowance

These can't use your ₹75,000 or ₹25,000 allowance:

  • Alcohol or wine over 2 litres (see what 2 litres means in bottles).
  • Cigarettes over 100, cigars over 25, or tobacco over 125 g.
  • Gold or silver that isn't jewellery, such as bars, biscuits and coins.
  • Televisions.
  • Firearms, and more than 50 cartridges.

Goods in commercial quantity, meaning more than you'd need for yourself and your family, can't come in as baggage at all, even if you offer to pay duty.

How much duty you pay

  • 35% of the value above your allowance. This rate comes from CBIC's baggage duty notification, updated for the 2026 rules.
  • Example: an Indian resident brings a new phone worth ₹1,00,000 and nothing else. ₹25,000 is above the allowance, and 35% of that is ₹8,750. The officer works out the final amount, which can include a surcharge.
  • The 35% rate doesn't cover alcohol over 2 litres, extra cigarettes, gold or silver bars and coins, flat-screen TVs and firearms. Those pay the normal customs duty, which is usually higher.
  • Gold has its own rules and duty rate for people who lived abroad for 6 months or more. See how much gold you can bring.

You pay at the Red Channel before you leave the airport. If you can't pay, Customs can keep the goods and give you a detention receipt, so you can clear them later or take them back when you fly out.

Green Channel or Red Channel?

Walk through the Green Channel only if you have nothing to declare. Declare your baggage and go to the Red Channel if you carry:

  • goods worth more than your duty-free allowance;
  • jewellery beyond what you wear every day, or more than your jewellery allowance;
  • gold bars or coins, a television, a drone or a satellite phone;
  • meat, fish, poultry or dairy products, or seeds, plants, fruits or flowers;
  • a pet;
  • more than ₹25,000 in Indian notes, foreign currency notes worth more than US$5,000, or more than US$10,000 in foreign currency notes and travellers' cheques together (see our cash and currency limits).

How to declare

Fill in the Indian Customs Declaration Form on the Atithi app or website (or on ICEGATE). You can file it up to 3 days before you land and change it until you arrive. If you couldn't file online, the officer can let you declare on arrival.

Walking through the Green Channel with goods you should declare can lead to confiscation of the goods, a fine and a penalty, and in serious cases arrest.

Things you can't bring into India

  • Narcotic drugs.
  • Wildlife products.
  • Fake Indian currency notes or coins.
  • Maps and books that show India's borders wrongly.
  • Fake branded goods (goods that break trademark or copyright rules).
  • Some live birds and animals.

Taking valuables abroad and bringing them back

Taking new jewellery, a camera or a costly laptop on a trip? Before you leave India, ask for an export certificate on the Atithi app. It lists the items, so you can bring them back duty free. It's valid until you first return to India, or for 6 months, whichever comes first.

Foreign tourists can get a temporary import certificate on arrival for costly things they'll take back, such as wedding jewellery. Show the items and the certificate to Customs when you leave.

Moving back to India

If you're moving home (transfer of residence), you get a bigger allowance for personal and household things, including one of each listed appliance such as a TV, fridge or washing machine.

Time abroadDuty-free value
3 to 12 months₹1.5 lakh
At least 1 year in the last 2 years₹3 lakh
2 years or more₹7.5 lakh

For the ₹3 lakh and ₹7.5 lakh limits, you can't have used this benefit in the past 3 years. For ₹7.5 lakh, your short visits to India in the last 2 years must add up to 6 months or less. Customs can relax some conditions in special cases.

Coming from Dubai or the Gulf?

The same rules apply whichever country you fly in from. The big three questions from Dubai travellers:

  • Gold: jewellery up to 20 g (men) or 40 g (women) is free only after more than a year abroad. Gold bars and coins always need declaring. See our gold rules.
  • iPhones and electronics: they use your ₹75,000 allowance. One new laptop is extra. See our rules for iPhones and laptops.
  • Alcohol: 2 litres per passenger. Bottles from the duty-free shop count too. See our alcohol and cigarette limits.

Flying the other way, from India to Dubai? The UAE has its own customs limits for gifts, cash and medicines. See what you can carry from India to Dubai.

Bringing back gifts? Weigh your bags before you leave for the airport. Gifts and bottles add up fast, and most airlines won't take a single bag over 32 kg. See the limits on our international baggage rules page.

Questions

What is the duty-free allowance in India in 2026?

₹75,000 per person for Indian residents, NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreigners on a visa other than a tourist visa, and ₹25,000 for foreign tourists, when you arrive by air or sea. This applies since 2 February 2026 under the Baggage Rules, 2026.

Is the duty-free allowance per person or per family?

Per person. Family members can't add up their allowances to clear one costly item, and infants get no allowance beyond their used personal things.

How much alcohol can I bring into India duty free?

Up to 2 litres of alcohol or wine per passenger. Alcohol over 2 litres doesn't count towards your allowance and pays duty.

How many cigarettes can I bring into India?

Up to 100 cigarettes, or 25 cigars, or 125 g of tobacco.

How much customs duty do I pay above the allowance?

35% of the value above your duty-free allowance. Alcohol over 2 litres, extra cigarettes, gold bars and coins and flat-screen TVs pay normal customs duty instead.

Can I bring an iPhone and a laptop from abroad?

Yes. One new laptop is duty free for passengers aged 18 or more. A new phone counts towards your ₹75,000 allowance (₹25,000 for foreign tourists). The phone you already use is a personal thing and is free.

Do I need to fill in a customs form when I land in India?

Only if you carry goods to declare, such as goods above your allowance, gold bars, pets or currency above the limits. File the declaration on the Atithi app up to 3 days before you land, then use the Red Channel.

Is there a duty-free allowance when entering India by road from Nepal?

No. Passengers arriving by land get no duty-free allowance, only their used personal things.
