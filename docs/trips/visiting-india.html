Visiting India for the first time: start here

India is one of the most rewarding trips in the world, and one of the most surprising the first time. This page is the short, practical "start here" for visitors from abroad: the visa, what happens when you land, what to pack, how to pay, and where to go first. Each part links to a full guide, and every rule comes from an official Indian government source, with the date we checked it.

  2. Pick your months and route. October to March suits most first trips. → Best time to visit India and first routes

The visa in one minute

  • Most visitors need a visa. For a holiday, the easy one is the e-Tourist Visa, for 30 days, 1 year or 5 years.
  • Use only indianvisaonline.gov.in. The government says it's the only official e-Visa website. Unofficial look-alike sites charge extra, so check that the address ends in .gov.in.
  • Apply at least 4 days before you arrive, and up to 120 days before.
  • Your passport must be valid for 6 months when you apply, with 2 blank pages.
  • Arrive at an e-Visa airport or seaport, such as Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Kochi, Goa or Jaipur. You can't enter by land with an e-Visa.

Fees by nationality, the photo rules and the full list are in our India e-Visa guide.

When you land

  • e-Arrival card: foreigners can fill it in online within 72 hours before arrival, on the e-Visa website, the Bureau of Immigration website or the "Indian Visa Su-Swagatam" app. It's not a visa.
  • Immigration takes your fingerprints and photo.
  • Yellow fever card: needed if you arrive within 6 days of leaving a yellow fever country.
  • Customs for foreign tourists: you can bring in goods worth up to ₹25,000 duty free, plus your used personal things, one laptop (if you're 18 or older), up to 2 litres of alcohol and 100 cigarettes. Above that, declare on the Atithi app and use the Red Channel. Details: Indian customs rules.
  • Cash: no limit on foreign currency, but declare notes worth over US$5,000, or over US$10,000 with travellers' cheques. You can bring up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. See cash and currency limits.

Travel tips for first-timers

  • Plan fewer places. Distances are long and roads are slow. A week is enough for the Golden Triangle (Delhi, Agra and Jaipur); don't add five more cities.
  • Dress to cover shoulders and knees in temples, old cities and small towns. Take your shoes off at every temple.
  • Use prepaid taxi counters or app cabs at airports and stations, and agree the fare first if an auto-rickshaw has no meter.
  • Change money only at banks and authorised money changers, and keep the receipt.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water, and go easy on street food for the first few days.
  • Book trains early. Popular routes fill up, especially from October to March. See the Indian Railways luggage rules.
  • Fog in December and January can delay trains and flights in the north, so leave spare time before your flight home.
  • The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays.

Safety and help

  • Emergency: call 112, India's single emergency number.
  • Incredible India tourist helpline: 1800-11-1363.
  • Read your own government's travel advice for India before you go, for example the UK's or the US's.
  • Keep your passport safe, and carry a copy and a photo of it on your phone.

Questions

Do I need a visa to visit India?

Most visitors do. For a holiday, apply for the e-Tourist Visa on the only official website, indianvisaonline.gov.in, at least 4 days before you arrive.

What is the best time to visit India for the first time?

October to March, for Delhi, Agra, Rajasthan, Varanasi, Goa and Kerala. It's dry and sunny, with cold mornings in the north from December to February.

What should I know before travelling to India for the first time?

Get the e-Visa only from the official .gov.in site, fill in the e-Arrival card within 72 hours before you land, dress to cover shoulders and knees for temples, plan fewer places, use prepaid taxis or app cabs, and drink bottled or filtered water.

How much can foreign tourists bring into India duty free?

Goods worth up to ₹25,000, plus your used personal things, one laptop, up to 2 litres of alcohol and 100 cigarettes. Declare anything above that on the Atithi app.

What is the emergency number in India?

  1. For tourist help, the Incredible India helpline is 1800-11-1363.
