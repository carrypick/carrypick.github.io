Leh Ladakh trip guide: best time, permits, roads and a 7-day plan

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Ladakh at a glance

Best timeJune to September; May and October are colder, and the Manali road may be shut
Weather in LehAbout 24–28 °C by day and 11–16 °C at night in June–August (IMD averages); very dry
HeightLeh airport is at about 3,256 m, one of the highest in the world
First 48 hoursComplete rest in Leh is compulsory, says the Leh administration
PermitsIndians pay the fees online on the Leh permit portal; foreigners need a Protected Area Permit
PhonesOnly postpaid SIMs work reliably, says Ladakh Police
Emergency112

Ladakh is India's high, cold desert: blue lakes, huge bare mountains, white monasteries and roads over passes above 5,000 m. It's also very high from the moment you land. Leh's airport is at about 3,256 m, and altitude sickness is the biggest risk on any Ladakh trip, so the government asks every tourist to rest for the first two days. This guide covers the best months, flying in or driving from Srinagar or Manali, permits for Indians and foreigners, altitude sickness, phones and cash, a 7-day plan and what to pack.

Best time to visit Ladakh

Long-term averages for Leh from the India Meteorological Department (IMD), as tabled on Wikipedia:

MonthDayNight
MayAbout 20 °CAbout 6 °C
JuneAbout 24 °CAbout 11 °C
JulyAbout 28.5 °CAbout 16 °C
AugustAbout 28 °CAbout 15 °C
SeptemberAbout 23 °CAbout 9 °C
OctoberAbout 17 °CAbout 0 °C
  • June to September is the main season. Both roads are usually open, the lakes are thawed, and Leh's days are warm. Leh is very dry: IMD's average is only about 35 mm of rain and snow in a whole year.
  • May is cold but quiet. Nights in Leh are about 6 °C on average, the high passes still have snow, and the Manali–Leh road may not be open yet (see below). Flying in is the easy way.
  • October gets cold fast. Leh's average night is about 0 °C, and the passes and lakes are far colder. The Manali road usually closes after the first heavy snow.
  • Winter (November to March) is for experienced travellers. Ladakh Tourism says winter temperatures can drop to −30 °C. You'll have to fly in, and many hotels and camps close.
  • On the passes and at the lakes, it's much colder than in Leh. Carry warm layers even in July.

Best time by bike or car: June to September, when both roads are normally open. Best time for snowfall: from about October, when the roads start to close, so plan on flying.

How to reach Ladakh

By air: flying to Leh

Leh's Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport (IXL) is the easiest way in, and it's open all year. On the airport's Wikipedia list, Air India flies from Delhi, Chandigarh, Jammu and Srinagar, IndiGo from Delhi, Chandigarh and Jammu (plus Mumbai in season), and SpiceJet from Delhi.

Flying straight to 3,256 m means your body has no time to adjust, so plan two days of rest (see below). Check the cabin bag rules for Indian airlines before you pack.

By road from Srinagar (Zoji La)

The Srinagar–Sonamarg–Zoji La–Kargil–Leh road is usually the first to open in spring. The Border Roads Organisation (BRO) clears it:

  • In 2025, BRO reopened Zoji La on 1 April after a 32-day closure, says PIB.
  • In the winter of 2025–26, BRO's Project Vijayak said the pass was closed for only four days, All India Radio reported in March 2026.
  • The 2027 opening is to be announced. Snow can still shut the pass for a day in spring or autumn.
  • Plan two days, with a night in Kargil. The climb is more gradual than flying, which helps with the altitude.

By road from Manali (Atal Tunnel and Baralacha La)

The Manali–Atal Tunnel–Baralacha La–Leh road crosses several high passes and opens later:

  • In 2026, BRO finished clearing it on 12 May, and it opened to light vehicles over Baralacha La on 15 May. These dates come from news reports in The Tribune; we couldn't find an official release.
  • The 2027 opening is to be announced. Don't plan the Manali road for early May.
  • Plan two days, with a night at Jispa or Sarchu, and an early start for the passes.
  • Outside cars: Manali charges a green tax, and Himachal charges an entry toll. See our Manali guide for the amounts and the official site.

Ladakh trip from Delhi

  • Quickest: a direct flight from Delhi to Leh, with Air India, IndiGo or SpiceJet.
  • By road: Delhi to Manali, which our Manali guide puts at about 550–570 km, then two days to Leh. Or fly or take the train to Jammu or Srinagar, then drive via Zoji La.
  • A good mix: drive in via Srinagar or Manali to adjust to the height slowly, and fly back to Delhi from Leh.

Ladakh permits for Indians and foreigners

Indian citizens don't need an Inner Line Permit for Ladakh's protected areas any more: the Ladakh administration removed it in 2021, news reports said at the time. But you still pay fees for the protected areas:

  • Register on the official Leh permit portal, lahdclehpermit.in, as "Domestic". It says the fee covers the environment (green) fee, the Red Cross Fund and a wildlife fee, and you can pay online without going to the Deputy Commissioner's office. We don't list the amounts: the portal shows them.
  • The domestic circuits on the portal include Nubra (Khardung La, Diskit, Hunder, Turtuk), Pangong (Chang La, Tangtse, Lukung), Tso Moriri (Korzok), Hanle and Umling La.
  • Print the permit or keep a copy on your phone. You'll show it at checkpoints. The portal warns about fake environment fee slips, so use only the official site or a registered travel agent.
  • Some places near the border stay closed to tourists, so check your route on the portal.

Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit (PAP) to go beyond Leh to places like Nubra, Pangong, Khardung La and Tso Moriri:

  • Apply through the same portal ("Overseas") or a registered Leh travel agent, who knows the current rules on group size and how long the permit lasts.
  • Citizens of Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Sri Lanka, and holders of diplomatic, official, UN and journalist passports, need permission from the Ministry of Home Affairs or the Ministry of External Affairs in Delhi, says the portal.
  • The rules may change: in August 2026 the Ladakh administration discussed a single PAP for the whole of Ladakh, except restricted areas. Nothing had been announced when we checked.

Altitude sickness and the first 48 hours

This is the most important part of a Ladakh trip. The Leh administration says all tourists arriving in Leh must have at least 48 hours of acclimatisation before going higher, to places like Khardung La and Pangong.

What Ladakh's official advice says:

  • Day 1: complete rest. Day 2: short walks around Leh only. Day 3: easy sightseeing in Leh, such as Shanti Stupa or Leh Palace. After that: go up by no more than about 500 m a day, says Ladakh Tourism.
  • Road travel doesn't help you adjust, says the Leh health advisory. So rest even if you drove in.
  • Drink 2–3 litres of water a day, eat light on the first day, and avoid alcohol, cigarettes and sleeping pills.
  • Warning signs: headache, nausea, cough, tiredness, poor sleep, loss of appetite and breathlessness. Rest, and don't go higher until you feel better.
  • Danger signs: breathlessness at rest, confusion or a wet cough. Go down at once and get medical help. SNM Hospital in Leh gives emergency oxygen.
  • Medicine: the official pages mention acetazolamide (Diamox), but they give different doses. Ask your doctor before the trip, especially if you have a heart or lung condition, are pregnant or are travelling with small children.
  • A pulse oximeter helps you keep an eye on your oxygen level. Ladakh Tourism gives above 90% as normal.

Phones, cash and fuel in Ladakh

  • Use a postpaid SIM. "Only post-paid mobile connections work in Ladakh due to security restrictions," says Ladakh Police. Prepaid SIMs from other states are unreliable. Network is weak or absent in many valleys.
  • Carry cash. ATMs are available in Leh but sparse elsewhere, says Ladakh Police, so take enough for Nubra and Pangong.
  • Fill up in Leh before long drives, and carry spare fuel on remote routes like Hanle or Tso Moriri. Petrol pumps are few and far apart. Ask your driver or hotel about the next pump before you set off.
  • Download offline maps and tell your hotel your route.

Ladakh trip plan: 7 days

This plan flies in and out of Leh and follows the official 48-hour rest rule.

Day 1: arrive in Leh and rest

  1. Fly to Leh and go straight to your hotel.
  2. Rest all day. Drink water, eat light and sleep early.

Day 2: short walks in Leh

  1. Stroll around Leh market slowly, and get your permit printed if you need it.
  2. Rest in the afternoon.

Day 3: around Leh

  1. Shanti Stupa for the view over Leh, and Leh Palace.
  2. Thiksey and Hemis monasteries in the Indus valley, if you feel well.

Day 4: Leh to Nubra Valley over Khardung La

  1. Leave early for Khardung La. Don't stay long at the top.
  2. Diskit monastery and the sand dunes at Hunder. Stay in Nubra.

Day 5: Nubra to Pangong

  1. Drive to Pangong Lake by the Shyok road if it's open, or back via Leh and Chang La.
  2. Sunset at the lake. Nights at Pangong are cold, even in summer.

Day 6: Pangong to Leh

  1. Morning at the lake, then back to Leh over Chang La.
  2. Shopping and a relaxed evening in Leh.

Day 7: fly home

Morning flights out of Leh. Keep a spare day if you can, in case the weather delays your flight.

More days? Add Turtuk in Nubra (1 day), Tso Moriri (2 days) or Hanle's dark-sky reserve (2–3 days). Each needs the right circuit on your permit.

Things to do in Ladakh

  • Monasteries: Thiksey, Hemis, Diskit and Lamayuru. Dress modestly and ask before you take photos inside.
  • High lakes: Pangong, and the quieter Tso Moriri. Don't drive or camp on the lake shore, and take your rubbish back.
  • Nubra Valley: sand dunes and camel rides at Hunder, and Turtuk village near the border.
  • Stargazing at Hanle: the Hanle Dark Sky Reserve, around the Indian Astronomical Observatory, is kept dark for stargazing. Leave headlights and torches off near the observatory.
  • Rafting, trekking and biking: go with a registered local operator, and only after you've acclimatised.

What to wear in Ladakh

What should I wear? Try the what-to-wear helper for Leh's temperatures by month.

Ladakh in summer is hot in the sun and cold in the shade, at night and on the passes. Pack layers:

LayerWhat to wear
1. Next to your skinCotton or quick-dry T-shirts by day, and a thermal set for nights and the passes
2. MiddleA fleece or warm sweater
3. OutsideA windproof jacket; a light down jacket for Pangong, Tso Moriri and October
4. ExtrasWoollen cap, gloves, warm socks, sunglasses with UV protection, sunscreen and lip balm
  • Sun protection matters most. The sun is very strong at this height, so wear a cap, sunglasses and sunscreen every day.
  • Shoes: comfortable walking shoes with a good grip. Add warm socks for the lakes.
  • Bikers: add riding gear, rain covers and a good pair of gloves.

For foreign visitors

  • Read your government's advice. The UK's travel advice (updated 7 October 2026, still current on 25 November) is against all travel to the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, naming Srinagar and Sonamarg. Ladakh is a separate union territory and isn't named, but the Srinagar–Zoji La road passes through Jammu and Kashmir, so fly to Leh or use the Manali road. The US advice (checked 15 November 2026) says "Do Not Travel" to Jammu and Kashmir, except eastern Ladakh and Leh. Travel against this advice can make your travel insurance invalid.
  • Permit: you need a Protected Area Permit for Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri (see above).
  • Visa: apply for the e-Tourist Visa only at indianvisaonline.gov.in.

Emergency numbers

  • Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): 112
  • Police control room, Leh district: 01982-258880
  • Chief Medical Officer, Leh: 01982-252012
  • SNM Hospital, Leh: 01982-252014
  • Tourism office, Leh: 01982-257788
  • Ministry of Tourism helpline, 24 hours, many languages: 1363 or 1800-11-1363

Questions

What is the best time to visit Ladakh?

June to September. Both roads are usually open, and Leh averages about 24–28 °C by day and 11–16 °C at night in June, July and August (IMD). May and October are colder, and the Manali road may be shut.

Do Indians need a permit for Ladakh?

Not an Inner Line Permit, since 2021. But you pay the environment fee, Red Cross Fund and wildlife fee for places like Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle on the official portal, lahdclehpermit.in.

Do foreigners need a permit for Ladakh?

Yes, a Protected Area Permit for Nubra, Pangong, Khardung La, Tso Moriri and other protected areas. Apply through lahdclehpermit.in or a registered Leh travel agent.

How many days are enough for a Ladakh trip?

At least 6–7 days if you fly in, because the first two days must be rest. Add 2–3 days for Tso Moriri or Hanle, and 4 days if you drive in and out by road.

Is the Manali–Leh road open in May?

Usually not in early May. In 2026 it opened to light vehicles on 15 May, news reports said. The 2027 date is to be announced. The Srinagar–Zoji La road usually opens earlier, and you can fly to Leh all year.

Does my phone work in Ladakh?

Only postpaid connections work reliably, says Ladakh Police. Prepaid SIMs from other states are unreliable, and many valleys have little or no network.

How do I avoid altitude sickness in Ladakh?

Rest completely for the first 48 hours in Leh, then go up slowly. Drink plenty of water and avoid alcohol. If you're breathless at rest or confused, go down and get medical help.
