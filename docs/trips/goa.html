Goa for Christmas and New Year: a simple trip guide

Updated 4 Nov 2026 · 10 min read

Goa at a glance

Christmas and New Year are Goa's busiest weeks. The weather is at its best, the churches are lit up, and visitors come from all over India and the world. This guide covers what you need to plan: the weather, which airport to fly into, how to get around, the beach rules that carry big fines, the New Year crowds, and what to pack.

Weather in December and January

Goa is warm and dry in winter. The India Meteorological Department's long-term averages:

MonthAverage highAverage lowRain
December32.9 °C20.9 °CAbout 2.5 mm
January32.4 °C20.2 °CAbout 1 mm
  • Days are hot and sunny, so carry sunscreen, a cap and water.
  • Evenings are pleasant. Goa Tourism says light woollens can be worn in December and January.
  • The sea is open for swimming, unlike in the monsoon. Swim only between 7 AM and sunset, when lifesavers are on duty, and only between the red-and-yellow flags. Never swim where there's a red flag, or after drinking.

Which airport: Mopa or Dabolim?

Goa has two airports, and picking the right one can save you a long taxi ride.

AirportWhere it isBest forRoughly how far
Manohar International Airport, Mopa (GOX)Pernem, far North GoaNorth Goa beaches: Arambol, Vagator, Anjuna, Calangute, BagaAbout 30 km to Calangute; over 100 km to Palolem
Dabolim (GOI)Near Vasco da Gama, central GoaPanaji, Old Goa and South Goa: Colva, Benaulim, PalolemAbout 30 km to Panaji, 35 km to Colva, 45 km to Calangute

Distances are approximate road distances. Both airports have official taxi counters with fixed fares, and Kadamba (the state bus company) runs airport buses to Panaji, Margao, Mapusa and Calangute.

Flying in? Check the cabin bag rules for Indian airlines and what you can carry on a flight. On the way home, remember that dry coconut and camphor are banned in all bags, and spice powders can't go in your cabin bag.

By train and road

  • Train: Goa is on the Konkan Railway. Thivim is the station for North Goa, Karmali for Panaji and Old Goa, Madgaon (Margao) for South Goa, and Canacona is about 3 km from Palolem. Every train stops at Madgaon. Mumbai to Goa takes about 8–9 hours on the fastest trains. See the train luggage rules.
  • Road: Goa is about 594 km from Mumbai, 632 km from Bengaluru and 471 km from Pune. Buses from Mumbai take about 12–16 hours.

Getting around Goa

  • Taxis: agree the price for the whole trip before you get in. Goa Tourism recommends registered taxis and state-backed apps like GoaMiles and Goa Taxi. News reports say Uber and Ola don't operate in Goa.
  • Bike taxis ("pilots"): motorbikes with yellow mudguards and yellow number plates that carry one passenger. Agree the fare first.
  • Buses: Kadamba and private buses connect the towns cheaply.
  • Ferries: free for people on foot and two-wheelers on routes like Betim–Panaji and Old Goa–Divar.

Renting a scooter or car

Scooters are the easiest way to explore, but Goa police are strict about rentals:

  • Rent only from a licensed rental. Police say legal rental vehicles have a black number plate with yellow letters. Renting out a private, white-plate vehicle is illegal, and the vehicle can be seized.
  • Always wear a helmet. The fine is ₹1,000, and you can lose your licence for 3 months.
  • Never drink and drive. Riding drunk can mean a ₹10,000 fine, up to 6 months in jail, or both. Last New Year's Eve, police set up extra checkpoints across Goa.
  • No riding or driving on the beach. The vehicle can be seized.
  • Carry your licence and passport. Foreign visitors need an International Driving Permit from their home country along with their licence.

Beach rules and fines

Goa's 2022 order under its tourist places law lists these as offences in tourist places, with a fine from ₹5,000 up to ₹50,000:

  • Drinking alcohol in the open, and breaking glass bottles. Drink only in licensed shacks, bars and restaurants.
  • Cooking in the open. Cooking equipment can be seized too.
  • Littering.
  • Touting, and selling tickets outside official counters.
  • Driving vehicles on the beach, and water sports outside the marked zones.

Also remember:

  • No nudity on beaches or in public places. It's punishable by law.
  • Don't photograph people who are swimming or sunbathing.
  • Drugs are a serious crime in India, with punishments from 6 months up to 30 years in jail.
  • Plastic: single-use plastic items are banned across India.
  • Turtle nesting beaches: keep away from marked nests at Morjim, Galgibag and Agonda.
  • Loudspeakers stop at 10 PM. From 24 to 31 December 2026 they're allowed until midnight, with permission, and Goa's order allows nothing after midnight.

Christmas and New Year in Goa

  • Book early. November to February is peak season, and Goa Tourism advises booking stays well in advance. Book only places registered with the Department of Tourism.
  • Expect traffic jams. Last New Year's Eve, roads around Calangute, Baga, Anjuna and Vagator were jammed from the evening. Police made the Calangute–Baga stretch from Bharat Junction to Tito's Circle a no-vehicle zone from 3 PM to 6 AM, ran one-way routes, and kept buses out of Calangute from 21 December to 31 January. Expect similar rules this year, and walk where you can.
  • Church festivals: the Feast of St Francis Xavier in Old Goa (3 December), the Immaculate Conception feast in Panaji (8 December), Christmas (25 December) and the Feast of the Three Kings (6 January).
  • Goa Tourism's events: its tentative calendar lists a Sea Food Festival and the Ramponkaranche Fest in Arambol in December 2026, a Food and Cultural Festival in Margao in January 2027, and Carnaval in February 2027.
  • Sunburn: news reports say the December 2026 Sunburn festival is in Mumbai, not Goa.
  • Clubs: after the nightclub fire at Arpora in December 2025, which killed 25 people, authorities inspected clubs and sealed several. Wherever you party, note the exits.

North Goa or South Goa?

North Goa is busier, with more nightlife, shacks and markets. South Goa is quieter, with wider beaches and more green cover.

BeachAreaIn one line

Candolim and SinquerimNorthNear Fort Aguada, a 1600s Portuguese fort with a lighthouse
AnjunaNorthNext to Chapora Fort, known for its Wednesday flea market
VagatorNorthCliffs and small coves below Chapora Fort
ArambolNorthRocky and sandy, with a freshwater lake behind the beach
Colva and BenaulimSouthLong, wide sands about 6 km from Margao
PalolemSouthA curved white-sand bay between two headlands, about 40 km from Margao
AgondaSouthSmall and quiet, and a turtle nesting beach

Beyond the beaches

  • Old Goa: the Churches and Convents of Goa are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, about 10 km from Panaji. The Basilica of Bom Jesus holds the tomb of St Francis Xavier.
  • Fontainhas, Panaji: the old Latin quarter by the Ourem creek, with colourful Portuguese-style houses, cafes and galleries.
  • Dudhsagar Falls: a four-tier waterfall, 1,017 ft tall, inside the Bhagwan Mahavir Sanctuary. Jeep trips usually restart in October after the monsoon. Check the current booking rules with Goa Tourism (helpline 9307915913) before you go.
  • Spice plantations: mostly around Ponda, with tours and Goan lunches.
  • Markets: the Saturday Night Bazaar at Arpora (from 5 PM, November to April), the Anjuna flea market on Wednesdays, and Mapusa's Friday market.

For foreign visitors

  • Visa: most nationalities need an e-Tourist Visa from the official site only, indianvisaonline.gov.in. Apply at least 4 days before you arrive (up to 120 days before). Your passport needs 6 months' validity and 2 blank pages. Both Goa airports, Dabolim and Mopa, accept e-Visa arrivals.
  • e-Arrival card: fill it online within 72 hours before you land. No agent is allowed to charge you for it.
  • No special permit: Goa isn't a protected or restricted area. A normal holiday of less than 180 days needs no FRRO registration.
  • Money: a visitor arriving by air may bring up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Declare foreign currency to Customs if your cash and travellers' cheques are over USD 10,000, or your cash alone is over USD 5,000. Change money only at banks or authorised money changers, and ask for a receipt.
  • Valuables: keep your passport and cash in the hotel locker, and carry a copy of your passport and visa.

Emergency numbers

  • Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): 112
  • Police: 100. Ambulance: 108. Fire: 101.
  • Goa Tourism helpline: 1364
  • Women's helpline: 1091

Questions

Which airport is best for Goa?

Fly into Mopa (GOX) if you're staying in North Goa, near Arambol, Anjuna, Calangute or Baga. Fly into Dabolim (GOI) for Panaji, Old Goa and South Goa beaches like Colva and Palolem.

Can you drink alcohol on Goa beaches?

No. Drinking in the open, including on beaches, is banned, with fines from ₹5,000 up to ₹50,000. You can drink in licensed shacks, bars and restaurants.

What is the weather in Goa in December?

Warm and dry. The average high is about 33 °C and the average low about 21 °C, with almost no rain. Evenings are pleasant.

Do foreigners need a special permit for Goa?

No. Goa isn't a protected or restricted area. Most foreign visitors need only an e-Tourist Visa from indianvisaonline.gov.in and the online e-Arrival card.

Can I rent a scooter in Goa?

Yes, from licensed rentals with black number plates and yellow letters. Wear a helmet (the fine is ₹1,000), never ride drunk, and carry your licence. Foreign visitors need an International Driving Permit.

Until what time can music play on New Year's Eve in Goa?

Until midnight for outdoor loudspeakers, with permission. Goa's order allows this only from 24 to 31 December 2026, and nothing after midnight. On other nights, loudspeakers must stop at 10 PM.
