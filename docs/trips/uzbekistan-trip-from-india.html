Uzbekistan trip from India: the e-visa, direct flights to Tashkent, a 7-day Samarkand and Bukhara plan and the best time to go

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 13 min read

Uzbekistan is one of the most talked-about new trips for Indians: the blue-tiled madrasas of the Registan in Samarkand, the old city of Bukhara, the walled town of Khiva, and the metro stations and bazaars of Tashkent. It's about 1,580 km from Delhi, under 3 hours by direct flight, and Indians can get an e-visa online in a few days. There are many links with India too: Babur, who founded the Mughal empire, was born in the Fergana Valley, and Indian films are popular. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day plan by fast train, the weather by month, getting around and food for vegetarians.

Uzbekistan visa for Indians

Indians can get an Uzbekistan e-visa online. India is on the list of countries that can apply, says the Embassy of Uzbekistan in New Delhi (read 23 Nov 2026).

  • Apply only on the official portal, e-visa.gov.uz. Many other websites look official and charge extra.
  • e-Visa fee: US$20 for a single entry, US$35 for a double entry and US$50 for multiple entries, says the portal. You pay online by card, and the bank may add a fee. The fee isn't refunded if the visa is refused.
  • Apply at least 3 working days before you arrive. The portal says an application is decided within 2 working days, not counting the day you apply.
  • Stay: up to 30 days, and you must enter within 90 days of the e-visa being issued, says the portal.
  • You need: a passport photo (35 × 45 mm), a scan of your passport's main page and an email address. Your details must match your passport exactly.
  • Carry the e-visa printed or on your phone, with the same passport you applied with.
  • Entry is still the officer's decision. Carry your return ticket and hotel bookings.

Uzbekistan visa through the embassy

You can also apply at the Embassy of Uzbekistan in New Delhi, for example for a longer stay. It lists the documents (an application form, one photo, and a passport valid 90 days beyond your stay, with a blank page) and its consular fees on its consular section page. It says a visa takes about 2 working days after you hand in your documents. For a normal tourist trip, the e-visa is simpler.

Uzbekistan without a visa: 5-day transit

India is on the embassy's list for 5-day visa-free transit at Uzbekistan's international airports. You need a ticket onward to another country, with the flight within 5 days of arrival, and your airline tells the border authorities. If you're flying Uzbekistan Airways through Tashkent, this lets you see Tashkent or Samarkand on the way. Confirm it with your airline before you book.

Indians don't get visa-free entry to Uzbekistan for a normal tourist trip, and a US, UK or Schengen visa doesn't change that on the official pages we read.

Flights from India to Uzbekistan

  • There are direct flights from India to Tashkent (TAS), says Tashkent Airport's airline list (Wikipedia, read 23 Nov 2026):
  • Uzbekistan Airways: Delhi and Mumbai.
  • IndiGo: Mumbai.
  • Centrum Air: Delhi and Hyderabad.
  • FlyOne: Delhi.
  • Flights are short: about 2½ to 3 hours from Delhi.
  • From other Indian cities, fly one-stop via Delhi or Mumbai, or via Dubai or Sharjah.
  • For Samarkand and Bukhara, take the fast train from Tashkent, or a domestic flight.
  • Air India doesn't fly to Uzbekistan, so we don't show it below.
  • Time difference: India is 30 minutes ahead of Uzbekistan all year.
  • Baggage: check the limits by airline in the international baggage rules and the carry-on size by airline guide.
  • Fares go up for Diwali, Christmas, New Year and Indian school holidays. Book early.

A 7-day Uzbekistan itinerary: Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva

Most first trips from India spend a week on the Silk Road cities, travelling between them by train. This plan starts and ends in Tashkent.

DayPlanEvening
1Land in Tashkent: Independence Square, the Hazrati Imam complex and Chorsu BazaarRide the Tashkent Metro and see its decorated stations
2Fast train to Samarkand (about 2 to 2½ hours): the Registan and its three madrasasThe Registan lit up at night
3Samarkand: Gur-e-Amir (Timur's tomb), Bibi-Khanym Mosque, Siyob Bazaar and the Shah-i-Zinda necropolisStay in Samarkand
4Train to Bukhara: the Ark fortress and the Po-i-Kalyan minaret and mosqueDinner near Lyabi-Hauz pool
5Bukhara: the trading domes, madrasas and old lanes, and the Samanid MausoleumStay in Bukhara
6Train or road to Khiva (6 to 7 hours by road): the walled inner town, Itchan KalaSunset from the city walls
7Fly from Urgench (near Khiva) to Tashkent for your flight home
  • Five days? Do Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara, and skip Khiva.
  • Ten days? Add the Fergana Valley, for silk and ceramics workshops and Babur's birthplace, or the mountains of Chimgan near Tashkent.
  • Book fast trains early. The Afrosiyob high-speed trains between Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara sell out in the busy months.
  • Mosques and mausoleums: take off your shoes where asked, cover your shoulders and knees, and women should carry a scarf.

Uzbekistan weather by month, and the best time to go

Long-term averages from Uzhydromet, Uzbekistan's weather service, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

  • April to early June and September to October are best: warm days for walking around the old cities, and cool nights.
  • Summer is hot and dry, about 34 to 36 °C by day in July, and hotter in Bukhara and Khiva in the desert. Start early and rest at midday.
  • Winter is cold, around freezing at night from December to February, with some snow. The sights are quiet and hotels cost less, but pack warm clothes.
  • Most rain falls from November to April, about 30 to 70 mm a month in Tashkent and Samarkand; summer is almost dry.

Getting around Uzbekistan

  • Fast trains: Uzbekistan Railways runs Afrosiyob high-speed trains from Tashkent to Samarkand and Bukhara, and slower trains to Khiva. Buy tickets on its official site or app, or at the station with your passport.
  • Domestic flights: Uzbekistan Airways flies from Tashkent to Samarkand, Bukhara and Urgench (for Khiva).
  • In Tashkent: the metro is cheap and easy, and ride-hailing apps work in the big cities.
  • Between towns by road, shared taxis leave from bus stations when full. Agree the fare first.
  • Learn a few words. Uzbek and Russian are spoken; English is common in hotels in Samarkand and Bukhara, but less so elsewhere. "Rahmat" means thank you. Many people know Hindi film songs.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Uzbek food is centred on meat, so vegetarians need to plan a little:

  • Non (bread): round tandoor bread, sold everywhere, and very good fresh.
  • Vegetarian dishes to ask for: vegetable samsa (with pumpkin or potato), salads, mastava or lagman made without meat, fried potatoes, and dried fruit and nuts from the bazaars.
  • Plov: the national rice dish is cooked with meat, but some restaurants make a vegetable version on request.
  • Fruit: melons, grapes, apricots and pomegranates are excellent in summer and autumn.
  • Ask about stock and fat. Soups and rice are often made with meat stock or mutton fat.
  • Indian restaurants: Tashkent has several Indian restaurants, and Samarkand and Bukhara have a few. Many tour companies arrange Indian meals for groups.
  • Jain travellers: carry ready-to-eat food, khakhra and theplas for the train days, and check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Uzbekistan

You have three ways to stay online in Uzbekistan:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at Tashkent airport and in phone shops. Shops ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Uzbek som (UZS). Cards work in big hotels and some shops in Tashkent, but carry cash for bazaars, taxis and small towns. ATMs and exchange counters are common in the cities; bring US dollars or euros to change.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Bargaining is normal in bazaars and souvenir stalls.
  • Carry your passport at all times; hotels need it at check-in.
  • Keep your hotel registration slips. Hotels register foreign guests with the authorities; if you stay with friends, ask about registration.
  • Photography: don't photograph military sites, police or the metro's security staff, and ask before photographing people.

What to wear in Uzbekistan

  • Dress modestly at mosques and mausoleums: cover your shoulders and knees, and women should carry a scarf. In the cities, normal casual clothes are fine.
  • Spring and autumn: light layers for the day and a jacket for evenings.
  • Summer: loose, light cotton clothes, a sun hat, sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • Winter (December to February): a warm jacket, thermals, a cap and gloves.
  • Comfortable shoes for cobbled lanes and long days on foot.

Help in an emergency

  • Emergency numbers in Uzbekistan: police 102, ambulance 103, fire 101.
  • Embassy of India, Tashkent: find its contact details on the website of India's Ministry of External Affairs before you travel.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your e-visa and your insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Uzbekistan?

Yes. Indians can apply for an Uzbekistan e-visa online on the official portal, e-visa.gov.uz, at least 3 working days before they arrive. Indians can also transit Uzbek airports without a visa for up to 5 days with an onward ticket.

What is the Uzbekistan visa fee for Indians?

US$20 for a single-entry e-visa, US$35 for a double entry and US$50 for multiple entries, says the official portal. The bank may add a fee.

How long does the Uzbekistan e-visa take?

The portal says an application is decided within 2 working days, not counting the day you apply. Apply at least 3 working days before you arrive.

How many days can I stay in Uzbekistan on an e-visa?

Up to 30 days. You must enter within 90 days of the e-visa being issued.

Can Indians visit Uzbekistan with a US visa?

A US, UK or Schengen visa doesn't give Indians visa-free entry on the official pages we read. Apply for the e-visa; it's quick and online.

Are there direct flights from India to Uzbekistan?

Yes. Uzbekistan Airways flies direct from Delhi and Mumbai to Tashkent, IndiGo from Mumbai, and Centrum Air from Delhi and Hyderabad.

How many days are enough for Uzbekistan?

Seven days covers Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva. With five days, do Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara.

What is the best time to visit Uzbekistan from India?

April to early June and September to October. Tashkent is about 22 °C by day in April and 21 °C in October, on Uzhydromet's averages. July is hot, and winter nights are near freezing.

Is Uzbekistan good for vegetarians?

It takes some planning: most dishes have meat. Ask for vegetable samsa, salads, bread, fruit and meat-free soups, and look for Indian restaurants in Tashkent.

What is the time difference between India and Uzbekistan?

India is 30 minutes ahead of Uzbekistan, all year.
