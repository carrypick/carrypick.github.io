Oman trip from India: the Oman visa for Indians, direct flights to Muscat, a 7-day plan and the best time to go

Updated 23 Nov 2026 · 15 min read

Oman is one of the closest trips abroad from India: about 2 hours' flight from Mumbai to Muscat, with direct flights from more than a dozen Indian cities. It has the grand mosque and old souq of Muscat, forts and date palms in Nizwa, a night in the Wahiba Sands desert, turquoise pools in Wadi Shab, and turtles at Ras al Jinz. Indians can apply for a tourist visa online, or visit for 14 days without a visa if they hold a valid US, UK, Schengen or similar visa. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day plan, the weather by month, getting around and food for vegetarians.

Oman visa for Indians

Indians need a visa for Oman unless they hold one of the visas or residence permits below. Oman's visas are issued by the Royal Oman Police (ROP), and tourists usually need the Tourist Visit Visa (Unsponsored), says Oman's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (read 23 Nov 2026).

  • Apply only on the official ROP site, evisa.rop.gov.om. Many other websites look official and charge extra.
  • The Tourist Visit Visa is granted "for a period of ten (10) days, fourteen (14) days or one month", says the ROP's list of visa types.
  • Apply at least 4 days ahead. The ROP's FAQ says processing times vary and recommends applying at least 4 days before you travel.
  • Use it within one month. An unsponsored tourist visa is valid for one month before use, says the FAQ.
  • The fee is shown on the ROP site when you apply. We couldn't read it on a public official page, so we don't quote one.
  • After approval, the visa and the payment receipt are emailed to you. Print the visa and carry it.

Oman visa for Indians with a US, UK or Schengen visa: 14 days free

This is what most people search for. Oman's Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists India in its "second group" of countries. Citizens of these countries can enter without a visa for 14 days if they either:

  • hold a valid visa for the United States, Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom, a Schengen country or Japan, or
  • are residents of a GCC country (the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait or Bahrain) in a profession that qualifies.

The ministry's conditions:

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months.
  • A return ticket, a confirmed hotel booking, health insurance, and enough money for your stay.
  • The 14 days can't be extended, or changed to any other visa.
  • Husband, wife and children travelling with you can use it too, even if they're not from that country.

Entry is always the officer's decision, so carry printouts of your other visa, return ticket, hotel booking and insurance. If your US, UK or Schengen visa expires soon, or you want to stay longer than 14 days, apply for the e-visa instead.

Visa on arrival in Oman for Indians?

The ROP and the foreign ministry don't list a visa on arrival for Indians. Get the e-visa before you fly, unless you qualify for the 14-day rule above. For a stopover, airlines can ask for a 72-hour Airport Transit Visa for you, says the ROP; check with your airline.

Flights from India to Oman

  • There are direct flights from many Indian cities to Muscat (MCT), says Muscat Airport's airline list (Wikipedia, read 23 Nov 2026):
  • Oman Air: Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kochi, Kozhikode, Thiruvananthapuram, Lucknow and Goa (Mopa).
  • SalamAir: Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Kozhikode and Lucknow.
  • Air India Express: Delhi, Mumbai, Kochi, Kozhikode, Kannur, Mangaluru, Thiruvananthapuram and Tiruchirappalli.
  • IndiGo: Mumbai, Hyderabad and Kochi.
  • Flights are short: about 2 hours from Mumbai and 3 hours from Delhi.
  • For Salalah, Oman Air and SalamAir fly direct from Kozhikode, or fly via Muscat.
  • Air India itself doesn't fly to Oman, only Air India Express, so we don't show Air India below.
  • Time difference: India is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of Oman all year. Oman doesn't change its clocks.
  • Baggage: check the limits by airline in the international baggage rules and the carry-on size by airline guide.
  • Fares go up around Eid, Diwali, Christmas and Indian school holidays. Book early.

A 7-day Oman itinerary: Muscat, Nizwa, the desert and Wadi Shab

Most first trips from India spend 5 to 7 days in the north, with a hired car or a driver. This plan makes a loop from Muscat.

DayPlanEvening
1Land in Muscat and restA walk on the Mutrah Corniche
2Muscat: the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque (in the morning), the Royal Opera House and the National MuseumMutrah Souq for frankincense and silver
3Drive to Nizwa (about 2 hours): Nizwa Fort and souq, and the old village of Birkat Al MouzStay in Nizwa
4Jebel Akhdar or Jebel Shams, Oman's mountains, for views over the canyonsStay in Nizwa or in the mountains
5Drive to the Wahiba Sands desert, with a stop at Wadi Bani Khalid's poolsA desert camp, with sunset on the dunes
6Drive to the coast: swim in the pools of Wadi Shab. Optional night trip to see turtles at Ras al JinzStay in Sur or Ras al Jinz
7Back to Muscat along the coast, past the Bimmah Sinkhole, for your flight home
  • Five days? Do Muscat, Nizwa and one night in the desert.
  • In July and August, go south to Salalah instead, for green hills, waterfalls and the monsoon mist.
  • The Grand Mosque opens to visitors in the morning, except Fridays. Cover your arms and legs, and women must cover their hair.
  • Roads are good, but the mountain road to Jebel Akhdar needs a 4×4, and the desert needs a 4×4 with a driver who knows the sand.

Oman weather by month, and the best time to go

  • October to March is best for Muscat, Nizwa, the desert and the wadis: warm days and pleasant evenings. December to February are the busiest months.
  • It rarely rains. Muscat gets about 13 to 17 mm in each of December, January and February, and almost none in summer.
  • May to September is very hot in the north, about 40 °C by day in Muscat and above 42 °C in Nizwa. Plan sightseeing for early morning.
  • Salalah's monsoon (khareef), July and August, brings drizzle on about 7 or 8 days a month and keeps days near 27 °C, when the rest of Oman is hot.
  • Desert and mountain nights are cool in winter. Carry a warm layer.

Getting around Oman

  • A hire car is the easiest way to see Oman: roads are good and signs are in Arabic and English. Check with the rental company whether your Indian licence is accepted, or carry an International Driving Permit.
  • With a driver: most tour companies offer a car and driver, which is best for the desert and mountain roads.
  • Buses: Mwasalat runs city buses in Muscat and intercity buses to Nizwa, Sur and Salalah.
  • Taxis and apps: use metered taxis or a ride-hailing app in Muscat, and agree the fare first if there's no meter.
  • Muscat to Salalah is about 1½ hours by air, or about 12 hours by road.
  • Learn a few words. Arabic is the language, and English is common in hotels and shops. "Shukran" means thank you. Many shopkeepers also speak Hindi, Urdu or Malayalam.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Oman is easy for Indian travellers: a large Indian community lives there, and Muscat has many Indian restaurants, including vegetarian ones.

  • Indian food: South Indian, Gujarati and North Indian vegetarian restaurants are common in Muscat, especially around Ruwi and Mutrah.
  • Omani food: shuwa and majboos are meat dishes, but you'll find dal, hummus, falafel, salads and flatbreads in many cafés.
  • Halwa and dates: Omani halwa, made with sugar, ghee, rosewater and nuts, and fresh dates are served with kahwa (cardamom coffee) everywhere.
  • Ask about stock. Rice and soups are often cooked with meat or chicken stock.
  • Jain travellers: most dishes use onion and garlic, but Indian restaurants in Muscat can usually cook without them. Carry ready-to-eat food for the desert and mountain days, and check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.
  • Ramadan: during the fasting month, don't eat, drink or smoke in public in daylight. Hotels serve food to guests.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Oman

You have three ways to stay online in Oman:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at Muscat airport and in phone shops. Shops ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply). Mobile signal is weak in the deep desert and some wadis.

Where to book your stay

In Muscat, stay near Qurum or Shatti Al Qurum for the beach and restaurants, or near Mutrah for the corniche and the souq. In Nizwa, stay in the town or in a heritage guesthouse in the old villages. Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Omani rial (OMR), divided into 1,000 baisa. Cards work in hotels, malls and most restaurants. Carry cash for small shops, souqs, fuel stations in the interior and desert camps.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Bargaining is normal in the souqs, not in malls or shops with marked prices.
  • Carry your passport or a copy, and your hotel booking.
  • Photography: don't photograph military sites, police or palaces, and ask before photographing people, especially women.
  • Public behaviour: public displays of affection and drinking alcohol outside licensed hotels and restaurants are not allowed.

What to wear in Oman

  • Dress modestly, outside the hotel pool and beach: cover your shoulders and knees. Women should carry a scarf for mosques.
  • For the Grand Mosque, women need long sleeves, long trousers or a skirt to the ankle, and a scarf over the hair; men need long trousers.
  • Winter (November to February): light cotton clothes for the day and a light jacket for evenings, the desert and the mountains.
  • Wadis: sandals or water shoes and a swimsuit you can wear with a T-shirt and shorts over it.
  • Sun: a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen all year.

Help in an emergency

  • Emergency number in Oman: 9999 for police, ambulance and fire.
  • Embassy of India, Muscat: contact details are on its website.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your visa (or the other visa you entered with), your insurance papers and your hotel booking on your phone.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Oman?

Yes, unless they hold a valid US, UK, Canada, Australia, Schengen or Japan visa, or a GCC residence permit, which allows 14 days without a visa. Others apply online for a Tourist Visit Visa on the Royal Oman Police e-visa site.

Can Indians visit Oman with a US visa?

Yes. Oman's foreign ministry says Indians with a valid visa for the US, Canada, Australia, the UK, a Schengen country or Japan can stay 14 days without a visa. Carry a passport valid for 6 months, a return ticket, a hotel booking, health insurance and enough money. The 14 days can't be extended.

What is the Oman visa fee for Indians?

The fee is shown on the Royal Oman Police e-visa site when you apply. We couldn't read it on a public official page, so check it there before you pay.

How long does the Oman e-visa take?

The ROP doesn't give a fixed time. Its FAQ recommends applying at least 4 days before you travel. The visa must be used within one month.

Is there a visa on arrival in Oman for Indians?

No visa on arrival for Indians is listed on the official pages we checked. Apply for the e-visa before you fly, or use the 14-day rule if you hold a US, UK, Schengen or similar visa.

Are there direct flights from India to Oman?

Yes, many. Oman Air, SalamAir, Air India Express and IndiGo fly direct to Muscat from cities including Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kochi, Kozhikode, Jaipur and Lucknow.

How many days are enough for Oman?

Five days covers Muscat, Nizwa and a night in the desert. Seven days adds the mountains, Wadi Shab and the turtles at Ras al Jinz. Salalah needs 3 or 4 more days.

What is the best time to visit Oman from India?

October to March, when Muscat is about 25 to 30 °C by day, on the Directorate General of Meteorology's averages. For Salalah's green monsoon season, go in July or August.

Is Oman good for vegetarians?

Yes. Muscat has many Indian vegetarian restaurants, and cafés serve hummus, falafel, dal and salads. Ask whether rice and soups use meat stock.

What is the time difference between India and Oman?

India is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of Oman, all year.
