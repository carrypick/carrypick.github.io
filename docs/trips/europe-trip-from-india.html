Europe trip from India: the Schengen visa, a 10-day Switzerland, Paris and Italy plan, and the best time to go

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 18 min read

When to applyAt least 15 days and not more than 6 months before you travel

A first Europe trip is a big dream for many Indian families and couples, and the most popular first plan is Switzerland, Paris and Italy in about 10 days. You need one visa for all three: the Schengen visa, which covers France, Italy, Switzerland and most of Europe. This guide covers which country to apply to, the documents, the multi-year visa rule for Indians, direct flights, a 10-day itinerary, the Swiss Travel Pass, the weather by month and food for vegetarians.

Schengen visa for Indians

Indians need a visa for France, Italy, Switzerland and the other Schengen countries. A Schengen visa allows a short visit of up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the whole Schengen area, says the European Commission's migration and home affairs department (read 21 Nov 2026).

Which country should you apply to?

The EU's rule decides it, not your choice:

  • One country: apply to the consulate of that country.
  • Several countries: apply to the country where you will spend the longest time.
  • Equal time in each: apply to the country you will enter first.
  • Apply where you live. As a rule, you apply to the consulate responsible for the place where you legally live, so most Indians apply in India.

On our 10-day plan below you spend the most nights in Switzerland, so you would apply to Switzerland. If you change the plan and spend most nights in France or Italy, apply to that country instead.

When to apply, the fee and the processing time

  • Apply at least 15 days before your trip, and not more than 6 months before it, says the EU. You may have to book an appointment first, and in peak months appointments fill up, so start early.
  • The fee is €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12. A visa service centre can charge an extra service fee.
  • Processing normally takes 15 days. It can take up to 45 days if the consulate needs to look more closely or asks for more documents.
  • Your fingerprints are taken when you apply (some people, such as young children, are exempt).

Documents for the Schengen visa

The EU lists these documents:

  • Passport valid for at least 3 months after the day you leave the Schengen area.
  • The visa application form.
  • A photo that meets the ICAO standard.
  • Travel medical insurance that covers emergency medical care, hospital stays and repatriation, including in case of death.
  • Proof of your trip: your plan, flight and hotel bookings, proof that you have enough money, and proof that you will come back to India (for example, your job, business or studies).
  • Anything else the consulate asks for. Each country's visa page in India lists its own checklist, so read it before your appointment.

The multi-year visa rule for Indians

Since 18 April 2024, the EU has special rules for Indians who live in India and apply there, says the EU Delegation to India (22 Apr 2024):

  • After two visas used lawfully in the last three years, you can be given a multiple-entry Schengen visa valid for two years.
  • After that, normally a five-year visa, if your passport is valid for long enough.
  • It is not automatic. You still have to meet the entry rules each time.

So your first visa will usually be for your trip dates only. Use it exactly as planned, and later trips get easier.

Do Indians need ETIAS?

No, not with a Schengen visa. The EU's page says ETIAS is an online form for visa-exempt travellers. Indians need a visa, so ETIAS is not for Indian passport holders.

A 10-day Switzerland, Paris and Italy itinerary

This is the classic first Europe trip from India: 3 nights in Paris, 4 nights in Switzerland and 3 nights in Italy. It uses trains between countries, so there are no airport queues in the middle of the trip.

DayPlanNight
1Land in Paris. A walk along the Seine and the Eiffel Tower in the eveningParis
2The Louvre, the Tuileries garden, the Champs-Élysées and the Arc de TriompheParis
3Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur in the morning, or a day trip to Versailles; a Seine river cruise at nightParis
4Fast train to Switzerland (Basel or Zurich), then on to Lucerne. The Chapel Bridge and the old townLucerne
5A mountain day from Lucerne: Mount Titlis, Pilatus or RigiLucerne
6Scenic train to Interlaken. A boat on Lake Brienz or Lake ThunInterlaken
7The Jungfrau region: Lauterbrunnen valley, Grindelwald or the JungfraujochInterlaken
8Train to Italy through the Alps, to Milan, then on to VeniceVenice
9Venice: St Mark's Square, a vaporetto (water bus) on the Grand Canal, Murano or BuranoVenice
10Train to Milan for the cathedral (Duomo), then fly home from Milan
  • Prefer Rome to Venice? Take the train from Switzerland to Milan and on to Rome for the Colosseum and the Vatican, and fly home from Rome. Air India and ITA Airways fly direct from Rome to Delhi.
  • Only 7 or 8 days? Choose two countries, for example Paris and Switzerland, or Switzerland and Italy. Fewer hotel changes means more time to enjoy each place.
  • With parents or kids? Keep one free afternoon every two or three days. Book big sights (the Louvre, the Eiffel Tower summit, the Jungfraujoch) online for a fixed time.
  • Snow in the Alps is most likely from December to March. In summer the high viewpoints still have snow, but the valleys are green, like the photo above.
  • Check train times and book seats on the national railway sites. Many fast trains between countries need a reservation.

Swiss Travel Pass: is it worth it?

Switzerland's trains, buses and lake boats work as one network, and the Swiss Travel Pass covers most of it with one ticket. From Switzerland Tourism's help page (updated 20 Nov 2026):

  • It covers all travel on trains, buses and boats, including panoramic trains such as the Glacier Express. Some panoramic trains need a seat reservation, booked and paid separately.
  • Public transport in more than 90 Swiss towns and cities is included.
  • Free entry to more than 500 museums.
  • Up to 50% off most mountain railways and cable cars. Some summit trips cost extra even with the pass, so check your mountain before you go.
  • 3, 4, 6, 8 or 15 days, in 1st or 2nd class. The Swiss Travel Pass Flex lets you choose the same number of travel days within one month.
  • Children aged 6 to 16 travel free with a parent who has a pass, using the free Swiss Family Card.

On our plan you are in Switzerland for 4 days (days 4 to 7), so a 4-day pass fits. It's most useful when you take several long trains and lake boats. If you will stay in one town and take only one or two trains, single tickets may cost less: compare both on the official SBB or Switzerland Tourism site before you buy, and add the total to our trip budget calculator.

  • May, June and September are the best months for a first trip: mild days, long evenings and green mountains. May and June are also a busy time, so book early.
  • July and August are the warmest, and Rome often goes above 30 °C. Zurich gets the most rain in summer, about 117 to 133 mm a month.
  • December is cold but special: Christmas markets, lights and snow in the Alps. Zurich averages about −2 °C at night. Days are short, so plan fewer sights per day.
  • Rome stays mild in winter, about 12 to 13 °C by day, but November is its wettest month (about 115 mm).

Getting around Europe

  • Trains are the best way between Paris, Switzerland and Italy. They go from city centre to city centre, and you can carry your bags yourself. Book fast trains early.
  • Swiss trains, buses and boats run like clockwork, and the timetables connect. Use the SBB app for times and platforms.
  • In Paris, use the Métro and RER trains. The RER B line runs from Charles de Gaulle Airport to central Paris.
  • In Venice, there are no cars. You walk or take the vaporetto (water bus). Pack a trolley that you can lift over bridges.
  • Validate your ticket before boarding regional trains and buses where a machine asks you to. Inspectors fine people without a valid ticket.
  • Watch your bag and phone in crowded places such as the Paris Métro, the Eiffel Tower area, Rome's Colosseum and Venice's St Mark's Square.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

  • Indian restaurants are easy to find in Paris, Zurich, Geneva, Milan and Rome, and many serve vegetarian and Jain food.
  • Italy is the easiest for vegetarians: pizza margherita, pasta with tomato or pesto, risotto with vegetables, and gelato. Ask "senza carne" (without meat).
  • In France, say "végétarien". Crêpes, cheese, bread, quiche with vegetables and pastries are everywhere. Soups and sauces may use meat stock, so ask.
  • In Switzerland, cheese fondue and rösti (potato) are vegetarian favourites. Supermarkets such as Coop and Migros have ready meals and salads for a cheap lunch.
  • Jain travellers should carry ready-to-eat food, khakhra and theplas for travel days. Check what you can pack in our can I carry it on a flight? list.
  • Tap water is safe to drink in Switzerland, France and Italy, and you can refill a bottle at public fountains in many towns.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Europe

You have three ways to stay online in Europe:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections. A Europe eSIM covers many countries on one plan. Check its country list against your route before you buy.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Where to book your stay

In Paris, stay near a Métro station: around the Eiffel Tower, the Latin Quarter or near Gare de Lyon if you take the train to Switzerland. In Switzerland, Lucerne and Interlaken are the easiest bases, both a short walk from the station. In Venice, hotels on the main island cost more but save travel time; Mestre, on the mainland, is cheaper and a short train ride away. Compare hotels for your dates:

Money and rules to know

  • France and Italy use the euro (€). Switzerland uses the Swiss franc (CHF). Cards work almost everywhere, including small cafés; carry a little cash for markets, toilets and tips.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Keep your passport stamps and visa in order. Overstaying even a few days can make your next visa harder to get.
  • Plugs: France and Italy use round two-pin plugs; Switzerland has its own three-pin plug. Carry a universal travel adapter.
  • Tourist tax: many cities add a small city tax per night to your hotel bill, paid at the hotel.

What to wear in Europe

  • Layers: a T-shirt, a warm sweater or fleece and a windproof jacket work in every season, because mountain viewpoints are cold even in summer.
  • In winter (December to February): a padded jacket, thermal innerwear, gloves, a cap and waterproof shoes. See our winter jacket for travel guide and winter clothes guide.
  • Comfortable walking shoes. You will walk 10,000 to 20,000 steps on most city days. See walking shoes for a Europe trip.
  • Cover your shoulders and knees in churches such as St Peter's Basilica in the Vatican and St Mark's in Venice.
  • A small umbrella or rain jacket for any month.

Help in an emergency

  • 112 is the emergency number across the EU, and it also works in Switzerland.
  • Embassy of India, Paris: contact details are on its website. India also has embassies in Bern (Switzerland) and Rome (Italy).
  • Keep a photo of your passport, visa and insurance papers on your phone and in your email.

Questions

Which visa do Indians need for a Europe trip?

A Schengen short-stay visa. One visa covers France, Italy, Switzerland and the other Schengen countries, for up to 90 days in any 180 days. Apply to the country where you will spend the most nights, or the country you enter first if the nights are equal.

What is the Schengen visa fee for Indians?

€90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12, says the European Commission. The visa service centre can add its own service fee.

How long does a Schengen visa take from India?

Normally 15 days after your appointment, says the EU, but it can take up to 45 days. Apply at least 15 days, and ideally a few weeks, before you fly, and not more than 6 months before.

Is travel insurance needed for a Schengen visa?

Yes. The EU asks for medical insurance that covers emergency medical care, hospital stays and repatriation, including in case of death.

Which country should I apply to for a Schengen visa if I visit Switzerland, France and Italy?

The country where you will spend the longest time. On our 10-day plan that is Switzerland (4 nights). If the nights are equal, apply to the country you enter first.

Can Indians get a multiple-entry Schengen visa?

Yes, with a good travel history. Since April 2024, Indians living in India who have lawfully used two Schengen visas in the last three years can get a two-year multiple-entry visa, normally followed by a five-year visa, says the EU Delegation to India. It isn't automatic.

How many days are enough for a Europe trip from India?

Ten days is enough for a first trip to Paris, Switzerland and Italy, with 3 or 4 nights in each. With only 7 days, pick two countries.

Is December a good time to visit Europe?

It's good for Christmas markets and snow in the Alps, but it's cold (Zurich averages about −2 °C at night) and days are short. For mild weather and green mountains, go in May, June or September.

Which Indian cities have direct flights to Europe?

Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru have direct flights to Zurich (Swiss) and Paris (Air France). Delhi also has direct Air India flights to Zurich, Paris, Milan and Rome, and ITA Airways flies Delhi to Rome.

Do Indians need ETIAS for Europe?

No. ETIAS is for travellers who don't need a visa. Indians need a Schengen visa instead.

Is the Swiss Travel Pass worth it?

It's worth it if you take several trains, buses and lake boats in Switzerland. It also gives free entry to more than 500 museums and up to 50% off most mountain railways. If you stay in one place, compare single tickets first.
