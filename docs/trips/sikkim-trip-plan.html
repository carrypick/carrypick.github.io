Sikkim trip plan: Gangtok, permits, the best time to go and a 6-day route

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Sikkim at a glance

Sikkim is a small Himalayan state between Nepal, Tibet and Bhutan. Most trips start in Gangtok, the capital, and go up to the high lakes and valleys: Tsomgo Lake and Nathu La in the east, and Lachung, Yumthang, Lachen and Gurudongmar Lake in the north. The high places need permits, the roads close after landslides and snow, and the weather changes a lot by month. This guide covers when to go, how to get there, which permits you need, the road status and a 6-day plan.

Best time to visit Sikkim

  • October to December: the clearest views. The monsoon is over, the air is dry, and Kanchenjunga is easiest to see in the mornings. November and December get very little rain in Gangtok.
  • January and February: cold, dry and snowy higher up. Gangtok is chilly but rarely below freezing. Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La and North Sikkim get snow, and permits can be stopped for days after heavy snowfall.
  • March to May: flowers and busy roads. Rhododendrons flower in the Yumthang valley, mostly in April and May. Rain starts to build in April, so clouds often hide the peaks. School holidays make May crowded.
  • June to September: heavy monsoon. Gangtok gets over 500 mm of rain a month in June, July and August. Landslides close NH10 and North Sikkim roads most years. Plan for delays, or go another month.

Want snow? Go to Tsomgo Lake or North Sikkim from about December to March, and keep a spare day in case permits are stopped after snowfall.

Getting to Gangtok

  • By air: fly to Bagdogra (IXB), near Siliguri in West Bengal. From there it's about 120 km of hill road to Gangtok by taxi. Travel sites say 4 to 5 hours; there is no official figure, and landslides can make it much longer.
  • Pakyong airport (PYG), near Gangtok, has no scheduled flights. The Civil Aviation Ministry told Parliament that no scheduled flights have run there since June 2024, as reported on 22 July 2025. An airport committee discussed restarting flights in April 2026, but we found no restart date. Book Bagdogra.
  • By train: New Jalpaiguri (NJP) is the nearest big station, with trains from Delhi, Kolkata and many other cities. Shared and private taxis go up from NJP and Siliguri.
  • No train to Sikkim yet. The Sevoke–Rangpo line, Sikkim's first railway, is now targeted for June 2028, Northeast Frontier Railway said in November 2026.

Going by train? Check the Indian Railways luggage rules. Flying? See the cabin bag rules for Indian airlines and what you can carry on a flight.

Is NH10 open?

NH10, from Sevoke near Siliguri along the Teesta river to Gangtok, is the main road into Sikkim. The 2023 Teesta flood damaged it badly, and it has been closed by landslides in every monsoon since:

  • 2025: closed several times. All India Radio reported it reopened on 17 August 2025 after a six-day closure.
  • 2026: a landslide at 20th Mile (Bardang, Pakyong district) closed it from the night of 7 July 2026. It partly reopened with traffic in turns around 10 August (EastMojo), and one-way traffic started again on 20 September after a mudslide at the same spot was cleared (UNI).
  • Repairs: the central government sanctioned Rs 993.64 crore to develop NH10 from Rangpo to Ranipool, All India Radio reported on 8 October 2026.
  • When NH10 is shut, cars go the long way through Lava and Pedong in Kalimpong district (NH717A). It takes much longer, and we couldn't confirm its status this season.

We found no report on NH10 for November 2026. Ask your hotel or driver on the day, and check the Sikkim Police and Kalimpong Police traffic updates before you set out.

Permits for Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La and North Sikkim

Indians don't need a permit for Gangtok, but you do need one for the high border areas. From the Sikkim government's Tourism and Civil Aviation page:

  • Areas that need a permit: Tsomgo (Changu) Lake, Baba Mandir and Nathu La in the east; Lachung, Yumthang, Yumesamdong (the Zero Point area), Lachen, Thangu and Gurudongmar Lake in the north; and Dzongu.
  • Who applies: permits for Tsomgo, Lachung, Lachen and Gurudongmar are issued online at the 3rd Mile police check post, through registered local agencies. In practice your Sikkim-registered tour operator or taxi applies for you. Nathu La permits come from the Permit Cell in Gangtok.
  • Documents: a passport, voter ID, Indian driving licence or a government ID card, plus two passport photos. The official page says Aadhaar is not accepted as proof of nationality, so carry one of the others even if your operator says Aadhaar works. Children need a birth certificate.
  • Apply at least one day ahead, in office hours. Give your operator your ID copies and photos before you arrive.
  • Tsomgo Lake and Baba Mandir: the police pass is valid for 2 to 5 days, depending on your plan.
  • Nathu La is for Indian citizens only, as a day trip. The official page says permits are issued Tuesday to Sunday, so Monday is closed. Many tour operators say Tuesday is closed too. Ask your operator before you plan around it.
  • Snow stops permits. After heavy snowfall, Tsomgo and Nathu La permits are often suspended for a few days, as in January 2025 (India Today NE).
  • Private cars: trips to the permit areas go in Sikkim-registered tourist taxis booked through registered operators. In August 2026 the state said it was preparing an online permit system in which only Sikkim drivers and operators would run permit-area tours. We couldn't confirm that it has started.

Is North Sikkim open?

North Sikkim has had a hard few years. After the October 2023 flood, Lachen and Gurudongmar were closed to tourists for about 887 days. Here is what the reports say since then:

  • June 2025: heavy rain and landslides, including one at the army camp in Chaten, stranded more than 1,600 tourists in Lachen, Lachung and Chungthang. Tourism in Mangan district (North Sikkim) restarted in September 2025, All India Radio reported.
  • 9 March 2026: permits for Lachen and Gurudongmar Lake started again (India Today NE).
  • 5 April 2026: the road near the Taram Chu bridge on the Chungthang–Lachen road collapsed, and Lachen closed again. Tourists were brought out by 9 April (EastMojo).
  • 29 August 2026: the Lachen road reopened. Since 10 September, tourist vehicles to and from Lachen and Lachung must cross the Toong check post by 4 PM, by order of the Mangan district collector (ANI, 11 September 2026).

We found no later report. Lachung and Lachen appear to be open, but whether Gurudongmar is open this winter is not confirmed, and snow often blocks Gurudongmar and Zero Point in winter. Your operator will know on the day the permit is applied for.

A 6-day Sikkim plan

This plan starts and ends at Bagdogra or NJP. Your operator may change the order to match permits, closed days and roads.

  1. Day 1: up to Gangtok. Leave Bagdogra or NJP by noon to reach Gangtok before dark. Evening walk on MG Marg, Gangtok's car-free main street.
  2. Day 2: Tsomgo Lake and Baba Mandir, and Nathu La if it's open that day and you're an Indian citizen. Start early: clouds and snow often come in after midday.
  3. Day 3: Gangtok to Lachung. A long day's drive into North Sikkim. Remember the 4 PM rule at the Toong check post.
  4. Day 4: Yumthang valley, and Zero Point if the road is open, then back to Lachung or on towards Gangtok.
  5. Day 5: back to Gangtok, or rest a day and visit Rumtek Monastery.
  6. Day 6: down to Bagdogra or NJP. Leave early, and don't book a flight at midday: landslides and traffic can hold you up.
  • Add a day for Lachen and Gurudongmar Lake (Gangtok to Lachen, then Gurudongmar at dawn and across to Lachung). Gurudongmar is very high, so read the health tips below first.
  • Add two days for Pelling in West Sikkim, for the Pemayangtse Monastery and the Rabdentse ruins.
  • Doing 3 or 4 days? Stay in Gangtok and do Tsomgo Lake, and skip North Sikkim: it needs at least two nights.
  • Keep one spare day in winter and in the monsoon for closed roads or stopped permits.

Things to do in Sikkim

  • Tsomgo (Changu) Lake: a high lake on the Nathu La road, frozen in parts in winter. Yaks wait by the shore for photos.
  • Nathu La: the pass on the border with Tibet, for Indian citizens with a permit. It's very cold and windy, even in summer.
  • Yumthang valley: a wide valley above Lachung, with rhododendrons in spring and snow in winter.
  • Gurudongmar Lake: a sacred lake far up in the north, when it's open.
  • MG Marg: Gangtok's car-free promenade for food and a walk.
  • Rumtek Monastery, the seat of the Karmapa, outside Gangtok.
  • Pelling: Pemayangtse Monastery, the Rabdentse ruins and the Singshore bridge.
  • Namchi: the giant statue of Guru Padmasambhava on Samdruptse hill, and Siddhesvara Dham with copies of the Char Dham temples.

Local rules

  • Plastic water bottles of 2 litres or less are banned across Sikkim since 1 January 2022, by a state notification. Carry a steel or filter bottle and refill it.
  • A Rs 50 tourist fee is collected by hotels and homestays at check-in, under the Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2025 (India Today NE, 15 March 2025). It covers up to 30 days; children under 5 don't pay.
  • MG Marg is a no-litter zone. Use the bins, and don't smoke or spit there.
  • Monasteries: dress modestly, walk clockwise around stupas and prayer wheels, and ask before you take photos inside.

What to wear in Sikkim

What should I wear? Try the what-to-wear helper for day and night temperatures and an outfit list by month.

  • Gangtok: layers. A sweater and a light jacket from October to March, a warm jacket for December and January nights.
  • Tsomgo, Nathu La and North Sikkim: your warmest clothes in any month. Thermals, a warm jacket, a woollen cap, gloves and waterproof shoes, more so if there's snow.
  • Rain: a rain jacket or poncho from April to October.
  • Sun: sunglasses and sunscreen for the high lakes, where the sun is strong.

Health and safety

  • Altitude: Gangtok is about 1,650 m, but Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La, Zero Point and Gurudongmar are far higher. Spend a night in Gangtok first, go up slowly, drink water, and come down if you get a bad headache, feel sick or get breathless. Children, older people and anyone with heart or lung problems should ask a doctor first.
  • Landslides: don't travel at night in the monsoon, follow police closures, and don't stop under loose slopes for photos.
  • Never sleep with a heater or coal fire burning in a closed room. It gives off carbon monoxide.
  • Carry some cash. Card and UPI payments may not work where the phone signal is weak.

For foreign visitors

  • Visa: apply for the e-Tourist Visa only at the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in.
  • All of Sikkim needs a Restricted Area Permit (RAP) for foreigners, on top of your visa. Since January 2026 Sikkim no longer gives paper permits: you apply online on the e-FRRO portal after you arrive in India and before you enter Sikkim (India Today NE, 8 January 2026).
  • Validity: the Sikkim government page says the permit is valid for 30 days and can be extended by 30 days. We couldn't confirm whether that is the same under e-FRRO.
  • Nathu La is closed to foreigners. Tsomgo Lake is allowed as a day trip with a separate pass. Lachung, Yumthang, Lachen and Thangu are allowed for 3 to 5 days, in a group of two or more through a registered travel agency.
  • Gurudongmar: travel sites say foreigners can go only as far as Thangu. This is not confirmed on the official page, so ask your agency.
  • Some nationalities (including Pakistan, China, Nigeria, Myanmar, Hong Kong and Taiwan) need clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs first. OCI card holders count as foreigners. Bhutanese citizens don't need the permit.

Emergency numbers and help

  • Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): 112
  • Police: 100
  • Cyber fraud: 1930
  • Ministry of Tourism helpline, 24 hours, many languages: 1363 or 1800-11-1363

Questions

What is the best time to visit Sikkim?

October to December for clear mountain views and dry weather, and March to May for flowers in the Yumthang valley. June to September is heavy monsoon, when landslides close roads most years.

When can I see snow in Sikkim?

From about December to March at Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La, Zero Point and around Lachung and Lachen. Gangtok itself rarely gets snow. Heavy snow can stop permits and close roads for a few days, so keep a spare day.

How many days are enough for a Sikkim trip?

Five or six days covers Gangtok, Tsomgo Lake and one night or two in North Sikkim. With three or four days, stay in Gangtok and do Tsomgo Lake. Add a day for Gurudongmar and two for Pelling.

How do I get a North Sikkim permit?

Your Sikkim-registered tour operator or taxi applies for you, at least a day before. Give them a passport, voter ID, driving licence or government ID (not Aadhaar, says the official page) and two passport photos.

Can I take my own car to North Sikkim or Tsomgo Lake?

Plan on a Sikkim-registered tourist taxi. Permits go through registered local agencies, and the state has said outside vehicles won't be allowed on the permit routes under its new system.

Is Nathu La open on Mondays?

No. The Sikkim government's page says Nathu La permits are issued Tuesday to Sunday. Some operators say it's closed on Tuesdays too, so check with yours. Nathu La is for Indian citizens only.

Is there an airport in Gangtok?

Pakyong airport is near Gangtok, but it has had no scheduled flights since June 2024. Fly to Bagdogra (IXB) and drive up.

Is NH10 open now?

It reopened for one-way traffic on 20 September 2026 after the monsoon landslides at 20th Mile. It closes often in the rains, so check with your hotel or driver, or the Sikkim Police, on the day you travel.

Do foreigners need a permit for Sikkim?

Yes. All of Sikkim needs a Restricted Area Permit for foreigners, applied online on e-FRRO since January 2026. Nathu La is closed to foreigners.
