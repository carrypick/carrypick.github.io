Hong Kong trip from India: pre-arrival registration, a 4-day plan with Disneyland and the weather in December

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Hong Kong is a short, easy city trip from India: about 5 hours by direct flight from Delhi, no visa for a short stay, and fast trains that reach almost everything. Families come for Hong Kong Disneyland and Ocean Park; couples and friends for the harbour views, Victoria Peak, markets and food. Indians don't need a visa for up to 14 days, but must complete a free online pre-arrival registration (PAR) first. This guide covers the PAR, flights, a 4-day itinerary with Disneyland, the weather in December, the Octopus card and food for vegetarians.

Hong Kong visa for Indians: the pre-arrival registration (PAR)

Indians can visit Hong Kong without a visa, but only after a successful pre-arrival registration, says Hong Kong's Immigration Department (page dated 5 Nov 2026, read 21 Nov 2026).

  • Up to 14 days per visit, no visa. You must apply for and complete the pre-arrival registration online before you visit or transit Hong Kong. It's only for visits; for work or study you need a visa.
  • It's free. Apply only on the Immigration Department's official online service. Don't pay websites or agents that offer to do it for you.
  • Who can apply: Indian nationals with a passport valid for at least 6 months, staying up to 14 days.
  • The result comes at once. The system checks your details automatically and shows the result straight away.
  • Print and sign the slip. Print the "Notification Slip for Pre-arrival Registration for Indian Nationals" on a blank white A4 sheet and sign it. For children under 16, a parent or guardian checks and signs it.
  • Carry the slip and the same passport. Airlines check both before boarding, and immigration checks them on arrival. Without them, you can be refused boarding or entry.
  • Valid for 6 months, for many visits. You can visit several times while it's valid, up to 14 days each time. You can register again only after it expires.
  • New passport? Register again. The PAR is linked to the passport you used. If you renew your passport or your details change, apply again.
  • Your details must match your passport exactly. A mistake makes the registration invalid.
  • Just changing planes at the airport? You don't need a PAR if you stay in the airport transit area, says the Immigration Department.
  • PAR refused? You must apply for a visit visa directly to the Immigration Department. It normally takes 4 weeks once all documents are in, and Chinese embassies won't accept it.
  • The officer still decides. Carry your return ticket, your hotel booking and enough money for your stay. A slip doesn't guarantee entry.

A 4-day Hong Kong itinerary with Disneyland

Most first trips from India spend 4 days: two in the city, one at Disneyland or Ocean Park, and one for Lantau or Macau.

DayPlanEvening
1Land, take the Airport Express, check in at Tsim Sha Tsui or CentralTsim Sha Tsui waterfront and the Avenue of Stars, then the harbour light show
2The Peak Tram to Victoria Peak, then Central, Mid-Levels escalators and the Star Ferry across the harbourTemple Street Night Market or the Ladies' Market in Mong Kok
3Hong Kong Disneyland on Lantau Island (the MTR Disneyland Resort Line from Sunny Bay)Stay for the night-time show, then back to the city
4Ngong Ping 360 cable car to the Big Buddha and Po Lin Monastery at Tung Chung, near the airportFly home, or stay one more day
  • Ocean Park instead of Disneyland? Ocean Park, with rides, pandas and an aquarium, is on Hong Kong Island and has its own MTR station. With 5 days, do both.
  • Adding Macau? Macau is a separate place with its own immigration and entry rules, reached by ferry or by bus over the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge. Check Macau's entry rules for Indians before you go. Your PAR is valid for several visits, so you can come back into Hong Kong on the same slip while it's valid.
  • With kids? Keep Disneyland and Ocean Park on separate days, and take taxis when little legs are tired. Trains have lifts at most stations.
  • Book Disneyland, Ocean Park and Ngong Ping 360 tickets online on the official sites before you go, and check opening days. The cable car can stop in bad weather.

Hong Kong weather in December, and the best time to go

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from the Hong Kong Observatory:

NovemberAbout 25 °CAbout 20 °CAbout 39 mm, on about 6 days

  • October to December is the best time for most trips: dry, sunny and comfortable for walking. December is the driest month, says the Observatory.
  • December evenings are cool, about 16 °C on average. Pack a light jacket or sweater, and a warmer layer for Victoria Peak and windy ferry rides.
  • January to March is cool and often cloudy or misty, which can hide the views from the Peak.
  • May to September is hot, humid and wet, and tropical cyclone (typhoon) signals are most common from June to October. When a high signal is up, trains, ferries and parks can close. Check the Observatory's warnings during your trip.

Getting around Hong Kong: MTR and the Octopus card

  • Get an Octopus card. It's Hong Kong's travel card: tap it on the MTR trains, buses, trams, the Star Ferry and in many shops and convenience stores. The Tourist Octopus is "specially designed" for visitors, says Octopus. See the Octopus website for the cards and where to buy them.
  • The MTR train network reaches almost every sight in this guide, including Disneyland, Ocean Park and Tung Chung for the cable car.
  • The Star Ferry between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central is cheap, short and one of the best views of the harbour.
  • Trams ("ding dings") run along the north of Hong Kong Island: slow, cheap and fun.
  • Taxis are red in the city, green in the New Territories and blue on Lantau. Ask your hotel to write the address in Chinese.
  • Google Maps works well for trains and buses. A local SIM or an eSIM helps.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Hong Kong is a big food city, but many Cantonese dishes use meat or seafood stock, oyster sauce or lard. A little care helps:

  • Look for Buddhist vegetarian restaurants. They're plant-based and common across the city, often near temples. Po Lin Monastery at the Big Buddha serves a vegetarian meal.
  • Indian restaurants are easy to find, especially in Tsim Sha Tsui (Chungking Mansions and nearby streets) and Central. Many serve vegetarian and Jain food.
  • Safe-ish choices: vegetable dim sum (ask about lard and meat), vegetable fried rice or noodles without oyster sauce, tofu dishes, egg tarts if you eat egg, and fresh fruit.
  • Say it clearly: "I am vegetarian, no meat, no fish, no oyster sauce". Show it written in Chinese on your phone.
  • Jain travellers may want to carry ready-to-eat food or khakhra for park days. Check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Hong Kong

You have three ways to stay online in Hong Kong:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Where to book your stay

For a first trip, stay in Tsim Sha Tsui (Kowloon) for the harbour views, shopping, Indian restaurants and easy trains, or in Central and Wan Chai on Hong Kong Island for the Peak and nightlife. Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Hong Kong dollar (HK$). Cards work in hotels, malls and most restaurants. The Octopus card pays for transport and small shops. Carry a little cash for markets and small eateries.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • No work, business or study on a visit, says the Immigration Department. Visitors can't take paid or unpaid work.
  • Keep the landing slip you get at immigration. It shows how long you can stay.
  • Don't eat or drink on the MTR trains or in stations.
  • Don't smoke where signs say no smoking, including many parks, beaches and public places.

What to wear in Hong Kong

  • In November to February: light layers, jeans or trousers, and a light jacket or sweater for the evenings and the Peak.
  • In summer: light cotton clothes, an umbrella and sunscreen; it's hot, humid and rainy.
  • Comfortable walking shoes: Hong Kong has hills, stairs and long walks in stations and theme parks.
  • A small umbrella for showers in any season.
  • A light layer for cold malls, trains and buses with strong air-conditioning.

Help in an emergency

  • Police, fire and ambulance: 999.
  • Consulate General of India, Hong Kong: contact details are on its website.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your PAR slip and your travel insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address in Chinese.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Hong Kong?

No visa is needed for a visit of up to 14 days, but Indians must first complete a free online pre-arrival registration (PAR), says Hong Kong's Immigration Department. For longer stays, work or study, apply for a visa.

What is the Hong Kong pre-arrival registration (PAR)?

It's a free online registration Indian nationals must complete before visiting or transiting Hong Kong. The result comes at once. You print the notification slip on white A4 paper, sign it and carry it with the same passport.

How long is the Hong Kong PAR valid?

Six months. While it's valid you can visit Hong Kong several times, staying up to 14 days each time, says the Immigration Department. You can register again only after it expires.

Is the Hong Kong PAR free?

Yes. The Immigration Department's online service is free of charge. Don't pay other websites to fill it in.

What if my Hong Kong PAR is rejected?

You must apply for a visit visa directly to Hong Kong's Immigration Department. It normally takes 4 weeks once all documents are received, and Chinese embassies and consulates don't accept these applications from Indians.

How many days are enough for Hong Kong?

Four days is enough for a first trip: two days in the city (Victoria Peak, the Star Ferry, markets), one day at Disneyland or Ocean Park, and one for the Big Buddha on Lantau. With 5 or 6 days, add the other theme park or a day in Macau.

Is December a good time to visit Hong Kong?

Yes, it's one of the best months. December is the driest month, about 20 °C by day and 16 °C at night, with rain on about 5 days, on the Hong Kong Observatory's averages. Christmas and New Year are busy, so book early.

Which Indian cities have direct flights to Hong Kong?

Delhi (Cathay Pacific, Air India and IndiGo), Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai and Hyderabad (Cathay Pacific).

Is Hong Kong good for vegetarians?

Yes, with a little care. Look for Buddhist vegetarian restaurants and the many Indian restaurants in Tsim Sha Tsui and Central, and ask for no meat stock or oyster sauce in Chinese dishes.

What is the time difference between India and Hong Kong?

Hong Kong is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India.
