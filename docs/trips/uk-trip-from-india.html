UK trip from India: London, the Standard Visitor visa, direct flights and a 7-day plan

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 15 min read

The UK is one of the most popular long-haul trips from India: London's palaces, museums and the Thames, the colleges of Oxford, the castle and old town of Edinburgh, and green countryside a short train ride away. Many Indians also have family and friends there. Indians need a visa, applied for online with an appointment at a visa centre in India, and the decision usually takes about 3 weeks. This guide covers the visa, documents, flights, a 7-day plan, the weather by month, getting around London and food for vegetarians.

UK visa for Indians

Indians need a visa to visit the UK as a tourist. It's the Standard Visitor visa, says GOV.UK (Standard Visitor pages, read 21 Nov 2026).

  • Apply online before you travel, then attend an appointment at a visa application centre to give your fingerprints and a photo and to show your documents. The earliest you can apply is 3 months before you travel.
  • Visa fee: £135 for a Standard Visitor visa of up to 6 months, says GOV.UK. If you visit often, there are long-term visitor visas of 2, 5 or 10 years, with up to 6 months per visit. Each family member needs their own application and appointment, and pays the fee.
  • How long it takes: you'll usually get a decision within 3 weeks of your appointment, says GOV.UK, and UK Visas and Immigration's processing times page also gives 3 weeks for Standard Visitor (updated 9 September 2026). It can take longer, for example if your documents need checking. GOV.UK says not to book your travel until you have a decision.
  • Faster decisions: GOV.UK says you can check whether you can pay for a faster decision when you apply.
  • You get an eVisa, a digital record of your status. To see it you create a UKVI account and link it to your eVisa. You get your passport back on the same day as your appointment.
  • You can't work in the UK on a visitor visa, and you must show that you'll leave at the end of your visit.

Documents and information for a UK visitor visa

GOV.UK says your passport must be valid for your whole stay, and that you must show you'll leave the UK at the end of your visit and can pay for your trip and your return journey (or that someone else is paying). In the online form you give:

  • your travel dates and where you'll stay
  • how much you think the trip will cost
  • your home address and how long you've lived there
  • your parents' names and dates of birth
  • how much you earn in a year, if you have an income
  • depending on your case, your travel history for the past 10 years, your employer's details, and the name and address of anyone paying for your trip or of family in the UK

At the appointment you bring the documents that show you're eligible. Documents not in English or Welsh need a certified translation.

Can Indians use the UK ETA?

No. The UK's electronic travel authorisation (ETA) is for people who don't need a visa, such as nationals of the US, Canada, Australia and EU countries. India isn't on GOV.UK's list of who can get an ETA (page updated 9 April 2026), and GOV.UK says other nationalities "cannot get an ETA". Indians apply for the Standard Visitor visa instead, even with a US or Schengen visa.

Apply only on GOV.UK. Other websites that offer UK visas may charge extra.

Flights from India to the UK

  • There are direct flights from many Indian cities, say the airports' airline lists (Wikipedia, read 21 Nov 2026):
  • To London Heathrow (LHR): Air India from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru; British Airways from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai; Virgin Atlantic from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru.
  • To London Gatwick (LGW): Air India from Ahmedabad and Amritsar.
  • To Birmingham (BHX): Air India from Amritsar.
  • A direct flight from Delhi to London takes about 9 to 10 hours.
  • From other Indian cities, fly one-stop via Delhi or Mumbai, or through the Gulf: Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Doha.
  • Time difference: India is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of the UK in winter, and 4 hours 30 minutes ahead in British Summer Time (late March to late October).
  • Baggage: check the limits by airline in the international baggage rules and the carry-on size by airline guide.
  • Fares go up for the Indian summer holidays, Diwali, Christmas and New Year. Book early.

A 7-day UK itinerary: London, Oxford and Edinburgh

Most first trips from India spend 4 or 5 days in London. With a week, add a day trip and Edinburgh, a long train ride north.

1Land in London and rest. Walk along the South Bank by the ThamesThe London Eye area
2Westminster Abbey, the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben, then Buckingham Palace and St James's ParkTrafalgar Square and Covent Garden

4Museums: the British Museum, then the Natural History Museum or the Science Museum in South KensingtonOxford Street and Piccadilly Circus
5A day trip to Oxford, or to Windsor Castle, or to the Warner Bros. Studio Tour for Harry Potter fansBack in London
6Train to Edinburgh. The Royal Mile and Edinburgh CastleThe Old Town

  • Many big London museums are free to enter, including the British Museum and the Natural History Museum. Some exhibitions charge.
  • Book popular sights ahead: the Tower of London, Windsor Castle and the studio tour sell out in the holidays.
  • Ten days? Add Bath and Stonehenge, the Lake District, or the Scottish Highlands from Edinburgh.
  • Short on time? A 4- or 5-day London-only trip works well, with one day trip.

UK weather by month, and the best time to go

Long-term averages from the UK's Met Office, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

OctoberAbout 16 °C / 9 °CAbout 61 mm, the wettest monthAbout 13 °C / 7 °C

  • May to September is best: the mildest weather and very long days, with daylight until 9 PM or later in June. It's also the busiest and most expensive season.
  • Rain is possible in any month, but it's usually light. London gets rain on about 6 to 11 days a month on average. Carry a small umbrella or a waterproof jacket.
  • December is cold and dark early, about 8 °C by day in London, but the Christmas lights and markets are a big draw. Edinburgh is colder and windier, around freezing at night.
  • Spring (April and May) and early autumn (September) have fewer crowds than July and August.

Getting around London and the UK

  • In London, tap your contactless card or phone. You can pay as you go with a contactless card or device on buses, the Tube, trams, the DLR, London Overground, the Elizabeth line and most National Rail trains in London, says Transport for London (TfL). No ticket is needed.
  • Touch in and out on the yellow reader at the start and end of each journey. On buses and trams, you only touch in.
  • Fares are capped in Zones 1 to 9, so you never pay more than a set amount in a day or a week (Monday to Sunday).
  • Use the same card or phone all day, and one card or phone per person. Touching in with your phone and out with your watch or card charges you more.
  • Cards from India work: most contactless cards issued outside the UK can be used, says TfL, but your bank may add overseas transaction charges. A forex card or an Oyster card are the alternatives.
  • Keep cards apart when you touch in, so the reader charges the card you mean to use.
  • Between cities, take the train. Oxford and Windsor are easy day trips from London; Edinburgh is a long ride north. Book long-distance tickets early for lower fares.
  • The UK drives on the left, like India.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

The UK is one of the easiest countries abroad for Indian food:

  • Indian restaurants are everywhere, from South Indian dosa places to Punjabi dhabas and fine dining.
  • Supermarkets like Tesco, Sainsbury's and Waitrose sell vegetarian ready meals, sandwiches, bread, fruit and yoghurt, and most have a world foods aisle with Indian brands.
  • Labels mark vegetarian and vegan food clearly, and allergens are listed.
  • Cafés and pubs usually have a vegetarian option: jacket potatoes, pasta, soups and veggie burgers.
  • Jain travellers: most restaurant food uses onion and garlic. Ask, or look for Gujarati restaurants, which often have Jain options.
  • Taking food from India? Check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in the UK

You have three ways to stay online in the UK:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at airports, phone shops and supermarkets.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the pound sterling (£, GBP). Cards and phone payments work almost everywhere, and many places are card-only.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Tipping: many restaurants add a service charge to the bill, so check it before you tip. Tipping in pubs and taxis is optional.
  • Plugs: the UK uses three-pin Type G plugs, different from Indian ones. Carry an adapter.
  • Stand on the right on Tube escalators so people can walk past on the left.

What to wear in the UK

  • Winter (November to February): a warm, waterproof coat, a sweater, gloves, a scarf and closed shoes. Add thermal innerwear for Scotland.
  • Spring and autumn: layers, a light waterproof jacket and comfortable walking shoes.
  • Summer: light clothes, but keep a sweater or light jacket for cool evenings and rain.
  • Comfortable walking shoes in every season: London means a lot of walking and stairs.

Help in an emergency

  • Emergency number in the UK: 999 (police, fire and ambulance). 112 also works.
  • High Commission of India, London: contact details are on its website. India also has consulates in other UK cities.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your visa decision and your insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for the UK?

Yes. Indians need a Standard Visitor visa to visit the UK as a tourist, says GOV.UK. You apply online and attend an appointment at a visa application centre.

What is the UK visa fee for Indians?

£135 for a Standard Visitor visa of up to 6 months, says GOV.UK. Long-term visitor visas cost £506 for 2 years, £903 for 5 years and £1,128 for 10 years, with up to 6 months per visit.

How long does a UK visa take for Indians?

Usually within 3 weeks after your appointment at the visa application centre, says GOV.UK. It can take longer if your documents need checking, so don't book travel until you have a decision.

What documents do I need for a UK tourist visa?

A passport valid for your whole stay, and proof that you'll leave at the end of your visit and can pay for the trip and your journey home. The online form asks for your travel dates, where you'll stay, the trip's cost, your income and, depending on your case, your travel history and employer's details, says GOV.UK.

Can Indians use the UK ETA?

No. India isn't on GOV.UK's list of nationalities who can get an ETA, so Indians apply for the Standard Visitor visa, even with a US or Schengen visa.

Are there direct flights from India to London?

Yes. Air India, British Airways and Virgin Atlantic fly direct to London Heathrow from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru, British Airways also from Hyderabad and Chennai, and Air India flies to London Gatwick from Ahmedabad and Amritsar.

How many days are enough for London?

Four or five days is enough for London's main sights with one day trip. With a week, add Edinburgh; with ten days, add Bath, Stonehenge or the Scottish Highlands.

What is the best time to visit the UK from India?

May to September, for mild weather and long days. December is cold, but London's Christmas lights and markets make it popular.

Can I use my Indian debit or credit card on the London Tube?

Usually, yes. TfL says most contactless cards issued outside the UK work for pay as you go, but your bank may add overseas transaction charges. Use the same card for every journey to get the daily cap.

What is the time difference between India and the UK?

India is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of the UK in winter, and 4 hours 30 minutes ahead during British Summer Time, from late March to late October.
