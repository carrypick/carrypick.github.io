Seychelles trip from India: no visa, the Travel Authorisation, direct flights, a 7-day island plan and the best time to go

Updated 24 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Seychelles at a glance

Before you flyA paid Travel Authorisation on seychelles.govtas.com or the Seychelles e-Border app, up to 30 days before travel
ProcessingStandard 24 hours, premium 6 hours
CarryA return ticket, a confirmed hotel booking and enough money
Direct flightsMumbai to Mahé (IndiGo)
Weather in DecemberVictoria about 30 °C by day and 24 °C at night, rain on about 21 days
TimeIndia is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of Seychelles
MoneySeychelles rupee (SCR)

The Seychelles are 115 islands in the Indian Ocean, and one of the easiest beach trips abroad for Indians: no visa, and a direct flight from Mumbai to Mahé. Think granite boulders on white sand at Anse Source d'Argent, the coco de mer palms of the Vallée de Mai on Praslin, bicycles on La Digue, and warm, clear water all year. It's a favourite honeymoon. This guide covers the entry rules and the Travel Authorisation, flights, a 7-day plan across three islands, the weather by month, getting around, food for vegetarians, and money.

Seychelles visa for Indians: no visa, but a Travel Authorisation first

Indians don't need a visa for the Seychelles. "There are no visa requirements for any traveller visiting Seychelles," says the official Travel Authorisation site (read 24 Nov 2026). You get a visitor's permit on arrival, and you must get a Travel Authorisation online before you leave India.

The visitor's permit, on arrival

Seychelles Immigration gives the visitor's permit at the airport, for a holiday, business or visiting family. You need:

  • A valid return or onward ticket for your stay.
  • Confirmed accommodation.
  • Enough money for your stay.
  • No other permit to live in the Seychelles, and you must not be a "prohibited immigrant".

The permit is free for the first 3 months. You can extend it, 3 months at a time, up to 12 months in all, for SCR 5,000 for each extra 3 months, online at permits.ics.gov.sc or at Independence House in Victoria.

The Seychelles Travel Authorisation (TA)

Every visitor needs an approved Travel Authorisation before departure, says Seychelles Immigration, and the official site's FAQ adds:

  • Apply only on seychelles.govtas.com or in the official Seychelles e-Border app. Other websites can look official and charge extra.
  • When: up to 30 days before you travel.
  • Processing time: standard in 24 hours, premium in 6 hours.
  • It isn't free. All non-citizen travellers, children included, pay for it. The total depends on the speed you choose and includes banking fees. We couldn't read a fixed fee on a public official page, so we don't quote one.
  • What you need: a scan or photo of your passport photo page and a photo of the traveller. If you stay one night or more, upload your hotel booking with its name and your check-in and check-out dates; staying with friends or family needs an invitation letter from the host.
  • One journey only. The TA can't be cancelled once you submit it, and you need a new one for each trip.
  • Yellow fever: you upload a vaccination certificate only if you travel from a country where the disease is endemic. India isn't on the WHO list, but check it if you fly via Africa.

Do I need anything else?

A passport valid for your whole stay, printouts of your return ticket and hotel booking, and travel insurance. Entry is always the officer's decision, so carry the papers with you, not only on your phone.

Flights from India to the Seychelles

  • IndiGo flies direct from Mumbai to Mahé (SEZ), the main international airport, says the Wikipedia page of Seychelles International Airport (read 24 Nov 2026). It is about 3,250 km, roughly 4½ hours.
  • One stop: Emirates via Dubai, Qatar Airways via Doha, Air Seychelles via Abu Dhabi or Colombo, Ethiopian via Addis Ababa and Kenya Airways via Nairobi also fly to Mahé.
  • Air India doesn't fly to the Seychelles, so we don't show it below.
  • Time difference: India is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of the Seychelles, all year.
  • Baggage: check the limits by airline in the international baggage rules and the carry-on size by airline guide. Small island planes and ferries have tighter limits, so a soft bag is easier than a big hard case.

A 7-day Seychelles itinerary: Mahé, Praslin and La Digue

Most trips from India split the week between Mahé, where you land, and Praslin and La Digue, a ferry ride away. Honeymooners often spend all seven nights on one or two islands instead.

1Land on Mahé and restSunset at Beau Vallon beach
2Mahé: Victoria's market and Hindu temple, the botanical garden, and a drive over the Sans Souci road to the west coastDinner by the beach

4Praslin: the Vallée de Mai, a UNESCO World Heritage forest of coco de mer palms; snorkel at Anse Georgette or a boat to Curieuse Island to see giant tortoisesStay on Praslin
5Ferry to La Digue: hire a bicycle and ride to Anse Source d'ArgentStay on La Digue

  • Five days? Stay on Mahé and do a day trip to Praslin and La Digue.
  • Book ferries ahead in busy months, and keep a spare hour on your last day: sea and weather can delay boats.
  • Swim where it is safe. Some beaches have strong currents, especially in the windy months, so ask your hotel which beaches are calm.

  • It's warm all year, about 28 to 32 °C by day and 24 to 26 °C at night, so there's no cold season.
  • June to September has the least rain in Victoria, about 90 to 160 mm a month, but showers still come on about 16 or 17 days a month, usually short.
  • December and January are the wettest months, with rain on about 20 days. Hotels are still busy over Christmas and New Year.
  • Rain is often a short, heavy shower, with sun before and after, so a rainy day still has beach time.

Getting around the Seychelles

  • Between islands: fast ferries link Mahé, Praslin and La Digue, and Air Seychelles flies small planes between Mahé and Praslin. Book ahead in busy months.
  • On Mahé and Praslin: hire a car, take a taxi, or use the public buses. Traffic drives on the left, as in India. Check with the rental company whether your Indian licence is accepted, or carry an International Driving Permit.
  • On La Digue: most people hire a bicycle or walk; there are very few cars.
  • Roads are narrow and hilly on Mahé, so drive slowly.
  • Languages: Seychellois Creole, English and French are all official, so English works everywhere.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

  • Creole food is rich in fish, seafood and curries, but vegetable and lentil curries, rice, breadfruit chips and fresh tropical fruit are common.
  • Indian food: there is a Seychellois Indian community, and Mahé has some Indian restaurants and shops; ask your hotel what's nearby.
  • Resorts usually cook vegetarian on request, and some can make Jain meals with notice. Say so when you book.
  • Ask about fish sauce and stock, which are used in many Creole dishes.
  • Jain travellers: carry ready-to-eat food for the island days, and check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.
  • Supermarkets in Victoria and on Praslin sell bread, fruit, snacks and drinks if you have a kitchen.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in the Seychelles

You have three ways to stay online:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at Mahé airport and in phone shops. Carry your passport.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply). Signal can be weak on remote beaches and small islands.

Where to book your stay

On Mahé, stay near Beau Vallon for an easy beach with restaurants, or in the quieter south and west for resorts. On Praslin, Côte d'Or (Anse Volbert) has calm water and places to eat. La Digue has small guesthouses close to the jetty. Your booking must show the hotel's name and your dates for the Travel Authorisation. Compare hotels for your dates:

Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Seychelles rupee (SCR). Cards work in hotels, resorts and many restaurants. Carry some cash for buses, small shops, markets and La Digue's bicycle hire.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Carry your passport or a copy, and your hotel booking.
  • Nature rules: don't take shells, coral or coco de mer nuts from beaches; the nuts are protected and sold only with an official permit.
  • Reef-safe swimming: don't stand on coral or touch turtles.

What to wear in the Seychelles

  • Light cotton and linen clothes, swimwear, a cover-up, sandals and water shoes for rocky beaches.
  • Sun: a hat, sunglasses and reef-safe sunscreen. The sun is strong all year.
  • A light rain jacket or umbrella for the showers, and a thin layer for air-conditioned rooms and the evening breeze.
  • In town and at temples or churches, cover your shoulders and knees; swimwear is only for the beach and the pool.

Help in an emergency

  • Emergency number in the Seychelles: 999 for police, ambulance and fire.
  • High Commission of India, Victoria: contact details are on its website.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your Travel Authorisation, your insurance papers and your hotel booking on your phone.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for the Seychelles?

No. No traveller needs a visa for the Seychelles. Indians get a free visitor's permit on arrival for up to 3 months, but must get a Travel Authorisation online before they leave.

Is the Seychelles Travel Authorisation free?

No. All non-citizen travellers, including children, pay for it on seychelles.govtas.com. The total depends on the processing speed you choose and includes banking fees. The visitor's permit itself is free for 3 months.

How long does the Seychelles Travel Authorisation take?

Standard processing takes 24 hours and premium takes 6 hours, says the official FAQ. You can apply up to 30 days before you travel.

What documents do I need for the Seychelles?

A passport, a return or onward ticket, a confirmed hotel booking, enough money for your stay, and an approved Travel Authorisation. The Travel Authorisation asks for your passport page, a photo and your hotel booking.

How long can Indians stay in the Seychelles?

Up to 3 months on the free visitor's permit. It can be extended 3 months at a time, up to 12 months, for SCR 5,000 for each extension.

Are there direct flights from India to the Seychelles?

Yes. IndiGo flies direct from Mumbai to Mahé, about 4½ hours. From other cities, fly one stop via Dubai, Doha, Abu Dhabi, Colombo, Addis Ababa or Nairobi.

How many days are enough for the Seychelles?

Six or seven days covers Mahé, Praslin and La Digue. With five days, stay on Mahé and take a day trip to the other two.

What is the best time to visit the Seychelles from India?

Any time: it's about 28 to 32 °C all year. June to September has the least rain in Victoria, and December and January the most, on the Seychelles Meteorological Authority's averages.

Is the Seychelles good for a honeymoon?

Yes. It's one of the most popular honeymoon trips from India: quiet beaches, resorts, no visa and a direct flight from Mumbai. Compare it with other honeymoon destinations outside India.

What is the time difference between India and the Seychelles?

India is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of the Seychelles, all year.
