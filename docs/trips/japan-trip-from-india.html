Japan trip from India: visa, a 7-day plan, December weather and cherry blossom

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 17 min read

Japan is on many Indian travellers' wish lists: bullet trains, Mount Fuji, Kyoto's temples, Tokyo's lights and the cherry blossom in spring. It's a longer trip than Southeast Asia, about 8 hours by direct flight from Delhi, and Indians need a visa. But the visa is now an eVISA through VFS, the fee for Indians is small, and trains make it easy to see a lot in a week. This guide covers the visa, flights, a 7-day itinerary, the Japan Rail Pass, the weather in December, cherry blossom dates and food for vegetarians.

Japan visa for Indians

Indians need a visa for a holiday in Japan, says the Embassy of Japan in India (visa page, read 21 Nov 2026).

  • It's a Japan eVISA, applied for through VFS. The eVISA started in India on 1 April 2024. You still submit your application at a Japan Visa Application Centre (VFS Global), but the visa comes in electronic form instead of a sticker in your passport. No prior appointment is needed, says the embassy.
  • Type: a single-entry short-term visa for tourism, for a stay of up to 90 days, says the embassy. Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) lists India among the places where people apply through an accredited agency (page dated 15 May 2026).
  • Visa fee for Indians: ₹500 for a single or multiple visa, from 1 July 2026, plus the VFS service fee, says the embassy. It warns that the visa fee and the service charge are all you have to pay: the embassy never asks for money by phone or email.
  • When to apply: applications are accepted from 3 months before your travel date. Processing times "can vary significantly" and can't be speeded up, says the embassy, so plan well ahead, especially for cherry blossom season.
  • Show the eVISA on your phone at the airport. You must open the "visa issuance notice" on a mobile device with internet. A PDF, screenshot, photo or printed copy is not accepted, say the embassy and MOFA.
  • Documents: VFS's Japan page for India has the current checklist. We couldn't read the list on an official page from here, so check it there before you go to the centre.
  • Your passport should have enough validity and blank pages. Most airlines check this before you board.

Before you land: Visit Japan Web

The embassy links to Visit Japan Web, Japan's official site for filling in the immigration and customs forms before you arrive. It's free. Don't pay other websites to fill it in.

A 7-day Japan itinerary: Tokyo, Mount Fuji, Kyoto and Osaka

Most first trips from India spend 7 days on Japan's "golden route": Tokyo, a day near Mount Fuji, then Kyoto and Osaka by bullet train.

DayPlanEvening
1Land in Tokyo and check in near a train station on the JR Yamanote LineShibuya crossing and Shibuya Sky, or a walk in Shinjuku
2Asakusa and Senso-ji temple, a boat on the Sumida river, then Ueno parkAkihabara for electronics and anime shops
3Meiji Jingu shrine, Harajuku and Omotesando; teamLab or Tokyo SkytreeOdaiba or Tokyo Tower lights
4A day trip to Mount Fuji: Lake Kawaguchi (Fuji Five Lakes) or HakoneBack to Tokyo, or stay a night by the lake
5Bullet train to Kyoto. Fushimi Inari's red gates early, then Kiyomizu-deraGion and Pontocho for old streets
6Arashiyama bamboo grove in the morning, then a half day in Nara with its deer and Todai-jiMove to Osaka; Dotonbori's lights and street food
7Osaka Castle, then fly home from Osaka Kansai, or go back to Tokyo
  • Tokyo Disneyland or Universal Studios Japan? Add a day. Tokyo Disneyland and DisneySea are near Tokyo; Universal Studios Japan is in Osaka. Book tickets online before you go.
  • With kids? Keep it to two bases (Tokyo and Osaka) and do Kyoto and Nara as day trips. Trains are safe, clean and on time.
  • Only 5 days? Choose Tokyo plus a Fuji day trip, or fly into Osaka and do Osaka, Kyoto and Nara.
  • Mount Fuji hides in cloud often, especially in summer. Winter mornings give the clearest views.
  • Shrines and temples: speak quietly, follow the signs on photos, and take off your shoes where you see a step up or shoe racks.

Japan Rail Pass: do you need it?

  • The Japan Rail Pass lets you ride JR trains across Japan as many times as you like, including most Shinkansen bullet trains, says the official JR site (read 21 Nov 2026). It comes for 7, 14 or 21 days, in Standard (Ordinary) or Green (first class) cars.
  • Official prices bought online: ¥50,000 for a 7-day Ordinary pass for an adult and ¥70,000 for a 7-day Green pass, says the official site. Agencies outside Japan charge a little more. Prices can change, so check the official JR Pass site when you buy.
  • The fastest Nozomi bullet trains between Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka need an extra special ticket for pass holders, says the official site.
  • Is it worth it? For only Tokyo to Kyoto and Osaka and back, single tickets may cost less. For a longer route (Hiroshima, northern Japan or several long trips), the pass often pays off. Add your real train fares in the trip budget calculator and compare.
  • Only buy from the official site or a JR-approved agency. Buying online on the official site lets you book reserved seats before you pick up the pass.

Japan weather in December, and the best time to go

Long-term averages (1991–2020) from the Japan Meteorological Agency:

MonthTokyo dayTokyo nightTokyo rain
NovemberAbout 17 °CAbout 9 °CAbout 96 mm
DecemberAbout 12 °CAbout 4 °CAbout 58 mm
JanuaryAbout 10 °C, the coldest monthAbout 1 °CAbout 60 mm
MarchAbout 14 °CAbout 5 °CAbout 116 mm
AprilAbout 19 °CAbout 10 °CAbout 134 mm
AugustAbout 31 °C, the hottest monthAbout 24 °CAbout 155 mm
OctoberAbout 22 °CAbout 15 °CAbout 235 mm, the wettest month
  • December in Japan is cold but mostly dry and sunny in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka. Kyoto averages about 12 °C by day and 4 °C at night in December, and Osaka about 12 °C and 5 °C, says JMA. Pack a warm jacket, gloves and layers.
  • Sapporo and Hokkaido are much colder: about 2 °C by day and below 0 °C at night in December, with snow. Go there for snow, not for a first trip.
  • Late March to early April is cherry blossom season, and October and November bring autumn colours. Both are the busiest and costliest times.
  • June to early July is the rainy season, and July to September is hot and humid, with typhoons possible from summer to autumn.
  • Winter lights: many Japanese cities have illuminations from November to February. Check dates on the official sites before you go.

Cherry blossom in Japan: when to go from India

The Japan Meteorological Agency records the date the first cherry blossoms (somei-yoshino) open at each city. The normal dates (1991–2020 averages) are:

CityNormal first-bloom dateIn 2026
Tokyo24 March19 March
Kyoto26 March23 March
Osaka27 March26 March
Sapporo1 May18 April
  • Full bloom usually comes about a week after the first flowers, and the petals fall within one or two weeks. That's why most trips aim for the last week of March and the first week of April.
  • The dates move every year. JMA starts its updates in spring; plan with some extra days and check JMA's page before you book.
  • Book flights, hotels and the visa early. Visa applications open 3 months before travel, so apply in late December or early January for a late-March trip.

Getting around Japan

  • Trains go almost everywhere and are very punctual. In cities, use the metro and JR lines; between cities, the Shinkansen bullet trains.
  • Get an IC card (such as Suica or PASMO in Tokyo, or ICOCA in Osaka) and tap it on trains, buses and at convenience stores. It saves buying a ticket for each ride.
  • From Haneda to the city, take the Tokyo Monorail or Keikyu Line. From Narita, the Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner. From Osaka Kansai, the JR Haruka to Kyoto or the Nankai line to Osaka.
  • Send big bags ahead. Hotels and convenience stores offer luggage delivery (takkyubin) to your next hotel, so you can travel light on the bullet train.
  • Taxis are clean but costly. Rear doors open on their own.
  • Google Maps works well for trains, with platforms and times. A pocket Wi-Fi or an eSIM helps.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Vegetarian food is harder in Japan than in Thailand or Singapore. Many soups, noodles and sauces use dashi, a fish stock, even when there's no meat on top.

  • Learn one phrase: "Watashi wa bejitarian desu" (I am vegetarian). Show it written in Japanese on your phone, with "no fish, no dashi, no egg" if you need.
  • Shojin ryori is Buddhist temple food: fully plant-based. Kyoto has many temples and restaurants that serve it.
  • Safe-ish choices: vegetable tempura (ask about the dipping sauce), inari sushi, cucumber sushi rolls (kappa maki), plain rice, edamame, fruit and sweets. Check the label on onigiri rice balls at convenience stores: ume (pickled plum) and kombu are usually fish-free, but not always.
  • Indian restaurants are easy to find in Tokyo and Osaka, and many serve vegetarian thali and dal. Search "Indian restaurant" and "vegetarian" on Google Maps near your hotel.
  • Jain travellers may want to carry ready-to-eat food, theplas or khakhra. But don't bring meat, fruit or vegetables into Japan: the embassy warns against it. Check what you can take on the flight in our can I carry it on a flight? list.
  • Tap water is safe to drink in Japan.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Japan

You have three ways to stay online in Japan:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Where to book your stay

In Tokyo, stay near a JR Yamanote Line station: Shinjuku or Shibuya for nightlife and shopping, Ueno or Asakusa for lower prices and temples, and Ginza or Tokyo Station for easy bullet trains. Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Japanese yen (¥). Cards work in hotels, shops and many restaurants, but carry some cash for small restaurants, temples and shrines. Convenience-store ATMs often take foreign cards.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Don't bring meat products, vegetables or fruit into Japan, says the Embassy of Japan in India.
  • Get travel insurance before you go: the embassy asks visitors to Japan to arrange it.
  • No tipping. It isn't expected in Japan, and staff may return the money.
  • Keep quiet on trains, don't talk on the phone, and queue on the platform. Rubbish bins are rare, so carry a small bag for your rubbish.
  • Follow traffic rules: the embassy links to a video on traffic rules for visitors.

What to wear in Japan

  • In December to February: a warm jacket, sweaters, thermals, gloves, a cap and comfortable closed shoes. Indoors and on trains it's heated, so wear layers.
  • In spring and autumn: a light jacket and layers; mornings and evenings are cool.
  • In summer: light cotton clothes, an umbrella and sunscreen; it's hot and humid.
  • Socks without holes, because you take your shoes off in temples, some restaurants and traditional inns.
  • Comfortable walking shoes: you walk a lot in train stations and temples.

Help in an emergency

  • Police: 110. Fire and ambulance: 119.
  • Embassy of India, Tokyo: contact details are on its website.
  • Japan visa questions: the embassy's Japan Visa Information Hotline is +91-11-7181-6614 (English, 24 x 7).
  • Keep a photo of your passport and your travel insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address in Japanese.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Japan?

Yes. Indians need a visa for a holiday in Japan. Since 1 April 2024 it's a Japan eVISA: you apply through a Japan Visa Application Centre (VFS Global) and get a single-entry tourist visa for up to 90 days in electronic form, says the Embassy of Japan in India.

What is the Japan visa fee for Indians?

₹500 for a single or multiple visa, from 1 July 2026, plus the VFS service fee, says the Embassy of Japan in India. The embassy says those are the only payments, and it never asks for money by phone or email.

How long does a Japan visa take for Indians?

The embassy doesn't give a fixed time. It says processing times "can vary significantly" and can't be speeded up. Applications are accepted from 3 months before travel, so apply early.

Can I show my Japan eVISA as a PDF or printout?

No. At the airport you must show the "visa issuance notice" on a phone or tablet with internet. PDFs, screenshots, photos and printed copies are not accepted, say the embassy and Japan's foreign ministry.

How many days are enough for Japan?

Seven days is enough for a first trip on the golden route: 3 days in Tokyo, a day trip to Mount Fuji, and 3 days in Kyoto, Nara and Osaka. With 10 days you can add Hiroshima, Disneyland or a slower pace.

Is December a good time to visit Japan?

Yes, if you like cold weather. Tokyo averages about 12 °C by day and 4 °C at night in December, with little rain and many sunny days, on JMA's averages. Mount Fuji views are often clear, and cities have winter lights. Christmas and New Year are busy, so book early.

When is cherry blossom season in Japan?

The first cherry blossoms usually open around 24 March in Tokyo, 26 March in Kyoto and 27 March in Osaka, on the Japan Meteorological Agency's normal dates. Full bloom is usually about a week later. Hokkaido blooms in late April or May.

Which Indian cities have direct flights to Japan?

Delhi (to Tokyo Haneda with Air India, ANA and JAL), Mumbai (to Haneda with Air India and to Narita with ANA) and Bengaluru (to Narita with JAL). Air India lists Delhi to Osaka Kansai from 2 February 2027.

Do I need a Japan Rail Pass?

Not always. The 7-day Ordinary pass costs ¥50,000 for an adult online, says the official site. For just Tokyo to Kyoto and Osaka and back, single tickets can cost less. For longer routes, the pass often pays off.

Is Japan good for vegetarians?

It's harder than most of Asia, because many dishes use dashi (fish stock). Look for shojin ryori (Buddhist plant-based food) in Kyoto, Indian restaurants in Tokyo and Osaka, and say clearly "no fish, no dashi".

What is the time difference between India and Japan?

Japan is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India.
