Vietnam trip from India: e-visa, a 7-day itinerary and the weather in December

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Vietnam has become one of the most popular trips abroad from India. There are direct flights from several Indian cities, and the country packs a lot into one week: Hanoi's old streets, the limestone islands of Ha Long Bay, the lantern-lit old town of Hoi An and busy Ho Chi Minh City. Indians need a visa, but it's an e-visa you apply for online. This guide covers the e-visa, the pre-arrival form, flights, a 5 or 7-day itinerary, the weather in December, getting around and food for vegetarians.

Vietnam visa for Indians

Indian ordinary passport holders need a visa for Vietnam. Vietnam's visa exemption for India covers only diplomatic and official passports (see our visa guide for the source). For a holiday, apply for the e-visa.

From Vietnam's official e-visa portal, run by the Immigration Department of the Ministry of Public Security (read 21 Nov 2026):

  • Apply online on evisa.gov.vn: fill in the form, pay the fee and get the result by email or on the portal.
  • Fee: US$25 for a single-entry visa, US$50 for a multiple-entry visa. It's paid on the portal and isn't refunded if the visa is refused.
  • Stay: the e-visa is valid for a maximum of 90 days, for single or multiple entries.
  • Enter and leave through the international airports, land crossings and seaports on the government's list. Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang airports are among the main ones, but check the list on the portal if you plan to cross a land border.
  • Processing time: we couldn't read it on the official portal on 21 Nov 2026. Apply at least a couple of weeks before you fly, and don't buy non-refundable tickets until the visa comes.
  • Use only the official portal. Many look-alike sites charge extra to apply for you.
  • Your passport should have at least 6 months left and blank pages. Most airlines check this before you board.

Pre-arrival form: fill it before you fly

  • Vietnam's Immigration Department "highly recommends" that passengers fill in the pre-arrival form at prearrival.immigration.gov.vn before they enter, to save time at the airport.
  • Vietjet says it's required for foreign passport holders landing in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Phu Quoc or Cam Ranh (Nha Trang). Fill it in to be safe.
  • It's free, done within 3 days before you arrive, and gives you a QR code to save on your phone. Use only the official site.

A 7-day Vietnam itinerary: Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Hoi An and Ho Chi Minh City

Vietnam is long, so a first trip of 7 days works best north to south, with two short domestic flights.

DayPlanEvening
1Land in Hanoi and check in near the Old QuarterWalk around Hoan Kiem Lake and the night market (on weekends)
2Hanoi: the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum area, the Temple of Literature, Train Street and the Old QuarterA water puppet show
3Ha Long Bay, about 3 hours by road: a day cruise, or an overnight cruise among the islandsOn the boat, or back in Hanoi
4Back to Hanoi and fly to Da Nang (about 1 hour 20 minutes)Hoi An old town and its lanterns
5Hoi An or Da Nang: the Marble Mountains, Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge, or a beach dayDa Nang's Dragon Bridge
6Fly to Ho Chi Minh City. See the Ben Thanh market, the old post office and the War Remnants MuseumA rooftop or a walk on Nguyen Hue street
7The Cu Chi tunnels or a half-day Mekong Delta trip, then fly home
  • Only 5 days? Stay in one region. In the north: Hanoi, Ha Long Bay and Ninh Binh (Trang An's boat rides and limestone hills). In the south: Ho Chi Minh City, the Mekong Delta and the Cu Chi tunnels, or a beach island like Phu Quoc.
  • Going in December or January? Hoi An and Da Nang are rainy then. Keep their days flexible, or swap them for Ninh Binh in the north, or Phu Quoc in the south, where the dry season starts.
  • 10 days? Add Ninh Binh after Ha Long, and Sa Pa's rice terraces in the far north (cold in winter).
  • At the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, dress formally, cover your shoulders and knees, keep quiet, and don't take photos inside, says Vietnam's tourism authority.
  • Book Ha Long cruises with a licensed operator. Trips can be cancelled in bad weather.

Vietnam weather in December and the best time to go

Long-term averages (1991–2020) for December from Viet Nam's Meteorological and Hydrological Administration, published by the World Meteorological Organization:

CityDecember dayDecember nightDecember rain
HanoiAbout 22 °CAbout 15 °CAbout 24 mm, on about 7 days
Da Nang (near Hoi An)About 25 °CAbout 21 °CAbout 272 mm, on about 21 days
Ho Chi Minh CityAbout 32 °CAbout 24 °CAbout 41 mm, on about 5 days
  • The north is cool from December to February. January is Hanoi's coldest month, about 20 °C by day and 15 °C at night, and the tourism authority says cold spells can bring lows of about 10 °C. Ha Long Bay can be misty and chilly. Pack a jacket.
  • Da Nang and Hoi An are wet from September to December. October is the wettest month, at about 658 mm. Rain falls on about 21 days in December, and then drops to about 22 mm in February. The tourism authority calls Hoi An "rainy, cool, cloudy" from September to January and sunny from February to August.
  • The south is dry from December to April. Ho Chi Minh City stays hot, about 32–35 °C, all year. The tourism authority calls December to April "clear skies, hot, sunny" there.
  • March to May has the best weather for the whole country, says the tourism authority. Hanoi is warm then (about 24–32 °C by day), and the centre and south are dry.
  • June to August is hot and wet in Hanoi, with about 230–335 mm of rain a month.

Getting around Vietnam

  • Fly between regions. Hanoi to Da Nang is about 1 hour 20 minutes and Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City about 2 hours, on several Vietnamese airlines. Buy the check-in bag with your ticket; it's cheaper than at the airport.
  • Trains run along the coast between Hanoi, Hue, Da Nang and Ho Chi Minh City. They are slow but scenic; the Hue to Da Nang stretch over the Hai Van Pass is the most beautiful.
  • In the cities, use ride-hailing apps for cars and motorbike taxis. You see the price first, and you don't need to bargain.
  • Taxis: use metered taxis from the airport's official rank, or agree the fare first.
  • Crossing the road: traffic in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City is full of motorbikes. Walk slowly and steadily, and don't run or stop suddenly.
  • Don't ride a motorbike without a helmet and an International Driving Permit, says the tourism authority. Your travel insurance may not cover you otherwise.
  • Watch your bag. The tourism authority warns of bag snatching by people on motorbikes, so carry your bag on the side away from the road.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

Vietnamese food uses fish sauce (nuoc mam) in many dishes, even vegetable ones, and broths are often made with meat or bones. A few words help:

  • "Chay" means vegetarian. Look for "quán chay" or "cơm chay" (vegetarian restaurant or vegetarian rice): Buddhist vegetarian restaurants are found in every city, and cook without meat, fish or fish sauce.
  • Ask for "không nước mắm" (no fish sauce) and "không trứng" (no egg).
  • For Jain food, add "không hành, không tỏi" (no onion, no garlic), and check about root vegetables. Buddhist vegetarian kitchens often skip onion and garlic already.
  • Safe choices: vegetarian pho (ask for a vegetable broth), fresh spring rolls with tofu, banh mi with vegetables, steamed rice with stir-fried greens, fresh fruit, coconut water and Vietnamese coffee.
  • Indian restaurants are easy to find in Hanoi's Old Quarter, Ho Chi Minh City's District 1 and Da Nang. Many serve pure vegetarian and Jain food.
  • Carry ready-to-eat food or khakhra for day trips like a Ha Long cruise, where meals are mostly seafood. Ask the cruise for a vegetarian meal when you book. Check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water, as the tourism authority advises.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Vietnam

You have three ways to stay online in Vietnam:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Where to book your stay

In Hanoi, stay in or near the Old Quarter, close to Hoan Kiem Lake: most sights are a walk away. In Hoi An, stay near the old town, or on An Bang beach; in Da Nang, along My Khe beach. In Ho Chi Minh City, District 1 is closest to the sights, markets and food. Compare hotels for your dates:

Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the Vietnamese dong (₫, VND). Hotels, restaurants and spas generally accept international cards, says the tourism authority. Carry cash for street food, markets and small shops.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Declare cash in Vietnam if you carry more than US$5,000 (or the same in rupees or other money) or more than 15 million dong, and gold jewellery of 300 g or more.
  • Vapes, e-cigarettes and heated tobacco are banned in Vietnam since 2025, including bringing them in and using them.
  • Duty-free limits: each adult can bring 1.5 litres of spirits (or 2 litres of wine, or 3 litres of beer) and 200 cigarettes. Other goods are duty free up to 10 million dong in total.
  • Medicines: up to 30 days' supply on your prescription, or up to US$200 in value. Strong painkillers and sleeping pills have tighter limits.
  • Dress respectfully at temples and pagodas: cover your shoulders and knees, and take your hat and shoes off when asked.

What to wear in Vietnam

  • In the north from December to February: long trousers, full-sleeved tops and a light jacket or sweater.
  • In Hoi An and Da Nang from September to December: a rain poncho, quick-dry clothes and sandals that can get wet.
  • In Ho Chi Minh City and the south: light cotton clothes, a cap and sunscreen all year.
  • A scarf or stole to cover your shoulders at temples and the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum.
  • Comfortable walking shoes for the Old Quarter and the old town of Hoi An.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes. Indian ordinary passport holders need a visa; only diplomatic and official passports are visa-exempt. Apply for the e-visa online on Vietnam's official portal, evisa.gov.vn, before you fly.

What is the Vietnam e-visa fee for Indians?

US$25 for a single-entry e-visa and US$50 for a multiple-entry one, paid on the official portal. The fee isn't refunded if the visa is refused.

How long can I stay in Vietnam on an e-visa?

Up to 90 days, with single or multiple entries, says Vietnam's e-visa portal.

Is there a visa on arrival in Vietnam for Indians?

Apply for the e-visa online before you fly. We couldn't confirm a visa on arrival for Indian tourists on an official page, so don't count on one.

How many days are enough for Vietnam?

Seven days covers Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Hoi An or Da Nang, and Ho Chi Minh City, with two domestic flights. With only 5 days, stay in one region: Hanoi, Ha Long and Ninh Binh in the north, or Ho Chi Minh City and the Mekong Delta in the south.

Is December a good time to visit Vietnam?

For the north and the south, yes. Hanoi is cool and dry (about 22 °C by day, 15 °C at night) and Ho Chi Minh City is hot and dry (about 32 °C). Da Nang and Hoi An are rainy in December, with rain on about 21 days, so plan fewer days there or go in February to August.

What is the weather in Vietnam in December?

It depends on the region. Hanoi averages about 22 °C by day and 15 °C at night, with little rain. Da Nang is about 25 °C, with about 272 mm of rain. Ho Chi Minh City is about 32 °C and mostly dry. These are long-term averages from Viet Nam's Meteorological and Hydrological Administration.

Which Indian cities have direct flights to Vietnam?

Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Kolkata and Kochi have direct flights to Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, on Vietjet, Vietnam Airlines, IndiGo, Air India and Akasa Air.

Is Vietnam good for vegetarians and Jains?

Yes, if you look for "chay" (vegetarian) restaurants, which cook without meat or fish sauce, and ask for no onion and garlic. Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang also have many Indian restaurants with pure vegetarian and Jain food.

What is the time difference between India and Vietnam?

Vietnam is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of India.
