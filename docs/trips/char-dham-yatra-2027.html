Char Dham Yatra 2027: dates, registration and an 11-day route

Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 13 min read

Char Dham, Uttarakhand at a glance

2027 datesTo be announced. Kedarnath's date is usually announced on Mahashivratri and Badrinath's on Basant Panchami
2026 datesGangotri and Yamunotri 19 April, Kedarnath 22 April, Badrinath 23 April
RegistrationCompulsory and free, on the Uttarakhand government's portal, WhatsApp 8394833833 or the 1364 helpline
HelicopterBook Kedarnath helicopters only on IRCTC's heli yatra site
Kedarnath trekAbout 16–17 km each way from Gaurikund
Best timeMay–June and mid-September to October. July to early September brings monsoon stoppages
Emergency112; health helpline 104; ambulance 108

The Char Dham Yatra is the pilgrimage to four Himalayan temples in Uttarakhand: Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath. The temples close for the winter in November and open again in spring. The 2027 opening dates have not been announced yet. This guide covers how the dates are fixed, the 2026 dates to plan with, the compulsory registration, helicopter bookings, the Kedarnath trek, health tips for older pilgrims, the monsoon and an 11-day route from Haridwar.

Packing? See our Kedarnath packing list: it works for all four dhams. Starting from Rishikesh? Read our Rishikesh guide and Rishikesh packing list. Choosing a month? See where to go in April and where to go in May.

Char Dham opening dates 2027

The 2027 dates are to be announced. On 25 November 2026 the Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC) site still said news would come soon, and the registration portal said the opening times would be announced soon. This is how the dates are usually fixed:

  • Kedarnath: the date is announced on Mahashivratri at the Omkareshwar temple in Ukhimath. In 2026 it was announced on 15 February.
  • Badrinath: the date is announced on Basant Panchami at the old Tehri royal court in Narendra Nagar.
  • Gangotri and Yamunotri: they open on Akshaya Tritiya. Panchang calendars put Akshaya Tritiya 2027 on Sunday 9 May 2027, so these two are *expected* to open around then. That is not confirmed until the temple committees announce it.

We'll update this page when the dates are announced. Until then, plan with the 2026 dates:

DhamOpened in 2026Closed in 2026
Yamunotri19 April (Akshaya Tritiya)11 November (Bhai Dooj)
Gangotri19 April (Akshaya Tritiya)10 November (Annakut)
Kedarnath22 April11 November (Bhai Dooj)
Badrinath23 April16 November

In winter, Kedarnath's deity is worshipped at Ukhimath, Gangotri's at Mukhba and Yamunotri's at Kharsali.

Best time for the Char Dham Yatra

  • May and June: the main season, with school holidays and long queues. Days are pleasant, but nights at Kedarnath can be near freezing: IMD recorded 4.7 °C there on 6 June 2026. Book early.
  • July to early September: the monsoon. Landslides close roads and the government stops the yatra on heavy-rain alerts. See the monsoon below.
  • Mid-September to October: clearer skies and fewer people, but colder. Snow is possible at Kedarnath by late October: it snowed there on 29 and 30 October 2026.
  • Early November: the temples close for winter, with the dates announced each year.

Registration is compulsory (and free)

Every pilgrim must register with the Uttarakhand government before the yatra. From the official registration and tourist care portal:

  • It's free. The portal calls it a free exercise. In February 2026 a "nominal fee" was talked about, but in 2026 registration stayed free. Check again for 2027.
  • What it asks for: you sign up with your mobile number and email (with an OTP), then enter your trip dates, how many people are going, which dhams and the date for each, and each pilgrim's details. Keep a passport-size photo and a valid photo ID ready (news reports name Aadhaar).
  • What you get: a Yatra Registration Letter with a QR code. Print it and keep it on your phone. It's checked at Sonprayag and at the dhams, and you need the registration number to book a helicopter.
  • Other ways to register: WhatsApp 8394833833 (save the number and send "Yatra"), the toll-free helpline 1364, or the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app.
  • Counters on the way, from the portal: Rahi Hotel in Haridwar, the Transit Camp and the Gurudwara in Rishikesh, Herbertpur and Nayagaon near Vikasnagar, and Barkot, Hina, Sonprayag and Pandukeshwar on the routes.
  • When it opens: in 2026 online registration opened on 6 March, and the offline counters on 17 April (All India Radio). The 2027 opening date is to be announced.
  • Driving yourself? The portal also asks you to register your vehicle with Uttarakhand's transport department.
  • Daily limits: no daily cap was set for 2026. Check for 2027.

Kedarnath helicopter booking: only on IRCTC

  • The only official booking site is heliyatra.irctc.co.in, the BKTC site says. Shuttles fly from Guptkashi, Sersi (Sirsi) and Phata.
  • Fake sites are common. Police warned pilgrims in May 2026 to use the IRCTC portal only (The Tribune), and Rudraprayag Police said they had blocked more than 550 fake websites, social media pages and payment links (ANI). Don't pay a Facebook page, a WhatsApp number, an ad or an agent at the helipad.
  • The name and ID you book with must match the ID you show at boarding.
  • Lost money to a fake site? Call the cyber fraud helpline 1930. The Kedarnath heli helpline on the Rudraprayag district site is +91 98709 63731.
  • Safety rules changed after two crashes. A helicopter crashed near Gaurikund on 15 June 2025, killing 7, and the Civil Aviation Ministry ordered stricter checks (All India Radio). After a crash in Chamoli in late October 2026 that killed 6, DGCA made a second, hill-trained pilot compulsory on Char Dham charter flights and asked for clearance for every flight, from 2 November 2026 (Outlook). How this changes 2027 fares and seats is not known yet.

Don't trust any site that offers 2027 helicopter seats before the temple dates are announced.

The Kedarnath trek

  • About 16–17 km each way from Gaurikund. The official portal says 16 km and 6 to 8 hours; the Rudraprayag district page says 17 km. Most people walk up in one day, stay the night near the temple and walk down the next day.
  • Kedarnath is about 3,580 m high (official pages give 3,580 to 3,586 m). The air is thin, so walk slowly and stop often.
  • Private vehicles stop at Sonprayag. Shared shuttle jeeps take you about 5 km to Gaurikund, where the walk starts.
  • Ponies, mules, palkis (chairs carried by four people) and pithus (baskets carried on a porter's back) are available. Book only at the official prepaid counters at Gaurikund or Sonprayag, where the district's rates are posted. We couldn't find an official rate card online, so we don't list prices.
  • Mobile phones and cameras are not allowed inside the temple, news reports say.
  • Rudraprayag yatra control room: 01364-297878 or 01364-297879.

Yamunotri has a shorter walk: about 6 km from Janki Chatti (2 to 3 hours), the last point you can drive to. Gangotri and Badrinath are by road.

Health advice for elderly pilgrims

All four dhams are above 2,700 m. Uttarakhand's health department advises, in its 2026 advisory (as reported by Garhwal Post):

  • Older people, and anyone with heart or lung disease, should get a full medical check-up well before the trip. Some reports say older pilgrims are advised to carry a fitness certificate; this is advice, not a rule we could confirm.
  • Carry enough of your regular medicines, your prescriptions and your doctor's phone number.
  • Get fit before you go, and go up slowly so your body gets used to the height.
  • Drink at least 2 litres of water a day, don't travel on an empty stomach, keep warm, and avoid alcohol and smoking.
  • Watch for warning signs: headache, dizziness, breathlessness, chest tightness, nausea, vomiting, cough or a racing heart. Go to the nearest medical relief post or call 104 (health helpline) or 108 (ambulance).

AIIMS Rishikesh's medical advisory for the yatra adds:

  • Take it easy for the first 48 hours at height.
  • Bring spares: inhalers, insulin and syringes if you use them, a glucometer if you're diabetic and a BP machine if you have high blood pressure.
  • Use sunscreen (SPF 50), UV sunglasses, and eye and saline nose drops for the dry air.
  • If you feel worse and there's no doctor, go down. Coming down about 1,000 m helps.
  • Ask your doctor about altitude-sickness medicine before you go.

The state runs 33 health screening points on the routes and tracks pilgrims' health on its e-Swasthya Dham system (All India Radio, March 2026).

Monsoon, landslides and stoppages

From July to September, heavy rain often closes the yatra for a day or more. The official portal calls the 45 km Joshimath–Badrinath road "landslide-prone". Recent stoppages, as reported by All India Radio and Business Today:

  • 2025: stopped for 24 hours on 29 June after an IMD red alert; pilgrims rescued near Sonprayag on 3 July; stopped from 1 to 5 September.
  • 2026: stopped on 20 July (resumed 21 July), on 28 and 29 July, and on 26 and 27 September (resumed 28 September).

If you travel in the monsoon:

  • Keep one or two spare days. Pilgrims are held at towns such as Rishikesh, Srinagar, Rudraprayag or Sonprayag when the yatra is stopped.
  • Check IMD's district warnings on mausam.imd.gov.in every day, and follow police and district updates.
  • Don't drive at night in the hills, and never cross a stream or landslide that police have closed.

An 11-day Char Dham route from Haridwar

The order goes west to east, as the official route pages do: Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath. The day count is ours, not an official plan. Distances are from the official portal.

  1. Day 1: Haridwar or Rishikesh to Barkot. Yamunotri is about 220 km from Haridwar.
  2. Day 2: Yamunotri. Drive to Janki Chatti, walk about 6 km up and back. Night in Barkot.
  3. Day 3: Barkot to Uttarkashi.
  4. Day 4: Gangotri and back to Uttarkashi. Gangotri is about 3,100 m high.
  5. Day 5: Uttarkashi to Guptkashi or Sonprayag. A long drive.
  6. Day 6: walk up to Kedarnath from Gaurikund (16–17 km), or fly from Guptkashi, Sersi or Phata. Night near the temple.
  7. Day 7: early darshan, walk down, and drive to Guptkashi.
  8. Day 8: Guptkashi to Badrinath via Rudraprayag, Karnaprayag and Joshimath. Kedarnath to Badrinath is about 230 km.
  9. Day 9: Badrinath darshan and Mana village, 4 km away, the last Indian village before the Tibet border.
  10. Day 10: Badrinath to Rudraprayag or Srinagar.
  11. Day 11: back to Rishikesh or Haridwar.
  • Add a spare day (12 days in all) in the monsoon or with older pilgrims.
  • Only Kedarnath and Badrinath (Do Dham)? Cut days 2 to 4 and start with day 5 from Haridwar: about 6 days.

Going by train? Haridwar and Rishikesh are the railheads: see the Indian Railways luggage rules. Flying? Dehradun's Jolly Grant airport is the nearest; check the cabin bag rules for Indian airlines.

What to wear and pack

  • Warm layers in every month: thermals, a sweater or fleece and a warm jacket. Nights at the dhams are cold even in June.
  • A rain jacket or poncho: rain can come any afternoon in the hills.
  • Walking shoes with a good grip, already worn in, and a walking stick for the Kedarnath and Yamunotri walks.
  • Your registration letter, photo ID and medicines in a waterproof pouch.

For foreign visitors

  • Visa: apply for the e-Tourist Visa only at the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in.
  • Every pilgrim must register before the yatra. Keep your passport with you, since it's your photo ID.

Emergency numbers and help

  • Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): 112
  • Health helpline: 104. Ambulance: 108
  • Yatra and tourist helpline (toll-free): 1364
  • Rudraprayag yatra control room: 01364-297878
  • Cyber fraud (fake helicopter sites): 1930

Questions

When will the Char Dham Yatra 2027 start?

The dates are to be announced. Kedarnath's date is usually announced on Mahashivratri and Badrinath's on Basant Panchami. Gangotri and Yamunotri open on Akshaya Tritiya, which panchangs put on 9 May 2027. In 2026 the four temples opened between 19 and 23 April.

When does Char Dham registration 2027 open?

Not announced yet. In 2026 online registration opened on 6 March and offline counters on 17 April. Register only on the official portal, registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in, on WhatsApp 8394833833 or on the 1364 helpline.

Is Char Dham registration free?

Yes, in 2026 it was free. A fee was discussed in February 2026 but not brought in. Check the portal for 2027.

Which documents do I need for the Char Dham Yatra?

Your registration letter with its QR code, a valid photo ID and a passport-size photo for registration, and your medicines and prescriptions. If you're driving, register your vehicle too.

How do I book a Kedarnath helicopter?

Only on IRCTC's official site, heliyatra.irctc.co.in, after you've registered for the yatra. Police have blocked hundreds of fake booking sites, so don't pay anyone else.

How long is the Kedarnath trek?

About 16 to 17 km each way from Gaurikund, and 6 to 8 hours up for most people. Kedarnath is about 3,580 m high.

How many days do I need for the Char Dham Yatra?

About 10 to 12 days from Haridwar by road. Kedarnath and Badrinath alone take about 6 days.

Is the Char Dham Yatra safe for elderly people?

Many older pilgrims go, but the health department asks older people and anyone with heart or lung problems to get a check-up well before the trip, carry their medicines, go slowly and call 104 or 108 if they feel unwell. A helicopter, pony or palki can save the Kedarnath walk.

Can I do the Char Dham Yatra in the monsoon?

You can, but expect stoppages. In 2026 the yatra was stopped on several days in July and September for heavy rain. Keep spare days and check IMD warnings.
