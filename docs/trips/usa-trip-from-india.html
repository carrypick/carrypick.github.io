USA trip from India: the B1/B2 visitor visa, direct flights, a 10-day plan and the best time to go

Updated 22 Nov 2026 · 16 min read

Visa validityUp to 10 years, multiple entries, for Indians
StaySet by the CBP officer when you land, on your I-94

The USA is the big trip many Indians save up for: the skyline and the Statue of Liberty in New York, the lights of Las Vegas and the Grand Canyon, Hollywood and the beaches of Los Angeles, the Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco, and the theme parks of Orlando. It's a long flight, about 15 to 16 hours direct from Delhi, and Indians need a visa with an interview, so plan early. This guide covers the B1/B2 visa, how to check the appointment waiting time, flights, a 10-day plan, the weather by month, US customs rules on food, getting around and food for vegetarians.

US tourist visa for Indians (B1/B2)

Indians need a visa for a holiday in the USA. The visa for tourism is the B-2 visitor visa, usually issued as a combined B-1/B-2 visa for business and tourism, says the US State Department (visitor visa page, read 22 Nov 2026).

  • Step 1: fill in the DS-160 online. This is the online visa application form. You upload your photo while filling it in, and print the confirmation page to take to your interview. Each person, including children, needs their own application.
  • Step 2: pay the fee. The visa application fee is US$185, non-refundable, says the State Department. For Indian citizens there is no extra visa issuance fee for a B-1/B-2 visa, says the State Department's reciprocity table for India. In India you pay in rupees at the consular exchange rate: ₹98 to the dollar from 25 May 2026, says the US Embassy in India (read 22 Nov 2026).
  • Step 3: book an interview. Interviews are held at the US Embassy in New Delhi and the US Consulates General in Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Kolkata. Follow the steps on the US Embassy and Consulates in India's visa page, which links the official appointment system. Fingerprints are taken as part of the process, usually at the interview.
  • Step 4: the interview. A consular officer decides whether you qualify. Take your passport, the DS-160 confirmation page and the fee receipt.
  • Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your stay in the USA.
  • Don't buy tickets until you have the visa. The State Department says there's no guarantee you'll get one.
  • You can't work on a visitor visa.

US visa appointment waiting time

Waiting times for interview appointments in India change often, by city and by season. Check the current wait for New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Kolkata on the State Department's official visa appointment wait time tool: pick the city and the visa type (B1/B2). We don't quote a number here because it changes. The State Department says to apply early.

What documents do you need?

  • Required: a passport valid for travel to the USA, the DS-160 confirmation page, the fee receipt, and a printed photo if the photo upload failed.
  • May be asked for: proof of the purpose of your trip, that you'll leave the USA after it, and that you can pay for it. Proof of your job and family ties may be enough to show you'll return, says the State Department.
  • An invitation letter is not needed, says the State Department. If you bring one, it isn't one of the things used to decide.

How long is the visa valid, and how long can you stay?

  • Validity: a B-1/B-2 visa for Indian citizens can be valid for up to 120 months (10 years), with multiple entries, says the State Department's reciprocity table for India.
  • Stay: the visa only lets you travel to a US airport and ask to enter. The CBP officer at the airport decides whether to let you in and for how long, and records it on your Form I-94. You can check your I-94 online on CBP's I-94 website.
  • Leave on time. Staying past the date on your I-94 voids your visa and can stop you getting US visas in future, says the State Department.
  • A valid visa in an old, expired passport is still valid. Travel with both passports.

Can Indians use ESTA?

No. ESTA is only for travel under the Visa Waiver Program, and India is not on the program's list of countries, says the State Department (read 22 Nov 2026). Indians need the B1/B2 visa even for a short holiday, and even with a UK or Schengen visa.

Flights from India to the USA

  • There are direct flights from India. Air India flies from Delhi and Mumbai to New York (JFK), and from Delhi to San Francisco and Chicago. American Airlines flies from Delhi to New York (JFK) and United flies from Delhi to Newark, near New York, say the airports' airline lists (Wikipedia, read 22 Nov 2026).
  • From other Indian cities, fly one-stop via Delhi or Mumbai, or through the Gulf (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha) or Europe (London, Frankfurt, Paris, Amsterdam).
  • A direct flight from Delhi to New York takes about 15 to 16 hours. You land the same day you leave because of the time difference.
  • Time difference: India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of New York and 13 hours 30 minutes ahead of Los Angeles, San Francisco and Las Vegas in the US winter. From mid-March to early November, during US daylight saving time, India is one hour less ahead (9 hours 30 minutes and 12 hours 30 minutes).
  • Baggage: check the limits by airline in the international baggage rules and the carry-on size by airline guide.
  • Fares go up for the Indian summer holidays, Christmas and New Year. Book early, but only after you have your visa.

  • New York is cold from December to February, around freezing at night, with rain or snow on about 10 days a month. Pack a proper winter coat.

Getting around the USA

  • Between the coasts, fly. Domestic flights are the only practical way to cover the long distances. Low fares often don't include a checked bag.
  • On the East Coast, trains run between Washington, DC, New York and Boston.
  • In New York, use the subway. You can pay at the subway gates and on buses by tapping a contactless card or phone.
  • In the West, many visitors rent a car. Driving is on the right. The rules for driving on an Indian licence differ by state, so ask the rental company and get an International Driving Permit from your RTO before you go.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing apps work in every big city.

Food for Indian travellers: veg and Jain tips

The USA is easy for vegetarians, and Indian food is easy to find:

  • Indian restaurants and grocery shops are in every big city: Jackson Heights in Queens and Edison in New Jersey near New York, the Bay Area near San Francisco, and around Los Angeles.
  • Vegetarian options are on almost every menu, and "vegetarian" and "vegan" are widely understood. Ask whether soups and rice use chicken or beef stock.
  • Easy veg choices: pizza, pasta, burritos and bowls with beans, salads, veggie burgers, and Thai, Chinese and Middle Eastern food (falafel, hummus).
  • Jain travellers: most restaurant food uses onion and garlic. Ask for food without them, or carry ready-to-eat food.
  • Carrying food from India? Declare it. CBP says all agricultural items must be declared on arrival and are inspected. Undeclared prohibited items are taken away and you can be fined. Check what you can take in our can I carry it on a flight? list.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in the USA

You have three ways to stay online in the USA:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at phone shops and some airport shops.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Money and rules to know

  • The currency is the US dollar (US$, USD). Cards are accepted almost everywhere, including contactless. Carry a little cash for tips.
  • Tipping is expected in restaurants, taxis and hotels. Check the bill: some restaurants add a service charge.
  • Prices usually don't include sales tax, which is added at the till and differs by state.
  • Cash from India: RBI lets you buy up to US$3,000 in foreign currency notes per trip, and you can carry up to ₹25,000 in Indian notes. Put the rest on a forex card. See how much cash you can carry abroad.
  • Over US$10,000? Report it. You must report cash and other monetary instruments over US$10,000 to CBP when you enter or leave the USA, says CBP.
  • Plugs: the USA uses 120 V and flat two-pin or three-pin plugs, different from India's. Read our power bank and travel adapter guide.
  • Health insurance: healthcare in the USA is very expensive. Buy travel insurance with medical cover before you fly.

What to wear in the USA

  • New York in winter (December to February): a warm, windproof coat, sweaters, thermal innerwear, gloves, a woolly cap and waterproof shoes.
  • Spring and autumn: layers and a light jacket, and comfortable shoes: you'll walk a lot in New York.
  • Summer: light clothes, sunglasses and sunscreen, especially for Las Vegas and the Grand Canyon. Carry a sweater for San Francisco, which is cool even in July.

Help in an emergency

  • Emergency number in the USA: 911.
  • Embassy of India, Washington, DC: contact details are on its website. India also has consulates in several US cities, including New York and San Francisco.
  • Keep a photo of your passport, your visa and your insurance papers on your phone, and save your hotel's address.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for the USA?

Yes. Indians need a B-2 tourist visa, usually issued as a B-1/B-2 visitor visa. You fill in the DS-160 online, pay the fee and attend an interview at the US Embassy or a US consulate in India.

What is the US tourist visa fee for Indians?

US$185 for the B-1/B-2 visa application, says the US State Department, and it's non-refundable. Indians pay no extra issuance fee for this visa. In India the fee is paid in rupees at the consular exchange rate.

What is the US visa waiting time in India?

It changes by city and by month, so we don't quote a number. Check the current wait for New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Kolkata on the State Department's official visa appointment wait time tool, and apply early.

Can Indians apply for ESTA?

No. ESTA is only for citizens of Visa Waiver Program countries, and India isn't one. Indians need the B1/B2 visa.

How long is a US tourist visa valid for Indians?

Up to 10 years (120 months), with multiple entries, says the State Department's reciprocity table for India. How long you can stay on each visit is decided by the CBP officer when you land and is shown on your I-94.

Are there direct flights from India to the USA?

Yes. Air India flies direct from Delhi and Mumbai to New York, and from Delhi to San Francisco and Chicago. American Airlines flies Delhi to New York and United flies Delhi to Newark.

How many days are enough for a USA trip?

Ten days is enough for New York, Las Vegas and California if you fly between them. With two weeks, add Washington, DC, Niagara Falls or Orlando. With a week, pick one coast.

Can I carry food from India to the USA?

Declare all food on arrival. CBP inspects agricultural items, and prohibited items that you don't declare are taken away and can bring a fine.

What is the time difference between India and the USA?

In the US winter, India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of New York and 13 hours 30 minutes ahead of California. During US daylight saving time (mid-March to early November), it's 9 hours 30 minutes and 12 hours 30 minutes.
