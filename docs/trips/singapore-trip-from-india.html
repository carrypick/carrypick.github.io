Singapore trip from India: visa, a 5-day itinerary and the weather in December

Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 14 min read

Visa timeUsually 3 to 5 working days

Singapore is one of the easiest trips abroad from India. Direct flights leave from many Indian cities, the city is clean and safe, and the MRT trains take you almost everywhere. Indians need a visa, but it is applied for through an agent in India and usually comes in 3 to 5 working days. This guide covers the visa, the SG Arrival Card, flights, a simple 4 or 5-day itinerary, the weather in December, Christmas events, getting around and the rules to know.

Singapore visa for Indians

Indian passport holders need a visa for Singapore, says ICA, Singapore's immigration authority. You can't get a visa at the airport.

  • How to apply: through an authorised visa agent appointed by the High Commission of Singapore in New Delhi. The agent sends your papers to the High Commission online. The list of agents is a PDF on the High Commission's visa page.
  • Visa fee: ₹2,100 per application. It isn't refunded, even if the visa is refused.
  • Agent's fee: agents may charge a service fee of up to ₹1,300 on top, says the High Commission.
  • Time: usually 3 to 5 working days, not counting the day you apply, weekends and holidays. Some take longer, so apply early for Christmas and New Year.
  • No flight ticket needed to apply. If you buy one first and the visa is refused, the loss is yours.
  • A visa doesn't guarantee entry. The officer at the airport decides. You may be asked for your return ticket and proof that you have enough money for the stay.

Documents for the Singapore visa

From the High Commission of Singapore in New Delhi (checked 21 Nov 2026):

  • Form 14A, filled in and signed by you.
  • Your passport, valid for at least 6 months on the day you enter Singapore.
  • One colour photo, 35 mm wide and 45 mm high, taken in the last 3 months, on a plain white background, with no border. Don't wear white clothes in it.
  • Sometimes a letter of introduction (Form V39A) from a local contact in Singapore. The agent will tell you if you need one.
  • Papers in Hindi must be translated into English.

Only passing through Singapore?

Indians may use the 96-hour Visa Free Transit Facility, says ICA. You need an onward air, ferry or cruise ticket out of Singapore within 96 hours, and a visa or long-term pass valid for at least 1 month from Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, the UK or the USA. You can't extend your stay on it.

SG Arrival Card: fill it before you fly

  • Every visitor must submit the SG Arrival Card online within 3 days before arriving, counting the day you land.
  • It is free on ICA's website and the MyICA app. ICA warns that it doesn't endorse sites that charge a fee to file it for you.
  • Your visit pass is electronic. The officer at the checkpoint issues an e-Pass, and it decides how many days you may stay, not your visa, says ICA.

A simple 5-day Singapore itinerary

Most first trips from India spend 4 or 5 days. This plan keeps one area per day, so you spend less time on trains.

DayPlanEvening
1Land at Changi. See the Rain Vortex waterfall at Jewel Changi Airport, then check inMarina Bay: the Merlion and the bay lights
2Gardens by the Bay: the Flower Dome and Cloud Forest, then the Supertree GroveThe Supertree light show at Gardens by the Bay
3Sentosa: Universal Studios Singapore, or the beaches and the cable carDinner at Sentosa or VivoCity
4Singapore Zoo or the bird park in the northNight Safari
5Little India and Chinatown in the morning, shopping on Orchard Road, then fly home
  • Only 4 days? Join days 4 and 5: do the zoo in the morning and Little India in the evening, or skip the zoo.
  • With kids? Keep day 3 for Universal Studios and add the S.E.A. Aquarium on Sentosa. Kids get tired in the heat, so plan indoor places for the afternoon.
  • A 6 or 7-day trip? Add a slow day at the beaches of Sentosa, the river cruise at Clarke Quay, or a day trip by ferry.
  • Book theme parks and the Night Safari online for December. Weekends and school holidays sell out.
  • Plan for rain. In December, keep museums, malls and the Gardens by the Bay domes for the wet afternoons.

Singapore weather in December

Long-term averages (1991–2020) at Changi from the Meteorological Service Singapore:

MonthRainRain days
NovemberAbout 252 mmAbout 19
DecemberAbout 332 mm, the wettest monthAbout 19
JanuaryAbout 222 mmAbout 13
FebruaryAbout 105 mm, the driest monthAbout 9
  • It's warm and humid every day. December averages about 30.5 °C by day and 24 °C at night, with humidity of about 85%. Pack cotton and linen.
  • December is the wettest month, but most showers are heavy and short. Carry an umbrella or a poncho and keep going.
  • The Northeast Monsoon runs from December to early March. It brings rainy spells and sometimes windy days.
  • Malls, the MRT and cinemas are cold. Carry one light layer.

Singapore in December: Christmas and New Year

  • Christmas on A Great Street: the Christmas lights on Orchard Road run from 17 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, says the Orchard Road Business Association.
  • Christmas Wonderland at Gardens by the Bay: from 27 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, 6:30 PM to 11 PM (last entry 10:15 PM). Entry is by ticket, for a chosen time slot. Buy online; booths at the gate add a S$3 charge.
  • Marina Bay Singapore Countdown: the New Year's Eve party at Marina Bay is "coming in December 2026", says its official site. Details weren't out on 21 Nov 2026. Marina Bay is very crowded that night, so plan your way back early.
  • Singapore Marathon on 5 and 6 December 2026: roads near Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay close in the early morning, and the Gardens' car parks close until 8 AM on 5 Dec and 11 AM on 6 Dec.
  • Hotels cost the most from Christmas to New Year. Book early.

Getting around Singapore

  • The MRT (metro) reaches the airport, Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, HarbourFront (the gateway to Sentosa), Little India, Chinatown and Orchard Road. It is the fastest way around. For the zoo and Night Safari, take a bus or taxi from the nearest station.
  • Pay by tapping a contactless bank card at the MRT gates and on buses, through SimplyGo. LTA charges an admin fee on cards issued outside Singapore, so ask your Indian bank about its foreign transaction charges too.
  • Singapore Tourist Pass: unlimited rides on the MRT and most buses for S$17 (1 day), S$24 (2 days), S$29 (3 days), S$37 (4 days) or S$45 (5 days), says SimplyGo. Buy it at selected SimplyGo ticket offices, service centres and kiosks.
  • EZ-Link card: S$10, of which S$5 is travel value and S$5 is the card cost (not refunded). Top up with at least S$2 in cash; cards issued outside Singapore aren't accepted at the top-up kiosks.
  • From Changi Airport to the city by MRT: ride from Changi Airport station (CG2) to Tanah Merah (EW4) and change to the East West Line, or to Expo (CG1/DT35) and change to the Downtown Line. The first train leaves at about 5:31 AM (5:59 AM on Sundays and holidays).
  • By taxi from Changi: about 30 minutes to the city, usually S$25 to S$45, plus an airport surcharge of S$6 (S$8 from 5 PM to midnight), says Changi Airport.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing apps are easy to find. They cost much more than the MRT, but help with luggage and late nights.
  • Walk a lot. Wear comfortable shoes. Many places are linked by covered walkways and underground malls.

Money, shopping and GST refunds

  • The currency is the Singapore dollar (S$). Cards are accepted almost everywhere. Keep a little cash for hawker centres and small shops.
  • Carrying cash: if you carry more than S$20,000 in cash (or the same in other currencies), declare it online on Form NP 727 before you arrive or leave, says ICA. See how much cash you can carry abroad for India's own rules.
  • GST refund for tourists: you can claim back GST on shopping if you spend at least S$100 (including GST) at one shop, says Singapore's tax office (IRAS). Up to 3 same-day bills from the same shop count. Show your original passport when you pay (a photo or photocopy isn't accepted), then claim at the eTRS kiosk at the airport before you leave, within 2 months of buying. Shops and refund agents may take a handling fee.

Phone, SIM and eSIM in Singapore

You have three ways to stay online in Singapore:

  • Roaming on your own SIM: the easiest, but often the costliest. Check your mobile company's international pack before you fly.
  • A local SIM card: sold at the airport and in phone shops. Shops usually ask for your passport to register it.
  • An eSIM: a data plan you buy online and install on your phone before you go, with no plastic card. Your phone must support eSIM and must not be locked to one network. On an iPhone, look in Settings > Mobile Service (or Cellular). On Android, look in Settings > SIM or Connections.

Install the eSIM at home over Wi-Fi and switch it on when you land. Most travel eSIMs give data only, with no local number, so keep your home SIM on for calls and bank OTP texts (roaming charges may apply).

Rules to know in Singapore

  • Chewing gum is banned, except dental and medicinal gum approved by Singapore's Health Sciences Authority, says Singapore Customs. Leave it at home.
  • Vapes and e-cigarettes are banned. Don't carry them, even in your bag.
  • No duty-free cigarettes. Singapore has no duty-free allowance for cigarettes or tobacco. Declare them at the Red Channel and pay the tax.
  • Keep it clean. Don't litter, and don't smoke where signs say no. Eating and drinking aren't allowed on the MRT.
  • Medicines: carry them in the original pack with your prescription.

What to wear in Singapore

  • Light cotton clothes, shorts, T-shirts and dresses for the heat.
  • One light jacket or shawl for cold malls, cinemas and the MRT.
  • Walking shoes and flip-flops for the pool or the beach.
  • For temples and mosques, cover your shoulders and knees.
  • An umbrella or poncho, sunscreen, sunglasses and a cap.

Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a visa, says ICA. Apply through an authorised visa agent appointed by the High Commission of Singapore in New Delhi. You can't get one on arrival.

What is the Singapore visa fee for Indians?

The visa processing fee is ₹2,100 per application, and it isn't refunded if the visa is refused. Authorised visa agents may add a service fee of up to ₹1,300, says the High Commission of Singapore in New Delhi.

How many days does a Singapore visa take for Indians?

Usually 3 to 5 working days, not counting the day you apply, weekends and public holidays. Some applications take longer, so apply a couple of weeks before a December trip.

What documents are needed for a Singapore visa from India?

Form 14A signed by you, a passport valid for at least 6 months on the day you enter Singapore, and one colour photo 35 x 45 mm on a white background taken in the last 3 months. Sometimes a letter from a local contact (Form V39A) is also asked for.

How many days are enough for Singapore?

Four or five days cover Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, Sentosa, the zoo or Night Safari, and Little India and Chinatown. Add a day or two for a slower trip with kids.

Is December a good time to visit Singapore?

Yes, if you don't mind rain. December is the wettest month, with rain on about 19 days, but showers are usually short. You get the Christmas lights on Orchard Road, Christmas Wonderland at Gardens by the Bay and the New Year's Eve countdown at Marina Bay. It is also the busiest and costliest time, so book early.

What is the weather in Singapore in December?

About 30.5 °C by day and 24 °C at night, with about 332 mm of rain on about 19 days, on long-term averages from the Meteorological Service Singapore. It's humid, about 85%.

What is the time difference between India and Singapore?

Singapore is 2 hours 30 minutes ahead of India.

Which cities in India have direct flights to Singapore?

Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Kochi, Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli, Vijayawada and Amritsar, among others, on Singapore Airlines, IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express and Scoot.

What is the SG Arrival Card?

An online form every visitor must submit within 3 days before arriving in Singapore. It's free on ICA's website and the MyICA app. Don't pay sites that offer to file it for you.
